# WebSocket gateway design

Date: 2026-11-20 · Status: draft for review (concerns 7, 11 and 12 are open for the product owner)
Intent: docs/intents/websocket-gateway.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-038

## Goal
A person with Toki open must see what happens in their conversations without reloading: a changed topic, a person added or removed, an archived channel, and (with the message API) every new message. Today the server only answers requests. This work adds the WebSocket address, the check that the connection belongs to a signed-in person and ends with the session, the in-memory map of who listens to which conversation, and the push of each event of the log to the people it concerns, once and in order. It also gives the channel changes their events, through the seam that the channel API left empty.

## Scope
- In:
  - The address `GET /ws`, its handshake, the session check and the Origin check (R1 to R6).
  - The first frame `ready`, the map of conversations each connected person listens to, and the rules that keep it right (R7 to R11).
  - The frames and event kinds written in `packages/shared`, and the one function `toEventObject` that makes an event object from a log row, with `eventObjectSchema`, the audience of each kind, the order and the once-only delivery (R12 to R19).
  - The event handler behind `recordChange`: the events of every change that reaches it (the nine channel changes, a sent message, the first message of a DM) with their payloads, and, only if the product owner accepts it (concern 11), of a newly opened group (R20).
  - Closing a connection: an ended session, a dead connection, a slow one, a flood of frames, a person with too many connections, a server that stops, a failure at the handshake (R21 to R26, R30).
  - The live-only notice of a changed person (R17) and the way later tickets add a live notice or a client frame (R27).
  - The `/ws` entry of the web dev proxy and the change to the "no route outside `/api/v1`" rule, which is a change of `apps/server/src/app.ts` made by this ticket (R1, R28).
  - The dependency `@fastify/websocket` (added by this ticket, Design, "Library") and the export of `checkSession` and a read of session health from `auth` (R2, R23).
  - Server tests, including tests with real connections.
- Out (not now):
  - The tables of the event log, `recordEvent` (the only writer, with the `pg_notify` inside it), `readEventsAfter`, `getLastEventNumber`, the numbering with no gap, the pruning after 7 days: TOKI-036 (the `realtime` module, its "The event contract"). This work writes none of them and adds no second writer or reader of the log (R14).
  - The message API, the system lines, the handler list (`createRecordChange`, `ChangeHandler`), `MessageEntry`, and hiding a new DM from the other person until its first message: TOKI-037. It calls `recordChange` with `message_created` and `direct_conversation_appeared`; this work turns those calls into events (R20), adds the kind `message.created` to its registry and adds the DM condition to the read of R8. This ticket needs TOKI-037 (Plan changes).
  - Everything on the client: opening the socket, the retry delays (1, 2, 5, 10, 30 seconds, D-129), answering `ping`, the offline banner, fetching what was missed, the reload after 7 days away and what to do on each close code: TOKI-039. Applying events to the cache and the optimistic send: TOKI-040. This work ships no client code.
  - The request that returns the events a client missed: TOKI-039. It uses the same audience rule (R15) and the same read of the log.
  - The typing signal and presence: TOKI-044 and TOKI-055. They use the mechanism of R27.
  - Activity items and push notifications: TOKI-065 and TOKI-074.
  - The production proxy rule for `/ws` (Caddy): TOKI-084. The measure of the 150 ms target (NFR-13): TOKI-089.
  - More than one server process. The design does not stand in its way (R14), but nothing is built or tested for it (D-009).
  - The audience of a role change and whether people outside a public channel hear about it: open questions for the product owner (concerns 7 and 12); the requirements R15 and R17 carry a marked proposal for each.

## Requirements
Handshake and authentication
- R1. `GET /ws` is registered by the gateway and is the only route outside `/api/v1` besides `/healthz`. The code on main refuses every other route: the `onRoute` hook of `apps/server/src/app.ts` throws `Route <url> is outside /api/v1` unless the url is `/healthz` or starts with `/api/v1/`, and the route-list test of TOKI-004 R10 lists only `/healthz`; this ticket changes both (change requests, Plan changes) to "exactly `/healthz` and `/ws`". Check: the app answers an upgrade at `/ws`; registering a route at `/outside` still throws; the route-list test lists `/ws` and fails on any other path outside `/api/v1`.
- R2. The connection is authenticated by the same check as a request: `checkSession(db, request)` of `apps/server/src/modules/auth/sessions.ts` (TOKI-022 R44), so a cookie session is accepted only from the cookie and a token session only from `Authorization: Bearer`, an ended session, an idle one and a deactivated account are refused with the codes of TOKI-022 R25 and R27, and a valid check sets `last_used_at` like any request. The function is not exported today (`modules/auth/api.ts` exports `createSession`, `endSessionOfRequest`, `endSessionsOfUser`, `getLastUsedAtByUser`, `onSessionsEnded`, `reportSessionsEnded`, and `index.ts` re-exports `api.ts`): this ticket adds `checkSession` to `api.ts`, and `app.ts` passes it to the gateway as a dependency (R29). The connection keeps the account id and the session id. Check: a cookie session and a token session connect; a token sent as a cookie and a cookie sent as a header do not; the export test of `auth` lists `checkSession`.
- R3. A request without a valid session still completes the upgrade, and the server then closes the connection at once with close code 4401 and, as the close reason, the code the REST answer would carry: `unauthenticated`, `session_ended` or `account_deactivated`. It sends no `ready` frame and no event, and the connection is never registered. Reason: a browser does not show the status of a refused upgrade, so a client could not tell a signed-out person from a network error (concern 2). A database error during the check is not a session problem: R30. Check: no session, an unknown secret, an ended session, an idle session and a deactivated account each give 4401 with the right reason and no frame.
- R4. A request whose `Origin` header is present and different from `appOrigin` is refused before the upgrade with status 403, the code `origin_not_allowed` and the error body of TOKI-004, and no connection opens. A request with no `Origin` (an app with a token) or with the app's own origin is accepted. Reason: a `GET` is exempt from the Origin rule of TOKI-022 R46, but a page of another site could open a socket with the person's cookie. Check: three requests, one of each case.
- R5. The route reads no query string for authentication: a request with `?token=<valid token>` and no header or cookie closes with 4401 `unauthenticated`. No log line holds a cookie, a token, a query string or an email address. The gateway writes one `info` line when a connection is accepted and one when it closes, each with the connection id, and the close line with the close code, the reason and the duration. Check: the captured log of a full test has no secret value, and holds one open and one close line per connection.
- R6. A plain `GET /ws` that is not an upgrade answers status 400 with the code `bad_request` in the error format of TOKI-004. It is produced by the plain `handler` of the same route (the library's route form has a `handler` for ordinary requests and a `wsHandler` for upgrades); this is an assumption about `@fastify/websocket` that the first test of the build checks against the installed version, and if the library answers a non-upgrade request itself, an `onRequest` hook of that route that tests the `Upgrade` header gives the 400 (concern 13). Check: one request.

Connection start and the map of who listens
- R7. After a valid check the server sends exactly one `ready` frame, `{ "type": "ready", "eventNumber": N }`, before any other frame. `N` is the number the connection starts after: every event with a number up to `N` is not sent on this connection (the client gets what it missed from its own number to `N` by the request of TOKI-039), and every event after `N` that concerns the person is sent on it, once, in order. For the first connection of a person `N` is the `M` of the snapshot of R8; for a later connection it is the number up to which the person's map is already applied. Check: a test with a pause between the snapshot and the registration (the seam of the Design, "Connection start") writes three events, one before the snapshot, one between the snapshot and the registration and one after; the connection gets `ready` with `N` equal to the last number the registered map includes, the events up to `N` are not sent, and every event above `N` is sent once, to the person's map in its state at that event.
- R8. The map of a person is the set of conversation ids from `listConversationIdsOfUser(client, userId)`, a new read of the `conversations` module that this ticket adds in `conversations/facts.ts` and exports from `conversations/index.ts`. It is not built on main (TOKI-028 R18 does not list it; only concern 8 of that spec names it). It returns the id of every conversation of which the person has a member row, an archived channel included (its members keep hearing: the restore, F26, and the strip "This channel was just archived"), and no other. The set and the event number `M` are read in one transaction, `READ ONLY` at `REPEATABLE READ`, with `getLastEventNumber(tx, workspaceId)` of TOKI-036 R19 (`workspaceId` is the one of the account; `realtime` may not import the bootstrap's `readEventNumber`). The transaction takes no gateway lock (Design, "Connection start"). The map has its own number, `mapNumber`, which is `M`; a map changes only for an event with a number above `mapNumber` (R9). A second connection of a person who already has a map reuses it and reads nothing from the database. The map of a person is dropped when their last connection closes. The condition that a DM is in the set of the person who did not open it only after its first message (F25, D-091) is added by this ticket with the `listLastMessageTimes` read of `messages` that `app.ts` injects (TOKI-037 R19 states the same rule for the bootstrap read and asks for it here, Plan changes); the set of the opener always has the DM, a group and a channel are not filtered. Check: a person who is in a public channel, a private channel, a DM they opened, a DM another person opened and that has no message, and an archived channel, and not in a second public channel and a second private channel, gets the first three and the fifth; after the first message of that DM the map gains it through R9 and the read gives the same set; a second connection runs no statement; the map is gone after both close.
- R9. The map follows the log, in number order, and an event changes the map before it is delivered. A conversation-scope event that gives people a conversation (`conversation.created`, `member.joined`, `members.added`) adds it to the map of each person named in its data, so the new member receives it; a user-scope event `conversation.appeared` adds `data.conversation.id` to the map of its person. A conversation-scope event that takes a person out (`member.left`, `member.removed`) removes the conversation from that person's map first, so the leaving person does not receive it, and the user-scope event `conversation.left` (R20) is what tells the leaving person: it is delivered to them and removes the conversation if it is still there. An event whose number is not above the `mapNumber` of a person changes that person's map by nothing (the snapshot of R8 already includes it). This is the rule of TOKI-036 ("Events": a conversation event goes to the members at the moment of delivery, and the person who has just left is addressed by a user event). Check: Lan is added to a private channel while connected: Lan receives `members.added` and every later event of the channel and none before it; Minh is removed: Minh receives `conversation.left` and no event of the channel after it, and the others receive `member.removed`; an event at or below a map's number leaves the map as it was; a map is the same as `listConversationIdsOfUser` for every connected person after each of the twelve channel routes of TOKI-029 (a test compares them).
- R10. A person has at most 20 open connections (several tabs and devices). The 21st is accepted and the oldest of that person is closed with code 4430 and the reason `too_many_connections`. A client does not reconnect after 4430 (the close table; TOKI-039 builds it), so the tab that was closed does not close the next oldest in a loop; that tab stays offline until the person reloads it. Reason: a connection that died without a close (a closed laptop) is noticed only by R24, and a reconnect loop must not pile them up. The number is a technical choice of this spec (concern 5), and the no-reconnect rule is concern 10. Check: 21 connections of one person leave 20, the first one closed with 4430.
- R11. The registry of connections holds, for each connection, only: the connection id, the account id, the workspace id, the session id, the time of the last frame received, the time of the last `ping` sent, and the socket. It holds no cookie, token or secret. Check: a test reads every field of the registry and finds no value of the session secret.

Events and delivery
- R12. Frames are JSON text, one object each, with a `type`. The server sends `ready` (R7), `event`, `live` and `ping`; the client sends `pong` and the frames that later tickets register (R27). The schemas of every frame, the registry of event kinds and the schema of `data` for each kind are in `packages/shared/src/realtime/` (`frames.ts`, `events.ts`; `frames.ts` exports `eventObjectSchema` on its own, the envelope of an event (`number` an integer, `kind` any string, `conversationId` a string or null, `createdAt` an ISO 8601 string, `data` an object), and builds the `event` frame from it, while the registry of `events.ts` checks `data` only for the kinds the handler writes and is not part of the frame schema that a client reads, so a client older than the server accepts a kind it does not know; a catch-up request (TOKI-039) reuses `toEventObject` and `eventObjectSchema`; TOKI-036 adds `event-log.ts` to the same folder), used by the server to build and by the tests to check. An `event` frame is `{ "type": "event", "event": { "number", "kind", "conversationId", "createdAt", "data" } }`: `number` is the number of the log row, unchanged; `kind` is the `type` column of the row and `data` its `payload` (the log and `recordEvent` say `scope`, `type` and `payload`; only this frame says `kind` and `data`, and the mapping is made by `toEventObject(row: StoredEvent): EventObject` in `apps/server/src/modules/realtime/event-object.ts`, which no other code duplicates, and `StoredEvent` is the row that `readEventsAfter` returns); `conversationId` is null for an event that has none; `createdAt` is ISO 8601. A `live` frame is `{ "type": "live", "kind", "data" }` and has no number. `ping` is `{ "type": "ping" }`. A client built earlier ignores a `type` or a `kind` it does not know, so adding one is allowed within a version. The registry holds the kinds this ticket's handler writes: `conversation.created`, `member.joined`, `members.added`, `member.left`, `member.removed`, `conversation.left`, `conversation.updated`, `conversation.archived`, `conversation.restored`, `settings.changed`, `message.created`, `conversation.appeared`. Check: every frame sent in every test is parsed with its schema; the `data` of each event kind of R20 is parsed with the schema of its kind; the frame of a log row has `kind` equal to its `type` and `data` equal to its `payload`; a test adds a kind to the registry and a test client ignores it; `eventObjectSchema` accepts an event of a kind that is not in the registry and refuses a `number` that is not an integer; `toEventObject` is exported from `realtime/index.ts`.
- R13. The gateway sends an event only after the transaction that wrote it has committed, and never sends the event of a rolled-back transaction. Check: a change that writes an event and then rolls back sends no frame and leaves the gateway's position unchanged; the same change committed sends one.
- R14. The gateway finds new events by reading the log after its own position, not from a callback of the writer. It writes no event and adds no write or read of the log: every event is written by `recordEvent(tx, input)` of TOKI-036 (R15 there), which numbers the event and sends `pg_notify('toki_events', <workspaceId>)` in the same transaction so that the signal arrives with the commit, and the gateway reads with `readEventsAfter(client, workspaceId, after, limit)` of TOKI-036 (R26 there; TOKI-039 reuses it). Nothing outside `modules/realtime/` calls `recordEvent` or `appendEvent` (TOKI-036 R25 tests it; this ticket's handler is inside `realtime`). The gateway holds one `LISTEN toki_events` connection (R29); on each signal, and after the listening connection is lost and restored, it drains the workspace of the signal (all workspaces with a connection after a restore): it reads in batches of 200, delivers each event in order, and moves the position of that workspace after each batch; the map changes and the position of a batch are applied in one synchronous step. The position of a workspace is made when its first connection starts, from the `M` of R8 (read with `getLastEventNumber`), so nothing older is replayed, and is dropped when the workspace has no connection left, so a later first connection takes its own `M` and never drains from a stale position (the log may have been pruned meanwhile). One drain runs at a time; a signal that arrives during a drain makes it run once more. A drain also runs from the sweep of R24 when 30 seconds passed since the last one, so a lost signal is repaired. Check: A appends event 1 and stays open, B waits for the lock of TOKI-036 R16 to append 2; nothing is sent; A commits; the gateway sends 1; B commits; it sends 2; the listening connection is ended from the database, an event is written, and it is delivered after the listener returns; with no signal, a sweep 30 seconds later delivers it; events written before the first connection are not sent; after every connection of a workspace closes and 7 days of events are pruned (the clock moved, `pruneEvents` run), a new first connection starts at its own `M` and sends no old event; the gateway's files contain no call of `recordEvent`, `appendEvent` or a second read of `events`.
- R15. The audience of an event comes from its scope in the log (TOKI-036 R13: `workspace`, `conversation`, `user`): a conversation event goes to every connected person whose map holds the conversation (R8, R9); a user event goes to every connection of that person; a workspace event goes to every connection of that workspace. A connection receives an event at most once, and the events of one connection arrive in increasing number order. A person who is not in a conversation never receives a frame that holds its id, name, topic, description, members or any event of it, an Admin who is not a member included. Proposal, not decided (concern 12): this also holds for a public channel, so a person who has not joined it hears nothing of its creation, rename or archive, and an open Browse list shows the change after a reload. Check: a scenario of the twelve channel routes and a DM with three connected people (a member, an Admin who is not a member, a person in another conversation); the frames of the two outsiders, recorded for the whole scenario, hold no id, name or topic of the private channel; an event is received once per connection (two tabs of one person get it once each).
- R16. A user-scope event is sent to every connection of that person and to nobody else. This work uses it for `settings.changed` (R20): a change of the person's own level, mute or star reaches their other tabs and devices (D-142, D-143: the values are the person's own and nobody else sees them). Check: Sam changes the star in one tab; Sam's second tab receives `settings.changed` and Lan, a member of the same channel, receives nothing.
- R17. When the `members` module reports a changed person through `onMemberChanged` (TOKI-024 R40: a role change, a deactivation, a reactivation), the gateway sends one `live` frame of the kind `person.updated` with `data` `{ "userId", "role", "status" }` of the target after the change. Proposal, not decided (concern 7): it goes to every connection of the workspace. The frame has no number and is not in the log, because the change is reported after its commit and not inside its transaction. A frame that is lost (a connection down at that moment) is repaired by the next load, as TOKI-024 concern 8 states: the server reads the role on every request. Check: an Admin changes a role; every connected person receives one `person.updated`; a connection opened after it receives none; the log has no new row.
- R18. The cost of one event does not grow with the number of people it does not concern. Delivering an event runs no database statement and does not scan the registry: the gateway keeps, next to the map of each person (person to conversations), a reverse index, conversation id to the set of connections that listen to it, and an index person to connections, both changed by the same step that changes a map (R9) and when a connection opens or closes. The audience of a conversation event is read from the reverse index, of a user event from the person index, and only a workspace event or a `publishLive` for the workspace visits every connection (R27). The text of the frame is built once and the same string is sent to every recipient. Reading the log costs one statement per batch. Check: with 200 connected people in one channel and 200 in other conversations (fake sockets that count their sends), one event of the channel gives 200 sends of one string, one call to the serialiser, no visit of a connection outside the 200 (a counter on the registry), and, counted with the wrapper of TOKI-031 R14, exactly one statement for the read of the batch and none for delivery. There is no assertion about milliseconds: the 150 ms target is measured by TOKI-089 (concern 4).
- R19. The gateway never queues events for a slow connection. Before each send it reads the socket's buffered amount; when it is above 1 MiB the connection is closed with code 4408 and the reason `slow_consumer`, and the event is not sent. The other connections are not delayed by it. The client reconnects and reads what it missed from its own number (TOKI-039). Check: a socket whose buffered amount is above the limit is closed with 4408 and the next connection of another person receives the event; a socket under the limit is not closed.

Conversation events
- R20. The events of a change. This work builds one handler for the list of handlers that `recordChange` runs inside the caller's transaction (TOKI-037 R18: `createRecordChange(handlers)` and `ChangeHandler` in `conversations/changes.ts`, written by TOKI-037; `app.ts` builds the list). The order is TOKI-036's and TOKI-037's: the system-line handler of TOKI-037 first, which inserts the line and returns it as `change.message`, and this event handler last, because it takes the counter lock at its first `recordEvent` and no lock may follow it (TOKI-036 R18). For each `Change` it is called with, it writes the events below with `recordEvent` of TOKI-036 (the fields `scope`, `type`, `payload`; `payload` is the `data` of the frame), after reading what it needs in the same transaction with `readConversationForEvents` (below), a read that takes no lock that can wait and runs before the first `recordEvent` of the call. The `Change` type is defined in `conversations/changes.ts`; `realtime` imports no other module (R29), so the handler is typed with a structural type `EventChange` of its own, whose fields are the ones it reads (`type`, `actorId`, `conversationId`, `userIds`, `at`, `fields?`, `message?`; a later ticket whose change carries another field adds it to `EventChange` as an optional field, see "Rows of later tickets" below), and `app.ts` passes the handler where a `ChangeHandler` is expected, which the compiler checks. A call that does not happen (a repeat, `changed` false, a refusal) writes no event. The handler is a table from the change `type` to one function that returns the events of that change, in `conversation-events.ts`; a `type` with no row writes no event. Events in the order written:

  | Change | Events (kind, scope, `data`) |
  |---|---|
  | `channel_created` | `conversation.created`, conversation, `{ conversation, userIds }`: the first members, who start listening |
  | `member_joined` | `member.joined`, conversation, `{ userId, conversation }`; also written for a person who joins #general by accepting an invitation (TOKI-037 R17 calls it from `addMemberToGeneral`) |
  | `members_added` | `members.added`, conversation, `{ userIds, addedBy, conversation }` |
  | `member_left` | `member.left`, conversation, `{ userId }`; then `conversation.left`, user (the person), `{ conversationId, reason: "left" }` |
  | `member_removed` | `member.removed`, conversation, `{ userId, removedBy }`; then `conversation.left`, user (the person), `{ conversationId, reason: "removed" }` |
  | `details_changed` | `conversation.updated`, conversation, `{ topic?, description? }`: the current value of each field named in `change.fields` (TOKI-037 R16 adds `fields`), and both when `fields` is absent |
  | `channel_archived` | `conversation.archived`, conversation, `{}` |
  | `channel_restored` | `conversation.restored`, conversation, `{ conversation }` |
  | `settings_changed` | `settings.changed`, user (the actor), `{ conversationId, notificationLevel, isMuted, isStarred }`: the values that apply now (`notificationLevel` through `effectiveNotificationLevel`) |
  | `message_created` | `message.created`, conversation, `{ message }`: the `MessageEntry` of `change.message` (TOKI-037 R10) |
  | `direct_conversation_appeared` | `conversation.appeared`, user, once for each of the two people of the DM, the creator included, `{ conversation }`: the `DirectConversationEntry` with that person's own level, mute and star and with `lastMessageAt` |
  | `direct_conversation_opened` (proposal, concern 11) | the same `conversation.appeared`, for each member of a newly created group |

  Whenever a change also carries `change.message` that it did not bring itself (the system line that the first handler wrote for `channel_created`, `member_joined`, `members_added`, `member_left` and a topic edit, TOKI-037 R16), the handler writes one more event after the events of the change, `message.created` with that line, so a client receives the line as a message of the conversation, and a person added by `members_added` receives their own "added" line because the map already holds the conversation (R9). The line is written once, here: TOKI-037 R16 has no writer of its own. For `message_created` the `message.created` row of the table is the only event.

  The extra `message.created` of the paragraph above is written only for the change types whose line the first handler of TOKI-037 wrote: the five named there and any type that the row of a later ticket marks as one (`message_pinned`, TOKI-048 R9). It is not written for a change whose message is its own and has its own event: `message_created` (R20, table), and `message_edited` and `message_deleted` (TOKI-047 R10, which write `message.edited` and `message.deleted` and nothing else).

  Rows of later tickets. This work does not give the handler a registry that other code fills at run time: the handler is the one writer of events (TOKI-036 R25), the changes are typed in `conversations/changes.ts` and the kinds are in `packages/shared`, so a later ticket adds its rows by editing three places in its own build, each with its own tests: a row in the table of the handler, the kind and its `data` schema in the registry of `events.ts`, and a row in `kinds.ts` (R12, Design, "Who hears what": the test that every kind of the registry has a row there and the reverse fails without it). If the change carries a field that `EventChange` does not hold, the ticket adds it to `EventChange` as an optional field of the same shape. The rows that other specs have asked for are not built by this ticket and are not in the list of R12: `thread_updated` writes `thread.updated` and adds `thread?` (TOKI-045 R8); `reaction_added` and `reaction_removed` write `reaction.added` and `reaction.removed` and add `reaction?` (TOKI-046 R8); `message_edited` and `message_deleted` write `message.edited` and `message.deleted` (TOKI-047 R10); `message_pinned` and `message_unpinned` write `message.pinned` and `message.unpinned` and add `messageId?` and `target?` (TOKI-048 R9), the first also with the extra `message.created` of its line. None of these kinds changes a map. A live kind that has no number and no log row, such as `draft.changed` (TOKI-051 R7), is not an event: it is sent with `publishLive` (R27), defined in its own ticket's shared file, and is in neither the registry of `events.ts` nor `kinds.ts`. Check: a test adds a row for a made-up change type and its event is written and parsed, and fails until the kind has its row in `kinds.ts`; a change type with no row writes no event; an `EventChange` with an optional field the handler does not read compiles.

  The first message of a DM (TOKI-036, "The event contract"; TOKI-037 R20): the send calls `recordChange` twice, `direct_conversation_appeared` and then `message_created`, so the handler writes `conversation.appeared` for the two people first and `message.created` after them, with consecutive numbers. The other person's map gains the DM at the first of them, and so they receive the message as an event, and so do the creator's other tabs. No `conversation.created` is written for a DM. A later message of a DM, and every message of a group or a channel, writes only `message.created`.

  `readConversationForEvents(tx, conversationId, personIds)` is a new read of the `conversations` module (file `facts.ts`, exported from `index.ts`, injected into the handler by `app.ts`). - R21. The gateway registers a listener with `onSessionsEnded` (TOKI-022 R50), which `app.ts` passes in (R29). ## Design
### The path of one event
1. A route changes something. Inside its transaction it calls `recordChange`; the system-line handler of TOKI-037 runs first, and the event handler of R20 runs last and calls `recordEvent` of TOKI-036, which numbers the event under the counter lock, writes the row and sends `pg_notify` (R14).
2. The commit makes the row and the signal visible together.
3. The gateway wakes (or the sweep wakes it), reads the rows after its position in order, and for each row: resolves the audience (R15), applies the map change (R9), and sends one text frame to each recipient (R18, R19).

The gateway reads the log and does not receive the event from the writer (TOKI-036 notes that two pushes after a commit can reach a client in another order). Reasons: the order is the log's order whatever the order of the commits' callbacks; a crash between the commit and a callback loses nothing the log still holds; no module needs a post-commit call; and a second server process would work with the same code.

### Connection start
1. Upgrade: Origin check (R4). The handler adds its `message`, `close` and `error` listeners before its first `await`, so no frame is lost, and counts itself as a handshake in progress (R21).
2. `checkSession` (R2); failure closes with 4401 (R3), a database error with 1011 (R30).
3. If the person has no map, the gateway opens the read-only `REPEATABLE READ` transaction and reads `listConversationIdsOfUser` and `getLastEventNumber(tx, workspaceId)`, giving the set and `M` (R8). It takes no lock: fan-out goes on meanwhile. If the workspace has no position, its position is made now and is `M`.
4. The map must be brought to the position of the workspace before the connection is registered, because the drain may have delivered events above `M` to the others while the transaction ran. The gateway reads, with `readEventsAfter`, the events above the map's number up to the position and applies only their effect on the map (R9), without sending them to this connection, and repeats while the position has moved on. This is the only wait of the start and uses no lock; it ends because the log is finite, and each round is one statement.
5. In one synchronous step, with no `await`: the gateway checks the set of ended sessions of R21 and the map's number against the position, registers the connection with `after` equal to the number the map includes (the position, or `M` when the position is lower: events up to `M` are then in the map and are not sent to this connection), and sends `ready` with that number. A person who already has a map skips 3 and 4 and takes the larger of the position and the map's number.
Because the start takes no lock and a step that registers cannot be interrupted, 200 people reconnecting after a restart cost 200 snapshots, each its own transaction, and do not stop the fan-out of others (the 150 ms target, concern 4). A test seam (an optional function called after the snapshot, like TOKI-031 R10) lets a test commit events and end a session at that point (R7, R21).

### Who hears what
| Scope in the log | Who receives it |
|---|---|
| conversation | the connected people whose map holds the conversation |
| user | every connection of that person |
| workspace | every connection |
| live (no log row) | as `publishLive` says |

Public channels are not announced to people who have not joined them in this proposal; whether that is right is concern 12 (D-116 forbids a preview of content, and Browse already lists public names and topics). The map is per person, not per connection, so a second tab costs no read.

Which kinds change a map is a table in `apps/server/src/modules/realtime/kinds.ts`: `conversation.created`, `member.joined` and `members.added` give the conversation to the people in `data.userIds` or `data.userId`; `conversation.appeared` gives it to its own person; `member.left` and `member.removed` take it from `data.userId` before delivery, and `conversation.left` takes it from its own person. A kind of a later ticket that needs this adds a row. A test checks that every kind of the shared registry has a row in this table and the reverse.

### Closing, codes and reasons
Close reasons are short ASCII strings (the 123-byte limit of the protocol).

| Code | Reason | When |
|---|---|---|
| 1001 | `going_away` | the server stops (R26) |
| 1009 | (library) | a client frame is too big (R25) |
| 4400 | `bad_frame` | a client frame is not a JSON object (R25) |
| 4401 | `unauthenticated`, `session_ended`, `account_deactivated`, `signed_out`, `replaced` | R3, R21 |
| 4408 | `slow_consumer` | R19 |
| 4410 | `heartbeat_timeout` | R24 |
| 4429 | `rate_limited` | R25 |
| 4430 | `too_many_connections` | R10 |
| 1011 | `server_error` | R30 |

The codes and reasons are constants in `packages/shared/src/realtime/limits.ts`. A client reconnects after every code except 4401 with the reasons `unauthenticated`, `session_ended`, `account_deactivated` (the person must sign in again), `signed_out` and `replaced` (this browser asked), and 4430 (the oldest tab of a person with too many; it stays offline until reloaded, R10); that table of client behaviour belongs to TOKI-039.

### What the connection does not do
- It has no subscription step: the person hears every conversation in their map (technical design: "pushes that event to every open connection of the conversation's members").
- It carries no request and no answer. Every read and write stays on `/api/v1`; the socket pushes and takes the few client frames of R27.
- It does not renew the session (R23).

### Limits
Constants of `packages/shared/src/realtime/limits.ts`: `HEARTBEAT_PING_SECONDS` 25, `HEARTBEAT_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` 60, `SWEEP_SECONDS` 5, `SAFETY_DRAIN_SECONDS` 30, `MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_PERSON` 20, `SOCKET_BUFFER_LIMIT_BYTES` 1,048,576, `CLIENT_FRAME_MAX_BYTES` 4,096, `CLIENT_FRAMES_MAX` 50 per `CLIENT_FRAMES_WINDOW_SECONDS` 10, `DRAIN_BATCH` 200, `SESSION_RECHECK_SECONDS` 3,600. Decided in this spec; the log fixes none of them. Reasons: 25 seconds keeps a connection alive behind a proxy that closes a silent one after 30 seconds or more; 60 seconds is two missed pings and a margin; 1 MiB is about a hundred long messages; 20 connections is far more than one person has and still stops a loop; 4,096 bytes is much more than any frame of the tickets that add one. A product owner can change a number without a design change.

### Library
`@fastify/websocket` (which uses `ws`) is not in `apps/server/package.json` today: this ticket adds it, with `ws` and `@types/ws` as dev dependencies for the test client. The version rule: one exact version of each, no range, the newest that declares support for the Fastify of the server (5.12.8) and is at least two weeks old, chosen at build and written to the lockfile. `maxPayload` is set to `CLIENT_FRAME_MAX_BYTES`. Behaviours this spec assumes and that the build checks first, each with a test, because the dependency is not installed and nothing here was run (concern 13): (a) the library's route form has a `handler` for a non-upgrade request and a `wsHandler` for an upgrade (R6); (b) a request refused in an `onRequest` or `preValidation` hook is answered before the upgrade (R4); (c) a frame above `maxPayload` closes with 1009 (R25); (d) the socket exposes `bufferedAmount` (R19); (e) the `listen` of the `postgres` client uses its own connection and calls its `onlisten` after a reconnect (R29); (f) closing the app closes the library's sockets only through the gateway's own `close()` (R26). A behaviour that does not hold changes the requirement that depends on it, not the test.

### Errors
- Not an upgrade -> 400 `bad_request`. Origin refused -> 403 `origin_not_allowed`. Both in the error format of TOKI-004.
- Session problem -> close 4401 with the reason (R3). A database error at the handshake -> close 1011 `server_error`, no `ready`, nothing registered (R30).
- A failure of the event handler or of `recordEvent` -> the transaction of the change rolls back and the route answers 500 (R20).
- A failure inside a drain (the database is down) is logged with the error and the drain is tried again by the next signal or the sweep. No connection is closed for it: the events stay in the log and the position does not move.
- A failure to send to one socket closes that socket and goes on with the others.

## Data safety
- A cookie, a token or a query string never reaches the log (R5). The registry holds no secret (R11).
- Events go only to the people whose map holds the conversation (R15); a frame of a private conversation, a DM or an unjoined channel never reaches anyone else. A client frame that names such a conversation is dropped silently (R25).
- The Origin of a browser connection is checked (R4), so another site cannot use a person's cookie to listen.
- The close reason names the kind of session end and nothing else.

## Open questions from the intent
- Where does a client learn why a connection was refused or closed? Answered: the upgrade completes and the server closes with 4401 and the reason, so the client reads it from the close event (R3, R21, the close table; concern 2).
- Who writes the events of a channel change, and when are they pushed? Answered: this work writes them inside the transaction through `recordChange`, as TOKI-029 concern 4 decided (merged), and pushes them after the commit by reading the log with the functions of TOKI-036 (R14, R20; concern 1).
- How does the gateway know which conversations a person listens to? Answered: a map per connected person, read from the member rows at the first connection and then kept by the events in number order, with the DM condition (R8, R9; concern 6). A newly created group is not answered: whether its members hear of it is concern 11.
- What happens to an open connection when its session ends, when a person is deactivated or a role changes? Answered for a session that ends and for a deactivation: the connection is closed with a reason (R21, R22), and a session that goes idle is closed by the recheck (R23, concern 3). Not answered for a role change: who receives the notice is concern 7.
- What does the gateway do about a dead connection and a slow client? Answered: R24 and R19; the numbers are in concern 5.
- What is the line with TOKI-039, TOKI-040 and TOKI-044? Answered: Scope, the Design table of close codes, R7 and R27.

## Areas of concern
1. The event log belongs to TOKI-036 and the events of a change to this ticket. Decided: events are written through `recordChange` by the handler of TOKI-038, by Thanh Truong (product owner) on 2026-11-19 in `docs/specs/2026-11-17-channel-api-design.md` (concern 4, merged; TOKI-029 concern 4). Decided in this spec (a technical choice): this ticket writes no `recordEvent`, no `appendEvent` and no read of the log; it uses `recordEvent`, `readEventsAfter` and `getLastEventNumber` with the signatures of the contract of TOKI-036 (PR 198, not merged: if it changes, R14 and R20 change), the log columns are `scope`, `type` and `payload` and only the gateway's frame says `kind` and `data` (R12), and the event types and payloads are this ticket's. The no-gap rule (TOKI-036 R16) is a condition of the position of R14: if a number could be skipped the gateway would skip an event. Owner: Thanh Truong.
2. A browser does not show the status of a refused WebSocket upgrade, so a 401 at the handshake would look like a network error and the client would retry forever. Decided in this spec: complete the upgrade and close with 4401 and a reason that equals the REST error code (R3, R21). Reason: the client of TOKI-039 reads the close event and calls `reportUnauthenticated` as sign-in screens R39 says the realtime connection will. Owner: Thanh Truong.
3. An open connection must end with its session (the intent's constraint), but a session that is idle for 30 days while a connection stays open and sends nothing is not refused by any request, and TOKI-022 R50 reports only explicit ends. Decided in this spec as a technical consequence of that constraint and of the merged idle rule (TOKI-022 R25, FR-03 AC-009): the gateway asks `auth` once an hour, with one statement for all open connections, which session ids are no longer valid and closes those connections (R23). It adds no read to a request and changes no rule of TOKI-022; it only applies the existing idle rule to a connection. Cost: one statement an hour, and one new function in `auth`. Owner: Thanh Truong.
4. The 150 ms target (NFR-13, D-113) cannot be asserted by a test that forbids real timers. Answered: this work keeps delivery free of statements and of scans, takes no lock at the start and asserts that (R18); the path of one message is still a commit, a `NOTIFY`, a read of the batch and a send, so two database round trips are in the budget. The measure is TOKI-089 ("Performance measurement"): it must run the real LISTEN path with 200 connections opened through `/ws` and messages sent through TOKI-037, so TOKI-089 needs TOKI-037 and TOKI-038 (Plan changes); a run that fakes the signal does not check the target. Owner: Thanh Truong.
5. The numbers of the heartbeat, the buffer, the client frames, the connections per person and the recheck (the Limits section) are not in the log. Decided in this spec as technical choices, each with its reason, kept as constants so they can change without a design change. Owner: Thanh Truong.
6. A DM must not be heard by the other person before its first message (F25), and TOKI-037 owns that rule (TOKI-037 R19). Decided in this spec as a technical consequence, following the contract of TOKI-036 and TOKI-037 (PR 198 and 197, not merged): the rule lives in one read, `listConversationIdsOfUser`, which gets the condition of TOKI-037 R19 with the injected `listLastMessageTimes`; the reveal is `conversation.appeared` for the two people and then `message.created`, written in that order by the handler for the two calls of `recordChange` that TOKI-037 R20 makes, so the other person's map holds the DM before the first message is delivered and the person receives it. The order the other way would lose the first message for the other person. If TOKI-036 or TOKI-037 changes the order, R20 changes. Owner: Thanh Truong.
7. Who hears a role change, a deactivation or a reactivation. TOKI-024 concern 8 (decided by the product owner on 2026-11-19) says the gateway carries the live update and that a reload repairs a lost one; it does not say who receives it. Not decided: the proposal of R17 is a `live` frame `person.updated` to every connection of the workspace, which is right only if the people list is the one reader and every person may see a role; the alternative is the target and the Admins only. A role change is reported after its commit, so it cannot be a numbered event (TOKI-024 R40). Question for the product owner: who must hear it. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
8. The production proxy must pass `/ws` with the upgrade and must not close an idle connection before 30 seconds. Decided in this spec: the 25-second `ping` of R24 keeps the connection busy, and the rule of the Caddy file is TOKI-084's, which is named here so it is not forgotten. Owner: Thanh Truong.
9. The scope of the events that take a person out of a conversation. Decided in this spec (a technical choice that follows the contract of TOKI-036, "Events", PR 198, not merged): the leaving person is addressed by a user-scope event, `conversation.left`, because on a replay they are no longer a member and a conversation event would not reach them; the members who stay get `member.left` or `member.removed` as conversation events. The live path applies the same rule (R9), so the live and the replayed streams agree. Owner: Thanh Truong.
10. The tab that is closed with 4430. Decided in this spec (a technical choice): the client does not reconnect after 4430, because a reconnect would close the next oldest tab and 21 live tabs would close each other forever (R10); the closed tab stays offline until it is reloaded. The alternative, refusing the new connection, would leave the person's newest tab dead. TOKI-039 builds the client side. Owner: Thanh Truong.
11. Whether a newly created group is announced to its connected members. Not decided. TOKI-030 R20 (merged) says no event is written for a new group, and its concern 7 leaves the reveal of a DM to TOKI-037; TOKI-037 concern 2 does not hide a group and writes only `message_created` for its messages. Without a rule, a group member who is connected when the group is created has the group in no map, hears none of its messages and sees the group only after a reload. Proposal: `directRoutes` gets a `recordChange` dependency and calls it once with `direct_conversation_opened` when the request creates the group row (not for a DM, not for an existing group), and the handler writes `conversation.appeared` for each member; this changes TOKI-030 R20 for that case. Question for the product owner: announce a new group (proposal) or leave it to a reload. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
12. Whether a person who has not joined a public channel hears its creation, rename, topic change or archive. Not decided. R15 gives such a person no frame with the channel's id, name or topic (D-116 forbids previewing content), but Browse already lists public names and topics, so a new, renamed or archived public channel is not seen in an open Browse until a reload. Proposal: as written, nothing is pushed to non-members. Alternative: a workspace-scope event for the public data of a public channel only (id, name, topic, archived state), never its messages. Question for the product owner. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
13. The library and the client are not installed and nothing here was run. Decided in this spec: the behaviours of `@fastify/websocket`, `ws` and the `listen` of `postgres` that the requirements assume are listed in the Design, "Library", and each is checked by a test at the start of the build; a behaviour that does not hold changes its requirement. The version rule is in the same section. Owner: Thanh Truong.
