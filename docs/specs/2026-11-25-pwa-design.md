# PWA design

Date: 2026-11-25 · Status: draft for review (concerns 1, 2 and 10 are open for the product owner)
Intent: docs/intents/pwa.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-077

## Goal
A Member or an Admin must be able to install Toki from a supported browser, to the home screen of a phone or to the desktop, and open it in a window of its own with Toki's name and icon (D-030, F36, AC-046). On an iPhone this install is also what lets Web Push work (D-108). This work gives the web app the manifest, the icons and the page links that make it installable. It adds no service worker and no cache: the service worker is for push only (D-030) and a page is never served stale (NFR-06).

## Scope
- In:
  - `apps/web/public/manifest.webmanifest` and five PNG icons in `apps/web/public/icons/`, with the two SVG masters they are drawn from in `apps/web/assets/icons/`, and the script that draws them (R1 to R7).
  - In `apps/web/index.html`: the manifest link, the favicon (it replaces the empty `data:,` icon TOKI-006 left; the real icons are this ticket's, not TOKI-011's or TOKI-015's), the Apple touch icon and the theme colour (R8). `preview.html` and `shell-fixture.html` keep their `data:,` icon: they are development pages.
  - The build check `check:pwa`, run at the end of the `build` script of `apps/web` (R10), and its test.
  - One browser test, `e2e/pwa.spec.ts`: Chromium's own installability test, no service worker and no cache (R12, R13).
  - The two new commands in `.claude/rules/web.md` (R14).
- Out (not now):
  - The service worker file `apps/web/public/push-sw.js` and its registration, the push, the card that offers notifications, the iPhone detection and the Preferences text that tells a person to add Toki to the Home Screen (F36 cases): TOKI-074. This ticket does not touch `push-sw.js` and adds no `icon` or `badge` to it; TOKI-074 sets the `icon` of a notification to `/icons/icon-192.png`, a file this ticket provides (R4). See "Line with TOKI-074" in Design.
  - An "Install" button or card of Toki's own. F36 and AC-046 name only the browser's install action, so none is built (concern 5).
  - Offline use, a cache of any kind and an update flow for an installed copy (D-030, NFR-06).
  - Cache headers and the content type of the manifest and the icons on the production server: TOKI-084 (Caddyfile), with the values of "Serving", as TOKI-010 did for the fonts (concern 8).
  - A theme colour that follows the account's saved look instead of the device (concern 3).
  - Splash screens for iPhone sizes, shortcuts, screenshots and share targets in the manifest: not in the log, not needed to install.

## Requirements
- R1. `apps/web/public/manifest.webmanifest` is valid JSON and holds exactly these keys: `id`, `name`, `short_name`, `start_url`, `scope`, `display`, `background_color`, `theme_color`, `icons`.
- R2. Their values are: `id` `"/"`, `name` `"Toki"`, `short_name` `"Toki"`, `start_url` `"/"`, `scope` `"/"`, `display` `"standalone"`. No other text is in the manifest, so no translated text is needed (concern 4).
- R3. `background_color` and `theme_color` are both `#F2F4EF`, the value of `--bg` in the light block of `packages/tokens/src/tokens.css`. The check reads that file through the package path `@toki/tokens/tokens.css` (not a relative `../../packages/` path) and takes the value of `--bg` from the block whose selector is `:root, :root[data-eff="calm"]`, so a change of the token fails the check.
- R4. `icons` holds exactly three entries, each with `src`, `sizes`, `type` `"image/png"` and `purpose`: `/icons/icon-192.png`, `192x192`, `any`; `/icons/icon-512.png`, `512x512`, `any`; `/icons/icon-maskable-512.png`, `512x512`, `maskable`.
- R5. `apps/web/public/icons/` holds exactly five PNG files: the three of R4, `apple-touch-icon.png` and `favicon-32.png`. The width and height read from each file are 192, 512, 512, 180 and 32 pixels (square).
- R6. The artwork. `apps/web/assets/icons/icon.svg` draws a square tile of the accent colour `#0F766E` with rounded corners (radius 29% of the side, the proportion of the logo mark of the sign-in card, 10 of 34 pixels) and, centred on it, the `bolt` shape of the icon set (`packages/ui-web/src/icons/paths.ts`) in `#FFFFFF`, 56% of the side high. `apps/web/assets/icons/icon-maskable.svg` draws the same shape on a tile with square corners that fills the whole image. The PNGs are drawn from them: `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png` and `favicon-32.png` from `icon.svg`; `icon-maskable-512.png` and `apple-touch-icon.png` from `icon-maskable.svg`. The check reads the pixels and proves that the bolt is drawn, not only a tile. In the files from `icon.svg`: the corner pixel is fully transparent; the pixel at the centre of the image, which lies inside the bolt (the point 12,12 of the 24-unit grid of `paths.ts` is inside the `bolt` polygon), is white (each channel at least 247, alpha 255); and at least 5% of all pixels are white, which the bolt satisfies (it covers about 8% of the tile at 56% of the side) and a plain tile does not. In `icon-maskable-512.png` and `apple-touch-icon.png`: no pixel is transparent, the corner pixel is the accent colour (within a difference of 8 on each channel), the centre pixel is white, at least 5% of all pixels are white, and every pixel that is not the accent colour lies inside the circle of radius 40% of the side around the centre, the safe zone that a maskable icon needs. The builder checks the 5% on the real files and, if the drawn bolt gives a different figure, sets the threshold at half of what the bolt covers; the rule that the bolt must show stays. The artwork itself is a proposal (concern 1).
- R7. `apps/web/scripts/make-icons.mjs`, run by `pnpm --filter ./apps/web icons:build`, draws the five PNGs from the two SVGs with `sharp`, a devDependency of `apps/web` at the exact version that TOKI-060 R13 pins for `apps/server` (its line is 0.34 or later), so the lock file holds one `sharp`; whichever of TOKI-060 and this ticket merges second takes the version of the first. TOKI-060 R13 limits `sharp` to `apps/server` and its import-graph tests look inside `modules/files`, so a build-time use under `apps/web/scripts` does not conflict, and lint does not check `apps/web` (`.claude/rules/web.md`). The lock file holds the platform packages of `sharp` for Linux (CI, `--frozen-lockfile`) and for macOS. Running the script twice gives files with the same pixels. It is the only way the PNGs are made; nobody edits one by hand. Nothing compares the committed PNGs with a fresh run of the script (the pixels of libvips differ a little between macOS and Linux, so an equality test would fail for no reason): the pixel proofs of R6 are the only link between the SVGs and the committed files.
- R8. `apps/web/index.html` holds exactly these, each once: `<link rel="manifest" href="/manifest.webmanifest">`; `<link rel="icon" type="image/png" sizes="32x32" href="/icons/favicon-32.png">` (the `data:,` icon is gone); `<link rel="apple-touch-icon" href="/icons/apple-touch-icon.png">`; `<meta name="theme-color" content="#F2F4EF" media="(prefers-color-scheme: light)">` and `<meta name="theme-color" content="#0E1214" media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)">` (two `theme-color` tags, exactly one per `media` value, and the check counts them by `media`). `#0E1214` is `--bg` in the block `:root:not([data-eff])` inside `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` of `tokens.css` (the block `:root[data-eff="focus"]` has the same value); the check compares both values with that file.
- R9. After `pnpm --filter ./apps/web build`, `apps/web/dist` holds `manifest.webmanifest` and the five icons at the same paths, with the same bytes as in `public/`, and with unhashed names (the manifest and the icons are addressed by fixed paths). Every `src` in the built manifest and every `href` of the five links of R8 in `dist/index.html` names a file that exists in `dist`. The Vite `base` stays `/`.
- R10. `pnpm --filter ./apps/web check:pwa` checks R1 to R6, R8 and R9 and exits 0 only when all hold. When one fails it exits non-zero and names the file and the requirement; when `dist/index.html` does not exist it names the build command. The `build` script of `apps/web` ends with it. The script imports what it needs from `node:` modules and from `sharp`, and passes `pnpm lint` and `pnpm format:check`.
- R11. No file of this change adds a service worker, registers one, calls `navigator.serviceWorker`, or registers or fills Cache Storage or any other cache (the browser test of R12 only reads `caches.keys()`). The web app stays installable without one (concern 7).
- R12. In the browser, with the app open and signed out at `/login` (Chromium, the dev server of the test harness): `navigator.serviceWorker.getRegistrations()` gives an empty list, and `caches.keys()` gives an empty list. TOKI-074 is built before this ticket (the plan: TOKI-077 needs TOKI-074) and registers its worker only for a signed-in person whose permission is granted (its R22), so there is none at `/login` signed out; its worker has no cache (its R19). The assertion holds before and after TOKI-074 merges. With the network cut off by the test, a reload does not show the page: the navigation fails. No stale page is served.
- R13. In the same setting, the test opens a Chrome DevTools Protocol session (`context.newCDPSession(page)`; Chromium only, which is the project used) and, after the page has loaded: `Page.getAppManifest` returns a `url` that is set, an empty `errors` list and `data` that parses with the three icons of R4; then `Page.getInstallabilityErrors` returns an empty list: Chromium finds the page installable with the manifest, the icons and no service worker. A negative control in the same file opens a page served by the test with no manifest link (a route that answers a minimal page on the same origin) and expects a non-empty error list, so an empty list cannot come from a browser that reports nothing. When Chromium lists an error for the real page, the test fails and prints the error name; no service worker is added to make it pass, and the finding goes to the product owner (concern 7). `127.0.0.1` is a secure context, and the `e2e` job runs for a change under `apps/*` and `e2e/*`.
- R14. The two commands, `check:pwa` and `icons:build`, are added to `.claude/rules/web.md`, the rule file of the area (`.claude/rules/ticket.md`, "Process"). No root script is added.
- R15. The change touches only `apps/web` (`public/`, `assets/icons/`, `scripts/`, `index.html`, `package.json`), `e2e/pwa.spec.ts`, `.claude/rules/web.md`, `pnpm-workspace.yaml`, the lock file and the list of root tests that runs the node test of the check (R10). `pnpm-workspace.yaml` gets one line under `allowBuilds`, `sharp: false`: pnpm 12 stops an install on a dependency script with no decision, and `sharp` has an install script (`node install/check.js`, and in 0.34.5 a source build when it fails). `sharp` ships its binary as a platform package, so the script is not needed and stays off, as `esbuild` does; whichever of TOKI-060 and this ticket merges second finds the line already there and keeps it. It adds no file under `packages/`, no text to `packages/i18n` and no request to the server.

## Design
### What "install" means
- Decided by the log: Toki is an installable PWA and its service worker is for push only (D-030); it installs from a supported browser with the browser's own action and opens in its own window (AC-046, TS-071); on a phone, after Add to Home Screen, it opens like an app of its own (F36). Offline use is not part of it: with the network off no stale page is served (NFR-06).
- Not decided by the log, so decided here (each is a technical choice the Blueprint leaves to the builder): the manifest values of R2 to R4. `display: "standalone"` is the mode that gives a window without the address bar, and Apple's rule for Web Push on an iPhone needs a Home Screen web app whose manifest says `standalone` or `fullscreen`, so it is also what D-108 depends on. `start_url` and `scope` are `/` because every route of the app is under `/` (`/login`, `/c/…`, `/admin`, `/prefs`) and `/` sends a signed-out person to sign-in and a signed-in person to Home. `id` is `/` so the identity of the app does not change if `start_url` changes later. No `orientation` is set (a person may rotate a phone or a tablet), no `lang` or `dir` (the app picks the language itself, D-018). No `display_override` and no `apple-mobile-web-app-capable` tag: not needed, as D-108 asks for the last two versions of Safari, which read the manifest.
- The browser needs no service worker to install Toki on the supported browsers; Chromium's own test says so (R13). If that proves wrong in a supported browser, the finding is reported and goes back to the product owner, because a worker with a fetch handler would touch D-030 (concern 7).

### Names and colours
- The home-screen name is `Toki`, the product name that `index.html` already uses as its title. The manifest is a static file and Toki is single-tenant with the workspace name in the database, so the workspace name is not used (concern 4).
- Colours come from tokens, not from new values: the page background `--bg` of the light look, `#F2F4EF`, for the manifest's `theme_color` (the browser bar and the window title bar) and `background_color` (the launch screen); and `#0E1214`, the dark `--bg`, for dark devices through the `media` of the two `theme-color` tags. The bar follows the device's setting, not the account's saved look (concern 3).

### Icons
- Sizes: 192 and 512 are the two sizes a Chromium browser wants for installing and for the app list; the maskable 512 lets Android crop the icon to its own shape; 180 is the size an iPhone uses for the Home Screen (it ignores the manifest icons for that and reads `apple-touch-icon`, and it rounds the corners itself, so that file has none); 32 is the tab icon.
- Artwork: the log decides no logo. The only logo of Toki that exists is the mark of the sign-in card (TOKI-023): the accent tile with a rounded corner, which shows the `bolt` icon while the workspace name is not known. The proposal is to use that mark as the app icon (R6). Whether the product owner wants it is concern 1.
- The maskable icon keeps the bolt inside a circle of 40% radius, the safe zone, so no shape of the phone cuts it.

### Installed on an iPhone
Safari's Add to Home Screen takes the name from the manifest, the icon from `apple-touch-icon.png` and opens the app without the address bar (R2, R5, R8). The Home Screen copy of a web app on an iPhone keeps its own storage, apart from Safari's, so a person who signed in in Safari will probably have to sign in again in it. The sign-in mail carries a code only and no link (D-157), so the code typed in the installed app works. This is not in the log and is concern 2; the technical design asks for a real iPhone to be tried before the notification work starts (techDeps, FR-18).

### Serving
Vite copies `public/` to the root of `dist` and serves it from the root in development, so the paths of R4 and R8 are the same in both. The files are public and are fetched with no session. TOKI-084's Caddy serves `dist`, and needs these values (concern 8): `/manifest.webmanifest` with `Content-Type: application/manifest+json` and `Cache-Control: no-cache`; `/icons/*` with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=86400` (the names do not change with the content, so not the long cache of hashed files); and a missing file under `/icons/` or `/manifest.webmanifest` answers 404, not the `index.html` fallback of the single-page app. TOKI-084 picks these up from its plan line, with `push-sw.js` (`Cache-Control: no-cache`, TOKI-074 R19), as the fonts spec did for TOKI-010. The build check of R10 is the last step of `build` and imports `sharp`, so the image build stage of TOKI-084 must install the devDependencies of `apps/web`, with the `sharp` platform package of the image's system, before it runs `pnpm --filter ./apps/web build`.

### Needs
TOKI-006 (`index.html`, Vite, the `apps/web` scripts), TOKI-009 (`tokens.css`), TOKI-011 (the `bolt` icon), TOKI-008 (Playwright and `playwright.config.ts`), TOKI-010 (the fonts check, as the pattern of a build check and its hand-off to TOKI-084) and TOKI-074 (the plan line: R12 assumes its worker). TOKI-084 consumes the files and the build command of this ticket.

## Testing
- R1 to R6, R8, R9: `check-pwa.test.mjs` (run with `node --test` and added to the root test list like `check-no-dev-pages.test.mjs`) calls the pure `findProblems` of the script on small fixtures: a good manifest and folder gives no problem; one fixture for each failure gives a problem that names the requirement: an extra key, `name` changed, `display` `"browser"`, `theme_color` unlike the token, a missing icon entry, a purpose changed, a 193-pixel file, a transparent corner in a maskable file, a glyph pixel outside the circle (a white square at the edge), a plain accent tile with no bolt (it must fail in every file, R6), a tile with a bolt too small to reach 5% white, a second `theme-color` tag with the same `media`, a second manifest link, a link to a missing file, a `data:,` icon. The pixel fixtures are drawn in the test with `sharp`. Then `pnpm --filter ./apps/web build` runs the script on the real files (R9, R10).
- R7: the test runs `make-icons.mjs` into a temporary folder twice and compares the pixels of the two runs, and compares their sizes with R5. The committed PNGs are tied to the SVGs only by the pixel proofs of R6.
- R12 and R13: `e2e/pwa.spec.ts` on the dev server of `playwright.config.ts`, Chromium. It opens `/login`, reads `getRegistrations()` and `caches.keys()`, sets the context offline, reloads and expects the navigation to fail, then opens a CDP session and expects `Page.getAppManifest` to give a `url`, no error and the three icons, and `Page.getInstallabilityErrors` to return no error; its negative control (a page with no manifest link) expects a non-empty list (R13). The test uses no timer. It proves only that the dev server and the browser keep no page for offline use; the production case (Caddy and the HTTP cache) is not proved here and belongs to TOKI-084's headers (concern 8).
- R11, R15: by reading the list of changed files of the pull request.
- By hand, in the pull request, before review: (a) Chrome on a desktop: the install action is offered, the installed app opens in its own window with the bar in the colour of R3 and opens signed in when the person was signed in in the browser (AC-046, TS-071); (b) Android Chrome: Add to Home Screen shows the maskable icon without a cut, and the installed app opens signed in; (c) a real iPhone: Add to Home Screen shows the name `Toki` and the Apple icon, the app opens without the address bar, and whether the person is signed in is written down (concern 2). On the same iPhone, with TOKI-074 built (it is a prerequisite), the QA record of this ticket also says whether a test push reaches the Home Screen app: the spec of TOKI-074 (its concern 12) leaves that real-device check to the manifest of this ticket; this ticket only records it and builds no push. The builds are served over a secure address or `localhost`, as browsers require.
- No test needs real data; the files hold no personal data.

## Data safety
- The manifest and the icons are public static files with no personal data and no workspace name. They are fetched without a session and cannot reveal who uses Toki.
- No cache stores any page or answer (R11, R12), so no signed-in content stays on a device after sign-out.

## Areas of concern

The log decides no logo and the prototype has no app icon (its browser tab has none; the only mark is the accent tile with a letter in the sidebar header and on the sign-in card). Proposal, not a decision: use the sign-in card's mark, the `bolt` on the accent tile (R6). On an iPhone the Home Screen copy of a web app probably has its own storage, so the session of Safari does not carry over and the person signs in once more with a code (D-157), which is the same mail and the same steps as at sign-in. The log does not say whether that is acceptable. Proposal, not a decision: accept one more sign-in on an iPhone, and say so in the Preferences text of TOKI-074; "signed in" in TS-071 then holds for Chrome and Edge, which share storage with the installed app. To be checked on a real iPhone (Testing). Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
3. The browser bar colour follows the device's light or dark setting, not the account's saved look (D-175: the look is saved to the account, and only `Match my device` follows the device; TOKI-078 applies it). Decided in this spec: the manifest has one colour and the two `theme-color` tags answer the device's setting, so a person who chose a look that differs from their device (Focus on a light device, say) sees the bar in the device's colour. Reason: it needs no script and no change of `ui-web`; TOKI-078, which owns the first-paint look and applies the saved look, owns the later change of the tag, and the product owner is asked to confirm that. 5. No Install button or card. Decided in this spec from AC-046 and TS-071 ("use the browser's install action") and F36, which names only Add to Home Screen. The card of F36 offers notifications, not installation. Owner: Thanh Truong.
6. The line with TOKI-074. Decided in this spec, checked against the spec of TOKI-074 (pull request 297): TOKI-077 owns the manifest, the icons and the links, and provides `/icons/icon-192.png`; TOKI-074 owns `push-sw.js` (including the `icon` and `badge` of the notification, `icon` being `/icons/icon-192.png` and no `badge`), its registration, iPhone detection, the D-108 rule and the Add to Home Screen text (its R26).
