# Overlays design

Date: 2026-11-16 · Status: draft for review
Intent: docs/intents/overlays.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-014

## Goal
The builder (Thanh and Claude) needs the three layers of the prototype as components before the screens that open them are built: the Menu that opens from a button, the Modal (a centred dialog) and the Sheet (the panel that rises from the bottom edge of a phone, the More sheet).
The prototype opens and closes these with its own script, which moves no focus into a layer, keeps none inside it, returns none to the opening button and gives a menu no arrow keys. After this change `ui-web` exports Menu, Modal and Sheet, drawn as in the prototype in Calm and Focus, with focus trapped inside a dialog and returned when it closes, Esc closing the layers in order, menus driven by the arrow keys (D-075), nothing hover-only (D-067), and a close request that tells the owner why it was made, so that the form guard of D-070 (TOKI-093) needs no change here.

## Scope
- In:
  - `Menu`, `MenuItem`, `MenuGroup`, `MenuSeparator` and the hook `useMenuTrigger`; `Modal`; `Sheet`.
  - Their CSS, carried over from the prototype with the prototype's class names, one file per component.
  - The overlay layer they share (internal to `ui-web`): the stack of open layers and the Esc key, the focus trap and focus return, the scroll lock, and the optional isolation of the page behind a dialog.
  - Tests, an examples file per component and the three entries of this group in `componentExamples` for the preview page.
- Shared with TOKI-012 and TOKI-013. The rules of "Contract for TOKI-013" in the TOKI-012 spec apply to this ticket too and are not repeated: folder layout, `forwardRef` on every component, owned attributes after the spread, handlers with native names, no text inside a component, jsdom and Testing Library for the whole package, `Example` and `componentExamples`, export lists that are "at least", the base rules of `base.css`, the icon lint rule. This ticket adds no dependency.
- Decided in this spec, three rules that differ from the contract (each reason is in the requirement named).
  - A handler that is not the handler of a DOM event is named for what it does, not for an event. `onRequestClose` (Modal, Sheet) and `onClose` (Menu) are such handlers: the owner may refuse the request by leaving `open` as it is, so neither is the native `close` event of a `dialog` element.
  - `tabindex`. TOKI-012 R21 says no component sets `tabindex`. The dialog panel sets `tabindex="-1"` (R5: it can hold focus when it has nothing tabbable) and every `MenuItem` sets `tabindex="-1"` (R18: roving focus by the arrow keys). Contract item 3 of TOKI-012 (the exceptions) therefore has two more entries, beside `Switch.onChange` and `Toast` `action.onPress`.
  - `outline`. TOKI-012 R21 says no component sets `outline: none` except the controls whose focus ring Table H gives. `.modal:focus-visible` sets `outline:0` (Table A): the panel is focused only by the layer, and a ring around a whole dialog is noise. The items of a Menu keep a visible focus (Table C).
  TOKI-012 R21 should say "except overlays (TOKI-014)" for both, so that a reader of that spec does not take this ticket for a breach.
- The line with TOKI-093, stated once. This ticket builds the layers and tells the owner why a close was asked: `"escape"`, `"outside"` or `"close"` for a dialog (R8). A layer never closes itself; the owner changes `open`. TOKI-093 builds what decides: the guard that, for a form with content, ignores an outside press and asks "discard?" on the close button (D-070, BR-21, AC-074 and AC-075), the check that a form has content, the confirmation dialog that names its target (a Modal that TOKI-093 fills), the Undo helper and the rule "never both" (D-069). Nothing in this ticket looks at what is typed in a dialog.
- The line with TOKI-015, stated once. The AppShell does not mount, host or position an overlay: every layer portals itself into `document.body` (Design, "The overlay layer"). The account card is the one place where the two specs meet in code, and they agree: the shell's `UserCard` spreads `account.triggerProps` on its `button.ucard`, and that is exactly what `useMenuTrigger<HTMLButtonElement>().triggerProps` returns (R24); `triggerProps.ref` is the ref of that button and is the Menu's `anchorRef` (R17), so the Menu's focus calls (R21, R22) reach a real button. The shell owns the card as a trigger; TOKI-099 owns what the Menu holds and renders it with `menuProps` beside the shell (the contract is in the AppShell spec, "Contract for TOKI-014 and TOKI-099"). What else the shell may rely on, and what it must not do (z-index below 20, scroll, side panels, the toast anchor), is listed in Design, "What the layer gives the AppShell".
- The line with the sign-in screens (TOKI-023). Their "Your session has ended" cover is a Modal with an opaque backdrop, no close button, an owner that ignores Esc and outside presses, a chosen first focus, and no focus return. R15 and R16 give exactly these, so that screen adds no cover of its own. While that cover is up the screen opens no other layer (concern 13).

## Requirements
- R1. `@toki/ui-web` exports at least these names, each from `packages/ui-web/src/components/<Folder>/` and re-exported by `src/components/index.ts`: `Menu`, `MenuProps`, `MenuItem`, `MenuItemProps`, `MenuGroup`, `MenuGroupProps`, `MenuSeparator`, `MenuSeparatorProps`, `menuAligns`, `menuItemVariants`, `menuCloseReasons`, `MenuCloseReason`, `useMenuTrigger`, `computeMenuPosition`, `Modal`, `ModalProps`, `Sheet`, `SheetProps`, `dialogBackdrops`, `dialogCloseReasons`, `DialogCloseReason`. The folders are `Menu/` (holds `MenuItem`, `MenuGroup`, `MenuSeparator`, `useMenuTrigger` and `menuPlace.ts`), `Modal/` (holds `Dialog.tsx`, which `Sheet` imports and which the package does not export) and `Sheet/`. Each folder has `<Name>.tsx`, `<Name>.css`, `<Name>.test.tsx`, `<Name>.examples.tsx` and `index.ts`, plus the helper files named here; `Sheet/Sheet.css` and `Menu/Menu.css` are the only CSS of their folders. `src/components/index.ts` gains three lines and `src/examples.ts` three imports and three entries; no other shared file of TOKI-012 or TOKI-013 changes (TOKI-012 R31). The test checks that each name is exported and does not require that nothing else is.
- R2. Conventions. Every component of this ticket is made with `forwardRef`; `ref` points at: `Menu` its `div.menu`, `MenuItem` its `button`, `MenuGroup` its `div`, `MenuSeparator` its `hr`, `Modal` and `Sheet` the element with `role="dialog"` (`div.modal`). `className` is added after the component's own classes (on the dialog panel for `Modal` and `Sheet`). Every other attribute of that element passes through except those the component owns; owned attributes are applied after the spread. Handlers keep native names except `onRequestClose` and `onClose` (Scope). The props types of R1 are exported. `open` is the only state: a layer is controlled and holds no open state of its own, so an owner that wants it open keeps it open.
- R3. No source file under `src/components/Menu`, `Modal` or `Sheet` or under `src/internal/overlays` holds a word of its own or imports `@toki/i18n` (test and examples files excepted). Rendering each component open, with every required text prop set to the empty string and no children, gives a `textContent` equal to the empty string.
- R4. With `open={false}`, or after unmount, a layer renders nothing: `document.body` gains no child, no scroll lock is held (R14), no page element is made inert (R15), no `keydown`, `pointerdown` or `focusin` listener of the layer is left on `document`, and the stack of open layers (R9) does not hold it.
- R5. `Modal` portals into `document.body`, as its last child at the time it opens, one `div.scrim` that holds one `div.modal` with `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal="true"` and `tabindex="-1"`. Two forms. (a) With `title`: the panel holds `div.mhd` (an `h2` with the title and an id from `useId`, and, only when `closeLabel` is given, an `IconButton` of TOKI-012 with `size="sm"`, `icon="x"` and `label` equal to `closeLabel`), then `div.mbd` with the children, then `div.mft` with `footer` only when `footer` is given; the panel has `aria-labelledby` equal to the id of the `h2`. (b) Without `title`, with `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby` (passed through to the panel): the panel holds the children directly, with no header, no body wrapper and no footer. Every other attribute passes to the panel.
- R6. `Sheet` renders as `Modal` does in R5, with these differences only: the scrim has the classes `scrim sheet`, and the panel has the classes `modal` and, from R25, a bottom placement. Its props are those of `Modal`. Same behaviour in every requirement from R8 to R16.
- R7. `initialFocusRef`, `backdrop`, `returnFocus`, `returnFocusRef` and `footer` are props of `Modal` and `Sheet`: `initialFocusRef` a ref to the element that gets first focus (R11); `backdrop` one of `dialogBackdrops`, `dim` (default) or `opaque` (R15); `returnFocus` a boolean, default `true` (R13); `returnFocusRef` an optional ref to the element that gets focus back when the dialog closes, which is the opener the owner names (R13); `footer` the content of `div.mft`.
- R8. A dialog never closes itself; it calls `onRequestClose(reason)` with one of `dialogCloseReasons`: `"escape"` when Esc is pressed while it is the topmost layer (R9); `"outside"` when a press begins on the scrim and ends on the scrim; `"close"` when the header's close button is pressed. A press that begins inside the panel and ends on the scrim (the end of a text selection) is not an outside press. After any of these, an owner that leaves `open` as it is leaves the dialog open with its content, focus and typed text unchanged. No reason is reported for a press on the panel. `onRequestClose` is required.
- R9. Esc and order. The open layers (dialogs and menus) form one stack, in the order their `open` turned `true`. One `keydown` listener on `document`, in the capture phase and present only while the stack is not empty, handles `Escape`: it asks the topmost layer to close (`"escape"`), and it calls `preventDefault` and `stopPropagation` on the event, so that no other handler (a React `onKeyDown` above the layer's owner, a listener on `document` in the bubble phase) sees it. One press asks one layer. With the stack [Modal A, Modal B, Menu], three presses ask the Menu, then B, then A. An `Escape` with `isComposing` true, or with `keyCode` 229 (an input method is composing; Safari reports the key that ends a composition after `compositionend`, with `isComposing` false and `keyCode` 229), is ignored by the layer and not stopped. With an empty stack the event is not touched. The order of the stack is taken from a counter that a layer reads at its first render with `open` true (and clears when `open` turns false), not from the order of effects: effects of a child run before those of its parent in one commit, so a Menu that is a child of a Modal and opens in the same commit would otherwise sit below it. The stack and the Esc listener are module-level, so one copy of `@toki/ui-web` in a bundle is a requirement (`apps/web` and the preview page are one bundle each).
- R10. One press closes one layer. A press (`pointerdown`) outside an open Menu that lies on the scrim of a dialog below it closes the Menu only: the dialog gets no `"outside"` request for that press. The Menu listens to `pointerdown` on `document` in the capture phase (as R9 does for `keydown`), because React attaches its own listeners below `document`, and a listener in the bubble phase would run after the scrim's handler and find no mark. It marks the press as handled (a `WeakSet` of pointer events in `src/internal/overlays`), and the scrim ignores a marked press. The test uses a Menu over a Modal whose `onRequestClose` closes on `"outside"`.
- R11. Focus in. When a dialog opens, focus moves into it, in this order of choice: the element of `initialFocusRef`; else the first element in the panel that has the attribute `data-autofocus` (the prototype's attribute, line 1185); else the first tabbable element of the panel in document order (the header's close button comes first); else the panel itself. An element that is not a control (plain text) can be the first focus when the owner gives it `tabIndex={-1}`.
- R12. Focus trap. While a dialog is the topmost layer: Tab on the last tabbable element of the panel moves focus to the first, and Shift+Tab on the first moves it to the last. Tab or Shift+Tab while focus is on the panel or on a `tabindex="-1"` element (an element that is not tabbable): with at least one tabbable element, Tab moves focus to the first and Shift+Tab to the last (Decided in this spec: the trap does not look for "the next one after" an element that is not in the Tab order); with no tabbable element, the key is prevented and focus stays on the element that has it (the panel, or the chosen element of R16). A focus that lands outside the panel and outside every layer above it (a script, a click on the page behind through a gap) is moved back to the first tabbable element or the panel. The trap acts only on a `keydown` whose target is inside the panel (`panel.contains(event.target)`, the DOM and not the React tree) and only while the dialog is the topmost layer: a Menu rendered as a React child of a Modal sends its `keydown` up the React tree to the panel's handler, and that event must pass without the trap touching it. A tabbable element is a link with `href`, an enabled `button`, `input` (not `type="hidden"`), `select` or `textarea`, a `summary` that is the first child of its `details`, an `audio` or `video` with `controls`, an element with `contenteditable` that is not `"false"`, or an element with a `tabindex` that is not `-1`; it has no `hidden` attribute, is not inside an element with `inert` or `hidden` or inside a `fieldset[disabled]` (except in its first `legend`), and, for a radio button, is the checked one of its group, or the first when none is checked. An `iframe` is not handled (concern 9). A Menu above the dialog is not trapped by it (R9, R20).
- R13. Focus return. When a dialog closes (`open` turns `false` or it unmounts), focus goes back to the opener, when `returnFocus` is not `false`, the opener is still in the document and `document.activeElement` is then the `body` (the browser's state when the focused element has been removed). The opener is the element of `returnFocusRef` when the owner gives it, and otherwise the element that had focus when the dialog opened. The ref exists because Safari (macOS and iOS) and Firefox on macOS do not give a button focus when it is pressed with a mouse or a finger, so a dialog opened that way finds the `body` focused and would return nothing: a screen that opens a dialog from a button passes that button's ref (the More tab's `moreTab.ref` of the AppShell is made for this; the examples of R30 do it). Decided in this spec; a pointer-tracking fallback was not chosen, so that the layer keeps no listener while nothing is open (R4). If the owner has already moved focus to another element in the same commit, that element keeps it. A dialog opened with focus on the `body` and no `returnFocusRef` returns nothing. A focus that cannot be given because the opener is not visible (`visibility: hidden`, `display: none`, as the bottom tab bar is while a chat is open, Design, "What the layer gives the AppShell" 5) is not given and no error is raised. With stacked dialogs, closing B returns focus to the element of A that opened B. The order of the steps is R33.
- R14. Scroll lock. While at least one Modal or Sheet is open, `document.body.style.overflow` is `hidden`; when the last one closes it is back to the value it had before the first one opened. A Menu does not lock. The `.modal` has `overscroll-behavior: contain` (Table A).
- R15. Opaque backdrop. `backdrop="opaque"` adds the class `opaque` to the scrim, whose background is `var(--bg)` (Table A), and, while the dialog is open, sets `inert` and `aria-hidden="true"` on every element of the page that is not the dialog's own portal and not skipped under R34 (the toast region and the elements that lead to it); when it closes each element gets back the `inert` and `aria-hidden` it had before. A layer that opens later (a Menu, another dialog) is a new child of `body` and is not affected: it is above the opaque dialog and interactive (concern 13). With `backdrop="dim"` nothing is made inert or hidden: the page behind is covered by the dim scrim, `aria-modal="true"` and the trap (R12). Decided in this spec; the reason is in Design, "Why not `inert` every time".
- R16. A dialog that cannot be dismissed is possible with the props above and nothing else: with no `closeLabel` the header has no close button; with an `onRequestClose` that does nothing, Esc, an outside press and every other key leave it open; with `returnFocus={false}` no focus is returned; with `initialFocusRef` on a `tabIndex={-1}` text element, that element has first focus. A test builds this dialog twice and presses Esc, clicks the scrim and tabs. With a panel that holds no tabbable element (the "session ended" form with no button, TOKI-023 R26), the dialog stays open, focus stays on the chosen element, and Tab and Shift+Tab leave it there (R12). With a panel that holds a button, the dialog stays open and Tab from the chosen element goes to the button (and Shift+Tab to the last tabbable element), and a second Tab stays inside.
- R17. `Menu` portals into `document.body` one `div.menu` with `role="menu"`, `data-menu` and `aria-orientation="vertical"`, and the children. It takes `open`, `anchorRef` (a ref to the opening element), `onClose(reason)`, `align` (one of `menuAligns`, `end` default or `start`), `openFocus` (`first` default or `last`), and `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`; the type accepts nothing without one of the two. `onClose` and `anchorRef` are required. Menu closes only through `onClose`. `anchorRef.current` is expected to be the element that opened the Menu and to be able to take focus (a `button`): the account card's `button.ucard` of the AppShell, whose ref is `triggerProps.ref` (Scope, "The line with TOKI-015").
- R18. `MenuItem` renders `button` with `type="button"`, `role="menuitem"`, `tabindex="-1"`, and as children an `Icon` of `icon` (an `IconName`, optional) followed by a `span` that holds the children. `variant` is one of `menuItemVariants`, `default` or `danger` (class `dng`). `disabled` sets `aria-disabled="true"` and not the `disabled` attribute: the item stays focusable (R20) and a click on it does nothing (R21). `onClick` is the native handler.
- R19. `MenuGroup` renders `div` with `role="group"`, `aria-labelledby` the id of its heading, a `div.mt` heading holding `label` (required), then the children. `MenuSeparator` renders `hr`. Both may sit anywhere among the items.
- R20. Menu keys. When a Menu opens, focus moves to its first item that is not disabled (the last, with `openFocus="last"`); when every item is disabled, to the first. ArrowDown moves focus to the next item and ArrowUp to the previous, wrapping from the last to the first and the first to the last; Home and End go to the first and the last. Disabled items are visited, so that a locked entry and its reason can be reached by keyboard. Items are found in document order through the groups. Enter and Space press the focused item (the browser's click on a `button`). Other keys do nothing (no type-to-select).
- R21. Choosing. A click on an item that is not disabled (mouse, touch, Enter or Space): the item's `onClick` is called first, with focus still on the item. Then, when the handler did not call `event.preventDefault()`, still in the same event, focus moves to the element of `anchorRef` and the Menu calls `onClose("select")`. A dialog that the handler opens is rendered after the handler returns, so it finds focus on the anchor and records the anchor as its opener (R13), not the item that is about to disappear. When the handler called `event.preventDefault()` the Menu stays open and focus stays on the item that was activated, so a keyboard user stays inside the Menu and the arrow keys keep working (R20). The owner decides what the open Menu then shows (the prototype replaces a menu by another one, line 2367). Decided in this spec: after every render while a Menu is open, when `document.activeElement` is the `body` (the item that held focus has gone because the owner swapped the items), focus moves to the first item that is not disabled, as when the Menu opens (R20); an owner that replaces the menu with another can give it a new `key` and gets the same result through a fresh open. A click on a disabled item calls nothing and does not close the Menu.
- R22. Closing a Menu. Esc: focus moves to the anchor and `onClose("escape")` is called (the stack of R9 decides which layer gets it). Tab or Shift+Tab: the default is prevented, focus moves to the anchor, `onClose("tab")` is called. A press outside the Menu, and not on the anchor: `onClose("outside")`, with no change of focus by the Menu. A press on the anchor is not an outside press, so the anchor's own click toggles the Menu. Whatever the reason, when the Menu has closed and `document.activeElement` is the `body`, focus moves to the anchor (the same rule as R13). When the anchor cannot take focus (it left the document, or it is not visible: `display: none` or `visibility: hidden`), the call does nothing, no error is raised, and focus stays where the browser leaves it; the account card is `display: none` under 720 px, where no Menu opens from it.
- R23. Position. `computeMenuPosition({ anchor, menu, viewport, align })` returns `{ left, top }` with `anchor` `{ left, top, right, bottom }`, `menu` `{ width, height }` and `viewport` `{ width, height }`, as the prototype's `posMenu` (lines 2103-2111): `left` is `anchor.right - menu.width` for `end` and `anchor.left` for `start`; `top` is `anchor.bottom + 4`; when `top + menu.height` is more than `viewport.height - 8`, `top` becomes `max(8, anchor.top - menu.height - 4)`; `left` is then limited to `max(8, min(left, viewport.width - menu.width - 8))`. The Menu is `position: fixed` and sets `left` and `top` from the function in a layout effect before the first paint, and again on `resize` of the window and on `scroll` of any element (capture), while it is open. The menu element has `max-height: calc(100% - 16px)` and scrolls (Table C), so a menu taller than the window stays reachable. An anchor with a zero rectangle (it is not in the document) gives `top` 4 and `left` 8: the function is not special-cased, and its tests have this case.
- R24. `useMenuTrigger<T extends HTMLElement>()` returns `{ open, setOpen, triggerProps, menuProps }`. `triggerProps` is `{ ref, "aria-haspopup": "menu", "aria-expanded": open, onClick, onKeyDown }`: a click toggles `open`; ArrowDown on the trigger opens it with `openFocus="first"` and ArrowUp with `"last"`. `menuProps` is `{ open, anchorRef, openFocus, onClose }`, where `onClose` sets `open` to `false`. `triggerProps` goes on an `IconButton`, a `Button` or a plain `button` without a cast, and it holds none of `children`, `className`, `type` and `aria-label`, so that the shell's `button.ucard` can spread it before the attributes it owns. A type test spreads it on each of the three (`useMenuTrigger<HTMLButtonElement>()`).
- R25. The three CSS files hold exactly the selectors of Tables A, B, C and D, with exactly the declarations listed (value for value, in any order and any white space), and no other rule. Each component's `.tsx` imports its CSS file; `Sheet.tsx` also imports `Modal.css`; `Dialog.tsx` imports `Modal.css`. `Modal.css` and `Menu.css` each hold one `@media (max-width: 719px)` block (Table D).
- R26. The CSS of the three files uses only the design system's names and values. (a) A colour is always `var(--…)`: the check fails on `#`, `rgb(`, `rgba(`, `hsl(`, `hsla(`, `color-mix(` and a named colour; `transparent` is allowed. (b) Every `var(--name)` names one of the 32 properties of `tokens.css` (TOKI-009 R10). (c) No selector contains `data-eff`, `data-look`, `data-accent` or `data-density`. - R33. Order of the steps, decided in this spec because each wrong order breaks something. On open, in one layout effect of the commit in which `open` turns `true`: the opener is recorded (R13), then the scroll lock is taken (R14), then the isolation is applied (R15), then focus moves in (R11). The opener comes before the isolation because the browser blurs a focused element that becomes inert. On close: in the layout cleanup, the isolation is released (R15) and the scroll lock released (R14), so that the opener is no longer inside an inert tree; then, in a passive-effect cleanup, which runs after React has removed the portal from the document, the focus is given back (R13), and only then is "`document.activeElement` is the `body`" tested. - R34. The toast region and the isolation. The toast region of TOKI-013 is skipped by the isolation of R15: a toast shown while an opaque dialog is open stays visible above the cover (R28) and is announced by its `aria-live` region. The region registers with the layer by carrying the attribute `data-layer-skip` on its element (the attribute is read by `isolate.ts`; the layer imports nothing from TOKI-013, R31). The isolation does not set `inert` or `aria-hidden` on an element that carries the attribute, or on an element that contains one (such as `#root`); it sets them on the other children of each such container, from the container down to the element, so that the shell's panes stay isolated while the region is not. A dim dialog isolates nothing (R15) and the attribute changes nothing for it. The attribute is set by `ToastRegion`, a one-line change that this spec asks of TOKI-013 and that this ticket does not make (R1: no other shared file changes); until it is made the test of Testing uses a stand-in element that carries the attribute.

### The overlay layer
Decided in this spec. The files are in `src/internal/overlays/`.
- Mount. Each open layer is rendered with `createPortal` into `document.body` and is its own last child, so a later layer is above an earlier one of the same `z-index` by order. There is no provider and no root element to add: the AppShell has nothing to mount. The reason: a provider is one more thing for every screen and every test to remember, and the layers need nothing that a provider would hold. The cost: React sends the events of a portal up the React tree, so a layer rendered inside a clickable row also sends its clicks and keys to that row, and a Menu rendered inside a Modal sends its keys to the Modal's panel (the trap ignores them, R12). A screen renders its layer beside the clickable element, not inside it.
- `<dialog>` is not used. Decided: a `div` with `role="dialog"` and `aria-modal`, as the prototype writes it. The native element opens in the browser's top layer, which is above every `z-index`, so the toast (R28) could not show above a dialog; and jsdom does not implement `showModal`, which the tests need.
- The stack (`stack.ts`). A module-level list of `{ id, order, kind, close }`, where `close` calls the latest `onRequestClose` or `onClose` of the layer and `order` is the counter of R9. A layer pushes itself when `open` turns `true` and removes itself when it turns `false` or unmounts; the list is kept sorted by `order`. The Esc listener of R9 is added with the first entry and removed with the last. `isTop(id)` tells a dialog whether it traps (R12). The same file holds the `WeakSet` of handled presses (R10).
- Focus (`focus.ts`). Finds tabbable elements (R12) and moves focus. jsdom has no layout, so it does not test visibility other than `hidden` and `inert`; a browser also skips elements with `display: none` through its own rules, and the Tab wrap uses the tabbable list only to find the first and last, so a hidden element at an end is the one limit (concern 9).
- Scroll lock (`scrollLock.ts`). A counter: the first lock stores `body.style.overflow` and sets `hidden`; the last unlock restores it. The page of phase 1 does not scroll its body (the shell fills the window), so no scrollbar width is compensated.
- Isolation (`isolate.ts`). For `backdrop="opaque"`: sets `inert` and `aria-hidden="true"` on the other elements of the page, remembering what each had, with a counter per element so two opaque dialogs restore correctly. An element that carries `data-layer-skip` (the toast region, R34) is left alone, and so is every element that contains one: the walk goes from each such container down to the element and isolates the siblings it passes.
- Why not `inert` every time. Decided in this spec. The toast region (TOKI-013 R21) is the one `aria-live` region of the toasts and sits inside `#root` with the page, so making the page inert as a whole would also silence it, and a toast shown while a dialog is open would not be announced. A dim dialog relies on `aria-modal="true"`, which current screen readers honour, and on the trap. An opaque dialog is a case where nothing behind may be read (journey F07; requirement FR-46), so it isolates; the toast region registers with the layer and is skipped (R34), so a toast over the cover stays visible and announced. The screen-reader behaviour is not tested in this spec (concern 5).
- A menu item that opens a dialog. Decided: R21 runs the item's `onClick` first and, when the handler leaves the Menu to close, moves focus to the anchor in the same event, before React renders what the handler asked for. The dialog that the handler opens then records the anchor as its opener (R13), not the item that is about to disappear, and returns focus to the button that opened the menu. When the handler keeps the Menu open (`preventDefault`), focus stays on the item, so a keyboard user is never left outside an open Menu.
- The order of the steps of opening and closing is R33.
- No animation. The prototype's dialog and menu have none, and R26 (e) keeps it so; `base.css` already holds the reduced-motion rule.
- The Esc rule and IME. Vietnamese is typed with an input method that uses Esc to cancel a composition, so `isComposing` and `keyCode` 229 are checked (R9; Safari reports the end of a composition with the second). Without them a person could lose a dialog by correcting a word.

### Sheet
Decided in this spec (the prototype draws no sheet). D-053 and the plan say "the More sheet" for narrow screens; the prototype draws it as a centred dialog (line 2155) with a class that has no rule, so a person on a phone sees a dialog in the middle of the screen, and at 360 px the dialog is as wide as the window less 24 px.

The More tab exists only under 720 px (journey F38), and the Sheet is shown when the screen opens it; if the window grows past 720 px while it is open, it stays, at 520 px wide and centred at the bottom, and its owner closes it. This keeps the layer free of layout state.

The prototype's `k.sheet` branch (line 2105) is never set by any caller, so nothing is lost. Whether other dialogs become sheets on a phone is not decided (concern 3).

### What the layer gives the AppShell
For TOKI-015, whose spec ("Contract for TOKI-014 and TOKI-099" and its statements on layers) agrees with each point below. The points the shell states differently are marked.
1. Where overlays mount: in `document.body`, by themselves (above). The shell renders no container for them and needs no provider. The `ToastProvider` of TOKI-013 is separate and the shell mounts it, as TOKI-013 says. The shell sets no `inert` and no `aria-hidden`; an opaque dialog does (R15), on the page elements other than the toast region and the elements that lead to it (R34), which includes the shell's panes inside `#root` and not the `ToastRegion`.
2. Z-order, bottom to top: the shell's own panes, side panels and bottom tabs (`z-index` 6 or less, the prototype's lines 510, 515, 555 and the shell's Tables F and G), then a dialog's scrim (20), then a Menu (30), then the toast region (40). The shell must keep every pane below 20 (it does: its highest is 6). The rule for the toast region, which is not a portal: `ToastRegion` is rendered by `ToastProvider` inside `#root`, after the shell's own elements (TOKI-013 R21; the shell's R23), and none of its ancestors may form a stacking context (a `position` with a `z-index` other than `auto`, `transform`, `opacity` below 1, `filter`, `isolation: isolate`, `contain`), because then its 40 would count only inside that ancestor and the toast could fall under the scrim's 20. `#root` and the provider's own ancestors form none; the shell's `div.app` is `position:fixed` and so does, which is why the region must stay after it and not inside it. TOKI-015 meets this by order today; its R23 should state the rule as a requirement and not leave it as a side effect (a request to that spec). The Meet bar (`z-index: 25`, line 434) is phase 4 and not built.
3. Scroll lock: while a Modal or Sheet is open the body does not scroll and the scrollable panes of the shell behind the scrim keep their own scroll position, which the layer does not touch. The shell does not rely on the body for scrolling (its `.app` is `position:fixed`), as the prototype's frame does not either. A Menu does not lock; it follows its anchor on scroll (R23).
4. Side panels. Between 720 and 1099 px a side panel overlays the chat (D-068). A dialog or Menu over it is above it (scrim 20 against the panel's 6) and covers it with the rest of the window. The panel stays open and mounted, and is not closed by the layer opening or closing. The panel closes on Esc with a listener on `document` in the bubble phase that ignores an event that is `defaultPrevented` (shell R11); the layers take Esc in the capture phase and stop it (R9), so the first Esc closes the dialog and the next one can close the panel, as in the prototype's order (2565-2571). Closing a panel with Esc is the shell's rule; this ticket only guarantees it is second.
5. Focus. The account card (`button.ucard`, through `triggerProps`) is the opening button of a Menu, and the More tab (`button.nt`, with `moreTab.ref`) is the opening button of a Sheet; the layers return focus to them (R13, R22). The More tab is not always visible: the shell hides the bottom bar while a chat is open and while the side pane is shown, with `transform` and `visibility: hidden` (shell R22; the prototype only moves it, line 515), and `focus()` on an element that is not visible does nothing. So focus is not returned to an opener that is not visible (R13, R22), and TOKI-092 closes the Sheet as soon as a destination is chosen (plan line of TOKI-092, GAP-39), so that the Sheet is already closed, and focus already returned, before the shell hides the bar. The account card is `display:none` under 720 px, so no Menu opens from it there.
6. The toast anchor and `useToastAvoid`. Layers neither register as an anchor nor as an obstacle: a layer is not part of the layout. A toast shows above the scrim at the place TOKI-013 chooses, which the shell sets with its toast anchor (concern 4). The region does register with the layer for one purpose only, to be skipped by the isolation (R34, concern 5); that does not move it.
7. Press and Back. Pressing Back with a layer open is not handled by the layer (concern 2), and the shell takes no part either (shell concern 4).
8. Narrow means the viewport (`max-width: 719px`), as in TOKI-012 R8; if the shell chooses a container, the media blocks of Table D follow it (TOKI-012 concern 8).

### Errors
- An owner leaves `open` as it is after a close request -> the layer stays open, with its content and focus unchanged (R8).
- The opener is no longer in the document when a dialog closes (a row that re-rendered) -> no focus is returned; the browser leaves focus on the `body` (concern 9).
- The anchor of a Menu is not in the document when it opens -> its rectangle is zero and the Menu is placed by the arithmetic of R23, 4 px from the top and 8 px from the left, and still closes by Esc, Tab and an outside press; focus is not moved to an anchor that cannot take it (R22).
- A Menu or a dialog unmounts while open (the screen goes away) -> its listeners, lock and isolation are released (R4); no focus is returned.
- `Escape` during composition (`isComposing`, or `keyCode` 229) -> ignored (R9).
- A Menu handler calls `preventDefault` -> the Menu stays open, focus stays on the item (R21).

## Data safety
- No layer reads, stores or sends what is typed in it. It holds no state about its content, writes nothing to storage and sends no request.
