# Sound and in-app notifications design

Date: 2026-11-25 · Status: draft for review (concerns 1, 2, 5 and 9 are open for the product owner)
Intent: docs/intents/sound-in-app-notifications.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-075

## Goal
A person who has Toki open and is reading one conversation must hear and see that something arrived in another one, without the app announcing what they already read, and must be able to open it with one press. The notification decision engine (TOKI-073) decides who is notified and hands each open screen a notice; the screen's notice centre judges whether it shows now, waits or is dropped, and whether its sound plays, and then calls a port named `sink` with two methods, `toast(notice)` and `sound(notice)`. This ticket builds that sink in the web app: the toast that the person sees, the sound file and its playing, and the step that lets Safari and the other browsers play a sound that code starts.
The toast component and its region already exist (TOKI-013, mounted by the shell, TOKI-015), and so do `platform.sound.play` and the web implementation (TOKI-018). What is missing is the content of each toast, the action of its button, the sound file, the way the sound is allowed, and the glue between the notice centre and these parts.

## Scope
- In:
  - The sound file made for Toki, its address and the check of its shape (R1, R2).
  - The sound feature of the platform: a new `unlock` method and the handling of an element that the browser refused (R3, R4).
  - The step that calls `unlock` on the first press, and arms it again after a refusal so that the first sound is not lost (R5).
  - The sink: the sound with its burst guard, the toast through a host, where the sink is built, and the hand-over to the notice centre of TOKI-073 (R6, R7).
  - The host component and what each toast shows and does: text, button, where it stands, how long it lives, how it stacks (R8 to R12).
  - The texts, in English and Vietnamese (R13).
  - Nothing else changes, and the checks in real browsers (R14, R15).
- Out (not now):
  - Who is notified, with which switch, and what is on the screen: TOKI-073 (the server's decision and the screen's judgement). This ticket applies no second filter: a notice that reaches the sink is shown.
  - The switches 'Play a sound' and 'Desktop notifications', their storage, their default and their screen: TOKI-102 and TOKI-078. This ticket never reads a switch; the notice carries `sound` already decided.
  - A test sound played when a person turns the switch on, and a message that tells the person the browser blocks the sound: not in the prototype and in no decision; if the product owner wants either, the screen that holds the switch (TOKI-078) is the place, and concern 9 asks the question.
  - The system notification, the permission and the card that offers them: TOKI-074. The Activity list, its badge and its label 'Back from snooze': TOKI-066. The tab title with the unread count: TOKI-054.
  - The toast of a reminder (Later's own host, TOKI-067) and every toast of Pause and Focus time (TOKI-076: start, extension, end and the summary, which it owns with their count and words): they use the same toast component, do not pass through this sink and play no sound (concern 10).
  - The toast component, its timers, its cap and its place: TOKI-013 and TOKI-015. This ticket gives it content and sets no duration.

## Requirements
The sound file
- R1. The file `apps/web/assets/sounds/notification.wav` holds the one sound of Toki: two soft notes, 880 Hz (A5) starting at 0 ms and 1318.51 Hz (E6) starting at 150 ms, each lasting 250 ms with a 5 ms attack and an exponential fall, so the first note is still dying when the second starts and the sound ends at 400 ms; peak at 0.25 of full scale (about -12 dBFS), 16-bit mono PCM at 22,050 Hz, so 8,820 samples and about 17.7 KB. It is made by `apps/web/scripts/make-notification-sound.mjs`, a Node script with no dependency; the script's header says that the sound is made for Toki and holds no third-party material, so no licence file is needed. `apps/web/src/main.tsx` imports the file with the `?url` suffix and calls `createWebPlatform({ sounds: { notification: url } })`, replacing the call that TOKI-018 left without an address. Check: a test reads the file and parses its RIFF header: format PCM, one channel, 22,050 Hz, 16 bits, 8,820 samples, a size under 40,000 bytes, and a peak sample between 0.2 and 0.3 of full scale; a second test runs the script into a temporary folder and compares each sample with the file's, allowing a difference of one least significant bit (`Math.sin` and `Math.exp` are not fixed to the bit by ECMAScript, so a different Node version may differ by one); a new script `apps/web/scripts/check-notification-sound.mjs`, run as `pnpm --filter @toki/web check:sound` and added to the `build` script of `apps/web/package.json` after `check:fonts`, checks that `dist` holds the file as a separate asset and that no built JavaScript file contains `data:audio`; a test of `main.tsx`'s platform call finds the address under the name `notification`.
- R2. The file is requested by no code before the first `unlock` or `play`. TOKI-018 R20 says no audio file is requested before the first `play`; with `unlock` the first request happens at the first press, so R20 is amended to "before the first `unlock` or `play`" (Plan changes), and the platform test of R2 replaces the old R20 test. `createWebPlatform` itself requests nothing. The file is not preloaded by a `<link>` and it is not part of the JavaScript. Check: a test with a recording fake `Audio` calls `createWebPlatform({ sounds })`, then renders no screen and presses nothing, and sees no `Audio` created; the built `index.html` has no `rel="preload"` for the file.

The platform
- R3. `Platform["sound"]` gains `unlock(): Promise<boolean>`. `unlock` never rejects and is single-flight: a call made while an unlock is running returns the same pending promise and starts nothing (on a tap, `touchend` and then `click` both reach it). It answers `false` at once, with no `Audio` created and no request, when a sound name has no address or `Audio` does not exist. Otherwise, for each sound name it creates the one `Audio` element of that name when there is none (the same element that `play` uses), sets its `volume` to 0, calls `play()`, and, when `play()` resolves, calls `pause()`, sets `currentTime` to 0 and `volume` back to 1; then the name is marked unlocked. It resolves `true` when every name is unlocked (also when called again, with no further `play()` on an element that is unlocked). When `play()` is refused the volume goes back to 1 and the rule of R4 applies: a rejection named `NotAllowedError` keeps the element and leaves the name locked, any other rejection drops the element, and both resolve `false`. A resolved silent play is not proof that the browser allows later sounds (WebKit and Chromium treat a muted element as not audible, and on iOS `volume` cannot be set), so `true` means only that the element has played from a press; R5 and R6 cover a later refusal. `unlock` must be called inside a press handler to have effect (R5); the method itself checks nothing about that. TOKI-018 R1 says later tickets add methods; the types test and every hand-written fake `Platform` in the repository gain `unlock`. Check, with a fake `Audio` whose `play()` resolves or rejects as the test says: `true` after a resolved `play()`, with the order `volume = 0`, `play`, `pause`, `currentTime = 0`, `volume = 1` recorded on the fake; two calls in a row, the second before the first resolves, return the same promise and make one `play()` and one volume sequence; a second `unlock` after the end makes no second `play()`; a `NotAllowedError` gives `false`, restores the volume, keeps the element, and a later `unlock` tries again on it; a `NetworkError` gives `false` and drops the element; a missing address or `Audio` undefined gives `false` with no `Audio` constructed; the call never rejects; the types test lists the new method.
- R4. `play` and the element. `play("notification")` uses the element that `unlock` made, so an element that a press unlocked is the one that code later plays (Safari treats an element, not the page, as allowed). A `play` called while an `unlock` is running waits for it to end, for at most `SOUND_UNLOCK_WAIT_MS` (1,000; the browser may leave a `play()` promise pending, which iOS can do for an element still loading), and then plays; when the unlock has not ended by then, `play` resolves `false` and touches nothing, and the unlock goes on to its end. So a notice that arrives during an unlock does not play at volume 0. A refusal by the browser's policy (the rejection's `name` is `NotAllowedError`) resolves `false`, keeps the element and marks the name locked again, so that the next press can unlock the same element. Any other rejection (the file cannot be loaded) resolves `false` and drops the element, as TOKI-018 R19 and its code do now. The other rules of TOKI-018 R18 to R20 stay: `play` never rejects, a `play` while the sound plays starts it again from the beginning, and two copies never play at once. The wait uses a timer given to `createSound` (default `setTimeout`) so that a test can control it. Check, with the fake `Audio`: an element created by `unlock` is the same object that `play` uses (one `Audio` constructed in all); a `play` started during a pending `unlock` calls the fake's `play` after the unlock's `pause`; with an unlock whose promise never settles, the `play` resolves `false` after the 1,000 ms of the fake timer and calls the fake's `play` not at all; a `NotAllowedError` keeps the element and marks it locked, a `NetworkError` drops it and the next call builds a new one; `play` after a lock resolves `false` and, after a new `unlock`, `true`; the TOKI-018 sound tests still pass.

The unlock step
- R5. `armSoundUnlock({ target, sound, clock })` in `apps/web/src/notifications/sound-unlock.ts` adds capturing listeners for `click`, `keydown` and `touchend` to `target` (the document) and returns `{ rearm(refusedAt?), disarm() }`. The first of those events calls `sound.unlock()` at once, in the same call stack as the event, with no `await` before it, so the press that caused the event is the press the browser sees. While that call is pending the step ignores further events (`touchend` then `click`, a double press, key repeat), and `unlock` is single-flight as well (R3). When `unlock()` resolves `true` the listeners are removed. When it resolves `false` the step has failed once: the `keydown` listener is removed (a key typed in the composer must not call `unlock` again and again), `click` and `touchend` stay, and after 3 failures in a row all three are removed; a failure that no press can heal (no address, no `Audio`, a file that cannot load) therefore ends after 3 presses with no further request. `rearm(refusedAt)` adds the three listeners when they are not there (it adds none twice) and resets the count of failures; it is called by the sink when a later `play` is refused (R6). A first sound must not be lost: when `refusedAt` is given and the next press comes no later than `SOUND_LATE_MAX_MS` (5,000, the life of a toast) after it by `clock`, the step calls `sound.play("notification")` in that press, at full volume, instead of `unlock()`; a real play started from a press is the one thing every browser allows and it unlocks the element for the later sounds, and the late sound is heard with its toast still on screen. A press after that time calls `unlock()`. `disarm()` removes the listeners. The step is armed once, in `apps/web/src/main.tsx`, right after `setPlatform`, before the first render, so that the first press on any screen, the sign-in screens included, counts. It reads no switch: the cost of unlocking for a person whose switch is off is one request of the 17.7 KB file after the first press, and it lets a press on the switch itself unlock the sound in the same gesture (Design, "The unlock flow"). Nothing is shown and nothing is stored. Check, with a fake target that records `addEventListener` options, a fake `sound` and the clock of TOKI-008: three listeners in the capture phase; the first `click` calls `unlock` once and, with the fake resolving `true`, removes all three; a `touchend` and a `click` before the first call resolves make one call; after `false` the `keydown` listener is gone and a `click` calls it again; three `false` in a row remove all three; `rearm` after removal adds three and a second `rearm` adds none; `rearm(t)` and a press 4,999 ms later call `play` once and `unlock` not at all, a press 5,001 ms later calls `unlock`; `disarm` removes them; the call happens before the handler returns (the test reads the call count inside the handler, with no await); a test of `main.tsx` finds the step armed before the render.

The sink
- R6. `createNoticeSink({ getSound, clock })` in `apps/web/src/notifications/sink.ts` returns `{ sink, attachHost, setOnRefused }` and is the `sink` that TOKI-073 R18 asks for. `getSound` is a function that returns `Platform["sound"]` and is called inside `sink.sound`, never when the sink is built, because `main.tsx` imports `./router` (and so `client.ts`) as a static import and static imports run before `setPlatform(createWebPlatform(...))`; `client.ts` passes `() => getPlatform().sound`, and `getPlatform()` throws before `setPlatform`. `apps/web/src/client.ts` (the file TOKI-073 R19 changes) builds the one sink at load with that function and the web clock (`apps/web/src/clock.ts`), exports it as `noticeSink`, and passes `noticeSink.sink` to `createNoticeCenter` in place of the stand-in. `setOnRefused(fn)` stores the function that the sink calls when a sound is refused; `main.tsx`, after `armSoundUnlock`, passes it `rearm` of the step (the sink does not know the step, and the step does not know the sink). Until it is set the function does nothing. `sink.sound(notice)` plays the sound with this guard: when fewer than `SOUND_MIN_GAP_MS` (1,000, concern 6) have passed since the last sound that played, or a `play` of the sink is still pending, it does nothing; otherwise it calls `getSound().play("notification")`, and when that resolves `true` it records `clock.now()` as the last sound; when it resolves `false` it records nothing, so a refused sound does not use up the window, and it calls the function of `setOnRefused` with `clock.now()` (R5 uses it for the late sound). A call to `sound` never throws and never waits for the promise. The sink reads no switch, no preference and no notice field except to pass it on: the centre already called it only for a notice whose `sound` is true and that is not being read. Check, with the clock of TOKI-008 and a fake platform: importing `client.ts` before `setPlatform` does not throw (a test imports it with no platform set); a first call plays; a second call 999 ms after the first that played does not, a third 1,000 ms after does; two calls in one tick make one `play`; a `false` from `play` calls the refused function once with the time and leaves the next call free to play; a `true` does not call it; a rejected promise from a broken fake is caught and nothing escapes; no `setTimeout` and no `Date.now(` occur in the file.
- R7. `sink.toast(notice)` passes the notice to the host that is attached (R8) and does nothing when none is attached, which is the case on every screen that has no shell (sign-in, Join) and after sign-out. `attachHost(host | null)` replaces the host and returns nothing; the host is `{ show(notice): void }`. One gap is covered: the realtime connection and the notice centre start with the store, and `NoticeHost` is mounted by `app-layout` a little later, so a notice handled in that gap would be judged `show` by the centre, forgotten, and lost while its sound plays. Until a host has been attached for the first time the sink keeps at most 3 notices (the cap of the toast component) that are no older than `NOTICE_HOST_WAIT_MS` (5,000, by `clock`) and hands them to the first host, in order, at `attachHost`; after any `attachHost(null)` the kept notices are discarded and nothing is kept again, so a notice of one session can never reach the next person. Apart from this gap a notice that arrives with no host is not kept: the centre keeps the notices that wait (TOKI-073 R18), and a second queue here would show a toast twice. Check: a notice with no host before the first attach is handed over once at `attachHost`; the fourth notice and a notice older than 5,000 ms are not; after a host and `attachHost(null)` a notice is dropped and a later host receives nothing; with a host a notice is passed once and unchanged; a second `attachHost(null)` after a host stops the calls.

## Design
### Where each part stands
```
server  --notification.created (user event)--> sync engine --> notice centre (TOKI-073)
                                                                 |  judges: show / wait / drop, sound yes / no
                                                                 v
                                                           sink (this ticket)
                                       .toast(notice) ---> NoticeHost --> useToasts().show --> toast region
                                       .sound(notice) ---> burst guard --> platform.sound.play("notification")
                                                                                 ^
 first press / key anywhere --> armSoundUnlock --> platform.sound.unlock() ------+  (same Audio element)
```
The centre is in `packages/client-core` and knows no component and no sound; the sink is in `apps/web` and knows both. The host is a component only because the toast API (`useToasts`) and the open-message hook (`useOpenMessage`) exist only inside React; the sink, which is built outside React in `client.ts`, reaches them through the attached host.

### What a toast shows and does

### The sound
- Which sound. One file, `notification.wav`, two soft notes of 0.4 s in all (R1). The prototype plays no sound and no decision names one; the platform spec (TOKI-018 concern 1) left the choice of the file to this ticket and the product owner accepted that (2026-11-18), so this ticket proposes the sound and the product owner may replace it (concern 3). The name `notification` is the only member of `SoundName`; a second sound would add a name and a file.
- Where the file lives and how it is shipped. `apps/web/assets/sounds/`, beside `assets/fonts/`. The file is above the 4 KB limit under which Vite writes an asset into the JavaScript, so the build makes it a separate hashed file and `main.tsx` gets its address from the `?url` import; `check:sound` of R1 proves it. It is not in the service worker (TOKI-074 uses one for push only, D-030) and not preloaded (R2).
- When it plays. A sound plays for a notice only when all of these hold: the server decided `sound` true (the person's switch 'Play a sound' is on, the level and Mute allow it, and Pause or Focus time does not hold it: TOKI-073 R2); the screen judged that the person is not already reading it (not in the conversation at the bottom with the window in use, and not in the open thread: TOKI-073 R17); the burst guard lets it through (R6); and the browser allows it (R3 to R5).
- When it is blocked, by who:
  | Reason | Who stops it | What happens |
  |---|---|---|
  | 'Play a sound' is off (also on another device, the switch is the person's, D-142) | the server: the notice has `sound` false (TOKI-073 R2, TS-199) | no call to the sink |
  | Pause or Focus time | the server holds the notice: no event at all (D-072, TOKI-073 R2, R10) | nothing arrives; badges still update |
  | Level 'Nothing', Mute (a mention still passes while its switch is on) | the server: not allowed (D-103, D-088) | nothing arrives |
  | The window is in use and the message is on screen at the bottom, or the thread is open | the screen: `sound` false (TOKI-073 R17) | no call to the sink |
  | A second sound within 1 second of one that played | the sink (R6) | no sound; the toast still shows |
  | The browser refuses: the page has had no press, or Safari has not played this element from a press | the platform: `play` resolves `false` (R4) | nothing shown; the unlock step is armed again with the time of the refusal (R6, R5), and the next press within 5 seconds plays the sound |
  | The file cannot be loaded | the platform: `play` resolves `false` and drops the element | no sound; the next press unlocks a new element, and after 3 failures in a row the step stops |
- A sound with the window not in use (a hidden tab) is allowed by the table of TOKI-073 and plays when the browser lets it (R14 (b) checks it).

### The unlock flow
1. The app starts. `main.tsx` sets the platform with the address of the file (R1) and arms the unlock step (R5). Nothing is requested and nothing is played.
2. The person presses a key or a button anywhere, for example the first button of the sign-in screen, the Send button or the switch 'Play a sound' itself. The step calls `platform.sound.unlock()` in that same event (R5).
3. `unlock` (one call at a time, R3) creates the element for `notification`, silences it (`volume` 0), plays it, and as soon as the play promise resolves pauses it, rewinds it and restores the volume (R3). The browser has now seen a press start this element, so it may allow this element to be started by code later (the behaviour Safari documents for media elements; concern 4 says how sure we are: a silent play may not count). The step removes its listeners.
4. Later a notice arrives, the centre calls `sink.sound`, and `play` restarts the same element at full volume (R4). Nothing the person did is needed.
5. If the browser refuses a later play (an iPhone that discarded the page's permission, a tab restored from the background, or a silent play that did not count), `play` resolves `false` and the sink arms the step again with the time of the refusal (R6). The next press, if it comes within 5 seconds, plays the refused sound at full volume inside the press, which every browser allows and which unlocks the element; a later press calls `unlock` as in step 2 and 3. So the first sound is heard late and not lost, while its toast is still on screen; after 3 failures in a row the step stops until a later refusal arms it again.
On iOS, `volume` cannot be changed by script, so the silent play in step 3 can be heard as a very short tick; it happens once for each page load (concern 4, R14).
Because the step is armed from the start, the person who turns on 'Play a sound' on another device and then opens this one is covered by their first press here, with no setting to find. A person whose switch is off pays one request of the file after the first press (R5).

### Why a sink and a host, and not the centre drawing toasts
`useToasts` and `useOpenMessage` are React hooks, so a function outside a component cannot call them. The alternative, making the centre a React component, would put the waiting list and its timers inside the render tree of one screen. The sink port that TOKI-073 R18 defines is the seam; the host is the part that has a React context (R8).

### Errors
- A sound that the browser refuses, or a file that cannot be loaded: `play` resolves `false`, the unlock step is armed again (at most 3 failures in a row), and nothing is shown or logged.
- A notice that arrives when no host is attached: kept for 5 seconds and handed over if it is before the first host, and dropped otherwise (R7).
- A notice that names a conversation or a person the store does not hold, or a conversation whose name is the empty text: the toast uses the texts for an unknown conversation and for "Someone" (R9).
- A bad notice never reaches the sink: the centre ignores it (TOKI-073 R18).
