# Pause and Focus time design

Date: 2026-11-25 · Status: draft for review (concerns 1 to 4 are open for the product owner)
Intent: docs/intents/pause-focus-time.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-076

## Goal
A person who has to concentrate must be able to make Toki quiet for a while and trust that it stays quiet on every device, ends on time even when every tab is closed, and tells them afterwards what they missed. Focus time must also show Do not disturb to colleagues and put the status "Focusing" on the person, and give back the earlier status at the end.
This ticket builds the whole path: the stored state, the routes that start, extend and end it, the job that ends it on time, the answer to "is this person held at this moment" that the notification decision engine (TOKI-073) and Later (TOKI-067) ask, the count behind the summary, the event that tells every device, the client store, the account menu entries, the strip at the top of the main area, the countdown on the account card, the summary toast, the two palette actions and the line under a direct message header.

## Scope
- In:
  - Shared lengths, schemas and the event payload (R1).
  - The table, the time rules and the three routes that write (R2 to R6), the one procedure that ends a period (R7) and the end job (R8).
  - The reads: the person's own state, the hold answer for TOKI-073, the active Focus periods for presence, and the line of a direct message's other person (R9, R10).
  - The change, the event and the calls to presence (R11, R12), wiring and boundaries (R13), and what is logged (R14).
  - The client: API methods, the store, the account menu entries, the strip and its class `.strip`, the account card countdown, the toasts, the palette actions and the direct message line (R15 to R22), the texts (R23) and what does not change (R24).
- Out (not now):
  - Who gets a toast, push or sound, and the table of notices that were held: TOKI-073. This ticket gives it `HoldPort` and reads `countHeldNotices` (R9, R7).
  - Reminders, their timer and `listRemindersFiredBetween`: TOKI-067. This ticket asks whether a reminder is held through the same port and reads the count of reminders that fired.
  - Setting a status by hand, `setMemberStatus` and `getMemberStatus`: TOKI-056. This ticket calls them.
  - The presence state and the dot: TOKI-055. This ticket calls `setDoNotDisturb` and `restoreDoNotDisturb`.
  - The Pause section of the Preferences screen: TOKI-078; the entries of the More sheet on narrow screens: TOKI-092. Both read the hooks of R16 and R17 and the registry of TOKI-099; they add no state.
  - The toast component and the sound: TOKI-075. The offline strip and the other tones of `.strip`: TOKI-082; the update strip: TOKI-104.
  - Calendar alerts and the "In a Meet" state (phase 4).

## Requirements
Shared
- R1. `packages/shared/src/quiet/` (exported from the package index) holds `lengths.ts` and `schemas.ts`. `lengths.ts`: `QUIET_KINDS` equal to `["pause", "focus"]`; `PAUSE_LENGTHS` equal to `["30m", "1h", "tomorrow"]` (30 minutes, 1 hour, until tomorrow 08:00, F33); `FOCUS_LENGTHS` equal to `["25m", "1h"]` (F33); `QUIET_LENGTH_MS` `{ "25m": 1,500,000, "30m": 1,800,000, "1h": 3,600,000 }`; `QUIET_REASONS` equal to `["started", "extended", "replaced", "ended_early", "ended"]`; and `PAUSE_TOMORROW_TIME` `"08:00"`. No `demo` choice exists (D-145). `schemas.ts`, all strict: `StartPauseBodySchema` `{ length, timeZone? }` (`timeZone` required when `length` is `tomorrow` and valid by `isValidTimeZone` of TOKI-094); `StartFocusBodySchema` `{ length, statusText }` (`statusText` follows the text rule of `SetStatusBodySchema`, TOKI-056 R2: trimmed, 1 to `STATUS_TEXT_MAX` code points by `statusTextLength`, no control character); `QuietPeriodSchema` `{ endsAt }` (an ISO 8601 string); `QuietStateSchema` `{ pause: QuietPeriod | null, focus: QuietPeriod | null }`; `QuietResponseSchema` `{ state, number: integer >= 0 | null }`; `QuietChangedDataSchema` `{ kind, reason, pause, focus, summary: { count: integer >= 0 } | null }` (the payload of R11; `pause` and `focus` are the state after the change); `PeerQuietResponseSchema` `{ until: ISO 8601 string | null }`. Check: schema tests accept each valid object and refuse an added key, a length outside its list (`25m` for a Pause, `30m` for a Focus, `demo`), `tomorrow` with no zone or with `Mars/Phobos`, a statusText of `""`, of spaces, of 101 code points and with `\n`, a reason outside the list and a negative count; a test reads the three tuples and the four numbers; the export-list test of `packages/shared` lists the names.

Server: data and time
- R2. The table `quiet_periods` (a Drizzle table in `modules/quiet/schema.ts`, not exported from `index.ts`, one forward-only migration `<number>_quiet_periods`, TOKI-005): `user_id` uuid not null, `workspace_id` uuid not null (foreign keys to `users(id)` and `workspaces(id)` written as SQL, `ON DELETE RESTRICT`, TOKI-019 R22), `kind` text not null with a check in (`pause`, `focus`), `ends_at` timestamptz not null, `held_since` timestamptz not null, `earlier_text` text null, `earlier_clear_at` timestamptz null, `focus_text` text null; primary key (`user_id`, `kind`), so a person has at most one Pause and one Focus; an index on (`ends_at`). No time column has a database default; the server writes the time from the clock (TOKI-008 R19). Checks: `ends_at > held_since`; a `pause` row has the three Focus columns null; a `focus` row has `focus_text` of 1 to 100 characters; `earlier_text` is null or 1 to 100 characters; `earlier_clear_at` is null when `earlier_text` is null. `held_since` is the start of the unbroken stretch of quiet that this row belongs to (R4, R5, R7). `earlier_text` and `earlier_clear_at` hold the status the person had when Focus started (null when there was none); `focus_text` is the text this ticket wrote as the status. A row whose `ends_at` has passed is never read as active (R9). Check: a migration test reads the columns, keys, checks and index from the catalog; each refused row (two rows for one person and kind, a Pause with a `focus_text`, a Focus without one, `ends_at` equal to `held_since`, a 101-character text) is refused by the database; `db:generate` reports no change.
- R3. The time rules are pure functions in `modules/quiet/time.ts`, taking the clock's `now` as a number: `pauseEndsAt(length, now, timeZone?)` returns `now + QUIET_LENGTH_MS[length]` for `30m` and `1h` and `atLocalTime(now, timeZone, 1, "08:00")` of TOKI-094 for `tomorrow`, and `null` when that is null; `focusStartEndsAt(length, now)` returns `now + QUIET_LENGTH_MS[length]`; `focusExtendedEndsAt(currentEndsAt, length)` returns `currentEndsAt + QUIET_LENGTH_MS[length]` (the reading of concern 2, one line to change). None reads a clock or a global. Check, with fixed numbers: 10:00 gives 10:30, 11:00 and, in `Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh`, 08:00 of the next local day; at 23:30 `tomorrow` is 08:00 of the day after the local date and not 08:00 of the same date; on a day the zone's clock is set forward `tomorrow` still gives a later instant; Focus of 25 minutes started at 10:00 and extended by 1 hour gives 11:25; a search of `time.ts` finds no `Date.now(` or `new Date(`.

Server: routes that write
- R4. `POST /api/v1/quiet/pause`, in the `/api/v1` scope (a valid session, TOKI-022 R24; the `Origin` rule of TOKI-022 R46 covers it), takes `StartPauseBodySchema` and answers 200 `QuietResponseSchema` with the state after the change and `number`, the number of the event it wrote. A body that fails the schema, or a `tomorrow` whose instant is null, is 400 `validation_failed`. In one transaction, in this order: (1) the person's Pause row is read `FOR UPDATE`; (2) `ends_at` is worked out (R3); (3) the row is written: when none is active (no row, or `ends_at` not above `now`) a new row with `held_since` equal to the `held_since` of an active Focus row of the person, or `now` when there is none, and reason `started`; when one is active the row is replaced with the new `ends_at`, its `held_since` kept, and reason `replaced` (the last request wins, D-121, so a second device or a double press cannot nest; concern 6); (4) the end job is moved: `jobs.cancelByKey(endPeriodJob, "<userId>:pause", { tx })`, then `jobs.enqueue(endPeriodJob, { userId, kind: "pause", endsAt }, { startAfter, key, tx })` (TOKI-064 R11, R12, R14); (5) `recordChange` once with the change of R11, as the last write, then `getLastEventNumber` for `number`. The route takes no account lock: a person whose account is deactivated is never a recipient of a notice (TOKI-073 R3), so a row written in that race holds nobody. Check, with the `TestClock` and a real database and the fake queue: a first request writes one row, one job whose `startAfter` is `endsAt` and key is `<userId>:pause`, one event, and answers the number of that event; a second request while it runs leaves one row with the new `endsAt`, the first `held_since`, one cancelled job and reason `replaced`; a request after `ends_at` (the job has not run) writes a new row with `held_since` equal to now; a transaction that throws after the call leaves no row, job or event; `tomorrow` without a zone is 400; no session is 401.
- R5. `POST /api/v1/quiet/focus` takes `StartFocusBodySchema` and answers 200 `QuietResponseSchema`. In one transaction: (1) the person's Focus row is read `FOR UPDATE`; (2) when it is active it is extended: `ends_at` becomes `focusExtendedEndsAt` (R3), nothing else of the row changes, the status is not written again, and the reason is `extended` (F33 and D-072: one period, not two; concern 2 is the open reading of how far); (3) otherwise a new row is written: `earlier_text` and `earlier_clear_at` from `getMemberStatus(tx, userId)` of TOKI-056 (`{ text, clearAt }` or null), `focus_text` the body's `statusText`, `ends_at` from `focusStartEndsAt`, `held_since` as in R4, reason `started`; (4) the end job is moved as in R4 with key `<userId>:focus`; (5) for a new row `setMemberStatus(tx, deps, { userId, text: statusText, clearAt: null, reason: "set" })` of TOKI-056 R6 is called (it locks the account row, refuses a deactivated account with 401 `account_deactivated`, and writes its own change); (6) `recordChange` for R11 as the last write of this module, then `getLastEventNumber` for `number`. All writes of this module come before the call of step 5, so the counter lock that `recordChange` takes (TOKI-036 R18) is the last lock; the two changes of one transaction are two events with consecutive numbers. After the commit the route calls presence (R12). Check: a first request writes the row with the earlier status of the person (a person with the status "Reviewing" that clears at 15:00 and a person with no status are two cases), writes the status "Focusing" through `setMemberStatus` and answers the number of the quiet event; a second request while it runs leaves one row, the same `earlier_text`, a later `ends_at`, one cancelled job and no second status write; a deactivated account is 401 and writes nothing; the two events of one start have consecutive numbers; a request with a statusText of 101 code points is 400.
- R6. `DELETE /api/v1/quiet/pause` and `DELETE /api/v1/quiet/focus` end that period now. Each answers 200 `QuietResponseSchema`; when the person has no such active period it writes nothing and answers the state with `number` null (a retry after a lost answer is safe, D-111). Otherwise it calls `endPeriod` (R7) with reason `ended_early`, and after the commit the Focus route calls presence (R12). Check: two `DELETE` calls give one event and the second answers `number: null`; ending a Focus restores the status (R7) and ends Do not disturb (R12); ending a Pause while a Focus runs leaves the Focus row and its strip; no session is 401.

Server: the end
- R7. `endPeriod(tx, deps, { userId, kind, reason, endsAt? })` of `modules/quiet/end.ts` is the one procedure that ends a period; the routes of R6 and the job of R8 call it. In order: (1) it deletes the row `where user_id = $1 and kind = $2` and, when `endsAt` is given, `and ends_at = $3`, `returning` the row; with no row it writes nothing and returns `{ changed: false, number: null }`. (2) It calls `jobs.cancelByKey` for the key of the row. (3) For `focus` it restores the status: the person's present status is read with `getMemberStatus`; when it is the status this ticket wrote (its `text` equals the row's `focus_text` and its `clearAt` is null) it is replaced: with the row's `earlier_text` and `earlier_clear_at` when `earlier_text` is not null and `earlier_clear_at` is null or later than `now` (the rule of the prototype's `endFocus`, F32, NR6), and cleared (`text` null, reason `cleared`) otherwise; when the present status is anything else the person changed it during Focus and it is left alone (concern 4 is the open reading). A `setMemberStatus` that throws `account_deactivated` is caught, by that code only, and the person gets no restore. (4) It reads the person's other row; the person is back to normal notifications when there is none with `ends_at` above `now` (F33: Focus ending under a longer Pause leaves notifications paused). (5) When the person is back to normal it works out the summary of R7 below, otherwise `summary` is null. (6) It calls `recordChange` for R11 as the last write, with the state that remains (the other row when it is active, else null) and reads `getLastEventNumber`. The summary is `{ count }` with `count` equal to `countHeldNotices(tx, userId, heldSince, to)` of TOKI-073 R10 plus the number of items that `listRemindersFiredBetween(tx, userId, heldSince, to)` of TOKI-067 R12 returns, where `heldSince` is the deleted row's `held_since` (every row of one unbroken stretch carries the stretch's start, R4 and R5) and `to` is the smaller of `now` and the row's `ends_at`, so a late job does not count what arrived after the end. The two counts are read through `SummarySources` (R13), because `modules/quiet` imports no other module. Check: with the `TestClock` and fake sources, a Pause alone gives `count` of both sources added, with the lower bound included and the upper excluded; a Focus that ends while a longer Pause runs gives `summary` null, restores the status and leaves the Pause row; the Pause that then ends gives the summary from the Focus's start (the earlier `held_since`); a Pause that began 5 minutes after a Focus began and ended after it gives one summary at its end from the Focus's start; a status the person set during Focus is left alone; a deactivated person gets the event and no restore; a row that is not there writes nothing; the change is recorded once and after the status write.
- R8. The end job. `endPeriodJob = defineJob({ name: "quiet.end-period", payload: z.strictObject({ userId: uuid, kind: enum, endsAt: ISO date-time }) })` is declared in `modules/quiet` and exported from its `index.ts`; its type is in `apps/server/src/job-types.ts` and its handler factory `createEndPeriodHandler(deps: JobDeps)` in `job-handlers.ts` (TOKI-064 R9). The payload holds an identifier, a kind and a time only (TOKI-064 R7). The handler runs in one `context.db.transaction` and calls `endPeriod` with reason `ended` and the job's `endsAt`; the row is matched on that exact time, so an extended or replaced period, an ended one and a second delivery of the same job do nothing. It reads no clock to decide (the job is handed over at `endsAt`, TOKI-064 R14); `context.clock` gives only `now`. The retry defaults of TOKI-064 R8 apply; an error message holds the user id and a code. The handler runs in the worker, which has no presence: the end of Do not disturb is carried by the time given at the start (R12). Check, on a jobs database (`createJobsTestDb`) with the library's `TestClock` at `DEFAULT_TEST_INSTANT`: a Pause of 30 minutes is a row after 29 minutes 59 seconds and gone, with one `ended` event, after a further `pollingSeconds` (2); a Focus extended by 1 hour before its first end keeps its row at the first end time (the first job is cancelled, and a forced delivery of it deletes nothing) and ends at the new one; the same job run twice gives one event; the payload walk of TOKI-064 R7 passes; a Focus ended by the worker restores the status with the `JobDeps` of `buildJobDeps` on a worker-mode queue.

Server: reads
- R9. Reads of `modules/quiet`, exported from `index.ts`. (a) `readQuietState(client, userId, now)` returns the `QuietState` of the person from the rows whose `ends_at` is above `now`. (b) `GET /api/v1/quiet` (a valid session) answers 200 `QuietResponseSchema` with `Cache-Control: no-store`: the state and `number`, the last event number read by `getLastEventNumber` in the same read-only transaction (as TOKI-031 R10 does), so a client applies the answer and the events after it in order. (c) `createHoldPort()` returns `HoldPort` of TOKI-073 R10 (`listHeld(client, userIds, at)`): the ids among `userIds` that have a row of either kind with `ends_at` above `at` (milliseconds), in one statement for any number of people, and no statement for an empty list. It is the real port that `app.ts` and `buildJobDeps` pass in place of the stand-in of TOKI-073 and the one Later's `reminderNotifier.isHeld` reaches through it (TOKI-073 R11); so a reminder that falls due during Pause or Focus is held with the same rule as a message (D-072, D-063). It reads no table of another module, and every id it is given is already a member of the one workspace (D-004). (d) `listActiveFocus(client, now)` returns `{ userId, until }[]` for the Focus rows with `ends_at` above `now`, for R12. Check: the state of a person with a Pause that ended a second ago and a Focus that runs holds only the Focus; the answer number equals the last event number; `listHeld` holds a person with a Pause only, one with a Focus only and one with both, not one whose row ended 1 ms before `at`, not a person with no row, and not another person; its statement count is one for 1 and for 200 people; `listHeld` satisfies the type `HoldPort` (a type test in the composition test); a held person's mention writes no event and one `held_notices` row, run through the real handler of TOKI-073 with this port (TS-060).
- R10. The line under a direct message header needs the other person's end time and nothing else. `GET /api/v1/quiet/dm/:conversationId` (a valid session) answers 200 `PeerQuietResponseSchema`: `until` is the latest `ends_at` of the other person's active rows, or null. It is allowed only when `getConversationFacts` of TOKI-028 gives a `dm` of which the caller is a member, and the other person is the one member that is not the caller (`listMemberIds`); anything else, including a group, a channel, a conversation the caller is not in and an id that does not exist, is the same 404 `not_found`. The answer holds no kind, no length, no count and no status; it is for the one person on the other side of a direct message and for nobody else (concern 7). It sets `Cache-Control: no-store`. Check: the other person of a direct message gets `until` while the Pause runs and null after; a stranger, a group, a channel and an unknown id are 404 with the same body; the answer has no other key; a person asking about their own direct message with themselves is 404 (messaging yourself is not supported).

Server: change, event, presence, wiring
- R11. The change and the event. `Change` of `modules/conversations/changes.ts` gains the member `QuietChange`: `{ type: "quiet_changed"; actorId: string; workspaceId: string; userIds: readonly [string]; at: Date; kind; reason; pause; focus; summary }` with no `conversationId`, `actorId` and the one id in `userIds` the person, `at` `new Date(clock.now())`, and `pause`, `focus` and `summary` as in `QuietChangedDataSchema`. The union is built by several tickets (TOKI-056, TOKI-065, TOKI-067, TOKI-102); whichever of them is built later adds its member to the union it finds. The event handler of TOKI-038 (R20) gets one row: `quiet_changed` writes the event `quiet.changed`, scope `user` (the person), payload `QuietChangedDataSchema`, with `recordEvent` and no read of the database; `EventChange` gains the structural member; the registry of `packages/shared/src/realtime/events.ts` gains the kind and `kinds.ts` a row saying it changes no map. The system-line handler of TOKI-037, the Activity handler of TOKI-065 and the notification handler of TOKI-073 ignore this type. A user event goes to every connection of that person and to nobody else (TOKI-038 R16) and is replayed to that person (TOKI-039), so every device learns of every change, including a device opened later. Check: with the real event handler and fake sockets, a start writes one `quiet.changed` received by both tabs of the person and by nobody else; the payload passes the schema; a rolled-back change leaves no event; the registry test, the kinds test and the export-list tests pass; no `system` message exists after a change; the handlers of TOKI-037, 038, 065 and 073 compile with the wider union.
- R12. Presence. For Focus only (F33, F34: Pause alone never changes presence, TOKI-055 R6), after the commit of a start or an extension the route calls `setDoNotDisturb(userId, endsAtMs)` of TOKI-055 R6, and after the commit of an early end `setDoNotDisturb(userId, null)`; an end by time makes no call, because the sweep of presence leaves Do not disturb at the time given at the start (TOKI-055 concern 11, a lag of up to 3 seconds for the dot is accepted there). When the app starts `app.ts` calls `restoreDoNotDisturb(entries)` with `listActiveFocus` (R9 (d)), so a restart does not drop a running Focus. Presence is memory of the app process and the worker has no access to it; this ticket adds no frame, route or function that sets a state by hand (F34, AC-129). Check, with a fake presence: a Focus start calls `setDoNotDisturb` once with its end, an extension with the later end, an early end with null, a Pause with nothing, and a start whose transaction rolled back with nothing; `restoreDoNotDisturb` is called once at start with the active Focus rows only; the job of R8 imports nothing of presence.
- R13. Wiring and boundaries. `modules/quiet/index.ts` exports `quietRoutes`, `endPeriodJob`, `endPeriod` (the factory `createEndPeriodHandler` is in `job-handlers.ts`), `createHoldPort`, `readQuietState`, `listActiveFocus` and the types `QuietDeps`, `SummarySources` and `QuietChange`; no table. `modules/quiet` imports no other module, only `@toki/shared`, `modules/jobs` through its `index.ts` and the database client: `app.ts` passes in `recordChange`, `getLastEventNumber`, `setMemberStatus`, `getMemberStatus` (`modules/members`), `setDoNotDisturb` (`app.presence`), `resolveDmPeer(db, actorId, conversationId)` (built in `app.ts` from `getConversationFacts` and `listMemberIds`), the clock, `jobs` and `summarySources`, where `SummarySources` is `{ countHeldNotices(client, userId, fromMs, toMs): Promise<number>; countRemindersFired(client, userId, fromMs, toMs): Promise<number> }`, the first the function of TOKI-073 R10 and the second `(await listRemindersFiredBetween(...)).length` of TOKI-067 R12. `JobDeps` (TOKI-064 R9, its table "Fields of `JobDeps`") gains one field, `summarySources`, built in `buildJobDeps`; the `holdPort` field that TOKI-073 adds gets `createHoldPort()` in place of its stand-in; `getLastEventNumber`, `jobs` and `recordChange` are base fields or TOKI-056's. `job-types.ts`, `job-deps.ts` and `job-handlers.ts` list the job, the field and the handler. Routes are registered under `/api/v1/quiet` (`GET /`, `POST /pause`, `POST /focus`, `DELETE /pause`, `DELETE /focus`, `GET /dm/:conversationId`). The export lists of the modules are updated. Check: the export test lists the names; `pnpm lint` finds no import of another module in `modules/quiet`; the route-list test lists the six routes; `buildJobHandlers` with fake deps returns the handler for `quiet.end-period`; the app and the worker build the same change-handler list.
- R14. What is logged and kept. No log line, job payload or error message of this work holds a status text; lines carry the user id, the kind and the reason. `statusText` is stored in `focus_text` and `earlier_text` (it is the person's own words) and travels only in the request and in the status events of TOKI-056; the event of R11 and the job carry none. A Postgres error is logged by code and constraint name only. Check: the captured log of a test that starts Focus with the text `secret-focus-text`, extends it, ends it and lets one expire holds no such text; the payload walk of TOKI-064 R7 passes; the event payload has no text key.

Client
- R15. API methods. `packages/client-core/src/api/` gains `api.quiet` with `get()`, `startPause(body)`, `startFocus(body)`, `endPause()`, `endFocus()` and `peer(conversationId)`, each through the client's request function with the response schema of R1, so an answer that does not parse is an error and not data. Check: a method test for each, with a fake transport, for a good answer, a 400, a 401 and an answer that fails its schema.
- R16. The store. `packages/client-core/src/quiet/` exports `createQuietStore({ api, clock, registerEventHandler, onReload, connection })`, built like the Later store (TOKI-067 R16: the app makes one in `client.ts`; the store imports none of the seams) with `getState()` `{ status, pause, focus, number, error }` (`status` `idle`, `loading`, `ready`, `failed`; `pause` and `focus` are `{ endsAt }` or null), `subscribe`, `subscribeNotices(listener)`, `load()`, `isQuiet()` (a Pause or a Focus is held), and the commands `startPause(length, timeZone?)`, `startFocus(length, statusText)`, `endPause()`, `endFocus()`, each resolving to `{ ok: true, notice }` or `{ ok: false, error }` and never rejecting. Rules: (a) `load()` is made when the conversation store becomes `ready` with a user, when the connection becomes `online` after being down and after `sync.reload()`; one load runs at a time and a second asked is queued once. (b) The store keeps the `number` of the answer that filled it; an answer or an event is applied only when its number is above it, and a `number` of null changes nothing, so an older answer never overwrites a newer event (D-121). (c) The event `quiet.changed` is applied through `registerEventHandler("quiet.changed", …)` of TOKI-040 R18 and replaces both periods with the event's. (d) A reset of the conversation store (sign-out, another person) empties the state and the listeners' memory. (e) `subscribeNotices` is called once for a change that is new to the store, with `{ kind, reason, summary, endsAt, replayed }` where `endsAt` is the end of the period named by `kind` after the change (null when it ended) and `replayed` is true when the event was applied while the connection was catching up (as TOKI-067 R16 (f2)), so no clock is compared. (f) The reference of `getState()` is kept when a change changes nothing. (g) A command does not run while `ensureOnline` is false; the web wrapper calls the gate (TOKI-093), not the store. Check, with a fake `api`, a fake clock and a fake sync: each rule; an event with a lower number than the held one does nothing; the same event twice calls the listener once; an event applied during catch-up has `replayed` true and a live one false whatever the page clock says; two commands in a row apply in order; a reset empties the state.
- R17. ## Design
### How it fits
Two rows at most for a person, one for each kind, are the whole state. A request writes the row, moves the end job and records one change in one transaction; the change becomes one user event that every device of the person applies in order. The decision engine and Later never read the table: they ask `HoldPort.listHeld` (R9), which answers true for a person while any row has `ends_at` above the moment asked. The end is a job with the exact end time, so a restart, a late worker or a second delivery cannot end a period that was extended.

Flow of the end:
```
job (endsAt) or DELETE --> endPeriod: delete row (exact endsAt for the job)
                       --> Focus: restore the status if it is still ours
                       --> other row still active? no summary : count held notices + reminders fired since held_since
                       --> recordChange(quiet_changed) --> event quiet.changed --> every device: strip off, summary toast
```

### The unbroken stretch and the summary
Pause and Focus run side by side. A row gets `held_since` of the other active row when it starts, so every row of one unbroken stretch carries the stretch's start. When the last row ends, its own `held_since` is the start of everything that was held, and the summary counts from there. A gap between two stretches starts a new `held_since`.
What is counted is what the engine says it held (TOKI-073 R10: one row for each notice that was allowed and then held) plus the reminders that fired in the stretch (TOKI-067 R12). A message that would not have notified (a plain message in a conversation at 'Mentions only', or a mention in a muted channel whose switch is off) is not counted, because it would not have been a notice at all. Held rows are kept 72 hours (TOKI-073 R10), so a summary counts at most the last 72 hours of a very long stretch (concern 11).

### Data model
- `quiet_periods` (R2): `user_id`, `workspace_id`, `kind`, `ends_at`, `held_since`, `earlier_text`, `earlier_clear_at`, `focus_text`; primary key (`user_id`, `kind`); index on `ends_at`.

### API
| Method | Path | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| GET | `/api/v1/quiet` | The person's state and the last event number (R9) |
| POST | `/api/v1/quiet/pause` | Start or replace a Pause (R4) |
| POST | `/api/v1/quiet/focus` | Start or extend Focus time (R5) |
| DELETE | `/api/v1/quiet/pause` | End the Pause now (R6) |
| DELETE | `/api/v1/quiet/focus` | End Focus time now (R6) |
| GET | `/api/v1/quiet/dm/:conversationId` | The other person's end time for the line under the header (R10) |

### Events, jobs and ports
| What | Kind | Scope or owner | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| a change of the person's quiet state | event `quiet.changed` | user | in the transaction of the change (R11) |
| end of a period | job `quiet.end-period` | worker | at `ends_at` (R8) |
| is the person held at this moment | `HoldPort.listHeld` | read by TOKI-073 | each notice and each reminder (R9) |
| Do not disturb | `setDoNotDisturb`, `restoreDoNotDisturb` | TOKI-055, app process | after commit, at start (R12) |
| the status | `setMemberStatus`, `getMemberStatus` | TOKI-056 | start, end (R5, R7) |

### Errors
- A body that fails its schema, or `tomorrow` with no instant: 400 `validation_failed`.
- No session: 401. A deactivated account that starts Focus: 401 `account_deactivated` from `setMemberStatus`; for a Pause nothing is refused (R4).
- An end with nothing running: 200 with `number` null.
- A direct message line for anything but a direct message of the caller: 404 `not_found`.
- A failing handler rolls the whole transaction back; the job is tried again by TOKI-064 R8, and an end with an already deleted row does nothing.

The prototype shows a toast every time ("Pause ended. Notifications are back on", with the count and an Activity button only when something arrived). TS-062 says the summary lists the mentions; the prototype and F33 show a count and a button to Activity, where the items are listed. A person with no device open at the end has no one to show a toast to, and the log does not say whether the summary waits. Proposal, not a decision: follow the prototype: a toast on every live device at the end, with the count and the Activity button when the count is above 0 and the plain "back on" words when it is 0, the same for Pause and Focus; the count stands for the list; no summary is kept for a device that opens later (the Activity badge and items are there). R20 builds this; the zero rule is one condition of the host. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
4. The status at the end when the person changed it during Focus. Not decided. F32 and NR6 say ending Focus "restores the earlier status", and TOKI-056 concern 8 leaves the case of a status set during Focus to this ticket; the log does not say whether the person's new choice or the earlier status wins. The prototype's `endFocus` restores the earlier status whatever happened. Proposal, not a decision: when the present status is the one this ticket wrote ("Focusing" with no clear time) it is replaced by the earlier status, or cleared when that had run out; when the person set or cleared anything else during Focus it is left as the person made it, because overwriting a status chosen on purpose would surprise them. R7 step 3 builds this, in one condition. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
5. Where the state is kept and how devices learn of it. Decided in this spec: a table with at most one row for each kind and person, one user event for each change with its number, and a read route, not a field of the bootstrap. Reason: the state is the person's own and changes often; the event is replayed to a device that was away, the route gives the first paint and the number to order the two (as Later does, TOKI-067), and the bootstrap's statement bound (TOKI-031 R14) stays as it is. Cost: the strip appears after one request on a fresh load. Owner: Thanh Truong.
6. Pause and Focus run together, and a second Pause replaces the first. Decided by F33: "Focus ends while a longer Pause is still running: notifications stay paused under that Pause", and by D-072 for Focus (extend, not nest); the prototype menu offers Pause while Focus runs. Decided in this spec: the two are independent rows, a person is held while either has not ended, the strip shows Focus when both run (the prototype), and a Pause started while a Pause runs replaces it with the new end, the last request winning as D-121 says for two saves; the screens never offer it (R17 shows Resume instead). Reason: replacing is the least surprising answer for a double press or a second device, and the log's only rule for Pause on Pause is none. Owner: Thanh Truong.
7. The line under a direct message header. Decided in this spec: this ticket builds it (R10, R22). The journey (F25, accepted as the baseline by D-091) has the line, and no plan line or spec owns it; the state it needs exists only here. The other person sees the end time of whichever of Pause and Focus ends last, in one sentence, and nothing else, and only in a direct message with that person, not in a group or by any other route; the line is read when the conversation opens and when the connection returns, so it can show a little longer than the Pause when the person resumes early. Reason: a live event to every colleague would tell the whole workspace; the journey asks only for the other person of a direct message. The words are a proposal under D-139 (R23). Owner: Thanh Truong.
8. The time zone of "until tomorrow 08:00". Decided in this spec: the zone the web app already uses for the person's dates (`useDateContext`, the browser's), sent with the request, the same reading as TOKI-056 concern 1 for "End of today", so the Preferences zone of TOKI-078 later replaces the source in one place. D-144 gives the profile zone the person set, which is not built yet. Owner: Thanh Truong.
9. What the menu and the palette offer. Decided in this spec: the menu offers the lengths of F33 and keeps the Focus options while Focus runs, because D-072 makes starting Focus again a real action and the prototype's menu offers only End then; the palette offers Pause for 30 minutes and Focus time for 1 hour (TOKI-071 concern 8 leaves the length to this ticket; the prototype's palette uses an hour for Focus and a 20-second demo for Pause, which D-145 removes). Reason: each is the nearest real choice to the prototype. Owner: Thanh Truong.
10. Presence. Decided in this spec: Focus calls `setDoNotDisturb` with its end and `restoreDoNotDisturb` runs at start; an end by time needs no call; Pause never touches presence (TOKI-055 R6, concern 11). Reason: presence is memory of the app process and the end runs in the worker; the time given at the start is enough, and the dot may lag the status by up to 3 seconds at the end, as TOKI-055 accepts. Owner: Thanh Truong.
11. What the summary counts and for how long. Decided by D-072 ("one summary lists what arrived") and F28 (a reminder that falls due while held counts in the summary): the count is the notices that the engine held plus the reminders that fired during the unbroken stretch of quiet, counted from its start to the end of the last period. Decided in this spec: a notice that would not have notified is not counted, and held rows live 72 hours (TOKI-073 R10), so a stretch longer than that counts only the last 72 hours. Reason: only the engine knows what would have notified; no cap of 72 hours is a product rule. Owner: Thanh Truong.
12. The strip leaves on the server's word. Decided in this spec: the strip and the card clock stay until the event of the end arrives and the countdown stops at `00:00`, instead of ending on the page's own clock. Reason: the end runs in the worker within the queue's poll of 2 seconds (TOKI-064 R14), a page clock can be wrong, and a strip that vanished before the status was restored would disagree with the card. A worker that is stopped leaves the strip at `00:00` until it runs again, while the hold itself ended on time (R9). Owner: Thanh Truong.
13. A deactivated person. Decided in this spec: a Pause is written without an account lock and holds nobody, because a deactivated person is never a recipient; a Focus start is refused by `setMemberStatus`; the end of a running period for a deactivated person deletes the row, writes the event and restores no status. Reason: the job must not fail for ever on a person who can no longer be written to; whether deactivation should end Focus is TOKI-055 concern 2's question and not repeated here. Owner: Thanh Truong.
14. The wording. Decided in this spec: the English words are the prototype's; the extension toast and the direct message line are not in it and are proposals under D-139; the Vietnamese words are a draft reviewed in TOKI-080. Owner: Thanh Truong.
