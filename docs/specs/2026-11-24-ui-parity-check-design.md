# UI parity check design

Date: 2026-11-24 · Status: draft for review
Intent: docs/intents/ui-parity-check.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-109

## Goal
The builder (Thanh and Claude) and the reviewer of a pull request need a machine to say where a built screen differs from the prototype, in Calm light and in Focus dark (and in Classic dark once the prototype draws it), at three widths (was: in Calm and in Focus; TOKI-142 R11). Today "matches the prototype" is checked by eye (TOKI-016 R21), the design values are compared with values written by hand in a spec (TOKI-016 R11, R15), and the screenshots are compared with the page's own earlier screenshots (TOKI-016 R24). After this change one check reads the prototype itself and writes a report that says what differs and where; the reviewer reads it, and a difference that is wrong becomes a ticket to fix that part. Each later ticket that builds a screen adds its screens to the check instead of comparing by eye.

## Scope
- In:
  - The reference: a fixed copy of the prototype in the repository, with its hash recorded (R1, R17).
  - The token check: the design values of the app compared with the same values of the prototype, for every look, accent and density (R2 to R4).
  - The screen check: a way for a ticket to declare a screen, reference images made from the prototype by one command, and a comparison of the app with them, run only in the Playwright container (R5 to R10, R13).
  - The report of the differences and its place in CI (R11, R15).
  - One sample screen, as proof (R14); a short guide for later tickets (R16).
- Out (not now):
  - Fonts: TOKI-010 owns the seven font files and their rules. This work uses them and adds none.
  - Screens that need seeded data, the Playwright config that runs them and its CI step. They need `seedPrototypeWorkspace` of TOKI-081 (end of M3) and belong to a ticket after TOKI-081 (concern 6). This work only lets a declaration say that it needs seeded data (R12).
  - The tests and the record of TOKI-016 (the machine check R2 to R20, the by-eye record R21, the screenshot test R24) and the specs of TOKI-008, TOKI-010 and TOKI-016. They are not edited and they stay as they are (concern 5). The line of the TOKI-016 spec that keeps the prototype out of the repository concerns that ticket's own work; this check is the exception, decided in concern 1.
  - Any screen or state that the prototype does not draw (TOKI-016 Table E lists this kind of difference); hover, active and focus-visible states; Safari and Firefox (TOKI-008 concern 5).
  - Making each later ticket use the check: that is in the spec of each of those tickets.
  - A change to the prototype.

## Requirements
- R1. The prototype is a fixed copy of the Blueprint's `docs/blueprint/prototype/index.html`, committed at `e2e/prototype/index.html`, and is opened by one function, `openPrototype`, in `e2e/support/prototype.ts`. The location is a constant in that file only. The prototype is drawn with the seven font faces of TOKI-010 and requests no other host. Check: a test records the requests of one load and none goes to another host (the prototype's `link` to Google Fonts, its line 4, is not used), and `document.fonts` lists the seven faces as loaded.
- R2. Look, accent and density are set on the prototype in the way it sets them itself (`applyLook`, line 1137: `data-look`, `data-eff` (was and still is the prototype's own attribute until the prototype ticket changes it), `data-accent`, `data-density` on the root element; the preference key `toki-proto-v2`, line 1120; these are words about the prototype, the app has `data-look` and `data-scheme` beside `data-accent` and `data-density`, TOKI-142 R1). The check sets a look and a scheme on each side: Calm light and Focus dark are the prototype's own Calm and Focus, set with `applyAppearance({ look, scheme })` in the app, and Classic dark is set once the prototype has drawn it (TOKI-142 R11). Check: in Calm with teal the prototype's `--accent` is `#0F766E` and in Focus with teal it is `#5EEAD4`; in Calm with indigo it is `#4F46E5` and in Focus with indigo `#A5B4FC`; in Calm with clay it is `#B4532A` and in Focus with clay `#FDBA8C`; in Calm with violet it is `#7C3AED` and in Focus with violet `#C4B5FD` (the prototype's lines 6 to 56, also TOKI-016 R11 and R15). Teal is the default and has no `data-accent` override; the other three accents are the prototype's `[data-eff][data-accent]` rules at its lines 49 to 54 (the app's are `[data-look][data-scheme][data-accent]`).
- R3. The token check compares, for each of the 24 combinations (2 looks × 4 accents, teal, indigo, clay and violet, × 3 densities: the look's own, `comfortable`, `compact`), each custom property of the app with the same property of the prototype, and records one row for each difference: the property, the combination, the prototype's value and the app's value. The app side is the preview page of TOKI-016 (`/preview.html`) with its own controls. Values are compared as the browser computes them (a colour as RGB, a length in pixels), not as written, so that `#0f766e` and `#0F766E` are the same. A difference is a result in the report (R11), not a failed test. The token check reads values and does not depend on rendering; it lives in the parity config (`playwright.parity.config.ts`, R5) so that one script, `pnpm test:e2e:parity`, runs both checks. Check: a changed token value in `packages/tokens` gives a row for that property in every combination where it applies; the unchanged repository gives none.
- R4. The list of properties comes from `@toki/tokens` (the 32 properties of TOKI-009 R10). The check holds no second list. A property that the prototype lacks is a difference ("the prototype has no value"). The six Meet variables that TOKI-009 R18 leaves out are not compared. A difference that the reviewer has accepted is one entry in `e2e/parity/known-differences.ts` with the property or the screen, the combination, the reason and the ticket that will fix it (or the word `accepted`). The report lists known differences apart from new ones, and lists an entry that no longer matches a real difference as "no longer a difference: remove the entry". Check: an entry for a real difference moves its row from "new" to "known"; an entry for a difference that does not exist appears under "no longer a difference".
- R5. A screen is declared in a file `e2e/parity/screens/<name>.ts` that exports one or more objects with: `id`; the prototype's section (`home`, `activity`, `later`, `files`, `mail`, `calendar`, `meet`, `tasks`, `people`, `admin` or `prefs`, the keys of its `HASH`, line 1200); the steps that bring the prototype to the state (clicks and key presses on named selectors); the route and the steps that bring the app to the same state; the selectors to mask; the looks and their schemes, each a look and a scheme (default: Calm light, Focus dark and Classic dark, the default scheme of each look; drawing all six on every screen is not asked for, TOKI-142 R11), and the widths of R6 (default all three) (was: the looks, default both); and whether it needs seeded data (R12). A file added under `e2e/parity/screens/` is picked up with no other edit (as TOKI-008 R12). The declarations and the tests live under `e2e/parity/`, which the default Playwright config ignores (as it ignores `real-server/**` and `sign-in-server/**`) and which a separate config, `playwright.parity.config.ts`, runs by the root script `pnpm test:e2e:parity` (the pattern of `playwright.sign-in.config.ts` and `test:e2e:sign-in`); the plain `pnpm test:e2e` therefore does not run them. Check: a file with one declaration adds, in the next `pnpm test:e2e:parity`, one comparison for each look, scheme and width that it names.
- R6. Every capture, of the prototype and of the app, uses the same settings: Chromium; three widths, one in each band of D-068 as TOKI-016 R21 does: `wide` 1280 × 800 (1100 and over), `mid` 900 × 800 (720 to 1099) and `narrow` 390 × 844 (under 720); device scale factor 1; the time zone `Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh` and the locale `en-US`; the colour scheme `light`, so that the prototype's choice of look (`effLook`, line 1136) follows the declared look only; reduced motion. The width is the browser window's, so that both sides use the same layout (the prototype's container rule at 720 px, line 107, and TOKI-012 R8). Check: the reference image and the app's image of a screen have the same width and height, or the comparison records a difference "sizes differ" with both sizes.
- R7. Both sides are made stable before a capture. The browser's time is fixed at Tuesday 10 November 2026, 10:42 in `Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh` (the prototype's `now()`, line 916, starts there, and TOKI-081 R9 gives its "today" the same value), so that the prototype's timers and times do not move. Animations are off. The capture waits for `document.fonts.ready`, scrolls every pane to its top and hides the caret. Only the element that holds the app is captured: `#app` for the prototype (line 817), which leaves out its demo bar (`.protobar`, line 71), and the app's root container for the app. The elements that show a running count (`[data-timer]`) and the selectors of the declaration are masked on both sides. Check: the reference command run twice in a row on the same screen writes identical files (no pixel differs).
- R8. The reference images are made from the prototype, never from the app, by `pnpm ui:reference <screen-id>` (or `--all`). The command runs in the official Playwright container image of the same version as `@playwright/test` in the lockfile, the image in which the screen check compares (R10), so that reference and app are drawn by the same Chromium and the same fonts; it stops with a message when Docker is not available or the versions differ, and writes nothing. The images are written to `e2e/parity/reference/<screen-id>-<look>-<width>-chromium-linux.png` and committed; the name holds the look because each look has one default scheme here (light for Calm, dark for Focus and Classic), so the images of Calm light and of Focus dark keep their names and their pixels (TOKI-142 R11). The command overwrites an existing image only with `--update`, and prints each file that it wrote. The pull request that adds or replaces an image says which images and why. Check: without `--update` the command leaves an existing image unchanged and exits non-zero; with it the image is written and listed; with another image version it writes nothing. A test of the screen check (R10) refuses to compare, and fails with a message that names both versions, when the version of the container image it runs in differs from the version of `@playwright/test` in the lockfile (a Vitest test of the helper that compares the two versions, with made-up versions).
- R9. The screen check captures the app as in R6 and R7 and compares it with the reference image by a function of `e2e/support/parity.ts`, so that a difference is a recorded result and not a failed test. A pixel is different when the comparison function of Playwright, `pixelmatch`, says so with its `threshold` at 0.1 (the distance of colour, on a scale of 0 to 1, above which two pixels differ) and its `includeAA` at false (a pixel that `pixelmatch` takes for anti-aliasing, the soft edge of text and shapes, is not counted as different). The image matches when the ratio of different pixels to all pixels is at most the limit, which starts at 0.02 (concern 2). The limit, the `threshold` and `includeAA` are set in one place, `e2e/parity/limits.ts`. A change that moves fewer pixels than the limit, such as one word, is not expected to show in the image check: it is the job of the token check (R3) and of the review by eye. Check: the sample screen of R14 matches; a change of the page background token or of the surface token in `packages/tokens`, which repaints most of the capture (far more than the limit), gives a difference in the report.
- R10. The screen check compares only inside the Playwright container image of R8, on Linux, locally and in CI, so that the reference and the app are drawn by the same Chromium and the same fonts. `pnpm test:e2e:parity` runs in that container (the same image rule as R8: the version of `@playwright/test` in the lockfile); it stops with a message and writes nothing when Docker is not available or the versions differ. The check never skips and there is no other system: a missing reference image, or a run outside the container, is a failed run, because the check cannot give a result. Check: with a reference removed, the test fails; run outside the container (or in an image of another version), the check fails with the message of R8 and compares nothing.
- R11. The check writes a report to `test-results/parity/report.md` and `report.json`. The first lines say how many checks were made and how many match, differ (new), differ (known), are no longer a difference, and were not run (R12). Each difference is one row that says where it is: for a token, the property, the combination, the two values; for a screen, the screen id, the look, the width, the ratio and the limit, the box around the differing pixels, and the paths of three images (the reference, the app and the difference), which are also attached to the Playwright report of the parity run. A row has a name that is the same on every run (the screen id with look and width, or the property with its combination), so that the reviewer can open one ticket for one part. Check: a deliberate difference gives a row with all of this and the three images; the same run twice gives the same row names.
- R12. A declaration that needs seeded data (`seed: true`) is accepted by the registry and is not run by this work; the report counts it under "not run: needs seeded data". The Playwright config that has the seeded database, its CI step and the seeding with the clock set to the instant of R7 belong to a later ticket after TOKI-081 (concern 6). Check: a declaration with `seed: true` adds no comparison and adds one to the "not run" count.
- R13. When the prototype cannot be brought to the declared state (a selector of a step is not found), the test fails and names the screen, the step and the selector, because the check cannot give a result. It never writes a reference from a page that is not in the declared state. Check: a step with a wrong selector fails with those three names and writes no file.
- R14. One screen is declared as proof: a screen of the app already built when this ticket is planned, that needs no seeded data and that the prototype also draws (concern 9). Its references (Calm light and Focus dark at the three widths, six images, unchanged by TOKI-142; Classic dark adds three when the prototype has drawn it) are committed. If no such screen exists when the ticket is planned (the app may have only the preview page of TOKI-016, which the prototype does not draw), R14 moves to the first screen ticket that builds one; the proof of this ticket is then the token check on the preview page (R3) plus the Vitest tests of the helpers (R18), and the pull request says so. Check: as in R9, the change of the page background or surface token gives a difference in the report for this screen, and putting it back removes it.
- R15. The checks run in the end-to-end job of CI that TOKI-008 R14 adds. This work adds no job and no browser install; it edits `.github/workflows/ci.yml` to add two steps to that job: one that runs `pnpm test:e2e:parity` (which uses the container of R8 and R10; the runner has Docker), and one that appends `report.md` to the job summary, whether or not the run found differences. CI never skips the check. Differences do not make the job red; the cases that do are those named in R8, R10, R13 and R17, where no result can be given. A failed run keeps its images and results, as TOKI-107 does, and a run with differences keeps the images of R11 in the same way. Check: a pull request with a deliberate difference has a green end-to-end job whose summary shows the row and whose kept results hold the three images; the default `pnpm test:e2e` does not run the parity tests.
- R16. `e2e/parity/README.md` says, in one page, how a ticket adds a screen: the declaration (R5), the reference command (R8), what to write in the pull request, how to read the report (R11) and how to record a difference as known (R4). Check: the file exists, and the sample screen was added by following it.
- R17. The hash (SHA-256) of `e2e/prototype/index.html` is committed in `e2e/parity/prototype.sha256`. Every run compares it with the hash of the file it opens and, when they differ, fails with the message "the prototype changed: make the references again and record the new hash", because the references would no longer come from this file (assumption of this spec). The hash of the file of this spec's date (2629 lines, 259,825 bytes) is `8afc5b13cbc2f0a487eb3041421e2a81667ae0d783e6f87e65332e8377b7bb35`. Check: a changed byte in the copy fails the run with that message.
- R18. `pnpm lint`, `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm test`, `pnpm format:check`, `pnpm test:e2e` and `pnpm test:e2e:parity` exit 0 on the finished branch. The pure functions of the helpers (R3, R4, R5, R11, R17) have Vitest tests.

## Design
### What "matches" means here
The check reports, in the states and the three widths declared, where the app draws other design values than the prototype (R3) and where its image is farther from the prototype's image than the limit (R9), the image check seeing changes that repaint more than the limit and leaving smaller ones, such as one word, to the token check and the review by eye. It does not prove: pixel equality; hover, active and focus-visible states; Safari and Firefox; states the prototype does not draw; anything outside the declared screens; text rendering outside the container image the images were made in (R10). It is a report for the reviewer and not a gate: the reviewer reads it, and a part that is wrong becomes a ticket to fix that part (concern 4). The by-eye record of TOKI-016 R21 and its test R24 stay as they are (concern 5).

## Testing
- Each requirement is written first and seen to fail before the code makes it pass (TOKI-008's rule). The pure parts (the normal form of a value, the list of differences, the registry, the report rows, the hash check) have Vitest tests that use made-up values. The rest are Playwright tests in Chromium, run by `pnpm test:e2e:parity` in the container of R8.
- The negative checks of R3, R4, R9, R11, R13, R14, R15 and R17 are part of the tests of the work or are run once by hand and recorded in the pull request: change one token value (for R9, R14: the page background or surface token, which moves far more pixels than the limit), break one selector, change one byte of the prototype copy, run in an image of another version. A change smaller than the limit, such as one word, is not an expectation of the image check.
- The determinism check of R7 is a test: the command is run twice and the files are compared.
- Data used is fake only: the prototype's own sample people and texts.

## Data safety
- The reference images and the prototype hold only the prototype's invented data (Hugepig Studio and its people). No secret, no real address and no customer data goes into the repository by this work.
- The test requests no host other than the app's own address (R1), so a run does not send anything out.

## Areas of concern
1. Where the prototype lives. TOKI-016 records that the prototype stays outside the repository, and CI cannot open the claude.ai link. The file is on the branch `docs/toki-095-blueprint-sources` (PR 12) and is not in the tree of `github/main` (checked on 2026-11-24). Decided by Thanh Truong (lead, as product owner) on 2026-11-24: a fixed copy (about 260 KB) is committed in the repository, with its hash (R1, R17). This reverses, for this check only, the line of the TOKI-016 spec that keeps the prototype out of the repository; that spec is not edited. Owner: Thanh Truong.
2. The limit of difference for images. TOKI-016 R24 allows `maxDiffPixelRatio` 0.01 at most against its own images; an implementation compared with a prototype differs more (text rendering, spacing). Decided by Thanh Truong (lead, as product owner) on 2026-11-24: start at 0.02, set in one place, tuned on the sample screen (R9). The per-pixel rule of R9 (`pixelmatch` `threshold` 0.1, `includeAA` false) was added after the review of PR 271 and confirmed by Thanh Truong (lead, as product owner) on 2026-11-24; it is tuned together with the ratio. Owner: Thanh Truong.
3. How a builder on macOS makes the Linux images (R10). Decided by Thanh Truong (lead, as product owner) on 2026-11-24: `pnpm ui:reference` runs in the official Playwright container image of the version in the lockfile (R8). The builder needs Docker for it; the screen check compares only inside that container, locally and in CI, and never skips (R10). The change from "skips on another system" to "container only" was made after the review of PR 271 and confirmed by Thanh Truong (lead, as product owner) on 2026-11-24; how the container run is built (the mount, the app server inside the container, the image tag) is left to the plan. Owner: Thanh Truong.
4. What happens when the app differs from the prototype. Decided by Thanh Truong (lead, as product owner) on 2026-11-24: the check states the result and says where it does not match; the reviewer reads it; a part that is wrong becomes a ticket to fix that part. A difference does not make the job red; it is reported (R11, R15). Cases where no result can be given (a missing reference, a run outside the container or in an image of another version, a state the prototype cannot reach, a changed prototype copy) do fail the run (R8, R10, R13, R17); that last point is an assumption of this spec. Owner: Thanh Truong.
5. The place of this check next to TOKI-016 R21 (by eye) and R24 (own images). Decided by Thanh Truong (lead, as product owner) on 2026-11-24: it sits beside them and nothing in TOKI-016 changes. Owner: Thanh Truong.
6. Screens with seeded data and the clock. TOKI-081 R9 gives `seedPrototypeWorkspace` the clock as a parameter and places the sample at the prototype's wall time when the clock is set to 10 November 2026, 10:42 at UTC+07:00 (its spec, open as PR 220); that is the instant of R7, so no change request on TOKI-081 is needed. The `e2e` job of CI already has a Postgres service and runs the sign-in config (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`, lines 108 to 126 and 150 to 152; the comment in `playwright.real-server.config.ts` that CI has no Postgres is out of date). A seeded parity config would follow the pattern of `playwright.sign-in.config.ts`. Answered with this evidence. Decided by Thanh Truong (lead, as product owner) on 2026-11-24: because TOKI-081 is built at the end of M3 and this ticket is in M1, the seeded config, its CI step and the seeded screens are split into a later ticket after TOKI-081; this ticket only lets a declaration say `seed: true` (R12). Owner: Thanh Truong.
