# Accessibility pass design

Date: 2026-11-26 · Status: draft for review
Intent: docs/intents/accessibility-pass.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-083
Needs: TOKI-078 (Preferences and the `lang` switch), TOKI-079 (People), TOKI-080 (translation), TOKI-082 (extra states), TOKI-092 (the More sheet), TOKI-104 (the update strip), TOKI-068 (the jump and its flash), TOKI-054 (`useNewDivider`), TOKI-008 (the browser runner). The plan's needs line is `TOKI-078` only; the change is in Plan changes.

## Goal
A person who uses only a keyboard, a screen reader or a device set to reduce motion must be able to do everything in phase 1 that a person with a mouse can (outcome I14: "usable by keyboard, with a state for every situation"). The pieces of the app already carry their own rules (names, focus traps, arrow keys, a live region for toasts). This ticket looks at the finished app as a whole: it builds the audit that proves those rules hold on every screen, closes the gaps that only show in the whole (a screen change that is silent, focus that is not moved after a jump, a motion that is not stopped), and writes down what it cannot fix without a product decision.

## Scope
- In:
  - The audit: an axe scan of every screen and state of Table A in the browser runner of TOKI-008, in both looks, both languages and three widths (R1 to R4); the contrast of the token pairs, computed (R5).
  - Keyboard-only runs: each dialog and menu of Table C (R6, R7), the keys of Table B (R8), reachability with no trap (R9), a visible focus that is not covered (R10).
  - Three small builds: focus to the message after a jump (R11, a proposal), an announcer for a screen change (R12, a proposal) with the unread count at an opening (R13, a proposal).
  - Reduced motion: a test of every motion in Table F (R14), no scripted smooth scrolling (R15), the flash of a jump under reduced motion (R16, a proposal).
  - Live regions: which exist and which must not (R17).
  - The manual screen reader pass and its record (R18); where findings go (R3, R19).
  - What goes beyond the plan line of this ticket ("keyboard, labels, focus, reduced motion"): the announcer (R12, R13), the contrast of the tokens (R5), the audit of live regions (R17) and the screen reader pass on two platforms (R18). They are added by this spec because the log's rules (D-075, NFR-03) cannot be shown to hold without them; the size of the ticket in Plan changes is set knowing it.
- Out (not now):
  - Changing a colour. The prototype's colours are the tokens (TOKI-009 concern 5); a target that needs a colour changed is a change of `@toki/tokens` in its own ticket after the owner decides (concern 2).
  - New shortcuts, a shortcut help screen, a skip link (concern 10).
  - `forced-colors`, `prefers-contrast`, 400% zoom, the text-spacing override and reflow at 320 px (concern 1).
  - Firefox and WebKit in the runner (TOKI-008 concern 5); a screen reader is used by hand (R18).
  - The Meet, Mail, Tasks and other later-phase screens of the prototype (scope box "Later phases"), the Guest portal (D-152), and every demo-only piece (D-145).
  - Every text of the interface: this ticket adds no visible text (R12, R13 reuse existing keys).

## What each owner keeps
- The per-component rules stay with the ticket that builds the component: names, focus trap and return (TOKI-014 R5, R12, R13, R20 to R22), the tab keys (TOKI-015 R14), the palette's combobox (TOKI-071 R3), the composer's Enter (TOKI-043 R2), the mention list (TOKI-050 R14), the message list's scroller (TOKI-041 R24), the row and its bar (TOKI-042 R19, TOKI-052 R5, R7, R21), the toast region (TOKI-013 R19, R21, R23), the status lines of extra states (TOKI-082 R2, R5, R8). This ticket does not rewrite them. It tests them in the whole app, and a failure it finds is fixed in the code of that component, in this ticket's pull request, when the fix keeps the decided look, text and behaviour (R19).
- The `lang` attribute and its switch: TOKI-078 R10. This ticket checks it (R4).
- The jump and its flash: TOKI-068. The focus after the jump and the flash under reduced motion are handed to this ticket by TOKI-068 (Scope, Out; concern 4 there); R11 and R16 build them.
- The tab title: TOKI-054 R5. This ticket changes nothing there (concern 6).

## Requirements
- R1. The states. `e2e/accessibility/states.ts` exports `STATES`, one entry for each row of Table A: an `id`, the route, the `look`-independent steps that reach the state (stubbed network answers of `e2e/support`, the stubs of TOKI-041 R25, the keyboard or pointer steps to open an overlay), and the signed-in role. `e2e/accessibility/cells.ts` exports `CELLS`, one entry for each cell of Table E (`id`, look, width and height, language, accent, density, and the state ids it runs). A unit test, `e2e/accessibility/states.test.ts`, fails when a row of `sections` in `apps/web/src/layout/sections.ts` has no state whose route matches it, when a route of `apps/web/src/routes/*.tsx` has none, when two states share an id and when a state is in no cell. It reads those two places as text (the `to` strings of `sections.ts` and the `path:` strings of the route files), as `e2e/support` imports nothing from `apps/` today, and compares routes after every `$name` and `:name` segment is turned into `:param`, so `/c/$conversationId` of `router.tsx` and `/c/:id` of Table A are the same route. A new section or route therefore cannot be added without a state. The test runs in `pnpm test` because `e2e/vitest.config.ts` gets one more `include`, `accessibility/**/*.test.ts` (Plan changes).
- R2. The scan. `e2e/support/axe.ts` exports `expectNoViolations(page, { stateId, cellId, strict })`. It runs `AxeBuilder` of `@axe-core/playwright` (already a dev dependency, used by `e2e/sign-in.spec.ts` and `e2e/join.spec.ts`) with the tags `wcag2a`, `wcag2aa`, `wcag21a`, `wcag21aa` and `wcag22aa`; with `strict` true it adds the tag `best-practice` and switches on the rule `label-content-name-mismatch` (criterion 2.5.3; axe marks it experimental, so it is named). `e2e/accessibility/axe.spec.ts` runs S04 to S20 with `strict` true and S01 to S03 with `strict` false. `strict` false is what the two older specs run today (`e2e/join.spec.ts` says "the WCAG levels, not the best-practice landmarks"), so the three pages that already carry their own scan keep their scope, and the two older specs import the helper with `strict: false` in place of their own copy of the tag list, which is the only change to them: what they check does not change. Whether `best-practice` becomes part of the target for S01 to S03 is a question of concern 1. For S04 to S20 `best-practice` brings `region`, `landmark-*`, `page-has-heading-one` and `heading-order`; a finding of these goes by R19 (the announcer's region and the toast region sit outside the landmarks, so if `region` reports one of them its owner, R12 or TOKI-013 R21, fixes it or the finding is registered). Before each scan the cell emulates `reducedMotion: "reduce"` (every audit file except `motion.spec.ts`) and the helper waits until `document.getAnimations()` is empty, so a toast in its slide (`tin`, 180 ms) or a skeleton in its pulse (`sk-pulse`, down to 0.35 opacity) cannot change a `color-contrast` result. The helper drops the violations that the register of R3 lists for `stateId` and for `cellId`, and fails with one line `rule: selector` for each that is left and for each entry that applied to this cell and matched nothing in it. `axe.spec.ts` calls it for each state of R1 in each cell of Table E that runs the state.
- R3. The register. `e2e/accessibility/known-findings.ts` holds the findings that this ticket does not fix, one entry each: `state` (an id of R1), `rule` (the axe rule id), `target` (a selector, optional), `cells` (a list of ids of Table E, optional: when it is missing the entry applies to every cell that runs the state), `reason` (what and why), `owner` (a person or ticket) and `concern` or `ticket` (where the fix is decided). An entry applies to a scan only for its `state` and a cell it names. The stale check is made by the scan of R2 in its own cell: it fails when an entry that applies to this cell matched nothing in it ("no longer a finding: remove the entry"). It needs no state shared between the parallel workers of Playwright and no reporter, and an entry for a finding that exists in one look only (the Undo label, below) names the cells of that look, so it is not looked for, and not stale, in the others. A unit test (`e2e/accessibility/known-findings.test.ts`) fails for an entry with a missing field, with a `state` that is not in R1, with a `cells` id that is not in Table E or a cell that does not run the state, and for an entry whose `rule` is one of `button-name`, `link-name`, `input-button-name`, `label`, `select-name`, `aria-input-field-name`, `aria-toggle-field-name`, `aria-command-name`, `aria-dialog-name`, `image-alt`, `role-img-alt`, `html-has-lang` and `html-lang-valid`: a missing name or language is never waived (D-075, NFR-03, NFR-05). The register is empty when the ticket is built if every finding was fixed. The Undo label of a toast in Focus (concern 2) is written once in each of the two files, because the two checks read different data: an entry here (state S18, rule `color-contrast`, target the toast's action, `cells` E2, E4 and E8, owner Thanh Truong, concern 2), which the browser scan reads, and the pair in `contrast-known.ts` (R5), which the token test reads. When the colour is changed both are removed in the same pull request, and each fails while it is left.
- R4. Language. For each state, one cell of Table E runs with the account's language `vi`. The test also checks `document.documentElement.lang` is `en` for English and `vi` for Vietnamese in the same run, and that after the language is changed in Preferences without a reload it changes with it (TOKI-078 R10). `html-has-lang` and `html-lang-valid` are in the scan and cannot be waived (R3).
- R5. Contrast of the tokens. `packages/tokens/tests/contrast.test.ts` (Vitest, no browser) computes the WCAG contrast ratio `(L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05)`, `L = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B` on linearised sRGB, for each pair of Table D in Calm and Focus and, for the pairs that use an accent, for each of the four accents, from the values of `packages/tokens/src/tokens.ts`. A pair that has a translucent colour is composed over its surface first. It fails when a pair is under its target and it is not listed in `packages/tokens/tests/contrast-known.ts` with its ratio and its owner, and fails when a listed pair is now at or above the target. The targets are the proposal of concern 1 (4.5 for text, 3 for a focus ring and for the edge of a control that is the only mark of the control), so this requirement fixes the numbers only as far as that concern is answered. At the time of writing `contrast-known.ts` lists the Undo label in Focus and the two `line` pairs of Table D (concern 2). The browser scan of R2 reads the real rendered colours (`color-contrast`); this test reads the tokens, so a colour that no state of Table A shows is still checked.
- R6. Dialogs. `e2e/accessibility/overlays.spec.ts`, for each dialog of Table C (O01 to O11): the dialog is opened with the keyboard only (Tab to the opener, Enter or Space; for the palette Ctrl+K); focus is inside the dialog at once, on the element TOKI-014 R11 or the owner chose; Tab and Shift+Tab pressed more times than the dialog has tab stops never leave it (TOKI-014 R12); Esc closes it, except where Table C says the owner asks first or ignores Esc; and after closing, `document.activeElement` is the opener, or, for a row of Table C that names a fallback (the opener left the page), the element with `data-focus-fallback`. TOKI-014 R13 returns focus only when the opener is still in the document and the `body` has focus, so for a row whose opener is gone and that names no fallback the run records where focus ends and expects `document.body`; the row says so, and a better place is a finding for R19, not a rule of this requirement. For O10 (the "session ended" cover) Esc does nothing and focus stays inside. NFR-03's "keyboard-only run of each modal and menu" is this requirement and R7.
- R7. Menus. For each menu of Table C (M01 to M06), opened by keyboard: focus is on the first item that is not disabled (TOKI-014 R20); ArrowDown and ArrowUp move and wrap; Home and End go to the first and the last; a disabled item is reached and Enter on it does nothing; Enter and Space choose the focused item and focus goes to the opener (TOKI-014 R21) unless the choice opens a dialog, which then returns focus to that opener (R6); Esc closes the menu and focus is on the opener. A source test (`e2e/accessibility/menus.test.ts`, which reads the files as text) lists every file of `apps/web/src` and `packages/ui-web/src` that renders `Menu` or calls `useMenuTrigger` and fails for a file that no row of Table C names, so a new menu cannot ship without its run.
- R8. Keys and keyboard-only runs. `e2e/accessibility/keyboard.spec.ts` follows Table B row by row and checks each result in the browser (a key, where, what happens). It then does the ten tasks of Table G with the keyboard alone (no pointer is used: a listener counts `pointerdown`, `pointerup`, `mousedown` and a `click` that has a pointer type or a `detail` above 0, and the test fails if it counts one; a `click` that a key makes on a button has `detail` 0 and no pointer type, and is not counted) from a signed-in start, in Calm at 1280 px; a task that needs a second person uses the stubs.
- R9. Reachability, no trap. For each state of R1 with no overlay open, the test presses Tab from the top of the page until focus leaves the document or returns to the first stop, with a limit of the number of focusable elements plus 5, and records the elements focused. It fails when the limit is reached (a trap) and when a visible, enabled element that is a tab stop by its role is not in the record. The test's selector for "a tab stop by its role" is: `a[href]`, `button`, `input:not([type="hidden"])`, `select`, `textarea`, `summary`, `[contenteditable="true"]` and `[tabindex]:not([tabindex="-1"])` (which holds the `role="button"` rows of Activity and Later, TOKI-066 R9, TOKI-067 R18, and the scroller of the message list), less a native control that is `disabled`, less anything `inert`, `visibility:hidden` or `display:none`, and less the members of a roving-tabindex group other than its one tab stop. It also records, for S04 to S08, how many Tab presses it takes to reach the first stop inside `main` (concern 10). The elements that are not tab stops by design are listed by role, not by selector: the tabs of a `tablist` that are not selected, `menuitem` and `option` rows (TOKI-015 R14, TOKI-014 R20, TOKI-071 R3). An element with `aria-disabled="true"` is a tab stop (TOKI-014 R18; TOKI-078 notes on `aria-disabled`). The same walk runs at 360 by 560 px, where the panes that are not shown have `visibility:hidden` and must not be in the record (TOKI-015 R22).
- R10. Focus is visible and not covered. For each stop of the walk of R9: the focused element has an outline of at least 2 px or a box shadow (the base rule `:focus-visible`, `2px solid var(--accent)`, TOKI-012), except the panel of a dialog (`.modal:focus-visible`, TOKI-014 Table A) and the elements in one list in the test with the reason; and the point at the centre of its box, after the browser has scrolled it into view, is the element or inside it (`document.elementFromPoint`), so no sticky bar, bottom tab bar, toast or strip covers it (WCAG 2.2 criterion 2.4.11; the level is the proposal of concern 1).
- R11. Focus after a jump (proposal, concern 7). One claim in the jump store and one hook in the row, both in `apps/web/src/conversation/jump/` and its row wrapper (TOKI-068). (a) The claim. The store of TOKI-068 R2 gets one member beside `pending` and `flash`: `focusClaim: { messageId, since } | null`. `request({ conversationId, messageId })` sets it with `since` the value of `performance.now()` at the press (a later request replaces it), `takeFocusClaim(messageId)` returns whether the claim is for that message and still valid and uses it up, and it is cleared by `drop`, by `clear()`, by the end of the flash (the timer of TOKI-068 R9) and by the life of the request (TOKI-068 R2). `JumpProvider` adds one passive listener on `document`, in the capture phase, for `keydown` and `pointerdown`, that keeps `event.timeStamp` as `lastInputAt`. The key or press that made the request is earlier than `since` and does not count; a key or a press after it does, because the person has moved on (typed in the composer, clicked another place). (b) The hook. `useFocusOnJump(ref, isTarget)` is called by `MessageRow` of TOKI-041, the one place that reads `useIsFlashed(item.id)`, which is a `useSyncExternalStore` selector (TOKI-068 R9), so the row renders again when its flash starts and the provider needs no subscription of its own. In an effect that runs after the commit that draws `data-target="true"` (and when a row mounts with it, as a row does that scrolls out of the virtual window and back during the 2 seconds), when `isTarget` is true it asks `takeFocusClaim(item.id)`. The claim is given only when `lastInputAt` is not after `since` and no element with `aria-modal="true"` is in the document, and it is used up, so a row that mounts again in the 2 seconds takes no second focus. This is the moment that TOKI-068 R10 gives: the list has settled on the message, however long a `loadAround` took. (c) The call. The hook calls `.focus({ preventScroll: true })` on `.message-outer[data-target="true"] > .message`, the inner box of the row. A confirmed user message has `tabIndex` 0, `role="group"` and a name there (`useRow(id).rootProps`, TOKI-052 R7) and takes focus as it is. The box of a row that has no tab stop (a deleted message drawn as a line, TOKI-068 R12, TOKI-042 R13, and a system line) gets `tabindex="-1"` from the hook just before the call, removed when it loses focus, so the call works and the box does not become a tab stop. A pending or failed message cannot be a target (it has no server id, and no caller of `useOpenMessage` passes one), and a message that the list hides has no row, so nothing flashes and nothing takes focus. `preventScroll` keeps the position that the list chose (TOKI-041 R14), so the jump never scrolls twice. A screen reader reads the name of the group (author and time, TOKI-042) and then the text; no extra text is spoken. A reply opened in the thread panel is not covered (concern 7). Check: browser test: a jump from the palette, from a search result and from a file card ends with `document.activeElement` equal to that box and the box in view; a jump to a deleted line (with replies) ends on its box; with a dialog open nothing moves; a character typed in the composer, or a click elsewhere, after the press and before the list has settled leaves focus where it is; a slow `loadAround` with no input still ends on the message. Store test with the fake `timers`: the claim is taken once, is replaced by a second request, and is gone after 2 seconds of flash and after `drop`.
- R12. The announcer. `apps/web/src/a11y/` holds `Announcer.tsx`, `useAnnounce.ts` and `useScreenName.ts`. `RootComponent` of `apps/web/src/routes/__root.tsx` mounts one `AnnouncerProvider` around the `Outlet` and the session cover; it renders its children and, after them, one `div.sr` with `role="status"` that is in the document from the first render and empty. It is mounted there and not in `AppShellRoute` because the root route's `notFoundComponent` (`NotFoundView`) is drawn by `RootComponent` for an address that matches no route, outside the `_app` layout and its `AppShellRoute` (TOKI-015 R30), while the conversation route and `ArchivedChannelPage` draw the same view inside the shell; one provider above both serves both. Outside a provider `useAnnounce` returns a function that does nothing and `useScreenName` does nothing, so a component test needs no provider. `useAnnounce()` returns a stable `announce(text)` that sets the text of that region. A `ScreenAnnouncer` inside the provider watches the location. The name of a screen is the page's own name when the page called `useScreenName(name)` (the conversation page calls it with the conversation's display name, as the header draws it, once the store holds it; the not-found view of TOKI-082 R9 calls it with its title, `t("states.notFound.title")`, so an unknown address, a channel that is not found and a no-access page all announce it), otherwise `t(row.labelKey)` of the row of `sections.ts` whose `match` takes the path. A path that no row matches and that no page named (`/login`, `/join`, `/emergency`) has no screen name. The watcher announces only when the screen changes from one named screen to another: the first screen seen, a change from a public page to a screen (a sign-in) and a change from a screen to a public page announce nothing, and a page load is announced by the browser. The screen is the section, and for Home the conversation: opening or closing a thread, changing the tab of a conversation and a change of the query do not announce. For an address under `/c/` the watcher waits for the conversation page's `useScreenName` and says the section label only if none has come after 1,500 ms on the app's `timers` (a design value, not a product rule), so that one opening speaks one screen and not "Home" and then the name. No focus is moved (the shell already moves focus on narrow screens, TOKI-015 R29, and this ticket leaves it as it is). The region is outside every `aria-hidden` and `inert` area: it is skipped by the isolation of TOKI-014 R15 like the toast region (`data-layer-skip`, TOKI-014 R34), so a screen change made while an opaque cover is open is still announced. Check: render tests of the provider (empty region from the first render, one announce sets the text, a second replaces it, no provider: no error) and of the watcher with a memory router (Activity then Later gives the two labels; `/c/a` then `/c/a/t/m` gives one; a conversation whose name arrives late announces when it arrives and not before; an unknown address gives the title of the not-found view; `/login` then `/` gives nothing); and the browser test (R17), which includes the not-found page.
- R13. The unread count at an opening (proposal, concern 4). The conversation page takes `count` once, in the same render in which it keeps `newFromId` from `useNewDivider` (TOKI-054 R7 keeps its id in state in that render, and the page keeps `{ conversationId, count }` beside it): `count` is the `count` of the conversation's slot (TOKI-054 R3, TOKI-053) read in that render, and it is not read again, so the read pointer that advances while the person reads cannot change or clear the line while it is spoken. When `newFromId` is defined, `count` is above 0 and the conversation is not muted, the page calls `useScreenName(name, detail)` with `detail` `t("common.unreadCount", { count })` (the key of TOKI-054 R11, "{count} unread") and the announcer says `{name} · {detail}`, with the separator the prototype uses between a name and its detail (line 1531). A muted conversation shows no count in the list (TOKI-033 R7, TOKI-054 R3 (a)), so it announces the name only; this is a proposal of the same concern. Nothing is announced for a message that arrives while the conversation is open (concern 4), and between the section label and the name nothing is spoken (R12 waits for the name). No new key. Check: render test with a count of 3, one, none and a muted conversation, and with a count that falls to 0 after the first render (the line is the same).
- R14. Reduced motion, every motion. `e2e/accessibility/motion.spec.ts` emulates `reducedMotion: "reduce"` and triggers each row of Table F. For each, `document.getAnimations()` holds no animation or transition whose computed duration is above 0.01 ms or whose iteration count is above 1. A control run without the emulation for each of F2 to F8 finds an animation longer than 100 ms (or, where the spec of that motion gives a shorter time, an animation of that time), so the check proves that it can see each motion. The test also reads the stylesheets of the page and the inline styles, and fails for a declaration of `animation-*` or `transition-*` with `!important` outside the global rule of `base.css` (TOKI-012 Table J), for an element with an inline `style` that sets `animation` or `transition`, and for a rule inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` that is not listed in the test with the reason; those are the ways to beat the global rule, and `getAnimations()` catches the animation that a script starts. It does not claim that each `animation-name` is covered by the global rule, because that holds by construction. No row of Table F is checked with a real timer: time is moved with `page.clock` as `e2e/parity/screens.spec.ts` does.
- R15. No scripted motion. A source test (`e2e/accessibility/no-smooth-scroll.test.ts`, which reads the files of `apps/web/src` and `packages/ui-web/src` as text) fails when a file of `apps/web/src` or `packages/ui-web/src` contains `behavior: "smooth"`, `behavior: 'smooth'` or `scroll-behavior: smooth` (the CSS rule of `base.css` cannot stop a script's scroll, so scripted smooth scrolling is not allowed unless it first reads `matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)")`; a file that does so is listed in the test with the line). Check: the test finds a planted `scrollTo({ behavior: "smooth" })` in a temporary file.
- R16. The flash under reduced motion (proposal, concern 3). In `flash.css` of TOKI-068 (whose test finds exactly one rule today: Plan changes), a block `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` gives `.message-outer[data-target="true"] > .message` `animation: none` and `background-color: var(--accent-soft)`. The tint stays as long as the attribute does (2 seconds, TOKI-068 R9) and is gone with it. Without the preference nothing changes. Check: with reduced motion emulated and a jump, the computed background of the row is the `--accent-soft` of the look while `data-target` is set, `getAnimations()` has no `toki-flash`, and after the clock has moved 2 seconds the background is the row's own; without the emulation the animation `toki-flash` runs.
- R17. Live regions. A browser test counts, in each state of R1, the elements with `aria-live`, `role="status"`, `role="alert"` or `role="log"` and compares the count and the kinds with the entry of the state in `e2e/accessibility/live-regions.ts`, which is written from Table I. It fails for a live region the entry does not list, and fails when the message list's scroller or `.typing` (the typing line) holds one: the list is not a live region and not `role="log"` (TOKI-041 R24) and the typing line has no `role` and no `aria-live` (TOKI-044 R15). It checks that a toast shown while an opaque dialog is open is in the region (TOKI-014 R34), and that an unknown address, with the not-found view drawn outside the shell, puts the title of the view in the announcer after a navigation from a screen (R12). TOKI-013 concern 15 puts `role` back on the toast if a screen reader announces nothing; if it does, Table I and the manual result of R18 change with it.
- R18. The manual pass. Once, before the pull request is ready, with VoiceOver in Safari on macOS and NVDA in Firefox on Windows, in English and Vietnamese, the eight tasks of Table H are done and the result of each is written (heard, not heard, wrong). The builder writes the results in an evidence file, `docs/qa/TOKI-083.evidence.txt`, the pattern of `docs/qa/TOKI-001.evidence.txt`; the QA record `docs/qa/TOKI-083.md` (layer `manual`) holds one row for each requirement and points to that file, and its `Approved`, `Result`, `Run` and `Version` are the QA's (`docs/definition-of-done.md`, `qa-ready.sh`). Who has a Windows machine with NVDA is not known to this spec: the builder runs the VoiceOver half, and the NVDA half is run in QA, by the QA's own manual rows, when the builder has no Windows machine. A task whose result is "not heard" or "wrong" is fixed or written in the register of R3 with the reader and version. Evidence holds no customer data.
- R19. Where the findings go. A finding of R2 to R10 and R17 is handled in this order: (1) fixed in the code that owns it, in this ticket's pull request, when the fix changes no decided look, text, size or behaviour; (2) otherwise written in the register of R3 with an owner and the concern or ticket that decides it; (3) the pull request lists the count of each and the entries of the register for the reviewer. A fix is never made by lowering an expectation, by adding a selector to an exclusion that the test does not already list with a reason, or by `aria-hidden` on a control to silence a rule. Check: the register unit test of R3 and a review of the pull request.
- R20. The runner and the cost. ### The announcer and the focus call
`apps/web/src/a11y/` is a new folder of three small files. The provider and the region are mounted once by `RootComponent` of `routes/__root.tsx`, around the `Outlet` and the session cover, so the not-found page of the root route, which is drawn outside `AppShellRoute`, has the same provider as the screens inside the shell. ### Technical choices of this spec
- No new shortcuts in this ticket. The log has Ctrl+K only; any other (Undo, composer focus, list arrows) is a feature and is concern 5.
- No skip link and no focus move on a screen change are in this ticket as proposals: concern 10 and concern 8.
- The register lives in code (`known-findings.ts`, `contrast-known.ts`) and is read by the tests, the way `e2e/parity/known-differences.ts` is, so a finding is tied to a state, a cell and an owner and goes stale loudly, in the cell where it was found.
- Chromium only in the runner; the screen readers by hand (R18). A cell fails the job; there is no "warning" level.

## Areas of concern
1. The target level. The log sets none (NFR-03 and D-075 are the floor). `e2e/sign-in.spec.ts` and `e2e/join.spec.ts` already scan with the tags for WCAG 2.2 AA, which is the only level in the repository. Not decided. Proposal, not a decision: WCAG 2.2 level AA is the target for R2, R5 and R10 (4.5 to 1 for text, 3 to 1 for the focus ring and for a control's edge where it is the only mark, a target at least 24 px, a focus not covered), with the narrow smallest window of 360 px (NR2) used for reflow instead of 320 px, so criterion 1.4.10 is claimed at 360 px and not at 320 px; and the `best-practice` rules on for S04 to S20 and not for S01 to S03 (R2). At level A, criterion 2.4.2 asks for a page title that describes the purpose, and the proposal of concern 6 keeps one constant title, so concerns 1 and 6 are answered together. A different level changes the tag list in one place (`e2e/support/axe.ts`) and the numbers of R5. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
2. Contrast and the prototype's colours. TOKI-009 concern 5 is Decided: the prototype's colours are taken unchanged and a target set later changes the tokens package in its own ticket; what is open here is the target (concern 1) and which colours fail it. With the proposal of concern 1 two kinds of pair fail (Table D): the Undo label of a toast in Focus (1.25 to 1.68; TOKI-013 concern 3 recorded the same numbers), and the edge of a control drawn with `line` (1.28 and 1.29 on `surface`, 1.15 and 1.40 on `bg`, against 3 to 1) where the edge is the only mark of the control. Not decided. Proposal, not a decision: change the Undo label in its own ticket to `--accent-soft` on the toast, as in Calm (about 10.4 to 11.7 to 1 for the four accents, computed from the tokens), which departs from the prototype in one colour; for the edge, list the pairs and the components that rely on the edge alone, and let the owner choose between marking those controls by more than the edge and darkening `line`, which changes the whole look. Until then the pairs stay in `contrast-known.ts` and the Undo label in the register of R3, and the build is green with them listed. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
3. The flash of a jump under reduced motion (carried from TOKI-068 concern 4). The global rule cuts the 2 second animation to 0.01 ms, so a person who asks for no motion sees no flash at all. Not decided. Proposal, not a decision (R16): a still tint of `--accent-soft` for the same 2 seconds with no animation. The alternative is to keep the prototype's behaviour (nothing seen), with focus on the message (R11) as the only sign. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
4. Announcing the unread count and arriving messages. The log has no rule. A message that arrives in the open conversation is spoken nowhere: the list is not a live region (TOKI-041 R24, so that a page of older messages is not read), and the toast host does not toast the open conversation (TOKI-073). Not decided. Proposal, not a decision: say the unread count when a conversation opens with a New divider, from the count of the render that keeps the divider (R13, text `{name} · {count} unread` from existing keys), say the name only for a muted conversation, and announce nothing for an arriving message, because a busy channel would flood a screen reader. The alternative is one polite line per arriving message from another person ("{name}: {start of the text}"), with a limit of one line every 5 seconds. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
5. Shortcuts and Undo by keyboard. The log has Ctrl+K only. A toast with Undo lasts 5 seconds (D-069), holds while focus is in it (TOKI-013 concern 2) and is last in the document, so a keyboard person reaches it only by tabbing to the end of the page; while a dialog is open the trap holds focus (TOKI-093 concern 8). Not decided. Proposal, not a decision: build none in this ticket; the cost of a person who misses Undo is the action done again. Options for the owner: a shortcut that presses Undo while a toast shows, a way to move focus to the toast region, arrow keys in the conversation list, a key to focus the composer. Each is a feature the prototype does not have (D-139). Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
6. The tab title for each screen. D-122 gives `(3) Toki` for the unread count and TOKI-054 R5 keeps one constant base for every screen; a page title that names the screen helps a screen reader user, and WCAG criterion 2.4.2 (level A, so part of any target of concern 1) asks for a title that describes the purpose. Not decided. Proposal, not a decision: keep it as it is (the announcer of R12 says the screen; axe's `document-title` rule only checks that a title exists, so it passes with `Toki` and does not prove 2.4.2); if the owner wants `(3) Activity · Toki`, it is one change of `baseTitle` into a function of the route in `tab-title.ts`, as TOKI-054 R5 says a later ticket may do, and it changes the screen-name part of D-122, not its `(3)` count. Answer it together with concern 1. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
7. Where the focus goes after a jump (handed over by TOKI-068, Scope, Out; its concern 4 there is Not decided). Not decided. Proposal, not a decision (R11): focus moves to the message's box with `preventScroll` when no key or press has come since the jump and no modal layer is open, because the box is already a tab stop with a name (TOKI-052 R7) for a confirmed message and the alternative leaves a keyboard person at the control they pressed, far from the message. The alternatives are to leave focus where the closing layer returned it, or to move it only after a keyboard jump. A reply opened in the thread panel (TOKI-068 concern 5) is not covered and keeps the focus the thread panel gives. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
8. The announcer's choices (R12). Not decided. Proposal, not a decision: one polite region for the whole app, in the document from the first render, with the screen being the section (and, for Home, the conversation) so that a thread, a tab or a query does not talk; no focus move on a screen change on wide screens, because moving focus away from the list when the person presses an item of it would break arrowing through conversations (the narrow layout is unchanged, TOKI-015 R29). A region that is added on demand is often not announced by screen readers, which is why TOKI-013 R21 has its region in the document from the first render. The alternatives are to announce every change including a thread and a tab, or to announce only the section on Home. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
9. What is not covered. Not decided. Proposal, not a decision: no work for `forced-colors`, `prefers-contrast`, 400% zoom, the text-spacing override, captions (no audio or video in phase 1) or WebKit and Firefox in the runner, because the log asks for none and each adds a look or a browser to maintain; they are named so that nobody reads the audit as covering them. Which of them are in or out follows the level of concern 1 (reflow at 320 px is already out there). Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
10. A skip link. The prototype has none (D-139) and the log has no rule. The shell has landmarks (`nav`, `aside`, `main` with `tabIndex={-1}`, TOKI-015 R3), which a screen reader user can jump between, but a sighted person who uses only the keyboard has to Tab through the navigation and the conversation list to reach `main`; R9 records that Tab count for S04 to S08. Not decided. Proposal, not a decision: no skip link in this ticket, and the recorded count goes in the pull request for the owner to read. The alternative is one link that shows on focus as the first tab stop of the page, which is a new element and a new text. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
