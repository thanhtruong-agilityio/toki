# Reactions design

Date: 2026-11-23 · Status: draft for review (concerns 1, 2 and 3 are open)
Intent: docs/intents/reactions.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-046

## Goal
A member of a conversation needs to answer a message without writing another one: press the smile button, pick one of 8 emoji, and a chip with a count appears under the message, for everybody who has the conversation open. Today the server has the messages and the numbered events, the client has the sync engine that applies them (TOKI-040), and the message entry has no place for a reaction.
This ticket builds the whole feature end to end: the table of reactions, the two requests that add and remove one, the two events, the reactions inside the message entry that history and send return, the client rules that apply the events, and the pieces of screen of this feature: the chip, the list of who reacted, the picker of 8 emoji and the row that holds the chips. The drawing of the message itself and the action bar that holds the smile button are other tickets' (Scope).
The rule that matters most: one reaction per person per emoji per message, kept by the database and not by the screen, so a double press, two tabs and a retry after a lost answer cannot make a second one (R1, R7).

## Scope
- In:
  - The table `message_reactions` and its migration, in a new module `apps/server/src/modules/reactions/` (R1, R2, R13).
  - The two routes that add and remove the signed-in person's reaction, who may use them, the locks, and the repeat rule (R4 to R7).
  - The changes `reaction_added` and `reaction_removed` that the routes give to `recordChange`, and the two events `reaction.added` and `reaction.removed` that the event handler of TOKI-038 writes for them (R8).
  - The key `reactions` of `MessageEntry`, the read that fills it for history and send, and the empty list for a deleted message (R9, R10).
  - The shared values: the 8 emoji, the schemas, one error code (R3).
  - The client: two methods of the API client, the pure rule that changes a message's reactions, the handlers registered with the sync engine, and the toggle with its answer and failure rules (R14 to R16).
  - The screen pieces: the chip `ReactionChip` with its list of names, the picker `ReactionPicker`, the row `ReactionRow` and the connector `MessageReactions` of the web app (R17 to R20), and their i18n keys (R21).
- Out (not now), with the owner:
  - The Message component (avatar, name, time, grouping, text, send states, the system lines): TOKI-042. This ticket gives it one slot's content, `MessageReactions`, to put under the text and the files of a message, and asks nothing else of it (R20).
  - The message action bar, its smile button, its hover and tap behaviour, the More menu: TOKI-052. This ticket gives it the picker to open from that button (R19, R22). The button is not built here.
  - The Activity item that a reaction gives the message's author, its text, its counting, "one item per message, updated with the latest names" (D-163), and the rule that reacting to one's own message gives none (F15): TOKI-065. This ticket gives it the two changes with everything it needs (R8).
  - Threads and the thread panel: TOKI-045. A reply is a message and takes reactions with the same routes; the thread panel draws `ReactionRow` itself and keeps its own copy of the reply, so TOKI-045 loads the reactions of its replies with the read of R9 and applies the events with the pure rule of R15 (R10, R22).
  - What a delete does to a message and its files: TOKI-047. This ticket only decides what the entry shows for a deleted message (R9).
  - Edit, pins, formatting: TOKI-047, TOKI-048, TOKI-049.
  - The offline banner and the state of the connection: TOKI-082. This ticket asks the existing gate before it sends (R16).
  - The sample reactions of the seed: TOKI-081 (R2 lists the rule it must keep).
  - Queuing a reaction while offline: not in phase 1 (D-079; the log puts it in phase 6).
  - A full emoji picker, a custom emoji, reactions on files or system lines: not in phase 1 (D-017).

## Requirements
Table and shared values
- R1. The table `message_reactions` exists after the migrations run and is empty, with exactly these columns: `workspace_id`, `conversation_id`, `message_id`, `user_id`, `emoji`, `created_at`. The primary key is (`message_id`, `user_id`, `emoji`) (`message_reactions_pkey`), so a person holds one reaction of each emoji per message and a second insert of the same triple is refused with the unique violation of that key. `emoji` is text and is one of the 8 values of `REACTION_EMOJI` (R3), compared exactly, as the bytes the list holds (`message_reactions_emoji_check`): `❤️` with its variation selector is accepted and `❤` without it is refused. `created_at` is `timestamptz`, required, written by the server from the clock of TOKI-008, with no default (TOKI-019 convention 3). Check: a test applies the migrations to a throwaway database, reads the column names, types, nullability and defaults from the catalog and counts zero rows; each of the 8 emoji is accepted; `❤` without the selector, `👋`, `👍🏽`, an empty string and a 2-emoji string are each refused by `message_reactions_emoji_check`; the same triple twice fails with `message_reactions_pkey` and `ON CONFLICT (message_id, user_id, emoji) DO NOTHING` leaves one row; the same person with a second emoji, and a second person with the same emoji, are accepted.
- R2. The keys and the triggers. (`message_id`, `conversation_id`) references (`id`, `conversation_id`) of `messages` through `messages_id_conversation_id_key` of TOKI-036 (`message_reactions_message_id_fkey`), so a reaction carries the conversation of its message; (`conversation_id`, `workspace_id`) references (`id`, `workspace_id`) of `conversations` through `conversations_id_workspace_id_key` (`message_reactions_conversation_id_fkey`); `workspace_id` references `workspaces.id` and `user_id` references `users.id` (`message_reactions_workspace_id_fkey`, `message_reactions_user_id_fkey`); every key is `ON DELETE RESTRICT`, written as SQL in the migration because a schema file may not import another module's table (TOKI-019 convention 6). A trigger refuses every update of a row (`message_reactions_no_update_trg`): a reaction is added or removed, never changed. A delete of a row is allowed: removing a reaction removes the row, because a reaction is a state and not history (D-029 keeps messages). The rule for TOKI-081: its sample reactions use only the 8 emoji, and the prototype's sample 👋 on the first #general message is refused by R1. Check, in plain SQL as the tests of TOKI-036 do: an insert with an unknown message, a conversation that is not the message's, a workspace that is not the conversation's and an unknown user is refused, each error naming its key; `pg_constraint` shows `ON DELETE RESTRICT` on all four; an update of any column fails and names the trigger; a delete succeeds; a delete of the message's user row, of the message row (refused by TOKI-036 R8) and of the conversation row is refused while a reaction points to it.
- R3. `packages/shared/src/reactions/` exports `REACTION_EMOJI`, the tuple `["👍", "❤️", "🎉", "😂", "👀", "🙏", "✅", "🚀"]` in that order (the order of the log, FR-35; D-017), and its type `ReactionEmoji`; `ReactionEntrySchema`, the strict object `{ emoji: ReactionEmoji, userIds: string[] }` (1 or more ids) and its type; the schema and type of the answer of the two routes, `{ changed: boolean }`; and the schemas of the two event payloads `{ messageId, emoji, userId }` (R8). The error-code registry gains `message_not_reactable` (409); `forbidden`, `not_found`, `validation_failed`, `channel_archived`, `account_deactivated` and `internal_error` are reused. The checks of the table are written from `REACTION_EMOJI` and the migration freezes the list it was generated with (as TOKI-028 R15 does), so a change of the list needs a new migration. Check: unit tests read the tuple (length 8, no duplicate, each entry equal to its code points as written here); a database test reads the definition of `message_reactions_emoji_check` from the catalog and finds the same 8 values; the registry has the new code and no duplicate; a type test imports every name from `@toki/shared`.

Routes
- R4. Two routes under `/api/v1`: `PUT /conversations/{id}/messages/{messageId}/reactions/{emoji}` adds the signed-in person's reaction, and `DELETE` on the same path removes it. Both need a valid session (TOKI-022 R24, 401), take no body and answer 200 with `{ "changed": boolean }` (R7). `{emoji}` is the emoji itself, percent-encoded in UTF-8; the server decodes it and compares it with `REACTION_EMOJI` exactly. They run these steps in this order, as TOKI-037 R1 does for a send: (1) the schemas of the path (`id` and `messageId` lower-case UUIDs, `emoji` one of the 8; 400 `validation_failed`); (2) `getConversationFacts(db, actor.id, id)` of TOKI-028: `null` is 404 `not_found`; a `private_channel`, a `dm` or a `group_dm` of which the actor is not a member is 404 `not_found` before any permission function runs (BR-10, D-045); (3) `canReadConversation` (TOKI-027 R5): a person who is not a member of a public channel gets 403 `forbidden`; (4) the locks of R6, after which the facts are read again and `canReadConversation` is called again with the locked actor; (5) the message: a `messageId` that is no row of this conversation is 404 `not_found` with the same body as for an unknown conversation; (6) `canPostInConversation` (TOKI-027 R5, which names reacting): an archived channel is 409 `channel_archived`; then the message rule of R5; then the write. The `Origin` rule and the media-type rule of TOKI-022 (R45, R46) cover both routes. No route compares a role (TOKI-027 R16). Check: a table test sends each case to each route and checks the status, the code and that the table is unchanged after a refusal; a request with no session is 401 with no read of conversations; positive cases in a public channel, a private channel, a DM and a group for both routes, so a loader that always refuses fails; each of the 8 emoji round-trips through its encoded path; `❤` without the selector, `👋` and a word are 400; an Admin who is not a member of a private channel gets 404 and has no extra right (D-047); a person who has not joined a public channel gets 403 and, after joining, the same request works; the statement log shows the lock statements before the lookup of the message and the lookup before the write.
- R5. Which messages take a reaction. A reaction may be put on a message of kind `user` that is not deleted (`deleted_at` null), whether it is a root, a reply or a reply also sent to the channel, and whether or not its author is the person (F15 gives no Activity item for one's own message, which presupposes it is allowed). A system line, or a message with `deleted_at` set, is 409 `message_not_reactable`, with no `details`, for both routes: a system line is a notice and not something a person said (D-100), and a deleted message shows no content to answer (D-013). A direct message whose other person is deactivated is read-only (F41): whether it takes reactions is concern 1; until it is answered the routes treat it like any other direct message. Check: a root, a reply and an own message take a reaction; a system line and a message with `deleted_at` set (by SQL) are 409 `message_not_reactable` for both routes and store nothing; a reply whose parent is deleted is not deleted and takes one.
- R6. Locks, in the order of TOKI-024 R39 that TOKI-037 R8 follows. The write runs in one transaction at `READ COMMITTED`: (1) `getAccountsByIds(tx, [actor], { lock: "share" })`, so a deactivated actor gets 401 `account_deactivated` and the permission function is called again with the locked role; (2) the conversation row `SELECT ... FOR SHARE`, which excludes a leave, an archive and a removal (they take `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, TOKI-029 R15) and does not exclude another reaction or a send; (3) the message row `SELECT ... FOR SHARE`, which excludes a delete or an edit of that message (the lock of TOKI-047 is `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` and is taken after the conversation row, concern 8); (4) the write of R7; (5) `recordChange` as the last statement, so the counter lock of TOKI-036 R18 is the last lock. Check, with two real connections and no sleeping: a reaction and an archive at the same moment end either with the reaction stored before the archive, or with the reaction refused 409 `channel_archived`; a reaction and the leave of the same person end with the reaction stored (leave second) or refused (leave first), never with a reaction of a person who has no member row; a reaction and a soft delete of the message (`FOR NO KEY UPDATE` set by SQL) end with the reaction stored before the delete or refused 409 `message_not_reactable`; two requests with the same emoji from the same person at the same moment leave one row, one `changed: true` and one `changed: false`, and one `recordChange` call; the isolation level read back inside the transaction is `read committed`; no statement after the first `recordChange` call takes a lock.
- R7. Add and remove, and what a repeat does. `PUT` runs `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (message_id, user_id, emoji) DO NOTHING` with `created_at` read once from the clock; when it inserts a row it answers `{ "changed": true }` and calls `recordChange(tx, change)` once with the type `reaction_added`; when the row was already there it answers `{ "changed": false }`, writes nothing and does not call `recordChange`. `DELETE` runs `DELETE ... WHERE message_id = $1 AND user_id = $2 AND emoji = $3 RETURNING`; when it removes a row it answers `{ "changed": true }` and calls `recordChange` once with `reaction_removed`; when there was none it answers `{ "changed": false }` and calls nothing, and it is not an error, so a retry after a lost answer is safe (D-111, D-126). The person is always the signed-in person: no request names another person's reaction. A reaction by a person on a message they already reacted to with another emoji adds a second row (FR-35: one of each emoji, several emoji allowed). The change is `{ type, actorId: the person, conversationId, userIds: [], at: the time of the clock, reaction: { messageId, emoji, messageAuthorId } }` (R8). Check: `PUT` twice gives `true` then `false`, one row, one `recordChange` call; `DELETE` twice gives `true` then `false`, no row, one call; `DELETE` of a reaction that never existed is 200 `false` with no call; two emoji by one person leave two rows; a `recordChange` handler that throws leaves no row and the answer is 500; a rollback after the call leaves no row; the change has the fields above and `messageAuthorId` is the author of the message, also when the author is the person.

Events
- R8. The two changes and their events. `Change` of `modules/conversations/changes.ts` (TOKI-037 R18) gains the types `reaction_added` and `reaction_removed` and the optional field `reaction: { messageId: string; emoji: ReactionEmoji; messageAuthorId: string }`; the `Change` type and its export are the one existing thing this ticket extends, and no other field changes. The event handler of TOKI-038 R20, the only writer of events (TOKI-036 R25), gets two rows in its table: `reaction_added` writes `reaction.added`, scope `conversation`, `conversationId` of the change, payload `{ messageId, emoji, userId }` with `userId` the `actorId`; `reaction_removed` writes `reaction.removed` the same way. The handler reads nothing from the database for them and takes no lock. The shared registry of TOKI-038 R12 (`packages/shared/src/realtime/events.ts`) gains the two kinds with the schemas of R3, and the table `kinds.ts` of TOKI-038 gets a row for each saying that neither kind changes anyone's map; its test that every registered kind has a row and the reverse then passes. Each event goes to the members of the conversation at delivery (TOKI-038 R15), the person who reacted included, so every tab of that person receives it. A call that does not happen (a repeat, a refusal) writes no event. The handler order is the one of TOKI-036 and TOKI-037: system lines, then handlers that take locks (the Activity handler of TOKI-065 sits here and reads `change.reaction`), then events last. Check: with the real event handler, a reaction writes one row of the log of the kind `reaction.added`, scope `conversation`, with the payload above, and a removal one of `reaction.removed`; a repeat writes none; the frame of each has `kind` equal to the row's `type` and `data` equal to its `payload`, and parses with the schema of its kind; a member of the conversation connected through the gateway receives it, and a connected person who is not a member (an Admin outside a private channel, a person of another conversation) receives no frame that holds the message id or the emoji; the registry and `kinds.ts` hold the two kinds.

Message entry
- R9. `MessageEntry` (TOKI-037 R15) gains the required key `reactions`: an array of `ReactionEntry` (R3), one for each emoji that at least one person holds on the message, so a chip whose count is 0 is not in it. The order of the array and of each `userIds` is fixed by the server and followed by the client (R15): the chips stand in the order of `REACTION_EMOJI` (this is a proposal, concern 2), and the ids of one chip in the order of the reactions, by `created_at` and then `user_id`. A message with `deleted_at` set has `reactions: []` whatever the table holds: the content of a deleted message is gone from the entry (as its `text` is, TOKI-037 R15), and whether TOKI-047 removes the rows is its own decision (concern 4). A system line has `[]`. A new message, the answer of a send and the payload of `message.created` hold `reactions: []`. Check: a schema test accepts an entry with `reactions` and refuses one without the key, with an emoji outside the list, with a chip with no id and with a duplicate emoji; a message with three reactions of two emoji by three people returns two chips in the order of the list and the ids in reaction order; the same message with `deleted_at` set returns `[]`; two reactions with the same `created_at` are ordered by `user_id`; every 200 and 201 body of the message routes still parses with the response schemas.
- R10. How the entry is filled. `reactions` exports `listReactionsOfMessages(client: DbClient, messageIds: string[]): Promise<Map<string, ReactionEntry[]>>` from its `index.ts`: one statement, `where message_id = any($1)` on the primary key, ordered by `message_id`, `created_at`, `user_id`, grouped in code; a message with none has no key in the map. The `messages` module imports nothing of `reactions` (R13): `app.ts` passes the function to `messageRoutes` as the optional dependency `listReactions`, and the code that turns rows into `MessageEntry` (TOKI-037 R15) calls it once for the ids of a page, never once for a message, and sets `reactions` from the map, `[]` when absent or when the message is deleted. When the dependency is not given the entry holds `[]`, so the tests of TOKI-037 stay valid. A history page therefore runs two statements instead of one, whatever the number of messages (this amends the sentence "one statement per page" of TOKI-037 R8 in code and tests); a send runs none, its message is new. TOKI-045 calls the same function for the replies of a thread and for `also_in_channel` replies, in its own change. Check: a page of 50 messages with reactions on 20 of them returns them in the right messages, and the statements counted on the connection are the page and one read of reactions (the same count for 5 and for 50 messages); `EXPLAIN` of the read shows `message_reactions_pkey` and no sequential scan with `enable_seqscan` off; without the dependency the entries hold `[]`; no file of `messages` imports `reactions` and the reverse is true only through `index.ts` (R13).

The log line of a request holds no emoji list and no names beyond the usual request line (TOKI-004 R7); the 500 handler logs the Postgres code and the constraint name only, as TOKI-037 R23 does; the answer holds no message text. A reaction in a private conversation is stored with that conversation id and is sent as an event of scope `conversation` only (R8), never `workspace`. Check: a forced constraint failure leaves the message id out of the captured log; the event of a private channel has scope `conversation`.
- R12. One migration, `<number>_message_reactions.sql`, made with `pnpm db:generate --name message_reactions` (TOKI-019 convention 8), creates the one table, its check, its keys written by hand as in `0002_conversation_tables.sql`, and the no-update function and trigger; it changes no existing table and no row, and is forward-only (TOKI-005). Check: the migration test of TOKI-005 passes; exactly one `CREATE TABLE` in the file; `pnpm db:generate` run again reports no change; the TOKI-028 test "one TRUNCATE empties every table" names `message_reactions` and passes.

The export tests of the three modules list the new names. Check: `pnpm lint` exits 0 (rule `toki/module-entry`); the export tests; `buildApp` registers the two routes and the route-list test lists them; a search finds no import of another module's `schema.ts`.

Client
- R14. Two methods of `createApiClient` (TOKI-032 R9; the rules of TOKI-040 R2: one request core, 10 seconds, no retry, a path part encoded, an optional `signal` last). `api.reactions.add(conversationId, messageId, emoji, signal?)` sends the `PUT` and `api.reactions.remove(...)` the `DELETE` of R4, encodes each path part with `encodeURIComponent` (so `❤️` is sent as its UTF-8 bytes), and returns `{ changed }` read with the schema of R3 in the form that drops unknown keys. An error answer ends as an `ApiError` of the existing kinds. Check, with the fake `fetch` of the existing tests: method, path and no body for each of the 8 emoji (the path holds `%E2%9D%A4%EF%B8%8F` for ❤️); a good answer parses; a 409 `message_not_reactable` and a network failure end as `ApiError` with their kinds.
- R15. The pure rule and the handlers. `packages/client-core/src/reactions/` exports `applyReactionChange(reactions: ReactionEntry[], change: { type: "added" | "removed"; emoji: ReactionEmoji; userId: string }): ReactionEntry[]`: `added` puts `userId` at the end of the `userIds` of the chip of that emoji (and makes the chip, in the position of the order of `REACTION_EMOJI`, when there is none), and does nothing when the id is already there; `removed` takes the id out of the chip and drops the chip when it is empty, and does nothing when the id is not there; when nothing changes it returns the same array (`===`), so a repeated event changes nothing and costs no new render. `registerReactionHandlers(sync)` registers, with `registerEventHandler` of TOKI-040 R18, one handler for `reaction.added` and one for `reaction.removed`; each parses `data` with its schema and calls `tools.patchMessage(conversationId, messageId, (held) => ({ ...held, reactions: applyReactionChange(held.reactions, change) }))`, returning `held` itself when `applyReactionChange` returned the same array, which `patchMessage` treats as no change. A message that is not held is not touched (the next load brings it with its reactions). TOKI-045 registers its own handler for the same kinds with the same function for the replies it holds (R22). The package keeps its rules (no DOM global, no text, no `Date.now(`, no timer). Check: `added` to `[]` gives one chip with the id; `added` of a second person gives one chip with two ids in order; `added` of an emoji that comes earlier in the list is placed before the chip that is there; the same event twice gives `===` arrays; `removed` of one of two ids leaves the chip, of the last drops it; `removed` of an absent id returns the same array; an event for a message that is not held changes no window; a handler registered on a fake engine runs for its own kind only; an event whose `data` fails its schema is skipped by the engine's rule (TOKI-040 R13); the model test: a random list of 200 events applied to a held message gives the same `reactions` as the server's read of R9 on the rows those events make (the rule of order is shared).
- R16. The toggle. `createReactionActions({ api, sync, me })` of `client-core` returns `toggle(conversationId, message, emoji): Promise<ToggleResult>`, where `message` is the held `MessageEntry`. It decides from the held message: `remove` when `me` is in the `userIds` of the chip of that emoji, `add` otherwise (TS-146: picking an emoji already held removes it). It sends the request, and when the answer is `{ changed }` it applies the same change as the event would, through `sync.patchMessage(conversationId, messageId, update)` (a method that TOKI-040 R18 gives to handlers as a tool and that this ticket asks it to expose on the engine as it does `sync.setLastMessageAt`, Plan changes), so the chip changes when the answer arrives and the event that follows finds nothing to do (R15 returns the same array). A second press on the same message and emoji while a request for it is running is ignored (one request in flight for each pair), and the result is `{ status: "ignored" }`. The result is `{ status: "added" | "removed" }`, or `{ status: "failed", kind }` with the kind of the `ApiError`; a failure changes nothing on the message: no chip is added or removed before the answer, so a reaction is never shown as added when it was not (TS-115). The function sends nothing when `message.kind` is `system` or the message has `deletedAt` set. Check: with a fake API and a fake engine, a press on an emoji that `me` holds sends `DELETE` and, on `{ changed: true }`, patches the chip away; a press on one that `me` does not hold sends `PUT` and patches it in; the answer `{ changed: false }` patches nothing that is not already so; two presses before the answer send one request; a 409 and a network failure give `failed` and no patch, and a later press works; the patch for an answer and the event for the same change leave one id in the chip.

Screen

- R18. Who reacted. ## Design
### How a reaction travels
1. The person presses the smile button of TOKI-052; `openFor` opens `ReactionPicker`; a pick (or a press on a chip) calls `toggle` (R16).
2. `ensureOnline()` passes; the request goes to `PUT` or `DELETE` (R4). The server locks the actor, the conversation and the message (R6), writes or removes the row (R7) and calls `recordChange` last (R8); the event handler writes the event under the counter lock (TOKI-036).
3. The answer comes back first and the chip changes on the person's screen; at the commit the gateway pushes the event to every member who has the conversation open, whose handler applies it with the same rule (R15). The person's own event then changes nothing.
4. Anyone who loads a page of history after that gets the reaction inside the message (R9, R10).

### Why one rule on both sides
The server orders chips and ids by one rule and the client applies each event with the same one (R9, R15), so a live screen and a freshly loaded one show the same row. The events carry what changed (a person, an emoji, a message), not a count, so a repeated or late event is safe to apply twice: adding a present id and removing an absent one do nothing.

### API
| Method | Path | Who | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| PUT | `/api/v1/conversations/{id}/messages/{messageId}/reactions/{emoji}` | members, a conversation that is not archived | Add the signed-in person's reaction; 200 `{ changed }` (R4, R7) |
| DELETE | same | same | Remove it; 200 `{ changed }` |

| Status | `error.code` | When |
|---|---|---|
| 200 | none | Stored or removed (`changed` true), or already so (`changed` false) |
| 400 | `validation_failed` | An id is not a lower-case UUID, or the emoji is not one of the 8 |
| 401 | as TOKI-022 (`account_deactivated` for a locked, deactivated actor) | No valid session |
| 403 | `forbidden` | A person who is not a member of a public channel |
| 404 | `not_found` | Unknown conversation; a private channel, DM or group the person is not in; a message that is not in the conversation |
| 409 | `channel_archived` | An archived channel |
| 409 | `message_not_reactable` | A system line or a deleted message |
| 500 | `internal_error` | A server failure, or `fact_missing` or `unknown_role` from a function |

The path carries the emoji because the two routes are then idempotent state changes of one named thing (a PUT and a DELETE of the same resource) and a retry needs no body or key (concern 5).

### Data model
`message_reactions` (module `reactions`): `workspace_id`, `conversation_id`, `message_id`, `user_id`, `emoji`, `created_at` (R1). Primary key (`message_id`, `user_id`, `emoji`). The primary key also serves the read of R10, which always filters by message. Names the tests assert: `message_reactions_pkey`; check `message_reactions_emoji_check`; keys `message_reactions_message_id_fkey` (composite), `message_reactions_conversation_id_fkey` (composite), `message_reactions_workspace_id_fkey`, `message_reactions_user_id_fkey`; trigger `message_reactions_no_update_trg`.
A message holds at most 8 chips, each with at most as many ids as the workspace has people (200 at the target of NFR-04). The worst entry of one message is then about 58 KB of ids; a typical message holds a few ids. This is accepted for phase 1 and TOKI-089 measures a page (concern 9).

### Screens (the pieces of this ticket)
1. The chip row under a message: chips in a wrapping row; a chip is the emoji and a count, outlined in the accent colour when the person reacted (F15).
2. The list of names: the native `title` on a hover-capable pointer, a small list on a long press or from the keyboard.
3. The picker: 8 emoji in a grid in the frame of a menu, opened from the smile button.

## Data safety
- Reactions are visible to the members of the conversation only. A private channel, a direct message and a group answer 404 to anyone else, and their events have scope `conversation` (R4, R11). A public channel answers 403 to a non-member.
- The server never trusts the screen: membership, the archived state and the kind of message are checked on every request (R4 to R6).
- The log holds no emoji list or names (R11). Tests use made-up names.

## Areas of concern
1. Whether a direct message with a deactivated person takes reactions. Not decided. F41 and D-091 say such a direct message becomes read-only, and TOKI-037 R7 applies that to sending only; no row of the log says whether a reaction is a write there. The archived channel is read-only too, and the product owner had to rule on each of its writes (TOKI-027 concern 3). Proposal, not a decision: refuse both routes with 409 `conversation_read_only` (the code of TOKI-037 R7), and draw the chips disabled, as for an archived channel; the other way is to allow reacting there. Until it is answered R5 lets such a conversation take reactions. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
2. The order of the chips of one message. Not decided. The prototype lists them in the order each emoji was first used (`toggleRx` appends); the log does not say. The difficulty with that order: when the person who first used an emoji removes it, the server's order after a reload and the order a live client keeps can differ, and the client has no times to repair it. Proposal, not a decision: the order of `REACTION_EMOJI` (R3, R9, R15), the same on the server, after an event and after a reload; the cost is that a chip can move when a new emoji with an earlier place is added. The other way adds the time of the earliest reaction to each chip and to the removal event. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
3. A page that was read before an event and merged after it. Not decided. TOKI-040 R4 lets a page replace a held message, and R5 keeps aside only `message.created` during a load. A reaction event that arrives while a page request is running, for a message that the page brings, is a no-op on the engine (the message is not held yet) and the page, read before the commit, then shows the old reactions with nothing to repair it. The window is one round trip; the same holds for edits and pins of TOKI-047 and TOKI-048. Proposal, not a decision: the engine remembers the patches that handlers made during a load and applies them again to the page before it merges, for every kind of patch. It is TOKI-040's to accept or to answer in another way. Owner: Thanh Truong, with the TOKI-040 reviewer.
4. What a delete does to reactions. Decided in this spec: the entry of a deleted message shows `reactions: []` (R9), so nothing a person reacted to is shown after its deletion; the rows are neither removed nor changed by this ticket, and whether TOKI-047 removes them is its own choice, because the entry is correct either way. Reason: TOKI-047 owns what a delete means for the row, its files and its thread (D-013, G10). Owner: Thanh Truong.
5. The shape of the two requests. Decided in this spec: `PUT` and `DELETE` on a path that names the emoji, answering `{ changed }`, instead of a `POST` with a body. Reason: a reaction is a state of (message, person, emoji); a `PUT` of a present reaction and a `DELETE` of an absent one are no-ops, so a retry after a lost answer, a double press and two tabs need no id or key (D-111, D-126), and the client decides add or remove from what it holds, not from a counter. The emoji is compared byte for byte with the list, so `❤` with no selector is refused (R1, R4). Owner: Thanh Truong.
6. No optimistic chip. Decided in this spec: the chip changes when the answer arrives (and again, as no-op, at the event), not when the person presses. Reason: TS-115 asks that a reaction that was not recorded is never shown as added, and phase 1 has no offline queue for reactions (D-079); a round trip is short and the answer, not the event, changes the screen, so the delay does not wait for the socket. Owner: Thanh Truong.
7. Module layout. Decided in this spec: a module `reactions` of its own (the technical design lists it), importing `messages` through one new export, and `messages` receiving the read of reactions from `app.ts`. Reason: the other direction would make an import cycle (`messages` reads reactions for its entries, `reactions` reads messages to check them) and the rule `toki/module-entry` forbids it. Owner: Thanh Truong.
8. Lock order and the message row. Decided in this spec: the reaction takes `FOR SHARE` on the message row after the conversation row, so it excludes a soft delete of that message and not another reaction; TOKI-047's delete takes the conversation row first, then the message row. Reason: without it a reaction could land on a message in the same moment as its delete, and the entry already hides such a reaction (R9) but the Activity item would not. Owner: Thanh Truong.
9. The size of the reaction list in an entry. Decided in this spec: the entry carries every id of every chip, with no limit and no "and 12 more". Reason: the chip must say whether the person reacted, and the list of names is the prototype's own title with no cap. The worst case is bounded by 8 emoji times the number of people (R9, Data model); TOKI-089 measures a page, and a cap would be a new decision on what the names show. Owner: Thanh Truong.
10. The long press. Decided in this spec: 500 ms, a move of more than 10 px cancels it, and the key `ContextMenu` or Shift+F10 opens the same list. Reason: D-162 gives no duration, and D-067 asks that no action depends on a pointer. Owner: Thanh Truong.
11. Who may react to what. Decided in this spec: any member of a conversation that is not archived may react to any `user` message that is not deleted, their own included, a reply included; a system line and a deleted message are refused (R5). Reason: F15 implies reacting to one's own message; the permission function covers reacting (TOKI-027 R5); the system line and the deleted message show no content a person said. Owner: Thanh Truong.
12. The names in the list. Decided in this spec: the `displayName` of each person from the conversation store, the signed-in person included, in the order of reaction; a person who is not in the store adds no part, the count stays (as the names of a group do, TOKI-032 R17). Reason: the prototype shows first names, the first load carries full names only (TOKI-032), and a deactivated person is in `people` with their name (TOKI-031 R19). Owner: Thanh Truong.
13. Extending the event handler. Decided in this spec: this ticket adds its two rows to the handler of TOKI-038 and to its registry (R8) and does not add a second writer. Reason: TOKI-036 R25 allows only the handler to call `recordEvent`, and the handler has no seam for a later ticket's change. Owner: Thanh Truong.
14. The line with TOKI-042 and TOKI-052. Decided in this spec: the chip, the row, the picker and their connector are this ticket's; the slot under the text and the files, and the smile button that opens the picker, are theirs, joined by `MessageReactions` and `useReactionPicker` (R20, R22). Reason: the prototype draws the row inside the message and the button inside the action bar, and neither ticket can be built without the other's contract. Owner: Thanh Truong.
