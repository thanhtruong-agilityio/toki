# Reactions design

Date: 2026-11-23 · Status: draft for review (concerns 1, 2 and 3 are open)
Intent: docs/intents/reactions.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-046

## Goal
A member of a conversation needs to answer a message without writing another one: press the smile button, pick one of 8 emoji, and a chip with a count appears under the message, for everybody who has the conversation open. Today the server has the messages and the numbered events, the client has the sync engine that applies them (TOKI-040), and the message entry has no place for a reaction.
This ticket builds the whole feature end to end: the table of reactions, the two requests that add and remove one, the two events, the reactions inside the message entry that history and send return, the client rules that apply the events, and the pieces of screen of this feature: the chip, the list of who reacted, the picker of 8 emoji and the row that holds the chips. The drawing of the message itself and the action bar that holds the smile button are other tickets' (Scope).
The rule that matters most: one reaction per person per emoji per message, kept by the database and not by the screen, so a double press, two tabs and a retry after a lost answer cannot make a second one (R1, R7).

## Scope
- In:
  - The table `message_reactions` and its migration, in a new module `apps/server/src/modules/reactions/` (R1, R2, R13).
  - The two routes that add and remove the signed-in person's reaction, who may use them, the locks, and the repeat rule (R4 to R7).
  - The changes `reaction_added` and `reaction_removed` that the routes give to `recordChange`, and the two events `reaction.added` and `reaction.removed` that the event handler of TOKI-038 writes for them (R8).
  - The key `reactions` of `MessageEntry` and its reader `listReactionsOfMessages`, which the one entry builder of TOKI-037 calls, so history, the replies of a thread, the answer of a send, the answers of edit and delete and every event carry complete entries; the empty list for a deleted message and the rule that a delete keeps the rows (R9, R10, concern 4).
  - The shared values: the 8 emoji, the schemas, one error code (R3).
  - The client: two methods of the API client, the pure rule that changes a message's reactions, the handlers registered with the sync engine, and the toggle with its answer and failure rules (R14 to R16).
  - The screen pieces: the chip `ReactionChip` with its list of names, the picker `ReactionPicker`, the row `ReactionRow` and the connector `MessageReactions` of the web app (R17 to R20), and their i18n keys (R21).
- Out (not now), with the owner:
  - The Message component (avatar, name, time, grouping, text, send states, the system lines): TOKI-042. This ticket gives it one slot's content, `MessageReactions`, to put under the text and the files of a message, and asks nothing else of it (R20).
  - The message action bar, its smile button, its hover and tap behaviour, the More menu: TOKI-052. This ticket gives it the picker to open from that button (R19, R22). The button is not built here.
  - The Activity item that a reaction gives the message's author, its text, its counting, "one item per message, updated with the latest names" (D-163), and the rule that reacting to one's own message gives none (F15): TOKI-065. This ticket gives it the two changes with everything it needs (R8).
  - Threads and the thread panel: TOKI-045. A reply is a message and takes reactions with the same routes. The replies route and the send answer of TOKI-045 build their entries through the same builder, so they carry `reactions` with no code of its own (R10); a live change reaches a reply held in a thread window through `patchMessage` with the parent key of TOKI-045 R15, called by the handlers of this ticket (R15). TOKI-045 registers no handler for the reaction events; its panel only draws the chips through `MessageReactions` (R20, R22).
  - What a delete does to a message and its files: TOKI-047. This ticket decides what a delete does to the reaction rows (nothing: they are kept) and what the entry shows for a deleted message (an empty list), R9 and concern 4.
  - Edit, pins, formatting: TOKI-047, TOKI-048, TOKI-049.
  - The offline banner and the state of the connection: TOKI-082. This ticket asks the existing gate before it sends (R16).
  - The sample reactions of the seed: TOKI-081 (R2 lists the rule it must keep).
  - Queuing a reaction while offline: not in phase 1 (D-079; the log puts it in phase 6).
  - A full emoji picker, a custom emoji, reactions on files or system lines: not in phase 1 (D-017).

## Requirements
Table and shared values
- R1. The table `message_reactions` exists after the migrations run and is empty, with exactly these columns: `workspace_id`, `conversation_id`, `message_id`, `user_id`, `emoji`, `created_at`. The primary key is (`message_id`, `user_id`, `emoji`) (`message_reactions_pkey`), so a person holds one reaction of each emoji per message and a second insert of the same triple is refused with the unique violation of that key. `emoji` is text and is one of the 8 values of `REACTION_EMOJI` (R3), compared exactly, as the bytes the list holds (`message_reactions_emoji_check`): `❤️` with its variation selector is accepted and `❤` without it is refused. `created_at` is `timestamptz`, required, written by the server from the clock of TOKI-008, with no default (TOKI-019 convention 3). Check: a test applies the migrations to a throwaway database, reads the column names, types, nullability and defaults from the catalog and counts zero rows; each of the 8 emoji is accepted; `❤` without the selector, `👋`, `👍🏽`, an empty string and a 2-emoji string are each refused by `message_reactions_emoji_check`; the same triple twice fails with `message_reactions_pkey` and `ON CONFLICT (message_id, user_id, emoji) DO NOTHING` leaves one row; the same person with a second emoji, and a second person with the same emoji, are accepted.
- R2. The keys and the triggers. (`message_id`, `conversation_id`) references (`id`, `conversation_id`) of `messages` through `messages_id_conversation_id_key` of TOKI-036 (`message_reactions_message_id_fkey`), so a reaction carries the conversation of its message; (`conversation_id`, `workspace_id`) references (`id`, `workspace_id`) of `conversations` through `conversations_id_workspace_id_key` (`message_reactions_conversation_id_fkey`); `workspace_id` references `workspaces.id` and `user_id` references `users.id` (`message_reactions_workspace_id_fkey`, `message_reactions_user_id_fkey`); every key is `ON DELETE RESTRICT`, written as SQL in the migration because a schema file may not import another module's table (TOKI-019 convention 6). A trigger refuses every update of a row (`message_reactions_no_update_trg`): a reaction is added or removed, never changed. A delete of a row is allowed: removing a reaction removes the row, because a reaction is a state and not history (D-029 keeps messages). The rule for TOKI-081: its sample reactions use only the 8 emoji, and the prototype's sample 👋 on the first #general message is refused by R1. Check, in plain SQL as the tests of TOKI-036 do: an insert with an unknown message, a conversation that is not the message's, a workspace that is not the conversation's and an unknown user is refused, each error naming its key; `pg_constraint` shows `ON DELETE RESTRICT` on all four; an update of any column fails and names the trigger; a delete succeeds; a delete of the message's user row, of the message row (refused by TOKI-036 R8) and of the conversation row is refused while a reaction points to it.
- R3. `packages/shared/src/reactions/` exports `REACTION_EMOJI`, the tuple `["👍", "❤️", "🎉", "😂", "👀", "🙏", "✅", "🚀"]` in that order (the order of the log, FR-35; D-017), and its type `ReactionEmoji`; `ReactionEntrySchema`, the strict object `{ emoji: ReactionEmoji, userIds: string[] }` (1 or more ids) and its type; the schema and type of the answer of the two routes, `{ changed: boolean }`; and the schemas of the two event payloads `{ messageId, parentId, emoji, userId }`, with `parentId` the id of the root when the message is a reply and `null` otherwise (R8). The error-code registry gains `message_not_reactable` (409); `forbidden`, `not_found`, `validation_failed`, `channel_archived`, `account_deactivated` and `internal_error` are reused. The check of the table is written from `REACTION_EMOJI` and the migration holds the 8 values as a literal list, so a change of the list needs a new migration; the database test below reads both and fails when they differ. Check: unit tests read the tuple (length 8, no duplicate, each entry equal to its code points as written here); a database test reads the definition of `message_reactions_emoji_check` from the catalog and finds the same 8 values; the registry has the new code and no duplicate; a type test imports every name from `@toki/shared`.

Routes
- R4. Two routes under `/api/v1`: `PUT /conversations/{id}/messages/{messageId}/reactions/{emoji}` adds the signed-in person's reaction, and `DELETE` on the same path removes it. Both need a valid session (TOKI-022 R24, 401), take no body and answer 200 with `{ "changed": boolean }` (R7). `{emoji}` is the emoji itself, percent-encoded in UTF-8; the server decodes it and compares it with `REACTION_EMOJI` exactly. They run these steps in this order, as TOKI-037 R1 does for a send: (1) the schemas of the path (`id` and `messageId` lower-case UUIDs, `emoji` one of the 8; 400 `validation_failed`); (2) `getConversationFacts(db, actor.id, id)` of TOKI-028: `null` is 404 `not_found`; a `private_channel`, a `dm` or a `group_dm` of which the actor is not a member is 404 `not_found` before any permission function runs (BR-10, D-045); (3) `canReadConversation` (TOKI-027 R5): a person who is not a member of a public channel gets 403 `forbidden`; (4) the locks of R6, after which the facts are read again and `canReadConversation` is called again with the locked actor; (5) the message, read with `getMessageForReaction(tx, id, messageId)` (R13), which looks for the message inside this conversation only: a `messageId` that is no row of this conversation is 404 `not_found` with the same body as for an unknown conversation, and the lookup of a message of another conversation takes no lock on it; (6) `canPostInConversation` (TOKI-027 R5, which names reacting): an archived channel is 409 `channel_archived`; then the message rule of R5; then the write. The `Origin` rule of TOKI-022 (R46) covers both routes, which change state; the media-type rule (R45) is for requests that have a body and these have none. No route compares a role (TOKI-027 R16). Check: a request for a message id that belongs to another conversation is 404 and the statement log holds no lock on that message; a table test sends each case to each route and checks the status, the code and that the table is unchanged after a refusal; a request with no session is 401 with no read of conversations; positive cases in a public channel, a private channel, a DM and a group for both routes, so a loader that always refuses fails; each of the 8 emoji round-trips through its encoded path; `❤` without the selector, `👋` and a word are 400; an Admin who is not a member of a private channel gets 404 and has no extra right (D-047); a person who has not joined a public channel gets 403 and, after joining, the same request works; the statement log shows the lock statements before the lookup of the message and the lookup before the write.
- R5. Which messages take a reaction. A reaction may be put on a message of kind `user` that is not deleted (`deleted_at` null), whether it is a root, a reply or a reply also sent to the channel, and whether or not its author is the person (F15 gives no Activity item for one's own message, which presupposes it is allowed). A system line, or a message with `deleted_at` set, is 409 `message_not_reactable`, with no `details`, for both routes: a system line is a notice and not something a person said (D-100), and a deleted message shows no content to answer (D-013). A direct message whose other person is deactivated is read-only (F41): whether it takes reactions is concern 1; until it is answered the routes treat it like any other direct message. Taking back a reaction is a write like adding one: `DELETE` is refused in an archived channel and on a deleted message in the same way (TOKI-027 R5 and its concern 3, decided by the product owner: reacting is refused with `channel_archived` in an archived channel; concern 11). Check: a root, a reply and an own message take a reaction; a system line and a message with `deleted_at` set (by SQL) are 409 `message_not_reactable` for both routes and store nothing; a reply whose parent is deleted is not deleted and takes one.
- R6. Locks, in the order of TOKI-024 R39 that TOKI-037 R8 follows. The write runs in one transaction at `READ COMMITTED`: (1) `getAccountsByIds(tx, [actor], { lock: "share" })`, so a deactivated actor gets 401 `account_deactivated` and the permission function is called again with the locked role; (2) the conversation row `SELECT ... FOR SHARE`, which excludes a leave, an archive and a removal (they take `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, TOKI-029 R15) and does not exclude another reaction or a send into a channel or a group (a send takes `FOR SHARE` there); in a `dm` a send takes `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` (TOKI-037 R8), so a reaction and a send into the same DM wait on each other and neither is refused; (3) the message row `SELECT ... FOR SHARE`, taken by `getMessageForReaction` (R13), which excludes a delete or an edit of that message (the lock of TOKI-047 is `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` and is taken after the conversation row, concern 8) and so also makes the reactions that an edit reads for its entry (R10) the ones committed before it; (4) the write of R7; (5) `recordChange` as the last statement, so the counter lock of TOKI-036 R18 is the last lock. Check, with two real connections and no sleeping: a reaction and an archive at the same moment end either with the reaction stored before the archive, or with the reaction refused 409 `channel_archived`; a reaction and the leave of the same person end with the reaction stored (leave second) or refused (leave first), never with a reaction of a person who has no member row; a reaction and a soft delete of the message (`FOR NO KEY UPDATE` set by SQL) end with the reaction stored before the delete or refused 409 `message_not_reactable`; two requests with the same emoji from the same person at the same moment leave one row, one `changed: true` and one `changed: false`, and one `recordChange` call; a reaction and a send into the same `dm` both finish, one after the other, with no refusal; the isolation level read back inside the transaction is `read committed`; no statement after the first `recordChange` call takes a lock. If concern 1 is answered as proposed, step (1) also locks the other person of a `dm` (concern 1 gives the exact statement).
- R7. Add and remove, and what a repeat does. `PUT` runs `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (message_id, user_id, emoji) DO NOTHING` with `created_at` read once from the clock; when it inserts a row it answers `{ "changed": true }` and calls `recordChange(tx, change)` once with the type `reaction_added`; when the row was already there it answers `{ "changed": false }`, writes nothing and does not call `recordChange`. `DELETE` runs `DELETE ... WHERE message_id = $1 AND user_id = $2 AND emoji = $3 RETURNING`; when it removes a row it answers `{ "changed": true }` and calls `recordChange` once with `reaction_removed`; when there was none it answers `{ "changed": false }` and calls nothing, and it is not an error, so a retry after a lost answer is safe (D-111, D-126). The person is always the signed-in person: no request names another person's reaction. A reaction by a person on a message they already reacted to with another emoji adds a second row (FR-35: one of each emoji, several emoji allowed). The change is `{ type, actorId: the person, conversationId, userIds: [], at: the time of the clock, reaction: { messageId, parentId, emoji, messageAuthorId } }` (R8). Check: `PUT` twice gives `true` then `false`, one row, one `recordChange` call; `DELETE` twice gives `true` then `false`, no row, one call; `DELETE` of a reaction that never existed is 200 `false` with no call; two emoji by one person leave two rows; a `recordChange` handler that throws leaves no row and the answer is 500; a rollback after the call leaves no row; the change has the fields above, `messageAuthorId` is the author of the message, also when the author is the person, and `parentId` is the root id for a reply and `null` for any other message.

Events
- R8. The two changes and their events. `Change` of `modules/conversations/changes.ts` (TOKI-037 R18) gains the types `reaction_added` and `reaction_removed` and the optional field `reaction: { messageId: string; parentId: string | null; emoji: ReactionEmoji; messageAuthorId: string }`; the `Change` type and its export are the one existing thing this ticket extends, and no other field changes. The handler of TOKI-038 R20 is typed with its own structural `EventChange` (`type, actorId, conversationId, userIds, at, fields?, message?`), because `realtime` imports no module, so `EventChange` gains the same optional field `reaction?: { messageId: string; parentId: string | null; emoji: string; messageAuthorId: string }`; without it the handler cannot read the message id and the emoji and `app.ts` does not type-check. The event handler of TOKI-038 R20, the only writer of events (TOKI-036 R25), gets two rows in its table: `reaction_added` writes `reaction.added`, scope `conversation`, `conversationId` of the change, payload `{ messageId, parentId, emoji, userId }` with `userId` the `actorId`; `reaction_removed` writes `reaction.removed` the same way. The handler reads nothing from the database for them and takes no lock. The shared registry of TOKI-038 R12 (`packages/shared/src/realtime/events.ts`) gains the two kinds with the schemas of R3, and the table `kinds.ts` of TOKI-038 gets a row for each saying that neither kind changes anyone's map; its test that every registered kind has a row and the reverse then passes. Each event goes to the members of the conversation at delivery (TOKI-038 R15), the person who reacted included, so every tab of that person receives it. A call that does not happen (a repeat, a refusal) writes no event. The handler order is the one of TOKI-036 and TOKI-037: system lines, then handlers that take locks (the Activity handler of TOKI-065 sits here and reads `change.reaction`), then events last. Check: with the real event handler, a reaction writes one row of the log of the kind `reaction.added`, scope `conversation`, with the payload above (`parentId` set for a reaction on a reply and `null` for a root), and a removal one of `reaction.removed`; a repeat writes none; the frame of each has `kind` equal to the row's `type` and `data` equal to its `payload`, and parses with the schema of its kind; a member of the conversation connected through the gateway receives it, and a connected person who is not a member (an Admin outside a private channel, a person of another conversation) receives no frame that holds the message id or the emoji; the registry and `kinds.ts` hold the two kinds.

Message entry
- R9. `MessageEntry` (TOKI-037 R15) gains the required key `reactions`: an array of `ReactionEntry` (R3), one for each emoji that at least one person holds on the message, so a chip whose count is 0 is not in it. The order of the array and of each `userIds` is fixed by the server and followed by the client (R15): the chips stand in the order of `REACTION_EMOJI` (this is a proposal, concern 2), and the ids of one chip sorted by `user_id`, the same rule on both sides (the server orders the `uuid` column, which is bytewise; the client compares the lower-case text of the ids, which gives the same order). The order does not depend on a time, because two reactions can share a `created_at` and two concurrent requests can commit in the other order of their clocks, so the arrival order is not a rule a client can repeat. A message with `deleted_at` set has `reactions: []` whatever the table holds: the content of a deleted message is gone from the entry (as its `text` is, TOKI-037 R15), and the rows are kept, because nothing in this ticket or in TOKI-047 removes them (concern 4). A system line has `[]`. A new message, the answer of a send and the payload of `message.created` hold `reactions: []`. Check: a schema test accepts an entry with `reactions` and refuses one without the key, with an emoji outside the list, with a chip with no id and with a duplicate emoji; a message with three reactions of two emoji by three people returns two chips in the order of the list and the ids in `user_id` order; the same message with `deleted_at` set returns `[]`; the ids of one chip come back in `user_id` order whatever the order and the `created_at` of the inserts (a test inserts three people in the reverse order and with equal times); every 200 and 201 body of the message routes still parses with the response schemas.
- R10. How the entry is filled, by one builder. `reactions` exports `listReactionsOfMessages(client: DbClient, messageIds: string[]): Promise<Map<string, ReactionEntry[]>>` from its `index.ts`: one statement, `where message_id = any($1)` on the primary key, ordered by `message_id`, `user_id`, grouped in code and put in the order of `REACTION_EMOJI`; a message with none has no key in the map. The `messages` module imports nothing of `reactions` (R13): `app.ts` passes the function as `readers.listReactions` to the one builder of TOKI-037, `buildMessageEntry(db, row, readers?)` and `buildMessageEntries(db, rows, readers?)` (TOKI-045 asks it of TOKI-037; this ticket adds its reader to it). The builder calls `listReactions` once for the ids of the non-deleted `user` messages it is given, never once for a message, and sets `reactions` from the map, `[]` when absent, when the message is deleted or a system line, and when the reader is not given (so the tests of TOKI-037 stay valid). Every code path that returns or sends a `MessageEntry` goes through the builder with the reader: the history, the replies route and the replies read of TOKI-045, the answer of a send that repeats a stored message (a new message passes no reader, it holds none), the answer and the event of an edit and of a delete (TOKI-047), the answers of pins (TOKI-048), and every event that carries an entry. In an edit the read runs inside the edit's transaction, after the message row lock (R6 step 3), so the entry holds the reactions committed before the edit and a reaction that commits after it is a later event. So an edit, which TOKI-047 R15 applies to the client by replacing the whole held message, keeps the chips. A history page runs two statements instead of one, whatever the number of messages (this amends the sentence "one statement per page" of TOKI-037 R8 in code and tests). This ticket owns the reader and this rule; the change to each caller is a request in Plan changes. Check: a page of 50 messages with reactions on 20 of them returns them in the right messages, and the statements counted on the connection are the page and one read of reactions (the same count for 5 and for 50 messages); a page of replies from the replies route holds the reactions of its replies; edit a message that has two chips: the PATCH body and the `message.edited` frame both hold both chips, and a delete of it gives `reactions: []` in its answer and its `message.deleted` frame while its rows stay (R9); `EXPLAIN` of the read shows `message_reactions_pkey` and no sequential scan with `enable_seqscan` off; without the reader the entries hold `[]`; no file of `messages` imports `reactions` and the reverse is true only through `index.ts` (R13).

Migration and module
- R11. Nothing a person did not ask for leaves the server. The log line of a request is the usual request line (TOKI-004 R7), which holds the path and so the one emoji of the request and the ids in it, and nothing else: no body, no list of emoji, no list of user ids and no names; the 500 handler logs the Postgres code and the constraint name only, as TOKI-037 R23 does; the answer holds no message text. A reaction in a private conversation is stored with that conversation id and is sent as an event of scope `conversation` only (R8), never `workspace`. Check: a forced constraint failure leaves the message id out of the captured log; a captured log of a request, a refusal and a read of a page holds no list of user ids, no names and no body; the event of a private channel has scope `conversation`.
- R12. One migration, `<number>_message_reactions.sql`, made with `pnpm db:generate --name message_reactions` (TOKI-019 convention 8), creates the one table, its check, its keys written by hand as in `0002_conversation_tables.sql`, and the no-update function and trigger; it changes no existing table and no row, and is forward-only (TOKI-005). Check: the migration test of TOKI-005 passes; exactly one `CREATE TABLE` in the file; `pnpm db:generate` run again reports no change; the TOKI-028 test "one TRUNCATE empties every table" names `message_reactions` and passes.
- R13. The module. Files: `modules/reactions/schema.ts` (not exported), `routes.ts` (`reactionRoutes`, the safe error), `write.ts` (the transaction of R6 and R7), `read.ts` (`listReactionsOfMessages`), `index.ts` exporting `reactionRoutes` and `listReactionsOfMessages` and no table. `reactions` imports `conversations` (`getConversationFacts`, the types) and `messages` through their `index.ts`; `messages` gains one export, `getMessageForReaction(tx, conversationId, messageId)`, that runs `where id = $1 and conversation_id = $2`, locks the row it finds `FOR SHARE` and returns `{ parentId, kind, authorId, deleted }` or `null`, so a message of another conversation is `null` and costs no lock on a row of a conversation the person may not read, so `reactions` reads no table of `messages` (TOKI-036 R24); `messages` imports neither `reactions` nor `realtime`, `conversations` imports neither, and the lists of dependencies are built in `apps/server/src/app.ts`, which registers `reactionRoutes({ db, clock, getAccountsByIds, recordChange })` and gives `listReactionsOfMessages` to the builder of `messages` as `readers.listReactions`. The export tests of the three modules list the new names. Check: `pnpm lint` exits 0 (rule `toki/module-entry`); the export tests; `buildApp` registers the two routes and the route-list test lists them; a search finds no import of another module's `schema.ts`.

Client
- R14. Two methods of `createApiClient` (TOKI-032 R9; the rules of TOKI-040 R2: one request core, 10 seconds, no retry, a path part encoded, an optional `signal` last). `api.reactions.add(conversationId, messageId, emoji, signal?)` sends the `PUT` and `api.reactions.remove(...)` the `DELETE` of R4, encodes each path part with `encodeURIComponent` (so `❤️` is sent as its UTF-8 bytes), and returns `{ changed }` read with the schema of R3 in the form that drops unknown keys. An error answer ends as an `ApiError` of the existing kinds. Check, with the fake `fetch` of the existing tests: method, path and no body for each of the 8 emoji (the path holds `%E2%9D%A4%EF%B8%8F` for ❤️); a good answer parses; a 409 `message_not_reactable` and a network failure end as `ApiError` with their kinds.
- R15. The pure rule and the handlers. `packages/client-core/src/reactions/` exports `applyReactionChange(reactions: ReactionEntry[], change: { type: "added" | "removed"; emoji: ReactionEmoji; userId: string }): ReactionEntry[]`: `added` puts `userId` into the `userIds` of the chip of that emoji at its place in `user_id` order (R9; and makes the chip, in the position of the order of `REACTION_EMOJI`, when there is none), and does nothing when the id is already there; `removed` takes the id out of the chip and drops the chip when it is empty, and does nothing when the id is not there; when nothing changes it returns the same array (`===`), so a repeated event changes nothing and costs no new render. `registerReactionHandlers(sync)` registers, with `registerEventHandler` of TOKI-040 R18, which allows several handlers for one kind and runs them in the order of registration (a request to TOKI-040, Plan changes), one handler for `reaction.added` and one for `reaction.removed`; each parses `data` with its schema and calls `tools.patchMessage(conversationId, messageId, update, parentId)` with the `parentId` of the payload, so the call reaches the message in the main window and, for a reply, the reply in its thread window (TOKI-045 R15), and `update` is `(held) => { const next = applyReactionChange(held.reactions, change); return next === held.reactions ? held : { ...held, reactions: next } }`. An `update` that returns `held` itself is a no-op that notifies nobody (TOKI-040, Plan changes). This is the only handler of the two kinds: TOKI-045 registers none, because its replies are patched through the parent key. This ticket registers no handler for `message.edited` or `message.deleted`: their entries are complete (R10), so the handlers of TOKI-047 and TOKI-045 replace the held message and the chips stay. ## Design
### How a reaction travels
1. The person presses the smile button of TOKI-052; `openFor` opens `ReactionPicker`; a pick (or a press on a chip) calls `toggle` (R16).
2. `ensureOnline()` passes; the request goes to `PUT` or `DELETE` (R4). The server locks the actor, the conversation and the message (R6), writes or removes the row (R7) and calls `recordChange` last (R8); the event handler writes the event under the counter lock (TOKI-036).
3. The answer comes back first and the chip changes on the person's screen; at the commit the gateway pushes the event to every member who has the conversation open, whose handler applies it with the same rule (R15). The person's own event then changes nothing.
4. Anyone who loads a page of history, a thread, or receives the event of an edit after that gets the reaction inside the message, because every entry is built by the one builder (R9, R10).

### Why one rule on both sides
The server orders the chips by `REACTION_EMOJI` and the ids by `user_id`, and the client applies each event with the same two rules (R9, R15), so a live screen and a freshly loaded one show the same chips and the same ids, whatever the order in which the requests committed or the events arrived. The events carry what changed (a person, an emoji, a message), not a count, so a repeated or late event is safe to apply twice: adding a present id and removing an absent one do nothing.

### API
| Method | Path | Who | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| PUT | `/api/v1/conversations/{id}/messages/{messageId}/reactions/{emoji}` | members, a conversation that is not archived | Add the signed-in person's reaction; 200 `{ changed }` (R4, R7) |
| DELETE | same | same | Remove it; 200 `{ changed }` |

| Status | `error.code` | When |
|---|---|---|
| 200 | none | Stored or removed (`changed` true), or already so (`changed` false) |
| 400 | `validation_failed` | An id is not a lower-case UUID, or the emoji is not one of the 8 |
| 401 | as TOKI-022 (`account_deactivated` for a locked, deactivated actor) | No valid session |
| 403 | `forbidden` | A person who is not a member of a public channel |
| 404 | `not_found` | Unknown conversation; a private channel, DM or group the person is not in; a message that is not in the conversation |
| 409 | `channel_archived` | An archived channel |
| 409 | `message_not_reactable` | A system line or a deleted message |
| 500 | `internal_error` | A server failure, or `fact_missing` or `unknown_role` from a function |

The path carries the emoji because the two routes are then idempotent state changes of one named thing (a PUT and a DELETE of the same resource) and a retry needs no body or key (concern 5).

### Data model
`message_reactions` (module `reactions`): `workspace_id`, `conversation_id`, `message_id`, `user_id`, `emoji`, `created_at` (R1). Primary key (`message_id`, `user_id`, `emoji`). The primary key also serves the read of R10, which always filters by message. Names the tests assert: `message_reactions_pkey`; check `message_reactions_emoji_check`; keys `message_reactions_message_id_fkey` (composite), `message_reactions_conversation_id_fkey` (composite), `message_reactions_workspace_id_fkey`, `message_reactions_user_id_fkey`; trigger `message_reactions_no_update_trg`.
A message holds at most 8 chips, each with at most as many ids as the workspace has people (200 at the target of NFR-04). The worst entry of one message is then about 58 KB of ids; a typical message holds a few ids. The worst page is `MESSAGE_PAGE_MAX` messages of 58 KB (TOKI-037 concern 1 has not set the value; at 50 it is about 2.9 MB, an HTTP answer and not a socket buffer), and the worst event, an edit of one such message, is about 58 KB against the 1 MiB buffer limit of TOKI-038 R19. This is accepted for phase 1; TOKI-089 measures a page next to it, and the reviewer of TOKI-037's page size has the figure here (concern 9).

### Screens (the pieces of this ticket)
1. The chip row under a message: chips in a wrapping row; a chip is the emoji and a count, outlined in the accent colour when the person reacted (F15).
2. The list of names: the native `title` on a hover-capable pointer, a small list on a long press or from the keyboard.

## Areas of concern
1. Whether a direct message with a deactivated person takes reactions. Not decided. F41 and D-091 say such a direct message becomes read-only, and TOKI-037 R7 applies that to sending only; no row of the log says whether a reaction is a write there. The archived channel is read-only too, and the product owner had to rule on each of its writes (TOKI-027 concern 3). Proposal, not a decision: refuse both routes with 409 `conversation_read_only` (the code of TOKI-037 R7), and draw the chips disabled, as for an archived channel; the other way is to allow reacting there. If the answer is to refuse, the check needs the same locks as a send in that DM (TOKI-037 R7 and R8): the other person's id from `listMemberIds` of TOKI-028 read before the lock, then `getAccountsByIds(tx, [actor, other], { lock: "share" })` for both people, in id order, in one statement, as the first lock of R6; so this proposal needs no second edit of the spec. Until it is answered R5 lets such a conversation take reactions. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
2. The order of the chips of one message. Not decided. The prototype lists them in the order each emoji was first used (`toggleRx` appends); the log does not say. The difficulty with that order: when the person who first used an emoji removes it, the server's order after a reload and the order a live client keeps can differ, and the client has no times to repair it. Proposal, not a decision: the order of `REACTION_EMOJI` (R3, R9, R15), the same on the server, after an event and after a reload; the cost is that a chip can move when a new emoji with an earlier place is added. The other way adds the time of the earliest reaction to each chip and to the removal event. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
3. A page that was read before an event and merged after it. Not decided. TOKI-040 R4 lets a page replace a held message, and R5 keeps aside only `message.created` during a load. A reaction event that arrives while a page request is running, for a message that the page brings, is a no-op on the engine (the message is not held yet) and the page, read before the commit, then shows the old reactions with nothing to repair it. The window is one round trip; the same holds for edits and pins of TOKI-047 and TOKI-048. Proposal, not a decision: the engine remembers the patches that handlers made during a load and applies them again to the page before it merges, for every kind of patch. The loss is the same for a message that is already held: a page read before the commit (`loadOlder`, `loadNewer`, or a `loadLatest` that refreshes) is merged after the event and, by TOKI-040 R4, replaces the held message with the older copy and erases the patch; and TOKI-040 R16 keeps aside only `message.created`, and only during head loads, so `loadOlder` and `loadNewer` have no protection. The re-apply of recent patches to the page must cover both cases. It is TOKI-040's to accept or to answer in another way. Owner: Thanh Truong, with the TOKI-040 reviewer.
4. What a delete does to reactions. Decided in this spec (a technical rule; the log says only that a deleted message with replies stays as a line, D-013 and F16): deleting a message removes no reaction row and changes none; the rows are kept. The entry of a deleted message shows `reactions: []` whatever the table holds (R9), and no route adds a reaction to it (R5). TOKI-047's delete writes `messages` only and tells nothing to `message_reactions`; the Activity item for a deleted source message is TOKI-065's. Reason: the delete is soft (TOKI-036 R8), so a row that points to the message stays valid, a removal would need a second write and its own events in the delete's transaction, and nothing shows a kept row. TOKI-047 R7 and its neighbour table should say "not removed" in place of "the owner's". Owner: Thanh Truong.
5. The shape of the two requests. Decided in this spec: `PUT` and `DELETE` on a path that names the emoji, answering `{ changed }`, instead of a `POST` with a body. Reason: a reaction is a state of (message, person, emoji); a `PUT` of a present reaction and a `DELETE` of an absent one are no-ops, so a retry after a lost answer, a double press and two tabs need no id or key (D-111, D-126), and the client decides add or remove from what it holds, not from a counter. The emoji is compared byte for byte with the list, so `❤` with no selector is refused (R1, R4). Owner: Thanh Truong.
6. No optimistic chip. Decided in this spec as an engineering choice for phase 1: the chip changes when the answer arrives (and again, as no-op, at the event), not when the person presses. The prototype updates at once; the log does not rule on an optimistic chip. D-079 and TS-115 forbid a reaction shown as added when it was not recorded; they do not forbid an optimistic chip that is rolled back on failure, so this is a choice and not a requirement. Reason: with no optimistic chip nothing needs a rollback, phase 1 has no offline queue for reactions (D-079), a round trip is short, and the answer, not the event, changes the screen, so the delay does not wait for the socket. Moving to an optimistic chip later changes the client only. Owner: Thanh Truong.
7. Module layout. Decided in this spec: a module `reactions` of its own (the technical design lists it), importing `messages` through one new export, and `messages` receiving the read of reactions from `app.ts`. Reason: the other direction would make an import cycle (`messages` reads reactions for its entries, `reactions` reads messages to check them) and the rule `toki/module-entry` forbids it. Owner: Thanh Truong.
8. Lock order and the message row. Decided in this spec: the reaction takes `FOR SHARE` on the message row after the conversation row, so it excludes a soft delete of that message and not another reaction; TOKI-047's delete takes the conversation row first, then the message row. Reason: without it a reaction could land on a message in the same moment as its delete, and the entry already hides such a reaction (R9) but the Activity item would not. Owner: Thanh Truong.
9. The size of the reaction list in an entry. Decided in this spec as an engineering choice for phase 1: the entry carries every id of every chip, with no limit and no "and 12 more". The log sets no cap. Reason: the chip must say whether the person reacted, and the list of names is the prototype's own title with no cap; a cap would be a new decision on what the names show. The worst case is bounded by 8 emoji times the number of people, about 58 KB for a message, and the worst page is `MESSAGE_PAGE_MAX` times that (Data model); TOKI-089 measures a page. Owner: Thanh Truong.
10. The long press. Decided in this spec: on touch a long press opens the list of names, a short press still toggles, and the key `ContextMenu` or Shift+F10 opens the same list (D-162, D-067: no action depends on a pointer). The values are proposals and not decisions of the log: D-162 says only "a long press", and the prototype has no long press (no touch handler on `.rx`). 500 ms (`REACTION_LONG_PRESS_MS`) and a cancel at 10 px of movement follow the usual touch convention and are constants, so a change of the numbers changes no requirement. Owner: Thanh Truong.
11. Who may react to what. Decided in this spec: any member of a conversation that is not archived may react to any `user` message that is not deleted, their own included, a reply included; a system line and a deleted message are refused (R5). Taking back a reaction is the same kind of write: `DELETE` is refused in an archived channel with `channel_archived` and on a deleted message with `message_not_reactable`, because the merged TOKI-027 R5 and its concern 3 (ruled by the product owner) make every write in an archived channel a refusal, reacting included, and BR-11 requires a conversation that is not archived for writing (D-077). So a person cannot take back a reaction in an archived channel; this is the rule of the merged spec, not a new product choice. Reason: F15 implies reacting to one's own message; the permission function covers reacting (TOKI-027 R5); the system line and the deleted message show no content a person said. Owner: Thanh Truong.
12. The names in the list. Decided in this spec: the `displayName` of each person from the conversation store, the signed-in person included, in the order of reaction; a person who is not in the store adds no part, the count stays (as the names of a group do, TOKI-032 R17). Reason: the prototype shows first names, the first load carries full names only (TOKI-032), and a deactivated person is in `people` with their name (TOKI-031 R19). Owner: Thanh Truong.
13. Extending the event handler. Decided in this spec: this ticket adds its two rows to the handler of TOKI-038 and to its registry (R8) and does not add a second writer. Reason: TOKI-036 R25 allows only the handler to call `recordEvent`, and the handler has no seam for a later ticket's change. Owner: Thanh Truong.
14. The line with TOKI-042 and TOKI-052. Decided in this spec: the chip, the row, the picker and their connector are this ticket's; the slot under the text and the files, and the smile button that opens the picker, are theirs, joined by `MessageReactions` and `useReactionPicker` (R20, R22). Reason: the prototype draws the row inside the message and the button inside the action bar, and neither ticket can be built without the other's contract. Owner: Thanh Truong.
