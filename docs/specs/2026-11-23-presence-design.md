# Presence design

Date: 2026-11-23 · Status: draft for review (concerns 1, 2, 3 and 6 are open for the product owner)
Intent: docs/intents/presence.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-055

## Goal
A person who looks at a direct message, a member list or an account card must see whether the other person is Active, Away or in Do not disturb, as the prototype's dot shows. This work builds the whole path: the activity the browser reports, the state the server works out and keeps in memory, the batched live frame and the light read that bring it to every client, the client store and hook, and the dot on the screens that already have a place for it. It also fixes the contract that Focus time (TOKI-076), Status (TOKI-056), the profile panel (TOKI-057) and People (TOKI-079) use.

## Scope
- In:
  - Shared definitions: the three states, the limits, the `activity` client frame, the `presence.changed` live frame and the snapshot answer (R1, R2).
  - The server `presence` module: the state of a person from their connections, the clock and Focus time, the Do not disturb input for TOKI-076, the sweep that ends Active and Do not disturb by time, the grace before a closed last connection turns the person Away, the batch, the snapshot route, and what it keeps and logs (R3 to R10).
  - The three additions to the `realtime` module that presence needs: a notice when a connection opens, a notice when it closes, and the connection id given to a frame handler as a fourth argument (R3). They are asked of TOKI-038 as a change request (Plan changes) before its module is built.
  - The client: `api.presence`, the store with its snapshot and batch rules, the activity sender, the browser activity source, the reset on a change of person, the wiring lines (R11 to R16).
  - The hooks `usePresenceDot` and `usePresenceMap`, the `presence` texts, and the dot on the direct message rows, the direct message header and its second line, the member rows of the Details panel, the people lists of the dialogs, the mention list and the account card (R17 to R19).
- Out (not now):
  - The look of the dot and the `Avatar` and `UserCard` props that carry it: TOKI-013 and TOKI-015 (this work passes them a state and a label).
  - Starting, extending and ending Focus time, the Focus table and the job that ends it (declared with `defineJob` and handled with `JobDeps` of TOKI-064, which hold nothing of presence), and the read that gives the active Focus periods at server start: TOKI-076. This work offers `setDoNotDisturb` and `restoreDoNotDisturb` (R6) and nothing calls them until TOKI-076 is built.
  - The status text, its presets and its timer, and the status part of the direct message line: TOKI-056.
  - The profile panel and its "presence in words" row: TOKI-057, which uses the hook of R17 and the texts of R19. The People cards: TOKI-079, same.
  - 'In a Meet' (phase 4), the pointer and keyboard idle detection of a native app (phase 3), guests (concern 3).
  - The socket, its retry and the offline banner: TOKI-039. The gateway's frames, limits and registry: TOKI-038, extended only as R3 says.
  - The read pointer's idea of "the window is in use" (TOKI-053): a different signal with a different rule (D-052); nothing here is shared with it (Hand-offs say why).

## Requirements
Shared definitions
- R1. `packages/shared/src/presence/` (exported from the package index) holds `states.ts` with `PRESENCE_STATES` equal to `["active", "away", "dnd"]` and the type `PresenceState` made from it (the three names of `presenceStates` in `packages/ui-web`, TOKI-013 R11, so no mapping is needed), and `limits.ts` with `PRESENCE_AWAY_AFTER_MS` (300,000), `PRESENCE_BATCH_MS` (2,000), `PRESENCE_TICK_MS` (1,000), `ACTIVITY_SEND_INTERVAL_MS` (30,000) and `PRESENCE_RECONNECT_GRACE_MS` (10,000, R5). Check: the export-list test of `packages/shared` lists the names; a test reads the six values; the states have no `offline` and no `meet`.
- R2. The same folder holds the schemas. What the server receives is strict: `activityClientFrameSchema` is `{ "type": "activity", "idleMs" }` with `idleMs` an integer from 0 to `PRESENCE_AWAY_AFTER_MS - 1`, and an extra key is a failure. What a client reads drops the keys it does not know (the default of `z.object`), so a later server that adds a key to a change or to the snapshot does not make an older client ignore every batch (TOKI-038 R12 keeps client reads tolerant): `presenceLiveFrameSchema` is the `live` frame of TOKI-038 R12 with `kind` `"presence.changed"` and `data` `{ "version", "changes" }`: `version` an integer of at least 1 and `changes` a non-empty array of `{ "userId", "state" }` (`userId` a non-empty string, the plain string that `members/schemas.ts` uses for a person's id, not a UUID check, so a test id passes; `state` a `PresenceState`, `away` included), each user once; `presenceSnapshotSchema` is `{ "version", "people" }`: `version` an integer of at least 0 and `people` an array of `{ "userId", "state" }` with `state` `active` or `dnd` only, each user once. The strict forms `presenceLiveFrameStrictSchema` and `presenceSnapshotStrictSchema` are the same shapes with an extra key refused; the server's tests check what it publishes and answers against them, and no client code imports them. Check: each schema accepts the shape above; the activity frame is refused with `idleMs` of -1, 300,000, 1.5 or a string, with a missing `idleMs` and with an extra field (so it cannot carry a state); the live frame is refused with a `number`, an empty `changes`, a repeated user and another `kind`; the snapshot is refused with `away`; a live frame and a snapshot with an extra key at any level pass the tolerant schemas, come back without the key, and are refused by the strict ones; the longest activity frame is under 60 bytes.

The server
- R3. Three additions to `apps/server/src/modules/realtime/`, which TOKI-055 asks of TOKI-038 as a change request (Plan changes; TOKI-038 R27 gives a handler the account id, the frame and `canListen` and does not fix the parameter list, so these add to it and do not change it; TOKI-038 R2 added `checkSession` to `auth` in the same way). If TOKI-038 is built without them, TOKI-055 adds them to the same files, with the same checks. (a) `onClientFrame(type, handler)` calls the handler as `handler(accountId, frame, canListen, connectionId)`; a handler that takes three parameters, as the typing relay does (TOKI-044 R4), is unaffected. (b) `realtime/index.ts` exports `onConnectionOpened(listener)` and `onConnectionClosed(listener)`, each returning the function that removes the listener. A listener gets `{ connectionId, accountId }`. The opened notice is sent after the `ready` frame has been sent and the connection is registered (R7 there), never for a connection closed during its handshake. The opened listeners are called synchronously, in the same step that registers the connection, and the gateway passes no frame of that socket to a handler before they have returned: the browser sends its first `activity` frame as soon as it sees `ready` (R13), so a listener that ran after an `await` would find the frame already dropped. The closed notice is sent once for every registered connection after it leaves the registry, whatever the close code (a session ended, a dead or slow connection, the 21st connection of a person, a shutdown). A listener that throws is logged without frame content and does not stop the others or the close. Check: a connection gives one opened and one closed notice with the same id; a connection refused at the handshake (4401) gives none; each close code of TOKI-038 gives one closed notice; a fake socket that delivers an `activity` frame in the same tick as `ready` reaches the presence handler with its connection already registered (the frame is not lost); a throwing listener leaves the connection closing and the other listener called; a handler registered with three parameters still works; the export-list test of `realtime` lists the new names.
- R4. `apps/server/src/modules/presence/` is a new module (the `presence` module of the technical design) with its public entry `index.ts`, and imports no other module: `app.ts` passes in `onClientFrame`, `publishLive`, `onConnectionOpened`, `onConnectionClosed` and the clock of TOKI-008. `createPresence(deps)` returns `{ setDoNotDisturb, restoreDoNotDisturb, getSnapshot, sweep, close }` (R6 to R8); `registerPresence(deps)` calls it, registers `onClientFrame("activity", handler)` once and the two connection listeners, and returns the same object. `close()` removes the two connection listeners, empties every map below and makes the handler do nothing (`onClientFrame` has no remover). The module keeps, in memory only: for each open connection `{ accountId, lastActivityAt: number | null }`, for each person the set of their connection ids, an optional `dndUntil` and an optional `held` (R5), the map of published states, the pending changes, the last flush time and the `version`. A person's entry is removed when they have no connection, no `held` and no `dndUntil`; the sweep clears a `dndUntil` that has passed and an expired `held` (R5, R7), so the memory is bounded by the people connected, in Focus time or inside the grace (each person has at most 20 connections, `MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_PERSON`). The handler ignores, with no answer and no close, a frame that fails `activityClientFrameSchema` and a frame whose connection id is not registered (with R3 a frame of a connection that has opened is always registered); for a valid frame it sets `lastActivityAt` of that connection to `max(current, now - idleMs)` with `now` read from the clock. The accepted rate is the gateway's (TOKI-038 R25); the handler does O(1) work. Check: an app built with the gateway answers an `activity` frame; an app built without `listen` registers nothing and starts; a search of the module for an import of another module finds none; a spy sees one registration of the type `activity`; an invalid frame, a frame with a `state` field and a frame of an unregistered connection change nothing; frames at 0 and then at 10 seconds with `idleMs` 20,000 leave `lastActivityAt` at the later value minus 20,000 only when that is later; after all connections close, the grace ends and Focus time ends, no entry of that person is left; `close()` removes both listeners and leaves no map entry; the export-list test of `presence` lists the names.
- R5. The state of a person at time `now` is, in this order: `dnd` while `dndUntil` is set and above `now` (concern 1 says what happens with no connection); otherwise `active` when at least one of their connections, or their `held` activity, has a `lastActivityAt` not null with `now - lastActivityAt < PRESENCE_AWAY_AFTER_MS`; otherwise `away`. So a person with no connection and no `held` is `away`, a connection that has opened and sent no `activity` frame is `away`, and several connections count together: one used connection is enough. A connection that closes takes its own activity with it, with one exception, the grace. When the last connection of a person closes, the module keeps that connection's `lastActivityAt` as `held` with `closedAt` for `PRESENCE_RECONNECT_GRACE_MS`, and the person's state is worked out from it as if the connection were open; at `closedAt + PRESENCE_RECONNECT_GRACE_MS` the `held` is dropped and the person is `away`. A connection that opens while a `held` exists starts with that `lastActivityAt` (opening is not activity, so a person who reloads is neither made Active nor turned Away) and the `held` is dropped. Why: a page reload or a retry of TOKI-039 (1, 2 and 5 seconds) closes the last connection and opens another within seconds, and without the grace each one would send an Away batch and then an Active batch to every connected person; 10 seconds is decided here (a technical choice, concern 7) because it covers a reload and the first retries, and costs only that a person who truly closes the last tab shows Active for up to 10 seconds longer. Check, with the `TestClock`: a frame with `idleMs` 0 at 0 s gives `active` at 299.999 s and `away` at 300 s (TS-130); a frame at 100 s with `idleMs` 60,000 gives `away` at 340 s; a new frame at 301 s gives `active` again (TS-131); a connection with no frame is `away`; two connections, one used at 0 s and one at 200 s, give `active` until 500 s; when the one used at 200 s closes at 250 s the person is `away` at 300 s; the only connection, used at 200 s, closing at 250 s keeps `active` until 259.999 s and gives `away` at 260 s; the same person reconnecting at 255 s stays `active` with no change in `pending` (no Away and no Active change is published), and a frame with `idleMs` 55,000 at 256 s changes nothing; a person who reconnects at 262 s after the grace ended is `away`; a closed connection that never reported leaves no `held` and is `away` at once; a person never seen is `away`.
- R6. Do not disturb is an input and never a frame or a route. `setDoNotDisturb(userId, until)` takes the id and an epoch time in milliseconds on the clock of TOKI-008, or `null`: a number above `now` sets or replaces `dndUntil` (starting Focus time while Focus time runs extends it, it does not nest, F33), `null` or a number not above `now` clears it. The state passes to `away` or `active` at `until` by the sweep (R7), whether or not anything else was called, so the end of Focus time that the worker process ends (TOKI-076 runs in the worker; the memory here belongs to the app process) needs no message across processes (the job that ends Focus time runs in the worker with the `JobDeps` of TOKI-064, which has no presence, and polls every `pollingSeconds` (2), so the Focus status text and the dot can differ by up to 2 seconds at the end of Focus time; accepted, the dot follows `until` exactly); an early end by the person (Resume, an API route in the app process) calls `setDoNotDisturb(userId, null)`. `restoreDoNotDisturb(entries)` takes `{ userId, until }[]` and calls the same setter for each; `app.ts` calls it once at start with the read that TOKI-076 supplies (nothing until TOKI-076 exists), so a restart does not drop a running Focus time. Pause alone never changes presence. No route, no frame and no function other than these two sets a state by hand (F34, FR-45 AC-129). Check: `setDoNotDisturb` with a time 25 minutes ahead gives `dnd` whether the person is active, idle or not connected (concern 1 interim), and the state is back to `active` or `away` at the time on the next sweep; a second call with a later time extends and one with an earlier time shortens; `null` ends it at once; a past time sets nothing; `restoreDoNotDisturb` of two entries sets both; a search of the module's routes and handlers finds no path that reads a state from a request.
- R7. The sweep and the batch. `sweep()` reads the clock and does three things in order: it recomputes (R5) every person whose state can change by time, which are the people with a published state other than `away`, the people with a pending change, the people with a `held` or a `dndUntil`, and not the rest (at 200 people a scan is cheap, and the cost does not grow with the people who are away); it puts each change in `pending` (a map from user id to state), so that a person who changes twice before a flush has one entry, and an entry equal to the person's published state is dropped; and it flushes when `pending` is not empty and `PRESENCE_BATCH_MS` have passed since the last flush (or there was none). A flush adds 1 to `version`, sets the published state of each person in `pending`, empties `pending`, and calls `publishLive({ workspace: true }, "presence.changed", { version, changes })` with `changes` in the order of first change. A frame and a version exist only when there is at least one change. The state changes made by an `activity` frame, a connection notice or `setDoNotDisturb` are put in `pending` at the moment they happen and are flushed by the next sweep, so one isolated change reaches people within one tick and a burst is one frame per `PRESENCE_BATCH_MS`. A change that comes from time (the 5 minutes, the grace, the end of Focus time) is found by the sweep at the next tick and waits for the batch window when a flush was less than 2 seconds earlier, so its worst delay is `PRESENCE_TICK_MS + PRESENCE_BATCH_MS`, 3 seconds, and not 1. `app.ts` calls `sweep()` every `PRESENCE_TICK_MS` from one timer that it clears in its `onClose` hook, and the timer is not unref-blocking; tests call `sweep()` themselves. Check, with the `TestClock`: three people become active within 0.5 s and one frame with three changes is published on the next sweep; a second wave 1 s later waits and is published at 2 s after the first flush, as one frame; a person who goes active and away inside one window gives no frame; an isolated change is published on the first sweep after it; two flushes are never closer than `PRESENCE_BATCH_MS` (a spy on `publishLive` and the clock); a person whose 5 minutes end at 300 s, with a flush at 299.5 s, is published by 303 s at the latest, and with no flush in the last 2 seconds at the first sweep from 300 s (TS-130 on the published frame); `version` rises by 1 per frame and the frame passes `presenceLiveFrameStrictSchema`; 200 people active together give one frame and, with the fake sockets of TOKI-038 R18, 200 sends of one string; with no change no `publishLive` call; the timer is cleared on close and no timer remains.
- R8. `getSnapshot()` returns `{ version, people }` from the published states, not from `pending`: every person whose published state is `active` or `dnd`, and `version` as of the last flush (0 before any). `GET /api/v1/presence`, registered by `app.ts` in the `/api/v1` scope, so it needs a valid session like every route there (TOKI-022 R24), answers 200 with that object (`presenceSnapshotSchema`) with `Cache-Control: no-store`. It takes no query and no body; any query key is 400 `validation_failed`. It does not filter by the caller: every signed-in account of the workspace hears the same list (the proposal of concern 6). It writes nothing and takes no lock. Check: no session gives 401; two people with a published `active` and `dnd` and one `away` give two entries and the version; a state that is still in `pending` is not in the answer and is in the next one; an `away` person is never listed; the answer passes `presenceSnapshotStrictSchema`; the same answer for a Member and for an Admin.
- R9. What the module keeps and says. Nothing of presence is written to the database or the event log, and `presence` adds no table and no migration (technical design: presence is held in memory). Frames and answers carry a user id and one of the three states and nothing else: no time, no device, no connection count, no "last seen". The module logs no line that holds a user id, a state or a frame at the default log level; the open and close lines of TOKI-038 R5 stay as they are. A deactivated account's sessions end and its connections close (TOKI-038 R21), so it is `away` at the next flush and is never listed. Check: the captured log of a test that runs ten activity frames, a flush and a snapshot holds no user id; the migrations folder has no new file; a frame and an answer parsed with their strict schemas have no other key; after `onSessionsEnded` closes a person's connections their state is `away` and the snapshot omits them.
- R10. Wiring in `app.ts`. When `db` and `listen` are given (the gateway exists, TOKI-038 R29) the app calls `registerPresence`, decorates the app with `presence` (so TOKI-076 receives `setDoNotDisturb` from `app.presence`, as the mail layer is decorated), registers the route of R8 and starts the timer of R7; without the gateway none of it exists and `/api/v1/presence` is not registered. `buildApp` options need no new field. Check: an app with the gateway answers the route and an `activity` frame; one without `listen` has neither and starts without error; the route-list test lists `/api/v1/presence` only in the first; the closing of the app leaves no open timer.

The client
- R11. `createApiClient` gains `api.presence.get(signal?)`, which sends `GET /presence` through the one request core (the 10-second rule of D-126, no retry, the 401 hook, an optional `signal` last, as TOKI-039 R17 and TOKI-051 R8 add theirs) and reads the answer with `presenceSnapshotSchema`, which drops unknown keys (R2), so a key a later server adds does not fail the read. Check: the method and path; a bad answer is an `invalid_response` error; an answer with an extra key is read without it; a 401 reaches the hook.
- R12. `createPresenceStore({ api })` in `packages/client-core/src/presence/` (exported from the index) returns `{ get, getState, applyLive, refresh, attachConnection, reset, subscribe }`. State: `status` `idle`, `loading`, `ready` or `failed`, `version` (null until the first snapshot) and the map from user id to state. `get(userId)` returns `null` while no snapshot has been applied, and afterwards the held state or `"away"` for a person who is not in the map. `getState()` is an immutable snapshot that is replaced and never changed (client-core R12). `refresh()` calls `api.presence.get()` once at a time (a call while one runs returns the same promise), never rejects and resolves `true` when a snapshot was applied; a snapshot always replaces the whole map and sets `version`, even to a lower number (a server that restarted starts again at 0), then applies the held batches (below) with a `version` above it. `applyLive(frame)` takes any value and does nothing unless it passes `presenceLiveFrameSchema` (the tolerant one: a frame with an extra key is applied without it). While a `refresh` runs, or before the first snapshot, a batch is held (at most 20, the oldest dropped; a snapshot is read after them all anyway). With a snapshot applied: a batch whose `version` is not above the held one is ignored; one equal to the held `version + 1` is applied in one step (one new state, one listener call, however many changes); a larger `version` is a gap, which means a batch was missed, so the batch is held and `refresh()` is called. `attachConnection(connection)` (a `ConnectionLike`, defined here in `packages/client-core/src/presence/connection.ts`: `{ getState(): { status: string }, subscribe(listener: () => void): () => void }`, the part of the connection of TOKI-039 R15 that the store reads; TOKI-040 R19 has its own inline shape and shares no type) calls `refresh()` each time the status becomes `online`; the status going to `offline`, `connecting` or `stopped` changes nothing. `reset()` drops the map and the held batches, sets `idle`, cancels nothing else and calls the listeners once; a `refresh` that was in flight when `reset()` ran is ignored when it ends. No clock and no timer: nothing here expires by itself. Check: before a snapshot `get` is `null`; after one it gives `active`, `dnd` and `away` for an unlisted id; a batch of 3 changes calls the listener once; versions 5, 5, 4 and 7 after a snapshot of 4 apply the first, ignore the next two and, for 7, hold it and call `refresh` once; a batch that arrives while the snapshot request runs is applied after the snapshot when its version is above it and dropped when not; a snapshot with a lower version replaces a higher one; a failed read leaves `failed`, keeps the old map and resolves `false`; two `refresh` calls make one request; `reset` during a request discards its answer; `online` calls `refresh` once and `offline` calls nothing; a frame with another `kind`, a `number` or a repeated user changes nothing; a frame with an extra key at any level is applied; `getState()` returns the same object while nothing changes.
- R13. `createActivitySender({ clock, timers })` in `packages/client-core/src/presence/` returns `{ note, flush, setSend, attachConnection }`. `note()` records `lastActivityAt = clock.now()` and sends at once, when a send function is set and none was sent in the last `ACTIVITY_SEND_INTERVAL_MS`, one frame `{ "type": "activity", "idleMs": 0 }`; inside the interval it schedules one `timers.after` for the end of the interval, if none is pending, whose callback sends the frame with `idleMs` equal to `clock.now() - lastActivityAt` (so the server learns the true time of the last activity and never an earlier one). `setSend(fn | null)` is as typing's (TOKI-044 R8): `fn(frame)` returns true when the frame went on an open socket; the sent time is remembered only on true, and a false queues nothing. `flush()` sends one frame, when `lastActivityAt` is not null and `clock.now() - lastActivityAt` is below `PRESENCE_AWAY_AFTER_MS`, with that difference as `idleMs`; it sends nothing otherwise. `attachConnection(connection)` (a `SendingConnectionLike`, in the same `connection.ts`: a `ConnectionLike` and `send(frame: unknown): boolean`, as TOKI-039 R14 and R15 define them) sets `setSend(connection.send)` and calls `flush()` each time the status becomes `online` (the frame cannot go before the `ready` frame, TOKI-039 R14), and `setSend(null)` when it is `idle` or `stopped`. A connection that merely reconnected, with nobody at the screen for 4 minutes, therefore reports `idleMs` 240,000 and is not made Active again (concern 8). Check, with the `TestClock` and the fake `timers`: ten `note` calls in one second send one frame; the next goes at 30.0 s, not at 29.9 s, with `idleMs` equal to the time since the last `note`; activity at 0 s and at 10 s sends the frame of 0 s at once and one at 30 s with `idleMs` 20,000; a `note` with no send function sends nothing and throws nothing; a function that returns false queues nothing; `flush` after 4 minutes without activity sends `idleMs` 240,000; `flush` after 5 minutes, and `flush` with no activity ever, send nothing; the pending timer is one at most and is cancelled by `setSend(null)`; no frame is longer than the schema allows.
- R14. The browser source, in `apps/web/src/presence/`: `watchActivity(host, note)` listens on the window and document for `pointerdown`, `pointermove`, `keydown`, `wheel`, `scroll`, `touchstart` and `focus` and for `visibilitychange` when the page has become visible, calls `note()` for each, with passive listeners (`scroll` with `capture: true` as well, because a scroll event does not bubble and the message list of TOKI-041 scrolls inside its own container; the removal passes the same `capture`), and returns the function that removes them. `host` is a small interface (`addEventListener(type, listener, options)`, `removeEventListener`, a `visibilityState` read) so a test gives a fake, and the file is in `apps/web`, where `client-core`'s ban on browser globals does not reach (the same reason `realtime.ts` reads `online` and `offline`, TOKI-039 R20). `bindPresence()` in `apps/web/src/presence/bind.ts` calls `watchActivity` once and `note()` once at that moment (opening Toki is activity), and `main.tsx` calls `bindPresence()` once before the app is mounted. A hidden page fires no pointer or key event, so a tab that is hidden stops reporting and the server turns the person Away 5 minutes after the last report. The sender is not given through `platform`: `TOKI-018` has no activity member, and the lint rule `toki/platform-apis` covers notifications, storage, the service worker and audio in `ui-web`, so this source is app code (a native app brings its own source with its own phase). Check: with a fake host each listed event calls `note` once and the removal function removes every listener; the fake host sees `scroll` added with `capture: true` and `passive: true` and removed with `capture: true`; `visibilitychange` to hidden calls nothing and to visible calls `note`; `bindPresence` makes one `note` and one set of listeners; a search of `packages/client-core` finds no `addEventListener`, `document` or `window`.
- R15. `apps/web/src/client.ts` makes one `presence` store and one `activity` sender and exports them, from the `api`, `clock` and `timers` of the file; `client.ts` imports nothing from `realtime.ts`. `apps/web/src/presence/session-reset.ts` exports `watchSessionForPresence(conversations, presence)`, built like `watchSessionForTyping` (TOKI-044 R17): it calls `presence.reset()` when `conversations.getUser()?.id` differs from the id it saw at the last notification, and when the conversation store's `status` goes back to `idle` after another status; `client.ts` calls it once. The three lines of `apps/web/src/realtime.ts` (the one file that wires the connection, TOKI-039 R20) are `presence.attachConnection(connection)`, `connection.subscribeLive(presence.applyLive)` and `activity.attachConnection(connection)`; whichever of this work and TOKI-039 is built second adds them, and its pull request says so (the rule of TOKI-040 R21, TOKI-044 R17 and TOKI-051 R14). The reset follows the pattern of TOKI-044's `watchSessionForTyping`, which this work reads as a reference and does not import. Check: `client.ts` exports both; a test of `watchSessionForPresence` with a fake store shows one `reset()` for a change of user, one for `null` and one for the return to `idle`, and none for an unchanged notification; the wiring test of TOKI-039 finds the three lines once, and a search of `apps/web/src` finds no other call of `subscribeLive` for presence.
- R16. While there is no connection the store keeps what it last knew and shows no new word: no dot changes, there is no Offline state and no screen writes one (D-036, FR-32), and the offline banner of TOKI-082 (D-102, D-140) says the data is a saved copy. On a page load there is no snapshot until the connection is online, so no dot is drawn (R17); presence is not written to the browser's storage, because a state saved for hours would be false. When the connection comes online again the snapshot of R12 replaces the old map. Check: with a store holding `active` for a person and a connection that goes `offline`, `get` still returns `active` and no listener is called; after `online` and a snapshot without the person it returns `away`; a search of the web app and `ui-web` for `Offline` as a presence word finds none; no presence key is written to storage (a spy on the platform storage).

## Design
### The path of one change
1. A person uses Toki. `watchActivity` calls `activity.note()`; the sender puts `{ type: "activity", idleMs }` on the socket at most once in 30 seconds.
2. The gateway hands the frame to the presence handler with the account id and the connection id (R3). The module sets that connection's `lastActivityAt` and recomputes the person: if the state changed, the change goes to `pending`.
3. On the next tick (at most 1 second) the sweep recomputes everyone who could change by time (a person whose 5 minutes ended, a grace that ended, a Focus time that ended) and flushes `pending` if 2 seconds have passed since the last flush. A change found by time is therefore visible within `PRESENCE_TICK_MS + PRESENCE_BATCH_MS`, 3 seconds at the worst; a change that an `activity` frame causes is visible at the next tick when no flush was in the last 2 seconds.
4. The flush publishes one `presence.changed` live frame to every connection of the workspace through `publishLive({ workspace: true })`. A client's `applyLive` applies it in one step.
5. A client that connects, or reconnects, reads `GET /api/v1/presence` and replaces its map, then applies the batches that follow.

### Decided in this spec
- Live frames, not events. Presence is held in memory and is false a few minutes later, so it has no place in the numbered log, which is kept 7 days and replayed after a reconnect (TOKI-036). A lost batch is repaired by the snapshot that every `online` makes (R12), not by replay. The typing signal and `draft.changed` are live for the same reason.
- Who hears whom: everyone in the workspace is the proposal of concern 6, built here as the interim; it is not a decision.
- Why a snapshot request and not the bootstrap. The bootstrap answer is large, is built in one transaction with the event number and is read once after sign-in; a reconnect would have to read it again for a list of a few hundred ids. The bootstrap spec also says it holds no presence (TOKI-031 R12), and presence has no event number to line up with. The separate answer is under 15 KB for 200 people.
- A version on every batch and snapshot, because a snapshot and a live frame race. The client holds batches until its snapshot is applied, applies only the batch that follows the held version by 1, and reads the snapshot again on a gap.
- The server decides the 5 minutes. The client reports the time since its last activity (`idleMs`) and not a state, so a browser that throttles the timers of a hidden tab cannot turn a person Away late or early, a client cannot choose a state, and the test of the 5-minute boundary uses the `TestClock` on the server with no real timer.
- Connection counting is by connection id, which TOKI-038's registry already has (R3), so two tabs, two devices and a reconnect are all the same case: one connection is one entry that has its own activity time, and a closed connection takes its activity with it.
- A connection that opens is not activity. A laptop that wakes and reconnects with nobody there must not shine as Active; the browser reports `idleMs` as it was (R13).
- The numbers (30 s report interval, 2 s batch, 1 s tick, 10 s reconnect grace) are technical choices, constants of `packages/shared`. The report interval is far below 5 minutes, so a person who is active is never Away for lack of a report, and because the frame carries `idleMs` the delay costs no accuracy at the end of activity. The batch bounds the cost to one frame per connection per 2 seconds, however many people change (200 people arriving together are one frame, not 40,000). The tick finds the end of the 5 minutes within 1 second, and the batch window can add up to 2 more, so the dot turns within 3 seconds of the 5-minute mark (R7).
- The reconnect grace. The last connection of a person closing does not turn them Away at once: for `PRESENCE_RECONNECT_GRACE_MS` (10 seconds, decided here, concern 7) the closed connection's activity is kept and a connection that opens inherits it. Without it every reload or retry of TOKI-039 would publish an Away batch and an Active batch to every connected person. The viewer's own offline case is a separate rule (R16).
- The activity events of R14 follow the test of FR-32 ("moves the pointer or types in Toki", TS-131) and add touch, wheel, scroll and returning to a visible page; whether a visible but unfocused window counts has no source and is left to this list.
- The server's state does not know about workspaces: the product is single-tenant (NFR), one `{ workspace: true }` audience reaches everyone. A multi-workspace server would need the audience to name a workspace.
- The direct message line rule is the prototype's (R19); the log gives the line's content (the direct message header shows "status or job title and presence"), the prototype gives the order.

## Data safety

## Open questions from the intent
- Who hears whose presence? Not answered. The log says where the dot appears (F34) but not whether it is limited to shared conversations; the proposal, built as the interim, is every signed-in person of the workspace (R7, R8), carried forward as concern 6; the guest case is concern 3.
- Is a connection that merely reconnected, with nobody at the screen, Active? Answered: no; a connection's activity is what the browser reports with its age, and a reload inside the 10-second grace keeps the activity it had (R5, R13, concerns 7 and 8).
- How many connections count, and what happens with several tabs and devices? Answered: every connection counts on its own, one used connection is enough, a closed one takes its activity (R3, R5, concern 9).
- How often does the browser report activity, and how often does the server send changes? Answered: at most one frame in 30 seconds from the browser, one batch in 2 seconds from the server (R7, R13, concern 7).
- What does a screen show while this device has no connection, since there is no Offline state? Answered: the last known dots stay, none is drawn before the first snapshot, nothing is stored on the device (R16, concern 5).
- Does Do not disturb win when the person in Focus time has no connected device? Not answered. Carried forward as concern 1.
- What does the dot of a deactivated person show, and what do guests hear? Not answered. Carried forward as concern 2 (the dot) and concern 3 (guests).
