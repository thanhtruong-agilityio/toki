# Web Push design

Date: 2026-11-25 · Status: draft for review (concerns 1 to 3 are open for the product owner)
Intent: docs/intents/web-push.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-074

## Goal
A person who has closed Toki, or left its tab hidden, must still learn that something needs them. TOKI-073 decides, for each person and cause, that a push is due, and hands over identifiers. This ticket makes the push real: the server's key pair, the registration of a person's devices, the job that sends the encrypted message to the browser's push service, the service worker that shows it, the click that opens Toki at the message, the card that asks the person on their first visit, the explanation of the iPhone rule, and the removal of a device's registration when its session ends.
Without it a person with Toki closed gets nothing, which is the situation F36 and FR-18 exist to prevent.

## Scope
- In:
  - The VAPID keys, their configuration area, the rows in `docs/environment-config.md` and `.env.example`, and the command that makes a pair (R1, R2).
  - The sending library behind one port, the shared schemas (R3, R4).
  - The routes that give the public key, register this device and remove it; the rules for an endpoint; one registration per endpoint (R5 to R9).
  - The removal of the registration of an ended session and of a session idle for 30 days (R10).
  - The real `PushPort` of TOKI-073, the job, the send-time check, the content of a notification, the rules of one send and the test notification (R11 to R17, R18).
  - The service worker file and its two handlers, its registration, and who owns it (R19, R20).
  - In the web app: `platform.push`, the iPhone rule, the click address and its handler, the registration kept in step with the browser, the first-visit card, the device block of Preferences, the removal at sign-out, the texts (R21 to R28).
- Out (not now):
  - Deciding that a push is due, the rule "no window of the person is in use", Pause and Focus, the Activity item and the retraction of a job not started: TOKI-073 (R2, R8, R9, R13). This ticket sends what it is handed.
  - Storing the three switches, and what "Desktop notifications" controls: TOKI-102 stores them; TOKI-073 gives `desktop` its meaning. This ticket reads the switch once more at send time (R14).
  - The toast, the sound and the notice centre: TOKI-075.
  - The Preferences page frame, its Notifications section with the three switches, and their labels: TOKI-078. This ticket builds the device block it will place (R26).
  - The manifest, the icons, the install prompt and the "Add to Home Screen" instructions: TOKI-077. The service worker file is this ticket's; TOKI-077 adds no caching to it (R19, concern 6).
  - The Caddy rules for the service worker file and the production image: TOKI-084 (R19 names the header it needs).
  - Pause and Focus themselves: TOKI-076. Email about missed mentions: phase 5. APNs and FCM channels: later (`push_channel` already allows them).
  - A message address for sharing (a link): TOKI-068 concern 6. R23 gives the one address a push click needs and nothing more.

## Requirements
Keys and configuration
- R1. The configuration gains the area `webPush`, read through the one reader of `apps/server/src/config.ts` (TOKI-004): `VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY`, `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY` and `VAPID_SUBJECT`, defined in `modules/notifications/web-push-config.ts` and exported from the module's `index.ts` as `webPushConfigSchema` and `webPushVariableNames`, like `auth` and `mail`. The public key is base64url of 65 bytes starting with `0x04`; the private key is base64url of 32 bytes; the subject is `mailto:` and an address, or an `https://` address. The private key must be the one that gives the public key (checked with `node:crypto`'s P-256 curve). The server entry and the worker (`worker.ts` adds `webPush` to its list of areas, TOKI-064 R17) require the area; `buildApp` takes it as optional, as for `auth`, so a test builds the app without it. A failure names the variable and never its value. Check: tests of the schema for each rule, a valid pair accepted, a pair whose halves do not match refused, a failure message holding no value; `loadProcessConfig(["server","database","webPush"])` with a variable missing throws a `ConfigError` with its name only.
- R2. `docs/environment-config.md` gets a section "Web Push" with three rows (`VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY` not secret, `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY` secret, `VAPID_SUBJECT` not secret; required `yes`, default `none`, supplied by "the operator of that installation"), and `.env.example` gets the three names (public and private empty, subject `mailto:admin@example.test`), so the test of TOKI-004 R13 passes. The script `vapid:keys` (root and `apps/server`, added to "Commands" of `CLAUDE.md`) prints exactly two lines on standard output, `VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY=<value>` and `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY=<value>`, made with `node:crypto`, and nothing else; the form is `pnpm --silent vapid:keys` and the builder pastes the lines into `.env`. The environment document says that changing the pair stops every registered device until it registers again (R22). Check: the names test; the script's output parsed by R1's schema, two runs giving different pairs.
- R3. Sending goes through the library `web-push` at one exact version (the newest published at least two weeks before the build; the pull request names it and the check that it handles the `aes128gcm` encoding and VAPID), reached only from `modules/notifications/web-push-sender.ts`. That file exports `WebPushSender`, `send(subscription, payload: string, options: { ttlSeconds: number; urgency: "normal"; timeoutMs: number }) => Promise<SendResult>`, with `SendResult` one of `{ kind: "sent" }`, `{ kind: "gone" }` (status 404 or 410), `{ kind: "retry" }` (status 429 or 5xx, a network error or a timeout) and `{ kind: "rejected", status }` (any other status). No test reaches the network: the handler takes the sender from `deps`, and tests pass a fake. Check: a test of the mapping from status to result with the library's own error type; a search of the tests for `fetch` and `https` finds no real call.
- R4. `packages/shared/src/notifications/web-push.ts` (exported from the package index) holds: `WebPushSubscriptionSchema`, strict `{ endpoint, keys: { p256dh, auth } }` with `endpoint` a string of at most 2,048 characters, `p256dh` base64url of 65 bytes and `auth` base64url of 16 bytes; `RegisterDeviceBodySchema`, strict `{ subscription, sendTest }` with `sendTest` an optional boolean; `PushPayloadSchema`, strict `{ v: 1, kind, title, body, tag, data }` with `kind` one of `message`, `mention`, `reminder`, `test` and `data` either `null` or strict `{ conversationId, messageId, parentId }` (UUIDs, `parentId` UUID or `null`); `PushClickSchema`, strict `{ type: "toki.openMessage", conversationId, messageId, parentId }`; and `PUSH_PREVIEW_MAX` equal to `NOTICE_PREVIEW_MAX` of TOKI-073 R1 (60 code points). Check: schema tests for each valid object and for an added key, a missing key and a wrong length of each key.

Registration
- R5. `GET /api/v1/notifications/web-push/key` with a valid session returns status 200 and `{ publicKey }`, the configured public key. It returns nothing else and needs no table. Without a session it returns 401 `unauthenticated` of TOKI-022. Check: the two cases.
- R6. `PUT /api/v1/notifications/devices/current` with a valid session and a body of R4 stores the registration of the session that carries the request: one row of `devices` with `workspace_id`, `user_id` and `session_id` of the request, `push_channel` `web_push` and `push_address` `{ endpoint, p256dh, auth }`. A session has at most one row (`devices_session_id_key`, TOKI-022 tables R20): when the session has one, the call replaces its address and returns 200; when it has none, the call inserts one and returns 201. The body of the answer is `{ registered: true }`. When `sendTest` is true the call also enqueues the job of R17 for that device, in the same transaction, whether the row is new or not; when it is absent or false it enqueues nothing. A body that fails R4 returns 400 `validation_failed`. Check: the 201, the 200, the replaced address, the test job present only with `sendTest`, and a rollback (a failure after the insert) leaving neither row nor job.
- R7. The endpoint is checked before anything is stored, because the server will post to it. It must be an `https://` address with no user name or password, no port other than 443, and a host that equals or ends with `.` plus one of `fcm.googleapis.com`, `push.services.mozilla.com`, `push.apple.com` and `notify.windows.com`, the push services of the browsers D-108 supports. Any other endpoint returns 400 `validation_failed` and stores nothing. The list is the constant `PUSH_SERVICE_HOSTS` in `web-push-sender.ts`'s neighbour `endpoints.ts`. Check: a table of accepted endpoints (one per service) and refused ones (`http://`, a loopback address, an address with a user name, a port, a look-alike host such as `evilfcm.googleapis.com.example.test`, a host that merely contains the name).
- R8. `DELETE /api/v1/notifications/devices/current` with a valid session removes the row of the session that carries the request and returns 204. With no row it returns 204 and changes nothing. It never touches a row of another session or person. Check: both cases, and a row of another session of the same person left in place.
- R9. One migration, `<number>_web_push_endpoint_index` (forward-only, TOKI-005 R11), creates the unique index `devices_push_endpoint_key` on `(push_address ->> 'endpoint')` of `devices`. In the transaction of R6, before the insert or update, the rows of any other session with the same endpoint are deleted, so a browser's endpoint belongs to the session that registered it last and the pushes of one person never reach the browser that another person signed in on. Two registrations of one endpoint that overlap leave exactly one row, the other call retrying once after a unique violation. Check: a test registers an endpoint for person A, then for person B: A has no row; two overlapping calls with two connections leave one row; the migration folder holds one new migration and `db:generate` reports no change.
- R10. A registration lives as long as its session. (a) `modules/notifications` registers a listener with `onSessionsEnded` of TOKI-022 R50 (`app.ts` passes the function in; the module imports no other module): for the ids it hears, it deletes the rows with those `session_id` values, in one statement, for any reason (`signed_out`, `revoked`, `replaced`, `deactivated`). (b) The scheduled job `notifications.prune-devices` (`defineJob` with `schedule: { cron: "17 3 * * *" }`, TOKI-064 R16) deletes the rows whose session is not live, asking the port `SessionLiveness.liveIds(client, ids)` that `app.ts` and `buildJobDeps` build from `getLiveSessionIds(client, ids)`, a function this ticket adds to the `auth` module's index (the one read of `sessions` for the purpose; ended or idle for more than 30 days counts as not live, as TOKI-026 R2; a change request to TOKI-022's module, like TOKI-024's `getLastUsedAtByUser`). The job reads the rows in pages of 500 and holds no session secret. Check: with real sessions, each of the four reasons removes the row and no other; a session idle for 30 days and one second is pruned and one idle for exactly 30 days is kept, with `TestClock`; a listener that throws does not stop the sign-out (TOKI-022 R50).
- R11. The routes of R5, R6 and R8 need a valid session (TOKI-022 R24), the `PUT` and `DELETE` obey the `Origin` rule (TOKI-022 R46), and no route returns an endpoint, a key or a row of `devices`. No log line of this work holds an endpoint, a key, a payload or the text of a message: a line carries the user id, the kind and counts only. Check: a captured log of a registration, a send, a failure and a removal searched for the endpoint, both keys and the message text; the answers of the three routes compared with their schemas.

Sending
- R12. The job type `notifications.send-push` (`defineJob`, TOKI-064 R6; added to `job-types.ts`) has a strict payload of identifiers and booleans only: `userId`, `workspaceId`, `kind` (`message`, `mention`, `reminder`, `test`), `conversationId`, `messageId`, `parentId`, `actorId`, `itemId`, `deviceId` and `viaChannel`; a value that does not apply is an omitted key, not `null`. It uses the defaults of TOKI-064 R8 (five retries, backoff) with `concurrency` 4. Its key is `push:<userId>:<messageId>` (`push:<userId>:<itemId>` for a reminder, `push:<userId>:test:<deviceId>` for a test), the key TOKI-073 R8 fixed. Check: the walking test of TOKI-064 R7 passes for the type; a payload with a free-text property is refused.
- R13. `createPushPort(deps)` of `modules/notifications/push-port.ts` is the real `PushPort` of TOKI-073 R8; it replaces the stand-in and its name, and `app.ts` and `buildJobDeps` build it. `enqueue(tx, notice)` reads, through `tx`, whether the person has at least one `web_push` row in `devices`: with none it enqueues nothing; with one or more it enqueues one `notifications.send-push` job through `jobs.enqueue(..., { key, tx })`, so a rollback leaves no job. `cancel(tx, userId, messageId)` calls `jobs.cancelByKey(sendPush, "push:<userId>:<messageId>", { tx })`. A person is one job, not one per device. Check: with real queue and database, no job for a person with no device, one job for a person with three; a rolled-back transaction leaves none; `cancel` stops a job not started and one for another person is untouched; the port satisfies the type test of TOKI-073 R8 (identifiers and the kind only).
- R14. The handler (`createSendPushHandler(deps)`, `defineHandler`) first reads the person's `web_push` rows; with none it completes. For `message`, `mention` and `reminder` it then makes the send-time check through the port `PushSourcePort.read(client, data)`, which returns `{ live, language, senderName, conversation, text }` and which `app.ts` and `buildJobDeps` build from the public reads of `members`, `conversations`, `messages` and `activity`. `live` is false, and nothing is sent, when: the message is deleted (TOKI-047), for `message` and `mention`; the Activity item `itemId` no longer exists, for `mention`; the person is no longer a member of the conversation (left, removed or the account deactivated); or the person's `desktop` switch (TOKI-102 R8, `loadNotificationPreferencesFor`) is off. Pause and Focus are not read again: TOKI-073 decided them when it handed the push over (R10 there). A kind `test` skips the check (R17). Check: a table over each case with real tables or fakes of the four reads: each false case sends nothing and completes the job (no retry); the live case sends; a deleted reply and a deleted top-level message both send nothing.
- R15. The content, built in `modules/notifications/push-payload.ts` as a pure function of `{ kind, language, senderName, conversation, text, ids }` to a `PushPayload` of R4. The words come from `translate(language, key, params)` of `@toki/i18n`, with the person's saved language (`users.language`, D-018): the server writes the whole text and the service worker only shows it. D-044 gives the three parts; the layout is a proposal (D-139): for a channel the `title` is the conversation name with its `#` and the `body` is `"<sender>: <start>"`; for a direct message the `title` is the sender and the `body` is the start; for a group direct message the `title` is the other people's names joined by commas and the `body` is `"<sender>: <start>"`. The start is the first `PUSH_PREVIEW_MAX` code points of the message text, line breaks and runs of white space made one space, with no formatting removed and no ellipsis added; it is empty when the message has no text (only files), and then the `body` holds the sender alone. `tag` is `msg:<messageId>` for `message` and `mention`, `item:<itemId>` for `reminder` and `test:<deviceId>` for `test`, so a send that is retried replaces the notification it already showed instead of adding a second. `data` holds the three ids, or `null` for `test`. Check: a table of the three conversation kinds in both languages, a start of exactly 60, 61 and 0 code points, an emoji at the cut (cut by code points, never inside one), a text with line breaks; the payload checked with `PushPayloadSchema`; a payload never longer than 1,024 bytes for a 60-code-point start and the longest allowed names.
- R16. One send. For each row the handler calls the sender (R3) with `ttlSeconds` 3,600, `urgency` `normal` and `timeoutMs` 10,000, all rows of the person together. The constants are `PUSH_TTL_SECONDS` and `PUSH_TIMEOUT_MS`. A `gone` result deletes that row. A `rejected` result is logged by status and device id and neither retried nor deleted. A `retry` result makes the handler throw after every row has been tried, so the job is tried again by TOKI-064 R8; a row that succeeded is sent again by that retry and the `tag` of R15 makes the browser replace the notification instead of showing two. The job completes when no row answered `retry`. Check: a table with a fake sender over `sent`, `gone`, `retry` and `rejected` for one and for several devices, with the row deletions and the throw asserted; the retry through the real queue with `TestClock`; the arguments of the fake checked for the three constants.
- R17. The test notification. After `PUT` with `sendTest` true (R6) the job has `kind` `test` and the `deviceId`: it reads that one row, builds a payload with the title `push.test.title` and the body `push.test.body` in the person's language (proposal: "Toki" and "Notifications are on", F36), tag `test:<deviceId>`, `data` null, and sends it to that device only. It is not subject to the `desktop` switch, to Pause or Focus, or to the send-time check, because it proves the registration and is the answer to a press of Turn on. Check: a registration with `sendTest` sends one payload to that device and none to the person's other devices; with the switch off the test is still sent; a registration without `sendTest` sends nothing.
- R18. Wiring. `modules/notifications/index.ts` exports `createPushPort`, `createSendPushHandler`, `createPruneDevicesHandler`, `registerDevicesRoutes`, `registerDeviceCleanup`, the job types, `webPushConfigSchema`, `webPushVariableNames` and the types `WebPushSender`, `PushSourcePort` and `SessionLiveness`; no table. `JobDeps` gains the fields `webPushSender` and `pushSource` and the config area `webPush` (TOKI-064 R9, R17); `pushPort` is the field TOKI-073 R20 added. The module imports no other module: `app.ts` and `job-deps.ts` pass in the functions that cross a boundary. Check: the boundary lint passes (TOKI-002); `buildJobHandlers` with fake deps returns the two handlers; a search of `modules/notifications` for imports of another module finds none.

The service worker
- R19. `apps/web/public/push-sw.js` is the service worker, served at `/push-sw.js` with the scope `/`. It is a plain script with no import and no build step, and it has exactly these listeners: `install` (calls `skipWaiting`), `activate` (calls `clients.claim`), `push` and `notificationclick`. It has no `fetch` listener and no cache, so it changes nothing about how pages load (D-030). On `push` it parses the payload and always calls `showNotification` before it ends (`event.waitUntil`): a push with no or invalid data shows a notification titled "Toki" with no body, because a browser that sees a push with no visible result may cancel the subscription. It shows `title`, `body` and `tag` of the payload and puts `data` (ids only) in the notification's data; it sets no `icon` and no `badge` (the icons are TOKI-077's, who may add them to this call) and no `renotify`. It holds no word of its own except the product name. In production the file is served with `Cache-Control: no-cache`, which TOKI-084 sets, so a new version reaches a browser within a day. Check: the script run in `node:vm` with fakes of `self`, `clients` and `registration`: the four listeners and no other; a valid payload calls `showNotification` once with the three fields and the ids; an empty, a non-JSON and a wrongly shaped payload each show the "Toki" fallback; the payloads of R15 pass `PushPayloadSchema` and are shown without change; no `fetch` or `caches` use in the source.
- R20. On `notificationclick` the worker closes the notification and reads the three ids from its data. With `data` null (the test) it only focuses an open window of the origin, or opens `/` when there is none. Otherwise it looks at the open window clients of the origin (`clients.matchAll({ type: "window", includeUncontrolled: true })`): when there is one, it focuses it (the visible one first) and posts to it `{ type: "toki.openMessage", conversationId, messageId, parentId }` (`PushClickSchema`); when there is none, it opens `/open-message?c=<conversationId>&m=<messageId>` with `&p=<parentId>` when the message is a reply. Check: in the `node:vm` run: one window gets focus and the message and no `openWindow`; two windows, the visible one is chosen; no window gives one `openWindow` with the exact address, with and without `p`; the test notification opens no message; an ids value with a character outside a UUID is not put in an address.

The web app
- R21. `packages/platform` gains `Platform["push"]` and its web adapter `web/push.ts`: `support(): "supported" | "needs_home_screen" | "unsupported"`, `subscribe(applicationServerKey: string): Promise<DeviceSubscription | null>`, `current(): Promise<DeviceSubscription | null>`, `unsubscribe(): Promise<void>` and `onOpenMessage(listener): () => void`, with `DeviceSubscription` the `{ endpoint, keys }` of R4. `support` is `needs_home_screen` when the device is an iPhone or iPad (the user agent holds `iPhone` or `iPad`, or holds `Macintosh` while `navigator.maxTouchPoints` is above 1) and the app is not running from the Home Screen (`navigator.standalone` is not true and `(display-mode: standalone)` does not match); otherwise `supported` when `serviceWorker`, `PushManager` and `Notification` exist and the page is a secure context; otherwise `unsupported`. `subscribe` registers `/push-sw.js` with scope `/`, waits for it to be ready and subscribes with `userVisibleOnly` true; when the browser already holds a subscription made with another key, it unsubscribes it first. It resolves `null` on any failure and throws nothing the caller cannot read. `onOpenMessage` listens for the worker's messages and calls the listener only with a message that passes `PushClickSchema`. `ui-web` calls none of this directly (R27). Check: the adapter against fakes of `navigator`, `Notification`, `PushManager` and `matchMedia`, in a table over desktop Chrome, Firefox, Safari on a Mac, an iPhone in Safari, an iPhone from the Home Screen, an iPad that reports `Macintosh` with touch, and a browser with none of the three; an old subscription with another key replaced; a message of the wrong shape ignored.
- R22. Registration is kept in step with the browser. While a signed-in shell is mounted and `support()` is `supported`: with the permission `granted`, once per page load after the first load of the store, the app reads the key (R5), calls `platform.push.subscribe(key)` (which makes the subscription when there is none, and replaces one made with another key), and sends it with `PUT` (R6) without `sendTest`; with the permission `denied`, it sends `DELETE` (R8), best effort, once per page load. A failed call is not shown and is tried again at the next page load. Check: a table over granted with and without a subscription, granted with the old key, denied, default (nothing is sent), and an unsupported browser (nothing is sent); a failure of each call leaves the app working.
- R23. The click address. A route `/open-message` is a child of the signed-in layout (`appLayoutRoute`), so a person who is not signed in is sent to `/login` with this address as `next` and returns to it after signing in (the guard's `next` rule, `returnAddress`). Its search is `c` and `m` (UUIDs) and optionally `p` (UUID); a search that fails is replaced by `/` at once. A valid search makes the route replace itself with `/` (a replace, so Back does not return here) and then call `open({ conversationId: c, messageId: m, parentId: p ?? null })` of `useOpenMessage()` (TOKI-068 R1) once. `AppShellRoute` also subscribes to `platform.push.onOpenMessage` while it is mounted and calls `open` with each message it hears. The route and the subscription add no search key to `/c/$conversationId` and do not read the router's state. Check: a memory router with a fake hook: a valid address calls `open` once and leaves `/` as the entry in place of `/open-message`; an invalid one calls nothing and shows `/`; a signed-out visit goes to `/login?next=` and returns; a message from the worker calls `open` with the three ids and a reply gets its `parentId`; an unmounted shell no longer listens.
- R24. The first-visit card, `PushCard` in `apps/web/src/push/`, is drawn by the shell (`ShellBody` of `routes/app-layout.tsx` renders it beside `ConversationStarters`). It is drawn when all hold: a person is signed in and the store is ready; `support()` is `supported`; the browser's permission is `default`; the person has not pressed Later on this device (the key `pushCard` of `platform.storage.device`); and the person's `desktop` switch is on (concern 2). It shows the text of `push.card.title` (F36: "Turn on notifications so you do not miss messages?") and two buttons, `push.card.turnOn` and `push.card.later`. The browser's prompt is shown only from the press of Turn on: `platform.notifications.requestPermission()` is called from that press and from no other place. On `granted` the card calls `subscribe` (R21), `PUT` with `sendTest` true (R6) and closes; on `denied` it closes and Preferences shows the blocked state (R26); on `default` (the prompt was dismissed) it stays. When the subscription or the call fails the card stays and shows `push.card.failed` with the buttons, and a second press of Turn on skips the prompt. Later writes the key and closes the card; what happens afterwards is concern 1. The card is a region with an accessible name, is reached with Tab, and nothing in it works by hover alone (D-067). Check: component tests with the fake platform: each condition makes it absent; the prompt is not requested before the press (the fake counts calls); the three answers; a failed call; the keyboard; the same card at 390 px and at 1280 px; a Playwright test in Chromium with the permission granted by the context and the subscription and the `PUT` stubbed.
- R25. The card is also what a person sees on a first sign-in by invitation (F01, F36): it follows R24 with no case of its own. A device that has pressed Turn on, or whose permission is `granted` or `denied`, never shows it again, in this browser or after a sign-out and sign-in. Check: the same fake platform after each of the three states; a second sign-in on the same device.
- R26. The device block, `PushSettings` in `apps/web/src/push/`, is mounted by `routes/preferences.tsx` below the devices section until TOKI-078 places it in its Notifications section (the edit is one import and one element, and TOKI-078 moves the block without changing it). It shows the state of this device: `needs_home_screen`: the explanation `push.settings.homeScreen` of D-108 and F36 and no button; `unsupported`: `push.settings.unsupported` and no button; permission `denied`: `push.settings.blocked` ("Notifications are blocked in the browser", F36) with the steps `push.settings.blockedSteps`; permission `default`: `push.settings.off` and a Turn on button that does what R24 does; permission `granted` with a registration: `push.settings.on`. The state is read when the page mounts and again when the page becomes visible, because the person may change the permission in the browser's settings. It shows the three switches of TOKI-102 nowhere. Check: component tests for the five states with the fake platform; a state change when visibility returns; the Turn on press requests the permission once and registers like the card.
- R27. Sign-out. At the start of the app the web app registers, through `registerBeforeSignOut` of TOKI-099 R8, one task that calls `DELETE /notifications/devices/current` (R8) and then `platform.push.unsubscribe()`, so the registration is removed while the session is still valid and the browser's subscription is dropped. A failure of either step does not stop the sign-out (the sequence's own 10-second rule applies, TOKI-099 R8), and the server's listener (R10) removes the row anyway when the session ends. `ui-web` calls no service worker or push function: the boundary rule of TOKI-002 holds, and a test that lints a file of `ui-web` with a `navigator.serviceWorker` call fails. ## Design
The push has five parts in a line: the engine decides (TOKI-073), the port hands over (R13), the worker sends (R14 to R17), the push service carries, the service worker shows and reacts (R19, R20). The server writes the whole words; the worker shows what it receives. Reason: the saved language is known to the server (D-018), the service worker then needs no catalog, and a notification can never show a half-translated text.

```
decision (TOKI-073) -- PushPort.enqueue(tx) --> job notifications.send-push  (one per person)
                                                   |  worker: rows of devices -> send-time check -> payload (R15)
                                                   v
                              push service (FCM, Mozilla, Apple, Windows) -- encrypted payload --> browser
                                                                                                    |
                                                  push-sw.js: showNotification (always)  <----------+
                                                  click: focus window + postMessage  |  or openWindow /open-message
                                                                  |                                 |
                                         platform.push.onOpenMessage                  route /open-message (guard -> /login?next=)
                                                                  \______ useOpenMessage().open(...) (TOKI-068) ______/
```

### Data model
No new table. `devices` (TOKI-019 R20) is used as it is: one row per session, `push_channel` `web_push`, `push_address` `{ endpoint, p256dh, auth }`. One migration adds `devices_push_endpoint_key` (R9). The three VAPID values are configuration, not data.

### API
| Method | Path | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| GET | `/api/v1/notifications/web-push/key` | the public key the browser subscribes with (R5) |
| PUT | `/api/v1/notifications/devices/current` | register or replace this session's device; optional test (R6) |
| DELETE | `/api/v1/notifications/devices/current` | remove this session's device (R8) |

### Errors
- 400 `validation_failed`: a body that fails R4, an endpoint that fails R7.
- 401 `unauthenticated` or `session_ended`: no valid session (TOKI-022).
- 403: a foreign `Origin` on `PUT` or `DELETE` (TOKI-022 R46).
- A failed send never changes the answer to the person's request; it is a job outcome (R16).

### Layout
```
apps/server/src/modules/notifications/

  devices.ts           R6, R8, R9    devices.routes.ts   R5, R6, R8

```

### Why these choices
- One job per person, not per device: TOKI-073 R8 fixed the key `push:<userId>:<messageId>` so a cancel needs no search. The cost is that a retry sends again to devices that already got it; the `tag` makes the browser replace, not add (R15, R16).
- An allow-list of push hosts (R7): the endpoint is chosen by the client and the server posts to it, so an open list would let a signed-in person make the server call any address. The four hosts are those of the browsers D-108 supports; a new service is one constant.
- The click address (R23) is a route that uses the hook and leaves, not a search key on the conversation: TOKI-068 concern 6 has not decided whether a message has an address, and this route promises none. It is for one purpose and can be replaced when that concern is answered.
- Registration per session, not per browser: the table already says so (`session_id` is unique), so ending the session removes the registration with one rule (R10), and one endpoint belongs to one session (R9).
- No `pushsubscriptionchange` handler: R22 repairs a changed subscription at the next page load. Until then the old endpoint answers 410 and its row is deleted (R16), so a person whose browser changed the subscription gets no push until they next open Toki (concern 11).

## Data safety
- A subscription (endpoint and keys) is stored in clear in `devices.push_address`, next to the user id, as the table's own design allows (TOKI-019 Data safety). Sending also needs the private VAPID key, which is a secret: never committed, never logged, never in a payload or an answer (R2, R11). Decided in this spec: the object is not encrypted at rest in phase 1; reason: the key to encrypt it would be one more secret next to the database it protects, and the VAPID key already stops a stranger from sending.
- The payload holds the sender, the conversation and the start of the message. It is encrypted for the one browser (`aes128gcm`) and the push service cannot read it. Job payloads and logs hold identifiers only (R11, R12).
- Whether a person has a device is private: no route and no event gives it to another person (R11).
- The service worker shows whatever the server sent it; it reads no message, makes no request and stores nothing (R19).

## Areas of concern
1. What Later does on the card. Not decided. F36 gives the card two buttons, Turn on and Later, and says nothing of what Later does next: whether the card never returns, returns at the next visit, or after some days. Proposal, not a decision: Later closes the card on this device and it does not return by itself; Preferences stays the place (R26). R24 builds that behaviour, so a different answer changes one condition. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
2. The card and the Desktop notifications switch. Not decided. The switch is one for the person on every device (D-142) and gates the push (TOKI-073 R2); the card registers one device. No decision says whether the card is offered when the switch is off, or whether Turn on switches it on. Proposal, not a decision: the card is not offered while the switch is off, and Turn on registers the device and leaves the switch as it is. R24 follows the proposal; the defaults of the switches are TOKI-102 concern 1. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
3. The push of a reminder. Not decided. F28 gives a reminder a push when the app is not open and says nothing of its words; D-074 gives a deleted source message a replacement row, which suggests the reminder still fires. Proposal, not a decision: the push shows the title `push.reminder.title` and, when the message exists, its sender, conversation and start as for any push (D-044); when the message was deleted, no push is sent, because there is nothing to show. R14 and R15 follow the proposal for the first part and treat the second as "not live". Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
4. The click address. Decided in this spec: a window that is open is told through the service worker's message and calls `useOpenMessage`; with no window the worker opens `/open-message?c=&m=&p=`, a route of the signed-in layout that calls the hook once and replaces itself with `/` (R20, R23). A signed-out person is sent to sign in and back by the guard's `next`. Reason: the Blueprint says only "opens Toki at that message"; TOKI-068 concern 6 (an address for one message) has no answer yet, so this ticket adds no key to the conversation's address and promises no address for sharing. Owner: Thanh Truong.
5. The words and the place of the card, the test notification and the push. Decided in this spec: every wording is a proposal under D-139 and sits in the catalog (R28); the card is drawn by the shell beside the conversation starters, as a small card with two buttons; the layout of a notification is R15. Reason: the prototype has none of these screens or words (a search for the card, a permission or a service worker finds only the two labels "Desktop notifications" and "Push notifications"), and D-139 says the design decides the final words. Owner: Thanh Truong.
6. Who owns the service worker. Decided in this spec: this ticket owns `apps/web/public/push-sw.js` and its registration (`platform.push.subscribe`, R21). TOKI-077 builds the manifest, icons and install prompt, may add `icon` and `badge` to the `showNotification` call, and adds no `fetch` listener or cache to this file (D-030: "service worker for push only"). A second worker file would need its own scope and would hide this one, so there is one. Reason: push is the only thing the worker does, and the PWA ticket needs the file to exist. Owner: Thanh Truong.
7. What the server may post to. Decided in this spec: the endpoint of a registration must be an `https` address on one of four hosts (R7). Reason: the server posts to the address a client gives; the four hosts are those of Chrome and Edge's services, Firefox, Safari and Windows. A push service on another host is one constant to add. Owner: Thanh Truong.
8. When the test notification is sent. Decided in this spec: only when the registration asks for it, which the app does on a press of Turn on (R6, R24, R26), not on the silent registration at each sign-in (R22). Reason: F36 shows the test as the answer to approval; a test at every sign-in on every device would be noise. Owner: Thanh Truong.
9. iPhone and iPad. Decided in this spec: the rule "push only from the Home Screen" is applied to an iPhone and to an iPad (R21), and the check also needs the browser's push features, so a device that gains push without the Home Screen is not blocked by a list. Reason: D-108 names the iPhone only; iPadOS has the same limit and reports itself as a Mac, so it is recognised by its touch points. Owner: Thanh Truong.
10. Bounds that are not product rules. Decided in this spec: the start of the message is 60 code points, the same as the toast (R4); the push service keeps an undelivered message for 3,600 seconds and a send is given 10 seconds (R16); the stale-registration job runs daily at 03:17 (R10); a group direct message is titled with the other people's names. Reason: a notification and a send need a size and a time; a push older than an hour reads as news while the badge and the Activity item already say what happened. They are constants and can change. Owner: Thanh Truong.
11. A changed subscription. Decided in this spec: no `pushsubscriptionchange` handler in the first version; the next page load repairs it (R22) and a rotated VAPID pair makes every device register again at its next page load (R2, R22). Reason: the event is rare, the service worker would need to make an authenticated request on its own, and the repair costs a person with a changed subscription only the pushes until they next open Toki. Owner: Thanh Truong.
12. Verifying on a real iPhone. Decided in this spec: this ticket builds the iPhone rule, its explanation and the registration, and tests them with fakes (R21, R26). Whether a push really reaches an iPhone Home Screen app depends on the manifest of TOKI-077, so the check on a real device, which the technical design asks for ("try on a real iPhone before the notification work starts", techDeps of FR-18), is recorded in the QA record of TOKI-077 and repeated for this ticket once the manifest exists. Reason: without a manifest with `display: standalone` there is no Home Screen app to test. Owner: Thanh Truong.
13. The Pause and Focus state at send time. Decided in this spec: the handler does not read Pause or Focus again (R14); TOKI-073 decided them when it enqueued the job, in the same transaction as the message. Reason: a second read would make two places that apply D-072, and a job runs within seconds of the change. Owner: Thanh Truong.
