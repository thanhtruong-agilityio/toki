# Extra states design

Date: 2026-11-26 · Status: draft for review (concerns 1 to 5 are open for the product owner)
Intent: docs/intents/extra-states.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-082

## Goal
The prototype draws Toki only while everything works. This work adds the states that a real session meets and that the earlier tickets left as plain stand-ins: a skeleton while data loads, the strip "Offline: showing saved messages" while the connection is down, one shared page for a page that does not exist or that the person may not open, and the same Retry, with a short error ID for a server error, wherever a read fails or has no answer after 10 seconds. It also gives the online check of TOKI-093 the real state of the connection, so that while disconnected only messages wait and every other write is blocked with an explanation.
After this change a person who opens the app sees the layout at once; a person who loses the network keeps reading, can still write a message that waits, and is told why anything else does not work; and a failure looks and behaves the same on every screen.

## Scope
- In:
  - `Strip` in `packages/ui-web` with the tone `offline` (R1; who creates the file is concern 1), and `Skeleton` (R2).
  - In `apps/web`: the rule that says the connection is down and the call of `setConnectionCheck` (R3, R4); the offline strip and its two places (R5, R6); the first load that fails (R7); the skeleton layouts and where they show (R8); the shared page for not found and no access (R9); `LoadFailed` with Retry and the error ID (R10, R11); the proof that writes are blocked while disconnected and only messages wait (R12); the replacement of the stand-ins of earlier tickets (R13); the texts (R14); the examples (R15).
- Out (not now):
  - The connection, its retry delays, its 3-second grace and its state: TOKI-039. The waiting message, its single automatic try and its states `sending`, `waiting`, `failed`: TOKI-040. Drawing the label "Waiting to send", "Not sent", Retry and Delete beside a message: the message component, TOKI-042. This work reads the connection and draws none of these.
  - The online gate and its texts `common.offline.blocked` and `common.offline.undoBlocked`: TOKI-093 (R4 only sets what the gate reads). The call sites that go through the gate belong to the tickets that build them.
  - The Pause and Focus strip: TOKI-076. The update-available strip, its text, its button and the forced reload: TOKI-104 (R1 draws the line). The strip "This channel was just archived": the archive tickets (TOKI-101, concern 5 there); it can use any tone of `Strip`.
  - What a tab shows after the server closed its socket for too many tabs (`stopped` by 4430): concern 5 here, which is concern 7 of TOKI-039.
  - Queuing reactions, edits, read markers and saves to Later while offline: phase 6 (D-060). Reading from a saved copy across a page reload: phase 6.
  - Server code: none. The error format and the request id come from TOKI-004 and are only read.
  - Focus order, labels and reduced motion as a whole: TOKI-083. The pieces here set `role`, `aria-busy` and a label, and stop their animation under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

The state of the connection
- R3. `apps/web/src/offline/is-offline.ts` exports `isOffline(connection, store)`, a pure function. `connection` is `{ status }` of `getState()` of the connection of TOKI-039 R15; `store` is `{ status, error }` of the conversation store of TOKI-032 R10. It returns `true` in two cases and `false` in every other: (a) `connection.status` is `offline`; (b) `connection.status` is `idle`, `store.status` is `failed` and `store.error` is an `ApiError` of `kind` `network` or `timeout` (the first load failed because the network is down: nothing started the connection, TOKI-039 concern 13, and F08 says "no connection at open: the banner shows"). `connecting` is `false`, so a drop shorter than the 3 seconds of TOKI-039 R15 shows nothing (F37); `online` is `false`; `stopped` is `false` (concern 5); `idle` with a `ready` store is `false`; `idle` with a `failed` store whose error is a 5xx, an `invalid_response` or a 4xx is `false` (the server answered, so it is not an offline state, and R10 shows the failure). `useOffline()` in `apps/web/src/offline/useOffline.ts` returns it for the current states with `useSyncExternalStore` on both subscriptions and re-renders only when the boolean changes. Check: `isOffline` for all five statuses of the connection crossed with the four statuses of the store and the five kinds of error (a table test of the whole product, with the expected value written for each row); `useOffline` with fake stores renders once for a change that leaves the value as it was.
- R4. `apps/web/src/offline/connection-check.ts` exports `bindConnectionCheck()`, which calls `setConnectionCheck(() => !isOffline(connection.getState(), conversations.getState()))` of TOKI-093 R17 once, with `connection` from `apps/web/src/realtime.ts` (TOKI-039 R20) and `conversations` from `client.ts`. The function reads both states at the moment it is called and keeps no copy and no subscription, so every caller of `ensureOnline()` and `isOnline()` (the gate of TOKI-093, the palette search of TOKI-071, the upload gate of TOKI-059) sees the state of the connection and not the browser's flag. `main.tsx` calls it once after `bindRealtime()`, before the app is mounted. It sends no request and shows nothing. Decided in this spec: the check is `false` only in the cases of R3, so a person is blocked only for a connection that the connection itself calls offline, and a check that cannot know (`connecting`, `stopped`, no connection yet) lets the action through, as TOKI-093 R17 says a broken check must. Check: with a fake connection and a fake store, the check returns `false` for `offline` and for a first load that failed on the network, and `true` for the other cases of the table of R3; a changed state is read by the next call with no re-bind; `bindConnectionCheck` called twice leaves one check; `useOnlineGate().ensureOnline()` gives `false` and one toast `common.offline.blocked` when the connection is `offline`, and `true` with no toast when it is `online`.

The first load that fails
- R7. `apps/web/src/offline/useFirstLoadRetry.ts` is called by `ShellBody` of `routes/app-layout.tsx`. While the conversation store is `failed` and `classifyApiError(store.error)` is `retry` (a network, a timeout or a 5xx), it calls `conversations.load()` again after a wait of `RECONNECT_DELAYS_SECONDS[min(k, 4)]` seconds of `@toki/client-core` (1, 2, 5, 10, 30, D-129) multiplied by `1 - J + 2 * J * random()` with `J` equal to `RECONNECT_JITTER` (0.2), `k` counting the failed tries from 0, with `timers.after(ms, fn, "first-load-retry")` and the `random` that `realtime.ts` uses. A failure of another class (`unreadable`, a 4xx) is not tried again by itself. The wait is cancelled when the store leaves `failed`, when the session stops being signed in and when the component unmounts; a press on Retry (R10) calls `load()` at once, cancels the pending wait and does not change `k`; `k` goes back to 0 when the store is `ready`. While the store is `failed` for the network the strip of R5 shows (R3 (b)); when a try succeeds the store is `ready`, the connection starts (TOKI-039 R20) and the strip goes with no toast. Decided in this spec (concern 7): the log says only that the app "retries on its own" (F08), so the delays of D-129 are reused, because they are the only retry delays in the log and a client that returns after a long outage should not meet others at one instant. Check: with fake timers, a fake `random` and a fake `api` that fails: the waits asked with the label `first-load-retry` after six failures are 1, 2, 5, 10, 30, 30 seconds for `random` 0.5, and 0.8 and 1.2 times those for 0 and just under 1; a `load()` that succeeds stops the loop and the next failure after a reset starts at 1 second; a 403 and an `invalid_response` are never repeated; a Retry press loads at once, cancels the wait and leaves `k`; unmount and sign-out cancel the wait.

## Design
### How the pieces fit
```
conversation store ----+
                       +--> isOffline() --> useOffline() --> OfflineStrip (main area, list notice)
connection (TOKI-039) -+         |
                                 +--> setConnectionCheck --> ensureOnline() --> every gated write (TOKI-093)

store idle/loading --> skeletons          call ends: timeout / 5xx / network --> LoadFailed (Retry, error ID)
store failed (network) --> strip + useFirstLoadRetry (1, 2, 5, 10, 30 s) --> load()
403 / 404 --> NotFoundView  (one page, no names)
```

### Where the strips sit
In `main`, from the top: the offline strip (R5), the Pause or Focus strip (TOKI-076 R18), the update strip (TOKI-104), then the page. A strip draws nothing when it has nothing to say, so the order only matters when more than one shows. `app-layout.tsx` is the one place that holds the order.

### The line with the neighbours
- `Strip` and `.strip`: R1 and concern 1. This ticket owns the `offline` tone, `OfflineStrip` and the three places named above. TOKI-076 owns the text, the button and the countdown of its strip; TOKI-104 owns the text, the button and the reload of the update strip.
- The connection state: TOKI-039 owns it and its grace; this ticket reads `status` and builds no timer for the strip. The first load that fails and the retry of it were assigned here by TOKI-039 concern 13.
- The waiting message: TOKI-040 owns its states and its single automatic try; TOKI-042 draws "Waiting to send", "Not sent", Retry and Delete (D-087); this ticket draws none of it.
- The gate: TOKI-093 owns the helper, `common.offline.blocked` and `common.offline.undoBlocked`; this ticket owns what the gate reads.

### Layout
```
packages/ui-web/src/components/Strip/     Strip.tsx, Strip.css, Strip.examples.tsx, index.ts  (tone offline; whole folder if 076 has not built it)
packages/ui-web/src/components/Skeleton/  Skeleton.tsx, Skeleton.css, Skeleton.examples.tsx, index.ts
apps/web/src/offline/                     is-offline.ts, useOffline.ts, connection-check.ts, OfflineStrip.tsx, useFirstLoadRetry.ts
apps/web/src/states/                      LoadFailed.tsx, ListSkeleton.tsx, ConversationSkeleton.tsx, MessagesSkeleton.tsx, PanelSkeleton.tsx, PageSkeleton.tsx, states.css
apps/web/src/routes/                      NotFoundView.tsx (body replaced), app-layout.tsx (strip, retry hook)
apps/web/src/admin/NoAccess.tsx           (body replaced)
apps/web/src/main.tsx                     one line: bindConnectionCheck()
packages/i18n/src/messages/{en,vi}/states.ts
e2e/offline.spec.ts
```
`ui-web` gets two components and no app code; `apps/web` imports the connection and the store, as `realtime.ts` does, and `ui-web` imports neither.

### Errors
- The server's 404 and 500 bodies are the format of TOKI-004 (`error.code`, `error.message`, `details.requestId` for a 500); the client reads the status and `requestId` and shows no server text (R10).
- A 401 with a session code leaves the screen to the session state (TOKI-023); `LoadFailed` draws nothing for it.

## Data safety
- The error ID is the first 8 characters of a random request id; it holds no person, no path and no content. No `message`, `details` or body of an error is drawn or logged by this work (R10).
- The shared page names no channel, person or path and is the same for 403 and 404, so it confirms nothing about a private channel (R9, D-131).
- Nothing is stored in the browser by this work.

## Areas of concern
1. Who builds the class `.strip`. Not decided. The plan line of this ticket says it owns `.strip`, and the TOKI-012 spec gave `.strip` to this ticket and asked that TOKI-076 and TOKI-104 need it (its concern 9, accepted on 2026-11-18). The spec of TOKI-076 proposes instead to build `Strip` itself, because M5 is built before M6, and keeps the owner question open (its concern 1); the archived-channel strip is a third user and no ticket owns it. Proposal, not a decision: follow the proposal of TOKI-076, so that TOKI-076 builds `Strip` with the tones `default` and `pause`, this ticket adds the tone `offline`, and TOKI-104 and the archived strip reuse it. What this ticket builds in either case: the tone `offline`, `OfflineStrip` and its places (R1, R5). If this ticket is the owner, it also builds the rest of the `Strip` folder, which R1 describes as TOKI-076 R18 states it, and TOKI-076 draws a plain `div` until then (the fallback in TOKI-076 concern 1). The line with TOKI-104: it uses `Strip` and builds none of it. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
2. The wording of the shared page for "not found" and "no access". Not decided. D-131 gives one page for 403 and 404, and D-139 calls "Not found or no access" a proposal. The journeys word two cases apart: "You do not have access to this conversation" (F10) and "You do not have access to this page" (F39); one page cannot say both without telling a 403 from a 404, which would show that a private channel exists. Proposal, not a decision: the three texts of R14 (title "Not found or no access", the description, the button "Go to Home"). Whether the page has a button at all is part of the question. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
3. The text of a blocked write. Not decided. F37 says pressing a write says "A connection is needed"; TOKI-093 ships `common.offline.blocked` "You are offline. Try again when the connection is back." and `common.offline.undoBlocked`, and its concern 4 and concern 7 leave the wording and whether every screen shows the same text undecided; D-091 accepts what the journeys do, not their wording (D-139). Proposal, not a decision: keep the two keys of TOKI-093 for every screen and add no text here. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
4. A button on the offline strip. Not decided. The prototype's strip has a button "Go online" that flips its simulator (line 1269); the log says only that the banner goes when the connection returns (D-102). Proposal, not a decision: no button (R5); the alternative is a button that calls `retryNow()` of the connection. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
5. A tab stopped by the server for too many tabs. Not decided; it is concern 7 of TOKI-039. The connection reports `stopped` with `stoppedBy` `too_many_connections`, which R3 does not call offline, so this ticket shows no strip and blocks nothing for it, and the messages of that tab are sent as with an `idle` connection (TOKI-040 R10). Proposal, not a decision: the proposal of TOKI-039 (the same strip), which R3 would take with one more case. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
6. The two places of the strip. Decided by F37 ("at the top of the main area and of the conversation list"), which D-091 accepts as the baseline; the prototype draws the strip only in the main area (line 1269), so on a wide screen both show. The list place is what TOKI-033 R3 reserved as `notice`. Owner: Thanh Truong.
7. How often the first load is tried again. Decided in this spec (a technical choice): the delays of D-129 with the jitter of TOKI-039 R11, because F08 says only "retries on its own". After the network returns the person waits at most 30 seconds (plus the jitter), and the browser's `online` event does not shorten it, because the connection is not started yet; the Retry button is the quick way. Owner: Thanh Truong.
8. The look of a skeleton. Decided in this spec: tokens, the prototype's pulse and the shapes of R8, because the prototype draws no skeleton and D-139 says the design decides the final form. A designer can change the CSS without a change in the requirements. Owner: Thanh Truong.
9. The error ID. Decided in this spec: the first 8 characters of the request id of TOKI-004 R9, because the log says "a short error ID" (D-131) and the id is a UUID. Owner: Thanh Truong.
10. The wording of the other new texts. Settled by D-139: every wording the prototype does not contain is a proposal and the design decides the final words, so the texts of R14 other than the ones in concerns 2 to 4 ship as written and are reviewed with TOKI-080. The Vietnamese form of address is D-188 (open; TOKI-080). Owner: Thanh Truong.
11. Which stand-ins this ticket replaces. Decided in this spec: the table of R13, because each of those specs names this ticket for the replacement; a write that fails is not a read and keeps its own line. A ticket that is not in the tree is skipped, so the order of builds does not matter. Owner: Thanh Truong.
