# Edit and delete messages design

Date: 2026-11-23 · Status: draft for review
Intent: docs/intents/edit-delete-messages.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-047

## Goal
A member needs to correct a message they sent and to take it back, and an Admin needs to remove another person's message in a conversation they belong to. The tables already hold `edited_at`, `deleted_at` and `deleted_by` (TOKI-036 R6), the permission functions `canEditMessage` and `canDeleteMessage` already decide who may (TOKI-027 R7), the send and history routes already return `editedAt` and `deletedAt` as `null` (TOKI-037 R15), and the confirmation helper already lists the two delete actions (TOKI-093, `deleteOwnMessage` and `deleteMemberMessage`). No route writes those columns and no screen offers the two actions.
After this change the author edits a message in place with no limit, a message edited shows "(edited)", a delete asks a confirmation that names its target and has no Undo, a deleted message with replies keeps its thread, a message deleted by an Admin always leaves a line that says so, and its author is told. The server refuses what the rules refuse, whatever the screen shows, and every open client shows the change through two new events.

## Scope
- In:
  - Two routes in the `messages` module: edit and delete (R1 to R9), their locks, their refusals and the one new error code.
  - The two changes `message_edited` and `message_deleted` that the routes give to `recordChange`, the two event kinds that the event handler of TOKI-038 writes for them, and the data the changes carry for the work that follows (R10, R11).
  - The key `deletedBy` of `MessageEntry` and the shared schemas of the two requests (R8).
  - The seam that removes the files of a deleted message (R12).
  - In `client-core`: two methods of the API client, the freshness rule that decides which copy of a message wins, the handlers of the two events, and the actions that the screens call (R13 to R15).
  - In `apps/web`: the Edit and Delete entries of the message menu, the in-place edit field, the "(edited)" label, the delete confirmation through `ConfirmAction`, the lines that stand in place of a deleted message, and the rule that says what the list draws (R16 to R21).
  - The texts, in English and Vietnamese (R22), and the tests (Testing).
- Out (not now), with the owner:
  - The tables and the columns: TOKI-036. This ticket adds no table, no column and no migration (R9). What it needs from them is in Design, "Lines with TOKI-036, TOKI-037 and TOKI-038".
  - The send route, the history route and the shape of `MessageEntry`: TOKI-037. This ticket adds one key to the entry (R8) and one rule to the text checks (R3). TOKI-037 names this ticket as the owner of edit and delete (its Scope).
  - The gateway, the numbering of events and who hears which event: TOKI-038. This ticket adds two rows to its registry and its event handler (R10).
  - The sync engine, the windows of messages and the registration of event handlers: TOKI-040. This ticket adds one method to the engine (R14), as a plan change.
  - The message list and what it virtualises: TOKI-041. The message row and the grey system lines: TOKI-042. This ticket gives them the pieces of R19 to R21; where the row places them is TOKI-042's.
  - The composer, its key rules, its auto-grow field and its length limit: TOKI-043. The edit field reuses them (R17); it is not a second composer.
  - Threads, the reply count, the thread panel and replies to a deleted root: TOKI-045. A reply is edited and deleted with the same routes (R1, R5), because a reply is a message of the table; its window and its count are TOKI-045's (R15, concern 4).
  - Reactions on an edited or deleted message: TOKI-046. Pins and the Pinned tab, including the rule that a deleted message leaves the tab: TOKI-048 (D-166). Mentions and the notification they cause: TOKI-050. Text formatting: TOKI-049. Files, their tables and their storage: TOKI-058 (R12). The Search column: TOKI-069. Activity items, including "message deleted by an admin" and the removal of an item whose source is deleted or whose mention was edited out: TOKI-065.
  - The local "Delete" of a message that shows "Not sent" asks nothing and is not a delete of a stored message: TOKI-040 and TOKI-042 (D-087, TOKI-093 `deleteUnsentMessage`).
  - The offline banner and the blocking of writes as a whole: TOKI-082. This ticket only calls the gate of TOKI-093 (R18).
  - A history of edits, and edit of a message by an Admin: not in phase 1 (F16, F42).

## Requirements
Edit
- R1. `PATCH /api/v1/conversations/{id}/messages/{messageId}` takes `{ "text": string }` and answers 200 with `{ "message": MessageEntry, "changed": boolean }`. It needs a valid session (401). The steps and their order are those of TOKI-037 R1, for this route: (1) the schemas of the path and the body (400 `validation_failed`; the body is strict, so a key other than `text` is 400); (2) `getConversationFacts(db, actor.id, id)`: `null`, or a private channel, DM or group of which the actor is not a member, is 404 `not_found`; (3) `canReadConversation`: a person who is not a member of a public channel gets 403 `forbidden`; (4) the locks of R9, after which the facts are read again; (5) inside the locks, the lookup of the message (R2) and `canEditMessage`; (6) the update (R3, R4). The actor of steps 1 to 3 is the account of the session; no route compares a role. Check: a table test sends each case and checks status and code; a request with no session is 401 with no read of a message; the statement log shows the lock statements before the lookup of the message and the lookup before the update.
- R2. Who may edit, and which message. The message is the row of `messageId` in the conversation of the path with `kind` `user`. No row, a row of another conversation and a system line are each 404 `not_found` with the same body as for an unknown conversation. For a user message, `canEditMessage(actor, conv, { authorId })` decides (TOKI-027 R7): only the author, who is a member of a conversation that is not archived, a Member and an Admin alike. `not_author` is 403 `forbidden`, also for an Admin (F42: an Admin never edits another person's message); `not_a_member` of a public channel is 403 and of a private one 404 (step 2 of R1 has answered it); `channel_archived` is 409 `channel_archived`; `fact_missing` and `unknown_role` are 500 `internal_error`, logged at error level (TOKI-037 R2). A message with `deleted_at` set is 409 `message_deleted` (a new code, R8), the same for the author and for anyone else who passed the permission check; a person who failed it gets the refusal of the check, so that the deleted state is not told to a person who may not edit. The error body holds no text of a message. Check: a table test with the author, another Member and an Admin who is not the author, in a public channel, a private channel, a DM and a group, each as member and as non-member, in an archived and a live channel; each refusal leaves the row unchanged; the author succeeds in each kind (a positive case, so a loader that always refuses fails); a system line, an unknown id and an id of another conversation are 404; an edit of a deleted message by its author is 409 `message_deleted`; the stubbed `fact_missing` and `unknown_role` are 500.
- R3. The text of an edit follows the rules of the text of a send, which TOKI-037 R4 states and exports as one schema: 1 to `MESSAGE_TEXT_MAX` (10,000) code points counted with `Array.from(text).length`, not only white space (what `trim` removes), no U+0000 and no lone surrogate, stored as sent with no trimming and no change of line breaks. This ticket imports that schema and writes no second copy. A text that fails is 400 `validation_failed` and nothing is stored. When the text is equal to the stored text, nothing is written, `edited_at` does not change, `recordChange` is not called, and the answer is 200 with the stored message and `changed` `false`; the compare is on the exact string. Decided in this spec (concern 8). When TOKI-058 lets a message carry files and no text, it changes the rule of the text for both routes in its own change (concern 6). Check: each text case of TOKI-037 R4 on this route with the same expected result; the same text sent twice leaves `edited_at` as the first edit set it and calls `recordChange` zero times the second time; a text that differs only by a trailing space is a change.
- R4. What an edit writes. An edit of a message that passes R1 to R3 updates `body` and `edited_at` of that row and no other column: `edited_at` is read once from the clock of TOKI-008 for the request, so the client sends no time. There is no time limit: a message of any age is edited (D-013, FR-11, AC-030). No earlier text is kept anywhere (F16: no edit history). The thread, the replies, the reactions, the pin, the saved copies in Later, the file links and `created_at` are not touched, so the message keeps its place in the list. A second edit sets `edited_at` again (it is the time of the last edit, TOKI-036 R6). Editing a reply, or a message sent with "Also send to channel", edits the one row, so both places change (D-160). Check: a test edits a message made one year earlier with the controllable clock and finds the new text and `edited_at` equal to the clock; a row-by-row compare finds every other column and every other row unchanged; a message with a reply, a pin row and a reaction row inserted by SQL keeps them; a second edit moves `edited_at`; an edit of a reply leaves its parent unchanged.

Delete
- R5. `DELETE /api/v1/conversations/{id}/messages/{messageId}` takes no body (a body is ignored; a query string key is 400) and answers 200 with `{ "message": MessageEntry }`, the row as it stands after the delete. The steps are those of R1 with the deletion in step 6, and `canDeleteMessage(actor, conv, { authorId })` in step 5 in place of `canEditMessage`. The `Origin` rule and the media-type rule of TOKI-022 (R45, R46) cover this route and R1's: a foreign `Origin` is 403 `origin_not_allowed`. Check: the same table test as R2 for this route; a foreign `Origin` is 403; a request with a session and a good id is 200 for the author.
- R6. Who may delete. `canDeleteMessage` allows the author (`as: "author"`) and an Admin who is a member of the conversation (`as: "admin"`; an Admin who deletes their own message is the author) (TOKI-027 R7, BR-24, BR-26). A Member who is not the author gets `not_author`, 403 `forbidden` (TS-054). An Admin who is not a member of a private channel, a DM or a group gets 404 and has no extra right (D-047, TS-053); an Admin who is not a member of a public channel gets 403. An archived conversation is 409 `channel_archived` for the author and for an Admin (TOKI-027 concern 3). The function already allows an Admin in a DM or a group they belong to, as the capability table reads (TOKI-027 concern 2); this ticket follows it. A message that is a system line, or is in no row of the conversation, is 404 as in R2. Check: a table test with Mai (Member, author), Sam (Member), Minh (Admin, member) and Lan (Admin, not a member), in a public channel, a private channel, a DM and a group; Sam deleting Mai's message is 403 and the row is unchanged (TS-054); Lan gets 404 in the private channel with no row changed and 403 in the public one (TS-053); Minh deleting Mai's message is 200 with `deletedBy` `"admin"`; Mai deleting her own is 200 with `"author"`; Minh deleting his own is `"author"`; the archived channel gives 409 for Mai and for Minh.
- R7. What a delete writes. A delete that passes R5 and R6 updates the row: `deleted_at` is the clock time of the request, `deleted_by` is the actor, and `body` becomes the empty string (decided in this spec, concern 5). The row is never removed (TOKI-036 R8), its place never changes, and its thread, replies, reactions, pin row and file links are not removed by this route: what each does with a deleted message is the owner's (Design, "What each neighbour does"). A delete of a message that is already deleted writes nothing, calls `recordChange` zero times and answers 200 with the stored message as it stands, whoever the actor is among those who pass R6; so an Admin who deletes a message that its author deleted a moment before does not replace `deleted_by` (concern 8). History keeps returning the row as TOKI-037 R14 says, in its place, with `text` `null`. Check: after a delete the row has `deleted_at` equal to the clock, `deleted_by` equal to the actor and an empty `body`, and every other column is as before; a message with 3 replies keeps the 3 replies, their text and their `parent_id`; the history of the conversation still lists the row, in its place, with `text` `null`, and a page `around` it works; a second delete by the author, and one by an Admin after the author's, answer 200, change nothing and leave `deleted_by` as the first set it; an Admin's delete of a message with no reply still writes the row (the line is the client's, R19).

Entry, events, seams
- R8. In `packages/shared/src/messages/`: `MessageEntry` of TOKI-037 R15 gains the key `deletedBy`, `null` for a message that is not deleted and `"author"` or `"admin"` for one that is, `"admin"` only when `deleted_by` differs from `author_id`; the entry carries no person id of the deleter, because the line shows no name (D-049) and the author is told who by the Activity item (R11). `MessageEntry` stays one strict object, and the tests of TOKI-037 R15 are updated with the key. The package exports the schemas of the two requests and the two responses, and the registry of error codes gains `message_deleted` (409). `text` is `null` for a deleted message (TOKI-037 R15), so no response and no event of this ticket holds the text of a deleted message. Check: a schema test accepts an edited message, a deleted-by-author and a deleted-by-admin entry and refuses `deletedBy` `"other"`, a deleted entry with a text and an entry with `deletedAt` set and `deletedBy` `null`; the registry holds the code once; every 200 body of the route tests is parsed with the response schema.
- R9. Transaction and locks. Each route runs in one transaction in the order of TOKI-024 R39, as TOKI-037 R8 does: (1) `getAccountsByIds(tx, [actor.id], { lock: "share" })`, so a deactivated actor gets 401 `account_deactivated`; (2) the conversation row `FOR SHARE`, so that an archive and a leave, which take `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` (TOKI-029 R15), cannot run between the check and the update, and edits and deletes of one channel do not wait on each other; (3) the facts are read again and `canReadConversation` is checked again; (4) the message row `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, which serialises two edits or an edit and a delete of one message and does not conflict with the `FOR KEY SHARE` that a reply insert takes on its parent (decided in this spec, concern 9); (5) the lookup, the permission function and the update; (6) `recordChange` is the last statement (R10). Two edits of one message at the same time both succeed, one after the other, and the last to commit stands (D-121); an edit that waits behind a delete is 409 `message_deleted`; a delete that waits behind an edit deletes the edited row. This ticket adds no table, no column and no migration, and writes no row except `messages`. Check with two real connections, the second waiting and the result checked after the first commits, never by sleeping: two edits with two texts leave one of the two and `edited_at` of the later; an edit and a delete leave a deleted row, and the edit answers 409 when the delete came first; an edit and an archive end with the edit stored in the live channel or refused 409; a reply insert into the message while a delete holds its row finishes with no deadlock (SQLSTATE `40P01` never); a person deactivated while the request waits gets 401; the isolation level read back inside the transaction is `read committed`; the migration folder is unchanged and the row counts of every table other than `messages` are unchanged (with a recording handler `events` and `event_counters` too).
- R10. The events. A route that wrote a row calls `recordChange(tx, change)` once, as the last statement of its transaction, after every lock and the update, with `type` `message_edited` or `message_deleted`, `actorId` the actor, `conversationId`, `userIds` empty, `at` the time written to the row and `message` the `MessageEntry` of the row after the update (the function of `messages` that TOKI-037 R15 uses to make an entry). `Change` of `conversations/changes.ts` gains the two types and the optional fields of R11. A repeat of R7, an unchanged edit (R3) and every refusal call it zero times. This ticket writes no event: the event handler of TOKI-038 writes `message.edited` and `message.deleted`, scope `conversation`, payload `{ message }`, one event for each call; this ticket adds the two kinds and their schemas to the registry of `packages/shared/src/realtime/` and the two cases and the two rows of `kinds.ts` (no change of any map) to the handler, which is a small change to code of TOKI-038 (Plan changes). The line handler of TOKI-037 R16 does nothing for these two types, and the rule of TOKI-038 that writes an extra `message.created` for a message that a change "did not bring itself" does not apply to them: their message is their own and they write only their own event. The gateway sends each event to the people whose map holds the conversation, after the commit, in number order (TOKI-038 R13 to R15), so the author's other tabs and every member hear it, and a person outside the conversation never does. Check, with a recording handler as the only handler: one call per written row with the fields above and a `message` equal to the 200 body; none for a repeat, an unchanged edit and a refusal; a statement log shows no write after the call; a handler that throws leaves the old row and the answer is 500; with the real handler (an integration test of the two together): the edit of a message in a channel with three connected members and an Admin outside gives one `message.edited` frame to each of the three, none to the outsider, and a payload equal to the 200 body; the delete gives one `message.deleted` whose `message.text` is `null`; the registry test finds the two kinds, each with a row in `kinds.ts`.
- R11. What the changes carry for the work that follows. `message_edited` carries `previousText`, the text before the edit, and `message_deleted` carries `authorId` (the author of the row), `deletedAs` (`"author"` or `"admin"`) and `deletedById` (the actor). These fields are for handlers that run in the same transaction, before the event handler; they are not stored and not in any event payload or response, so no earlier text is kept (F16) and the identity of an Admin is told only where TOKI-065 chooses to tell it. This ticket registers no handler for them. The owners, each in its own change: TOKI-050 and TOKI-065 compare `previousText` with the new text, notify a person newly mentioned and remove the earlier item of a person whose mention was edited out (F16, F13; the actor is never notified, D-071); TOKI-065 creates the item "message deleted by an admin" for the author when `deletedAs` is `"admin"` (F42, D-049) and removes the Activity items whose source is the deleted message (F13); TOKI-048 takes the message out of the Pinned tab when `message_deleted` arrives (D-166); TOKI-069 updates and clears its search entry; TOKI-067 keeps the count and the rows of Later in step when a saved message is deleted. Any of these that takes a lock goes before the event handler (TOKI-036 R18). Check: a recording handler receives `previousText` for an edit and `authorId`, `deletedAs`, `deletedById` for a delete, equal to the values of the test; the 200 body and the event payload of each have none of the three keys (a schema test); the test of R10 finds the same change in the same transaction.
- R12. The files of a deleted message. TOKI-058 owns the tables of files and the storage, and does not exist before this ticket, so this ticket cannot remove a file; the requirement of D-043 and AC-033 (after a delete the files can no longer be opened and are not in Files) is split as follows, decided in this spec (concern 6). This ticket makes the delete a change that a handler can act on inside its transaction (R10, R11: `message_deleted` with the message id and the conversation) and states the rule; TOKI-058 registers a change handler before the event handler that, for `message_deleted`, removes the links of the message's files so that the signed addresses are refused and the files are not listed in Files, and queues the removal of the stored objects through the job queue of TOKI-064; the test of AC-033 and TS-051 is TOKI-058's. This ticket's own test proves the seam: a recording handler registered before the event handler receives the change of a delete inside the delete's transaction and can write a row that is rolled back with it. Check: that test; a handler that throws rolls the delete back and the answer is 500.

Client
- R13. Two methods of `createApiClient` (TOKI-032 R9 and TOKI-040 R2, same rules: one request core, 10 seconds, no retry, a path part encoded, an optional `signal` last, neither `headers` nor `timeoutMs`). `api.messages.edit(conversationId, messageId, text, signal?)` sends the `PATCH` of R1 with the body `{ text }` and returns `{ message, changed }`; `api.messages.remove(conversationId, messageId, signal?)` sends the `DELETE` of R5 with no body and returns `{ message }`. Both read the answer with the response schemas of R8 in the form that drops unknown keys (TOKI-032 R3). A failed request ends as an `ApiError` of the existing kinds. Neither request is queued, kept in the browser or retried: they are not messages that wait (D-079, D-097; F11 gives the waiting state to sending only). Check: with the fake `fetch` of the existing tests, each method sends the method, path and body as written, parses a good answer and keeps an unknown key of the answer; an error answer ends as an `ApiError`; a timeout (the injected timers fire the 10-second timer) ends as `timeout` and sends nothing again.
- R14. Which copy of a message wins. `client-core` exports `isFresher(held, incoming)` (`packages/client-core/src/messages/freshness.ts`), a pure function that says whether `incoming` may replace `held`, which are two `MessageEntry` of one id: never when `held.deletedAt` is set (a deleted message does not come back); always when `incoming.deletedAt` is set and `held.deletedAt` is not; otherwise when `Date.parse(incoming.editedAt)` is not earlier than `Date.parse(held.editedAt)`, a `null` counting as earlier than any time, so an event that arrives after a newer answer, or twice, cannot put back an older text. The engine of TOKI-040 gains one public method `applyMessage(conversationId, message)`, which replaces the held message of that id in the window of that conversation when `isFresher` allows it and does nothing when the message is not held (the same effect as `patchMessage` of its handler tools, R18 there, and like `setLastMessageAt` it is for a caller that has no event, here the answer of the person's own request); it replaces the window by a new object and calls the listener of that conversation once, and not at all when nothing changed. This is a plan change to TOKI-040 (Plan changes). Check: a table test of `isFresher` over (held, incoming) pairs: a deleted held is never replaced, a deleted incoming always replaces an undeleted held, a later `editedAt` replaces an earlier one, an earlier or equal one does not replace a later one, `null` is the oldest; `applyMessage` on a held message replaces it and calls the listener once, on an unheld id and on a refused replacement changes nothing and calls no listener; the window of another conversation is `===` before and after.
- R15. Events and answers reach the windows. `client-core` exports `createMessageActions({ api, sync })` (`packages/client-core/src/messages/actions.ts`), built once in `apps/web/src/client.ts` after `sync`, which does two things. It registers, through `sync.registerEventHandler` (TOKI-040 R18), a handler for `message.edited` and one for `message.deleted`; each parses `data.message` and calls `tools.patchMessage` with `isFresher` (R14) on the window of its conversation, so a message that is held is replaced and one that is not held is not fetched and not placed; a handler that finds a message it holds only as a waiting item changes nothing. And it returns `{ edit(conversationId, messageId, text), remove(conversationId, messageId) }`, each calling the method of R13 and resolving, never rejecting, with `{ ok: true, message, changed }` or `{ ok: true, message }`, or `{ ok: false, error }` where `error` is the `ApiError`; on success each calls `sync.applyMessage` with the answer, so the screen of the person who acted is right before the event arrives and the event, which is then not fresher, changes nothing. The windows of threads belong to TOKI-045, which registers its own handler for the two kinds for a reply that its window holds and uses `isFresher` (Plan changes). Check: a `message.edited` event for a held message replaces it and for an unheld one changes nothing and makes no request; a `message.deleted` replaces a held message by the deleted entry; the same event twice, and a `message.edited` that arrives after a later answer, leave the window `===` after the first change; `edit` with a good answer calls `applyMessage` once and the later event changes nothing; a failed `edit` returns `{ ok: false, error }` and changes no window; the number of `api` calls of an event is zero.
- R16. The line with the other screens. The pieces this ticket builds are in `apps/web/src/features/message-actions/` (Design, "Layout"); the row that shows them is TOKI-042's and the list is TOKI-041's. This ticket exports: `useMessageActions(message, conversation)` (R17), `MessageEditField` (R18), `EditedLabel` (R19), `DeletedMessageLine` and `deletedDisplay` (R20), `DeleteMessageConfirm` (R21). Check: a type test imports each name; the folder imports no other feature's file and `pnpm lint:boundaries` exits 0.

Screens
- R17. The entries and who sees them. `useMessageActions(message, conversation)` returns `{ canEdit, canDelete, deleteAs, startEdit, requestDelete, editing }`, where `canEdit` and `canDelete` come from the shared `canEditMessage` and `canDeleteMessage` with the signed-in person and the facts of the held conversation entry (kind, general, archived, member), and are `false` for a system line, for a message with `deletedAt` set, and for a waiting or failed item of TOKI-040 (they are not stored messages). The Edit entry is in the "More" menu of the author's message only, and the Delete entry is in the menu of the author's message and, for an Admin who is a member of the conversation, of another person's message (F16, F42); neither is shown when the function refuses, an archived conversation included. The server decides (R2, R6): the entries are a convenience, and a menu that was open when the person lost the right gets the refusal as an error (R18, R21). Only one message is edited at a time in a window: `startEdit` on another message ends the first without saving, as the prototype does (one `S.editing`). Check: a component test over the cases of R2 and R6 for the menu entries (author, other Member, Admin member, Admin not a member, archived, system line, deleted, a pending item), each with the expected set of entries; starting a second edit ends the first and keeps its message unchanged.
- R18. Edit in place (F16). Choosing Edit turns the text of the message into a field in place, with Save and Cancel, filled with the current text, focused with the cursor at the end, and growing with its text. Enter saves, Shift+Enter makes a new line, and Esc cancels with no question; neither Enter nor Esc acts while a word is being composed (the key rule and the auto-grow field are those of TOKI-043, which exports them; they are imported here, not rewritten). The length limit is the composer's (`MESSAGE_TEXT_MAX`, TOKI-043). Save: (1) when the text, compared as `edit` would, is only white space, nothing is sent, the field closes and the message is unchanged, as the prototype does (its `saveedit` ignores an empty text) (decided in this spec, concern 12); (2) when it equals the current text, nothing is sent and the field closes with no "(edited)" (R3); (3) otherwise `ensureOnline()` of TOKI-093: when it returns `false` nothing is sent, the field stays open with its text and the explanation toast of the gate is shown (D-079, F11: edits are locked offline); (4) otherwise `edit` of R15 runs, and while it runs the field and both buttons are `disabled` (D-111) and the press cannot be repeated. On `ok` the field closes and focus returns to the message's "More" button. On any error the field stays open with the person's text and one plain toast `common.error.actionFailed` is shown, so a text is never lost; the person presses Cancel to leave (decided in this spec, concern 12; D-131's mapping of 409 to a fresh copy applies to the message in the window, which the event or the next load brings, and the shared page for 403 and 404 is TOKI-082's). The field is not guarded by the unsaved-form dialog (TOKI-093 concern 6, F16: Esc cancels). The field has the accessible name "Edit message" (the prototype's `aria-label`). Check: a component test in jsdom with `user-event`: Edit shows the field with the text, focused, cursor at the end; Enter calls `edit` once with the text; Shift+Enter adds a line and sends nothing; Esc closes with no call; Enter and Esc during a composition (`compositionstart` before, `compositionend` after) do nothing; Save on white space makes no call and closes; Save on the same text makes no call and closes; with `setConnectionCheck(false)` no call is made, the field stays and the gate's toast shows; while a deferred `edit` runs the field and buttons are `disabled` and a second press makes no second call; a good answer closes the field and focus is on the menu button; each failing answer (500, 409 `message_deleted`, 403) keeps the field open with the text and shows the toast; the accessible name is found by role.
- R19. The "(edited)" label. `EditedLabel` is drawn beside the time of the message when `editedAt` is not `null`, and not otherwise; it holds the text of the key `messageActions.edited` and no time (the log gives a label only; the edit time is not shown). It appears for everyone at once through the event (R15) and survives a reload because `editedAt` is in the history (TOKI-037 R15). ## Design
### API
| Method | Path | Who | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| PATCH | `/api/v1/conversations/{id}/messages/{messageId}` | the author, a member, not archived | Edit the text; 200 `{ message, changed }` (R1 to R4) |
| DELETE | `/api/v1/conversations/{id}/messages/{messageId}` | the author; an Admin who is a member | Soft delete; 200 `{ message }` (R5 to R7) |

| Status | `error.code` | When |
|---|---|---|
| 200 | none | Stored; also an unchanged edit (`changed` false) and a repeated delete (R3, R7) |
| 400 | `validation_failed` | Path or body fails its schema; the text rules of R3 |
| 401 | as TOKI-022 | No valid session; a deactivated actor (`account_deactivated`) |
| 403 | `forbidden` | Not the author (edit), not the author and not an Admin member (delete), or not a member of a public channel |
| 403 | `origin_not_allowed` | A foreign `Origin` |
| 404 | `not_found` | Unknown conversation; a private conversation the person is not in; no such user message in the conversation |
| 409 | `channel_archived` | The conversation is an archived channel |
| 409 | `message_deleted` | An edit of a message that is deleted |
| 500 | `internal_error` | A server failure, `fact_missing` or `unknown_role` |

A 409 means the screen was out of date (D-131).

### Lines with TOKI-036, TOKI-037 and TOKI-038
- TOKI-036: `body`, `edited_at`, `deleted_at` and `deleted_by` are the editable columns and the immutable-columns trigger leaves them open (R7 there); `messages_deleted_check` wants `deleted_at` and `deleted_by` set together, and `body` may be the empty string (R4 there), so a deleted row with an empty body passes every check. The delete is an `UPDATE`, because a trigger refuses every `DELETE`.
- TOKI-037: the routes live in its module and use its row-to-entry function, its text schema and its lock helper; the entry already carries `editedAt` and `deletedAt` and `text` `null` for a deleted row (R15), and history already returns a deleted row in place (R14). A repeat of a send that arrives after an edit or a delete returns the row as it stands (R5 there), which this ticket makes true.
- TOKI-038: the two new kinds are added to its registry in `packages/shared/src/realtime/` and its event handler gets two cases; the handler of the system line does nothing for them. Its rule that a change which carries a `message` it did not bring itself also writes a `message.created` is for the changes that the line handler served, and is stated here not to apply (R10).
- TOKI-040: the handlers use `registerEventHandler` and `patchMessage`; `applyMessage` is the one addition (R14). `message.created` never replaces a held message (R4 there), so a replayed creation cannot undo an edit; `isFresher` makes the two new events safe to replay in the same way.

### What each neighbour does with a deleted or edited message
This is the line of ownership, stated once. This ticket gives each of them the change (R10, R11) and the entry (R8) and writes none of their code.

| Neighbour | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| TOKI-045 threads | Keeps the thread of a deleted root; accepts a reply under a deleted root; owns `replyCount` and the window of a thread; handles the two events for a reply it holds | D-013, D-096, AC-032 |
| TOKI-046 reactions | Owns the rule for a reaction on a deleted message (none is added) and the rows of an edited one (kept) | none in the log: its own |
| TOKI-048 pins | A deleted message leaves the Pinned tab; an edited one stays pinned with its new text | D-166 |
| TOKI-050 mentions, TOKI-065 Activity | Notify the person newly mentioned by an edit; remove the earlier item of a mention edited out; remove the items whose source is deleted; tell the author of an Admin's delete | F16, F13, F42, D-049, D-071 |
| TOKI-058 files | Makes the files of a deleted message unreachable and removes them from storage and Files | D-043, AC-033 |
| TOKI-067 Later | Keeps the count and the rows in step when a saved message is deleted | the Later requirement: a listed item's message is deleted, count and rows still agree |
| TOKI-069 search | Updates its entry for an edit and removes it for a delete | none in the log: its own |

An Admin may delete another person's message in a 1:1 DM or a group that they belong to, because the capability table says "where a member" and the permission function follows it (TOKI-027 concern 2); an Admin does not read or delete in a conversation they are not in (D-047).

### Decided in this spec
These are technical choices that the Blueprint leaves to the builder.
- The routes are `PATCH` and `DELETE` of `/conversations/{id}/messages/{messageId}`, under the conversation as the send and history routes are, so the conversation facts and the membership rules are the same steps.
- An edit of an unchanged text writes nothing; a repeated delete answers 200 and writes nothing (concern 8).
- A delete empties `body` (concern 5).
- The entry says `deletedBy` as `"author"` or `"admin"`, not a person (R8).
- The files are removed by TOKI-058's handler, not here (concern 6).
- The row lock is `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` (concern 9).
- One freshness rule, `isFresher`, for events, answers and TOKI-045's windows (R14, concern 11).
- Focus after a delete goes to the list region (concern 10).

## Areas of concern
1. The wording of the edit and delete screens. Not decided. The prototype contains "Edit", "Delete", "Edit message", "Save", "Cancel", "(edited)", "Delete this message?" and the toast "Message deleted". It does not contain the grey lines "Message deleted" and "Message deleted by an admin", the Admin confirmation text, the text for a message with no text, or any Vietnamese text. The log quotes the two lines (D-013, D-049, D-096, AC-032, AC-034), and D-139 says that a wording in quotes that the prototype lacks is a proposal. Proposal, not a decision: the English texts of R22, and the Vietnamese texts written by the builder and reviewed by TOKI-080. Changing a word changes a catalogue entry and no code. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
2. A dialog or the line under the message for the delete confirmation. Not decided. Journey F16 and the prototype (line 1369) draw a line under the message, "Delete this message?" with Delete and Cancel; D-069 and AC-078 say a confirmation that names its target, not how it is drawn; the merged TOKI-093 builds only a dialog and forbids a call site to use `ConfirmDialog` directly (its R11 (d)). Proposal, not a decision: the dialog of R21, because the helper is the only way a call site can offer a confirmation and a dialog traps focus and works the same on a phone; if the owner wants the inline line, only R21 and its test change, and TOKI-093 gets a second form. The build of R21 uses the dialog until this is answered. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
3. Editing or deleting one's own message in a direct message whose other person was deactivated. Not decided. F25 and F41 replace the composer by "This account has been deactivated" and TOKI-037 R7 refuses a send; nothing says whether the existing messages may still be edited or deleted. The permission functions do not know the case, so R2 and R6 allow it, which is the build until it is answered. Proposal, not a decision: refuse both with 409 `conversation_read_only` as a send is refused, so that a read-only conversation changes in no way; the refusal is one more step in R1 and R5 and one more case in their tables. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
4. What "has replies" counts for a deleted message. Not decided. F16 says a message with replies becomes a "Message deleted" line and a message with none disappears. Nothing says what happens when every reply of a deleted root is deleted afterwards: the line stays if deleted replies count, and disappears if they do not. The count is TOKI-045's key; R20 takes a number from it. Proposal, not a decision: the count holds only replies that are not deleted, so a thread that has nothing readable left stops showing the line. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
5. What a delete does to `body`. Decided in this spec: the text is emptied in the same update. Reason: the person asked for the message to be gone, no feature reads the text of a deleted message (the entry gives `text` `null`, TOKI-037 R15, and Activity, Later and search drop their items), an edit keeps no earlier text (F16), and the table allows an empty body (TOKI-036 R4); keeping the text would leave in the database what the product says is deleted. The cost: an Admin's delete cannot be undone or audited from the table; if the owner wants an audit copy it is a new column and a migration. TOKI-036 R6 leaves this to this ticket. Owner: Thanh Truong.
6. Which ticket removes the files of a deleted message. Decided in this spec: TOKI-058, through a change handler registered before the event handler; this ticket ships the delete as a change and the seam test (R12). Reason: the tables and the storage of files do not exist until TOKI-058, which needs only TOKI-036, so this ticket cannot test a removal and TOKI-058 can. The plan line of TOKI-047 names the files; its "files leave storage" part is carried out by TOKI-058 and tested by AC-033 there. The other way, making this ticket need TOKI-058, would delay it behind the largest ticket of M4. Owner: Thanh Truong.
7. The entry and the events of an edit and a delete. Decided in this spec: `deletedBy` is `"author"`, `"admin"` or `null` and carries no person (R8); the events are `message.edited` and `message.deleted` with `{ message }`, the same shape as the list, so one parser serves both (R10); the earlier text and the identity of the deleter travel only on the change, inside the transaction (R11). Reason: the line shows no name (D-049), a payload that held the earlier text would copy to every client what F16 says is not kept, and TOKI-050 and TOKI-065 need the earlier text only on the server. Owner: Thanh Truong.
8. Repeats. Decided in this spec: an unchanged edit answers 200 with `changed` `false` and writes nothing; a delete of a deleted message answers 200 and writes nothing, so `deleted_by` stays the first deleter. Reason: a double press and a retry must be safe (D-111, D-132) and must not show "(edited)" for no change or turn an author's delete into an Admin's line. The permission check runs before this answer, so a person who may not act still gets the refusal. Owner: Thanh Truong.
9. The locks. Decided in this spec: the account `FOR SHARE`, the conversation row `FOR SHARE`, then the message row `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` (R9). Reason: `FOR SHARE` on the conversation row keeps an archive and a leave out of the window between the check and the update (TOKI-037 R8); `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` on the message row serialises two writes of one message and does not conflict with the `FOR KEY SHARE` that a reply insert takes on its parent, so a delete never deadlocks with a reply; the event handler's counter lock stays last (TOKI-036 R18). This rests on the lock-mode table of PostgreSQL; the two-connection tests prove it when built. Owner: Thanh Truong.
10. Where focus goes after a delete. Decided in this spec: to the list region of the conversation that held the message. Reason: the message and its menu are gone or changed into a line, and TOKI-014 returns focus only to an opener that is still in the document (TOKI-093 concern 6 leaves this to the call site). Owner: Thanh Truong.
11. Which copy of a message wins. Decided in this spec: `isFresher` (R14): a deleted message is never replaced, a delete always wins over an undeleted copy, and of two edits the later `editedAt` wins; the answer of the person's own request is applied at once and the event that follows changes nothing. Reason: events can arrive twice or after a newer answer (a catch-up, TOKI-040 R13), and `message.created` already never replaces a held message (TOKI-040 R4); D-121 says the last save wins. Owner: Thanh Truong.
12. The edit field. Decided in this spec: one message is edited at a time in a window, and starting another ends the first without saving (the prototype has one editing message); a save of white space sends nothing and closes the field with the message unchanged (the prototype's `saveedit` ignores an empty text); no unsaved-form dialog, because F16 says Esc cancels; on any error the field stays open with the text, so a text is never lost. Reason: each follows the prototype or the journey, and the safe choice where neither speaks. Owner: Thanh Truong.
