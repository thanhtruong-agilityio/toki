# Notification decision engine design

Date: 2026-11-25 · Status: draft for review (concerns 1 to 6 are open for the product owner)
Intent: docs/intents/notification-decision-engine.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-073

## Goal
When something happens that may need a person, Toki must decide, for each person and for that one cause, whether they get a toast, a push notification and a sound. The inputs are spread over earlier work: the level and mute a person set for a conversation (TOKI-029, `conversation_members`), the three switches (TOKI-102), Pause and Focus time (TOKI-076), and what the person's windows are doing. Without one engine every screen would apply its own half, and the rules of D-071, D-072, D-088 and D-103 would drift apart.
This ticket builds that engine on the server, in the module `notifications`: the pure decision, the handler that runs it inside the transaction of the change, the numbered event that tells a person's open screens, the queued push, the record of which windows are in use, the record of what Pause or Focus held, the real answers behind the `reminderNotifier` that Later (TOKI-067) left open, and the toast of a snoozed Activity item that comes back. It also builds the last check that only a screen can make (the window is in use; the conversation or thread is on screen) and the report of whether the window is in use.
It does not draw a toast, play a sound, register a device or send a push (TOKI-075, TOKI-074), and it does not store Pause or Focus (TOKI-076). The lines with each are drawn in "Lines with the neighbouring tickets".

## Scope
- In:
  - The shared names, the notice event and the window frame (R1).
  - The pure decision from its inputs to `{ toast, sound, push, held }` (R2).
  - Who is considered and which causes make a notice (R3, R4).
  - The handler, its place in the list of `recordChange`, and the reads it needs (R5, R6).
  - The event `notification.created` (R7).
  - The push port and the retraction of a push not yet sent (R8, R14).
  - The table of open windows and the frame that fills it (R9).
  - The port for Pause and Focus, the record of held notices and its read and cleanup (R10).
  - The real `reminderNotifier` (R11) and the toast of a snoozed item that came back (R12).
  - The client: the report of the window (R16), the check of a notice against the screen (R17), the notice centre with its waiting list (R18), and the state of what is on screen (R19).
  - Wiring, texts, nothing else (R20 to R23).
- Out (not now):
  - The toast component, its text, its 5-second life, its View button, stacking and the sound player with its Safari unlock: TOKI-075. This ticket hands it the notice and the answer "show" and "play".
  - Web Push: VAPID keys, the service worker, device registration, the browser permission, the card that offers notifications, the words of a system notification and its click: TOKI-074. This ticket decides that a person gets a push and hands over identifiers.
  - Pause and Focus time (the state, the requests, the end-of-period job, the strip, the summary toast and the count it shows): TOKI-076. This ticket gives it the answer a held notice produces and the count of them (R10).
  - Activity items, their rules and their events: TOKI-065. This ticket reads what a change made and never creates an item.
  - Reminders, their timer and their toast: TOKI-067. This ticket gives the push and the "held" answer (R11).
  - The three switches, their storage and their screen: TOKI-102 and TOKI-078. The level and mute of a conversation, their request and their screen: TOKI-029 and TOKI-035.
  - Email about missed mentions (a later phase, D-016) and guests (phase 5).

## Requirements
Shared
- R1. `packages/shared/src/notifications/` (exported from the package index; TOKI-102 adds `preferences.ts` to the same folder) gains `notice.ts` with: `NOTICE_KINDS` equal to `["message", "mention", "back_from_snooze"]` and its type; `NoticeDataSchema`, a strict object `{ kind, conversationId, messageId, parentId, alsoInChannel, actorId, itemId, viaChannel, preview, sound }` (`conversationId`, `messageId`, `parentId`, `actorId`, `itemId` and `preview` are a string or null, `alsoInChannel`, `viaChannel` and `sound` booleans) which is the `data` of the event of R7; `WindowFrameSchema`, a strict object `{ type: "window", inUse: boolean }` (R9); and the numbers `NOTICE_PREVIEW_MAX` (60 code points, the "first 60 characters" of F13) and `NOTICE_TOAST_MAX_AGE_MS` (60,000, a proposal, concern 5). Check: schema tests accept each valid object and refuse an added key, a missing key, a kind outside the list and a non-boolean; a type test imports each name from `@toki/shared`.

The decision
- R2. `decideNotice(input)` in `apps/server/src/modules/notifications/decide.ts` is a pure function (no database, no clock, no random) from `{ kind, viaChannel, level, muted, preferences, held, windowInUse }` to `{ toast, sound, push, held }`, four booleans. `level` is the level that applies to the person for that conversation (the stored one, or `mentions` for a channel and `all` for a direct message or group: `effectiveNotificationLevel` of `@toki/shared`, D-046); `muted` is the person's mute; `preferences` is `NotificationPreferences` of TOKI-102; `held` says Pause or Focus covers this moment (R10); `windowInUse` says one of the person's windows is in use (R9). The rule, in this order:
  1. Whether the kind is allowed. `message`: only when `level` is `all` and `muted` is false. `mention`: only when `level` is not `none` (the level decides, D-103) and, when `muted` is true, only when `preferences.mentionsInMuted` is true and `viaChannel` is false (D-088; the `viaChannel` part is concern 4). `back_from_snooze` and `reminder` (R11) read neither the level nor the mute, because the person asked for them. A kind that is not allowed gives all four values false and is recorded nowhere.
  2. Hold. When allowed and `held` is true the answer is all false and `held` true (D-072: no toast, push or sound).
  3. Otherwise: `toast` is true for `message`, `mention` and `back_from_snooze` and false for `reminder` (its toast is TOKI-067's, R11); `sound` is `preferences.sound` for `message` and `mention` and false for the other two (concern 3); `push` is `preferences.desktop` and no window in use, and false for `back_from_snooze` (concern 3). `preferences.desktop` and `preferences.sound` are the only places the two switches act, and a toast is never switched off by a preference (concern 9).
  Check: a table test over every combination of the four kinds, the three levels, `muted`, the three switches, `held` and `windowInUse`, written as data and compared with the rows below (it is the executable form of the table in Design); the cases of AC-039 (muted, switch on: a mention notifies; switch off: nothing), the level `none` (a mention gives nothing, also with the switch on and the channel muted, concern 10), 'Mentions only' (a plain message gives nothing, a mention does), 'All messages' (a plain message gives toast, push and sound as the switches allow), and a held mention (all false, `held` true); the function imports nothing and `Date.now(`, `new Date(` and `Math.random` do not occur in it.

- R3. Who is considered. For every cause of R4, a person is considered only when they are not the author or actor of the change (D-071), have a member row in the conversation (D-058, D-135) and have an `active` account (a deactivated person cannot sign in). The rule is that of `selectRecipients` of TOKI-065 R3 and is written again here because `notifications` imports no other module; the two are kept equal by one shared fixture of the people named in TOKI-065 R3 (the actor, a non-member, a person who left, a deactivated person, an active member), run against both. Nobody is considered for a system line (D-100), for a reaction (F15, TOKI-046), for a pin, or for a message that is deleted. A person for whom Activity made no item is still considered for a `message` notice (R4). Check: the fixture returns only the active member for each of the causes; a system line, a reaction and a deleted message give no notice.

- R4. Which causes make a notice. The handler (R5) looks at three kinds of change and nothing else:
  (a) `message_created` of a message of kind `user` that is not deleted. Each person in `change.activity` with an `upsert` effect of kind `mention` and `isNew` true gets a `mention` notice (the item exists, so the person was named: TOKI-065 R4, `viaChannel` from the item). Every other person considered (R3) whose effective level is `all` gets a `message` notice, when the message is in the main list or was also sent to the channel (D-160: "unread count and notification level apply"); a reply that was not also sent to the channel gives no `message` notice (concern 1). A person has at most one notice for one message: the `mention` notice wins.
  (b) `message_edited`: each person in `change.activity` with an `upsert` effect of kind `mention` and `isNew` true (the edit added the mention) gets a `mention` notice; nobody else, and a person who was already told gets nothing (F16: "Editing to add a mention notifies the newly mentioned person").
  (c) `activity_changed` (TOKI-065 R13, R21) with an `upsert` effect whose item has `backFromSnooze` true and `isNew` false: a `back_from_snooze` notice for that person (F27: "returns to the top, unread, labelled 'Back from snooze', with a toast").
  Reaction, being added, a changed role, a message deleted by an admin and a thread item (kind `thread`) make no notice: a reaction is explicit (F15, TS-148), the others are concerns 1 and 2, whose proposal is that they stay Activity items only. The table of causes is one constant, `NOTICE_CAUSES`, so that the answer to a concern is one line. Check: with a recording handler and Activity effects as input, each case of (a), (b) and (c) gives the notices named; the effects of a reaction, of `added_to_channel`, `role_changed`, `message_deleted` and `thread` give none; one person never has two notices for one message; an edit that adds @Sam gives Sam one notice and gives Mai, already told, none; a reply that was not also sent to the channel gives no `message` notice to a person at level `all`; a reply also sent to the channel gives one.

The handler and its reads
- R5. `createNotificationHandler(deps)` of `modules/notifications/handler.ts` is a `ChangeHandler` (TOKI-037 R18) that does R2 to R4 for the changes of R4 and nothing for any other type. It sits after the activity handler (TOKI-065 R13, which it reads) and before the event handler (it returns what that handler writes), so the list built by `buildChangeHandlers` is: system line, the handlers that take locks or add jobs, activity, notifications, events. It takes no lock and adds jobs and rows only, so the counter lock stays the last (TOKI-036 R18). It returns `{ notices }`, an array of `NoticeEffect`: `{ userId, workspaceId, data: NoticeData }` for each person with `toast` true. `createRecordChange` is changed once more, in the way TOKI-065 R13 changed it: a handler may return `{ message?, activity?, notices? }`, `Change` gains the optional field `notices`, and the handlers after read it. Whichever of this ticket and TOKI-065 is built second adds its field to the union the first one wrote and says so in its pull request. The handler works in a fixed number of statements, whatever the number of people: one read of candidates (R6), one of accounts (`getAccountsByIds`, injected), one of preferences (`getNotificationPreferencesFor` of TOKI-102 R8), one of the hold (R10), one of the open windows (R9) and only when at least one candidate has `desktop` on, one insert of held notices, and one job insert for each person who gets a push (R8). A handler that finds no notice returns nothing and issues no read after the candidates. Check: the statement count (the wrapper of TOKI-031 R14) is the same for a channel of 5 people and one of 200 at level `all` for everybody, apart from the job inserts; a change of another type issues no statement; a handler that throws rolls the change back; the handler list has the activity handler before this one and the event handler after it.

- R6. One new read of `conversations`, `listNotifyCandidates(client, conversationId, extraUserIds, includeLevelAll)`, exported from its `index.ts` (TOKI-028 R18, the export test is updated). One statement over `conversation_members` joined to its conversation. It returns, for each member row, `{ userId, level, muted }` with `level` the effective level (stored, or the default of the conversation's kind, D-046), for the members whose effective level is `all` when `includeLevelAll` is true, and for the members named in `extraUserIds`; it never returns a person who is not a member. So the cost grows with the people who can be notified and not with the members of a big channel. Check: a channel of 200 where 3 chose `all` and 2 are named returns 5 rows; a direct message returns the other person at level `all` by default; a person with a stored level `none` is returned with `none`; a non-member named in `extraUserIds` is not returned; the statement count is one.

The event
- R7. Each `NoticeEffect` is written by the event handler of TOKI-038 (R20 there, "Rows of later tickets") as one event of kind `notification.created`, scope `user` (the person), no `conversationId`, `data` `NoticeData` of R1, after the other events of the change; the effects of one person are written one each. `preview` is the first `NOTICE_PREVIEW_MAX` code points of the message text for `message` and `mention`, and null for `back_from_snooze` and for an empty text; `sound` is the `sound` of R2; `itemId` is the Activity item for `mention` and `back_from_snooze` and null for `message`; `alsoInChannel` is true when the message of a reply was also sent to the channel. The schema joins the registry of `packages/shared/src/realtime/events.ts`; `kinds.ts` gets a row saying it changes no map; `EventChange` gains the optional field `notices?`. A user event goes to every connection of that person and to nobody else (TOKI-038 R16) and is replayed to that person (TOKI-039). Check, with the real handler and fake sockets: a mention writes one `notification.created` for the mentioned person, received by both tabs of that person and by nobody else; a rolled-back change leaves no event; a person with `toast` false gets none; the payload passes the schema; the registry and `kinds.ts` tests pass.

Push
- R8. The push is handed over, not sent. `PushPort` is `{ enqueue(tx, notice: PushNotice): Promise<void>; cancel(tx, userId, messageId): Promise<void> }` with `PushNotice` `{ userId, workspaceId, kind: "message" | "mention" | "reminder", conversationId, messageId, parentId, actorId, viaChannel, itemId }` (identifiers and booleans only; no text, so a job payload obeys TOKI-064 R7). For each person with `push` true the handler calls `push.enqueue(tx, notice)` inside the transaction of the change, so a rollback leaves no push. A push is one per person and not one per device: TOKI-074 sends it to every registered device of that person whose browser allows it (D-142; the table row "App closed on every device: to every device with notifications on"). Until TOKI-074 gives the real port, `app.ts` and `buildJobDeps` build a stand-in that does nothing, named so in the code. The key TOKI-074 uses for the job is `push:<userId>:<messageId>` (or `push:<userId>:<itemId>` for a reminder), so `cancel` can find it (R14). Check: with a fake port, a mention for a person with `desktop` on and no window in use calls `enqueue` once, with identifiers and no text; a person with `desktop` off, one in use, one held and one at level `none` call it zero times; a transaction that throws after the call leaves no call that was committed (a fake that records per transaction); a type test checks that `PushNotice` holds no string property other than identifiers and the kind.

The windows in use
- R9. Whether a person has Toki in use is stored, because the worker process that fires a reminder has no sockets (TOKI-064 R9, R17). The table `notification_windows` (Drizzle table in `modules/notifications/schema.ts`, one migration `<number>_notification_engine_tables` together with R10, forward-only; not exported from `index.ts`): `connection_id` text primary key (1 to 64 characters), `workspace_id` and `user_id` uuid not null with foreign keys to `workspaces(id)` and `users(id)` written as SQL in the migration (TOKI-019 R22, `ON DELETE RESTRICT`), `in_use` boolean not null, `updated_at` timestamptz not null with no database default; an index on (`user_id`) where `in_use`. The client frame `{ "type": "window", "inUse": boolean }` (R1, R16) is registered with `onClientFrame("window", handler)` of the gateway (TOKI-038 R27; the fourth argument, `connectionId`, is the addition that TOKI-055 R3 asks for, and this ticket needs it too); the handler upserts the row of that connection with the person's workspace (read once with `getAccountsByIds`, injected) and `clock.now()`. `onConnectionClosed` (TOKI-055 R3) deletes the row. When the app starts and registers the listeners it deletes every row, because no connection survives a restart; a client sends its state again at each `ready` (R16). `windowInUse` of R2 is "a row of that person has `in_use` true". The rule is for the person and not for the device: one window in use stops the push for all of the person's devices (the table rows: with the app open and in use there is no push; "App closed on every device" is the case where there is none; concern 8). A frame that fails `WindowFrameSchema` is ignored. Check: a frame of true sets the row, a frame of false sets it false, two connections of one person with one true give `windowInUse` true, closing the true one gives false, a restart empties the table, the frame of another person's connection never changes a person's row, a deactivated person's closed connection leaves no row, the migration check reads columns, keys and index from the catalog, `db:generate` reports no change, and `updated_at` comes from the `TestClock`.

Pause and Focus
- R10. The seam for Pause and Focus time and the record of what it held. (a) `HoldPort` is `{ listHeld(client, userIds, at): Promise<ReadonlySet<string>> }`: the ids among `userIds` for whom Pause or Focus time covers the moment `at` (milliseconds on the clock of TOKI-008), in one statement for any number of people. The owner of the state and of the real port is TOKI-076; until it is built `app.ts` and `buildJobDeps` give a stand-in that holds nobody, named so in the code. This ticket calls it with `clock.now()` and never reads a Pause or Focus table (D-072). (b) A notice that is allowed and held is recorded: the table `held_notices` (same migration): `id` uuid primary key (UUIDv7 default), `workspace_id`, `user_id` (foreign keys as in R9), `kind` (`message`, `mention` or `back_from_snooze`; a check), `conversation_id` (null allowed), `message_id` (null allowed), `created_at` timestamptz not null with no database default, an index on (`user_id`, `created_at`). The handler inserts the rows of one change in one statement, in the transaction of the change. (c) `countHeldNotices(client, userId, fromMs, toMs)` of `modules/notifications`, exported from `index.ts`, returns the number of rows of that person with `created_at` at or after `fromMs` and before `toMs`. TOKI-076 reads it for the summary together with `listRemindersFiredBetween` of Later (TOKI-067 R12): what a held reminder is, is Later's; what every other held notice is, is this table. What the summary counts and how it words it are TOKI-076's. (d) The job type `notifications.prune-held`, scheduled hourly (`{ cron: "0 * * * *" }`, empty payload), deletes rows older than `HELD_NOTICE_KEEP_HOURS` (72, a bound that exceeds the longest Pause, "until tomorrow 08:00", D-072, and is no product rule; concern 13) in batches; it is declared in `apps/server/src/job-types.ts` with its handler in `job-handlers.ts` (TOKI-064 R9). A held notice makes no event and no push, and the Activity item, the badge and the unread count are not touched (D-072: "Badges still update"). Check: with a fake port that holds Lan, a mention gives no event, no push call and one row; Mai, not held, gets the event; a plain message to a held person at level `none` writes no row (it would not have notified); `countHeldNotices` includes the lower bound and excludes the upper, ignores another person's rows and rows of a rolled-back change; the prune job with the `TestClock` removes a row 72 h 1 min old and keeps one 71 h 59 min old; the migration check as in R9.

Reminders and the snooze
- R11. The real `reminderNotifier`. `createReminderNotifier(deps)` of `modules/notifications` returns the `ReminderNotifier` of TOKI-067 R11 (`isHeld(userId, at)`, `notify(input)`), built by `buildJobDeps` in place of the stand-in of that ticket. `isHeld` is `holdPort.listHeld(db, [userId], at)` holding the person. `notify(input)` (called by the fire handler only when the reminder is not held, after its commit) reads the person's preferences and the open windows, applies `decideNotice` with kind `reminder` and, when `push` is true, calls `push.enqueue(db, notice)` with `kind` `reminder`, `itemId` the Later item and no actor. It writes no event and no row: the toast of a reminder is drawn by `LaterHost` from the `later.changed` event, which carries `held` (TOKI-067 R22), and the sound is concern 3. A reminder is not tied to a level or a mute (R2). A failing `enqueue` throws and is logged by the queue (TOKI-067 R8); the reminder stays due. Check: with fakes, a reminder for a person with `desktop` on and no window in use enqueues one push of kind `reminder`; with a window in use, `desktop` off or the person held (`isHeld` true) none; `isHeld` is true only for a held person; the muted conversation of the reminder's message changes nothing.

- R12. The toast of a snoozed item that came back. It is case (c) of R4: the wake job of TOKI-065 (R21) records `activity_changed` with an `upsert` effect, `isNew` false and `backFromSnooze` true; the handler, which runs in the worker with the same list (TOKI-064 R17), makes a `back_from_snooze` notice. It is held by Pause and Focus (R2, R10), is not subject to the level or the mute, plays no sound and sends no push (concern 3). Check: with the `TestClock` and the real wake handler, the event `notification.created` of kind `back_from_snooze` is in the log once, with `itemId` the item; the same wake with the person held writes a row of `held_notices` and no event; an item woken for a muted conversation still notifies; the unsnooze by hand (no `backFromSnooze`) makes none.

Retraction
- R13. What is already sent is not recalled. A push already handed to a device cannot be recalled (the table row "Minh edits out @Lan, or deletes the message"), and the Activity item is removed by TOKI-065. This ticket retracts what it can before it is sent: for the people of a `remove` effect of `change.activity` and the people of `mentionDelta.removed` of a `message_edited`, with the message of the change, the handler calls `push.cancel(tx, userId, messageId)` (R8, `cancelByKey`, which stops only a job that has not started, TOKI-064 R14). A push job that has started, and a push for a person who got a `message` notice for a message that was then deleted, are covered by the send-time check that TOKI-074 owns (it reads the message and the Activity item again and sends nothing for a deleted message or a removed item); this is a hand-off and not a guarantee of this ticket. Check: a mention edited out calls `cancel` for that person and not for others; a delete calls it for each person whose item was removed; an edit that changes no mention calls it for nobody.

The client
- R14. The notice reaches the client. The event `notification.created` is handed, through `registerEventHandler("notification.created", handler)` of the sync engine (TOKI-040 R18), to the notice centre of R18. The engine applies it with no effect on the conversation store. Events replayed after a lost connection are handed the same way and are judged by their age (R17). Check: an event applied twice (a replay with the same number) is judged once (the centre keeps the numbers it has seen); an event of another kind is not handed.

- R15. The fifteen situations. For each of the fifteen rows of the table "When you are mentioned" in Design, the pair of the server's decision (R2, R4, R9, R10) and the screen's judgement (R17) gives the toast, push and sound of that row, and nothing in the engine gives a different result for a row. Check: one scenario test for each row, with Minh's message `@Lan` (`@channel` for row 12, in a thread reply for row 13, edited or deleted for row 14, `@Lan` sent by Lan for row 15), Lan's level, mute, switches, hold and window rows set as the row says, the server side run through the real handler list and the fake ports and the screen side through `evaluateNotice` and the notice centre with a fake view; each asserts the three results of the row, and row 14 also asserts the `cancel` call and the dropped waiting toast.

- R16. The window report. `createWindowReporter({ windowUse })` in `packages/client-core/src/notifications/` returns `{ setSend, attachConnection }`, built like `createActivitySender` of TOKI-055 R13: `setSend(fn | null)` takes the function that returns true when a frame went on an open socket; `attachConnection(connection)` sets it and sends `{ "type": "window", "inUse": windowUse.isInUse() }` each time the connection's status becomes `online` (the frame cannot go before `ready`, TOKI-039 R14) and each time `windowUse.subscribe` reports a change whose value differs from the last one sent; a value equal to the last one sent is not sent twice; `setSend(null)` when the status is `idle` or `stopped`. `WindowUse` is the port of TOKI-053 R21 (`isInUse()`: visible and focused; concern 6); this ticket adds no second source. At most one frame is sent for one change, so the gateway's limit of 50 frames in 10 seconds (TOKI-038 R25) is not reached by focus changes. Check, with a fake `WindowUse` and a fake connection: `online` sends the current value; a change sends once; an equal value sends nothing; `offline` and a new `online` send again; `setSend` returning false queues nothing; no browser global is used in `client-core`.

- R17. The check against the screen, `evaluateNotice(notice, view, nowMs)` in `packages/client-core/src/notifications/evaluate.ts`, is pure. `notice` is the `NoticeData` of R1 with `createdAtMs` (the event's `createdAt`); `view` is `{ inUse, conversationId, threadRootId, atBottom }`. It returns `{ toast: "show" | "wait" | "drop", sound: boolean }`. Definitions: for `message` and `mention`, the notice is on screen in the main list when `view.conversationId` equals the notice's and the message is in the main list (`parentId` null) or was also sent to the channel; it is on screen in a thread when `view.conversationId` equals the notice's, `parentId` is not null and `view.threadRootId` equals it; `back_from_snooze` is never on screen. The toast: `drop` when `nowMs - createdAtMs` is above `NOTICE_TOAST_MAX_AGE_MS` (concern 5); otherwise `wait` while `view.inUse` is false (the table row "Waiting when she returns"); otherwise `drop` when the notice is on screen (F36, row 1 and 2: no toast for what is on the screen); otherwise `show`. The sound: false when `notice.sound` is false; false when the window is in use and the notice is on screen in the main list with `atBottom` true (the message is seen at once, row 1) or on screen in an open thread (concern 1); otherwise true, also when the window is not in use and also when the conversation is on screen but scrolled up (rows 2 and 4). The sound is judged once, when the notice arrives. Check: one test for each of the fifteen rows of the table in Design, written as data and run through the function (the executable form of that table), plus: a reply in a thread whose panel is open drops the toast; a reply with the panel closed shows it; a notice 61 seconds old is dropped and one 59 seconds old is not; `back_from_snooze` in the conversation of its item still shows; the function reads no clock and no global.

## Design
### Where it runs and why
The decision runs on the server, in the transaction of the change that causes it (`recordChange`, TOKI-037 R18), because three of its inputs exist only there: who follows a thread (Activity), what every other person set for the conversation, and Pause and Focus. A decision made in the transaction can never describe a message that rolled back, and its event and its push are ordered with the message's own event. The one input that only a screen knows (is the window in use, which conversation and thread are on screen, is the person at the bottom of the list) is applied last by the screen (R17), because the server would be one network round trip late and would announce what the person is already reading (F36).
Two outputs leave the server. The in-app toast and sound travel as the numbered user event `notification.created` (R7), to every open connection of the person, and each screen judges it for itself. The push is a queued job (R8) for TOKI-074, decided here by the rule "no window of this person is in use" (R9).

### The window frame and the push rule
A client reports `{ type: "window", inUse }` when it connects and each time the window enters or leaves use (R16). The server keeps one row for each open connection (R9). The push rule is for the person: when any of the person's windows is in use, no push is queued for the person at all, because the person is looking at Toki and the toast is the notification; when none is, a push is queued. This reads "App open … Push none" and "App closed on every device … Push to every device" of the table (concern 8). The conversation that is on screen needs no rule of its own on the server: a conversation on screen means a window in use, which already stops the push (F36: "No push arrives while that very conversation is on screen").

### Pause and Focus, and the line with TOKI-076
TOKI-076 owns the state: when Pause or Focus time starts, what it covers, ending, extension, the status, the strip and the summary. This ticket needs one answer from it: which of these people are held at this moment (`HoldPort.listHeld`, R10). It gives back one thing: a row for each notice it held, and the count. A reminder that is held is Later's own state (TOKI-067 R11, concern 3 there): its `held` flag is `isHeld` of R11, and 076 counts it through `listRemindersFiredBetween`. The window between the hold read and the commit of a Pause that starts at that instant is the length of one handler run and is accepted, as in TOKI-067.

### Data model
- `notification_windows` (R9): one row per open connection; `connection_id`, `workspace_id`, `user_id`, `in_use`, `updated_at`.
- `held_notices` (R10): one row per held notice; `id`, `workspace_id`, `user_id`, `kind`, `conversation_id`, `message_id`, `created_at`.
Both in one migration. No other table of another module is read directly: conversations through `listNotifyCandidates`, accounts through `getAccountsByIds`, preferences through the module's own read, the hold through `HoldPort`.

### Events and frames
| What | Kind | Scope or direction | `data` | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a notice for one person | event `notification.created` | user | `NoticeData` | in the transaction of the cause (R7) |
| the window state | client frame `window` | client to server | `{ inUse }` | on `ready` and on change (R16) |

### Errors
- A failure of the handler rolls the whole change back, as every handler does (TOKI-037 R18). A failing `PushPort.enqueue` in the handler is a failure of the change; the real port only inserts a job, so it fails only when the database does.
- A bad `window` frame is ignored; one over 4,096 bytes or beyond the rate closes the connection (TOKI-038 R25).
- A notice that fails its schema on the client is ignored.

## Data safety
- A notice event is for one person only (scope `user`); no other person's connection receives it.
- `preview` is a copy of the first 60 code points of a message in a user event that stays in the log for its retention window (TOKI-036 R20). The `message.created` event of the same message holds the same text for everyone in the conversation, so this adds no new reader; a deleted message's text is emptied (TOKI-047) and a message that is deleted makes no notice.
- Push jobs and held rows hold identifiers only; no log line holds a preview or a value of a switch (R21).
- Whether a person has a window in use is private to the server: no route and no event gives it to another person, and presence (TOKI-055) shows nothing of it.

7. Where the decision runs. Decided in this spec: on the server, in the transaction of the change, with a numbered user event for the toast and sound and a queued push, and a last check on the screen. Reason: the followers, the other people's settings and Pause and Focus are known only to the server; an event written in the transaction cannot describe a rolled-back message; the screen alone knows what it shows. Cost: one user event for each person who may get a toast (for `@channel` in a channel of 200 that is up to 199 more events while the counter lock is held, on top of those of TOKI-065 concern 12; TOKI-089 measures it), and one job insert for each person who gets a push. A live frame sent after the commit would avoid the lock, but the handler has no after-commit seam. Owner: Thanh Truong.
8. The state of the windows, and the push rule for a person. Decided in this spec: the state is stored in a table and is for the person, one window in use stops every push of that person. Reason: the reminder fires in the worker, which has no sockets; the table "App open … in use: Push none" and "App closed on every device: Push to every device" are about the person, not about one device. Cost: a write for each change of a window (a handful a day for one person) and a delete at each close. A phone in the pocket with Toki closed and a laptop in use gets no push; the card of F36 and the settings are TOKI-074's. Owner: Thanh Truong.
9. What the three switches control. Decided in this spec: 'Desktop notifications' controls the push, 'Play a sound' the sound, and the toast is controlled by neither. Reason: the prototype's phone label of the first switch is "Push notifications"; F13 says "A sound plays if sound is on" and gives the toast no condition; TOKI-102 leaves the meaning to this ticket. 'Notify me for @mentions even in muted channels' is the muted exception of R2. Owner: Thanh Truong.
10. How the level and the mute combine. Decided in this spec: both must allow. The level 'Nothing' stops a mention even in a muted channel with the switch on, and Mute stops a plain message even at 'All messages'. Reason: row 7 says 'Nothing' gives none for a mention; D-103 says the level decides; D-088 adds an exception to Mute only. Owner: Thanh Truong.
11. The seam for Pause and Focus, and the record of what was held. Decided in this spec: TOKI-076 owns the state and the real `HoldPort`; this ticket owns the rows of held notices and `countHeldNotices`, kept 72 hours. Reason: only this engine knows what would have notified, and a message in a conversation at 'All messages' has no Activity item to count; the owner of the state is the owner of the time. The summary's count rule and wording are TOKI-076's. Owner: Thanh Truong.
12. Reminders. Decided in this spec: Later keeps the due state, the event and the toast (TOKI-067 R22); this ticket gives `isHeld` and the push (R11). Reason: the toast is drawn from Later's own event, so a second toast event would double it; the push needs the window state and the preferences that live here. Owner: Thanh Truong.
13. Bounds that are not product rules. Decided in this spec: the preview is 60 code points (F13), held rows are kept 72 hours and pruned hourly (R10), and a notice event is one for each person. Reason: a preview and a table need a size; 72 hours exceeds the longest Pause. They are constants of the code and can be changed. Owner: Thanh Truong.
14. Retraction of a push not yet sent. Decided in this spec: this ticket cancels the job of a person whose mention was edited out or whose item was removed, and leaves the rest to the send-time check of TOKI-074. Reason: the job's key names the person and the message, so a cancel needs no search; a push already running or sent cannot be recalled (the table). Owner: Thanh Truong.
