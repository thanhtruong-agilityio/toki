# Notification decision engine design

Date: 2026-11-25 · Status: draft for review (concerns 1 to 6, 8 and 9 are open for the product owner)
Intent: docs/intents/notification-decision-engine.md (Thanh Truong)
Ticket: TOKI-073

## Goal
When something happens that may need a person, Toki must decide, for each person and for that one cause, whether they get a toast, a push notification and a sound. The inputs are spread over earlier work: the level and mute a person set for a conversation (TOKI-029, `conversation_members`), the three switches (TOKI-102), Pause and Focus time (TOKI-076), and what the person's windows are doing. Without one engine every screen would apply its own half, and the rules of D-071, D-072, D-088 and D-103 would drift apart.
This ticket builds that engine on the server, in the module `notifications`: the pure decision, the handler that runs it inside the transaction of the change, the numbered event that tells a person's open screens, the queued push, the record of which windows are in use, the record of what Pause or Focus held, the real answers behind the `reminderNotifier` that Later (TOKI-067) left open, and the toast of a snoozed Activity item that comes back. It also builds the last check that only a screen can make (the window is in use; the conversation or thread is on screen) and the report of whether the window is in use.
It does not draw a toast, play a sound, register a device or send a push (TOKI-075, TOKI-074), and it does not store Pause or Focus (TOKI-076). The lines with each are drawn in "Lines with the neighbouring tickets".

  - The pure decision from its inputs to `{ toast, sound, push, held }` (R2).
  - Who is considered and which causes make a notice (R3, R4).
  - The handler, its place in the list of `recordChange`, and the reads it needs (R5, R6).
  - The event `notification.created` (R7).
  - The push port and the retraction of a push not yet sent (R8, R13).
  - The table of open windows and the frame that fills it (R9).
  - The port for Pause and Focus, the record of held notices and its read and cleanup (R10); the cleanup also bounds the rows of open windows (R9).
  - The real `reminderNotifier` (R11) and the toast of a snoozed item that came back (R12).
  - The client: the notice reaches the centre and a waiting toast is dropped when its item or message goes (R14), the report of the window (R16), the check of a notice against the screen (R17), the notice centre with its waiting list (R18), and the state of what is on screen (R19).
  - Wiring, texts, nothing else (R20 to R23).
- Out (not now):
  - The toast component, its text, its 5-second life, its View button, stacking and the sound player with its Safari unlock: TOKI-075. This ticket hands it the notice and the answer "show" and "play".
  - Web Push: VAPID keys, the service worker, device registration, the browser permission, the card that offers notifications, the words of a system notification and its click: TOKI-074. This ticket decides that a person gets a push and hands over identifiers.
  - Pause and Focus time (the state, the requests, the end-of-period job, the strip, the summary toast and the count it shows): TOKI-076. This ticket gives it the answer a held notice produces and the count of them (R10); the summary toast is drawn by it, not through the notice centre.
  - Activity items, their rules and their events: TOKI-065. This ticket reads what a change made and never creates an item.
  - Reminders, their timer and their toast: TOKI-067. This ticket gives the push and the "held" answer (R11).
  - The three switches, their storage and their screen: TOKI-102 and TOKI-078. The level and mute of a conversation, their request and their screen: TOKI-029 and TOKI-035.
  - Email about missed mentions (a later phase, D-016) and guests (phase 5).

## Requirements
Shared
- R1. `packages/shared/src/notifications/` (exported from the package index; TOKI-102 adds `preferences.ts` to the same folder) gains `notice.ts` with: `NOTICE_KINDS` equal to `["message", "mention", "back_from_snooze"]` and its type; `NoticeDataSchema`, a strict object `{ kind, conversationId, messageId, parentId, alsoInChannel, actorId, itemId, viaChannel, preview, sound }` (`conversationId`, `messageId`, `parentId`, `actorId`, `itemId` and `preview` are a string or null, `alsoInChannel`, `viaChannel` and `sound` booleans) which is the `data` of the event of R7; `WindowFrameSchema`, a strict object `{ type: "window", inUse: boolean }` (R9); and the numbers `NOTICE_PREVIEW_MAX` (60 code points, the "first 60 characters" of F13), `NOTICE_WAIT_MAX_MS` (60,000: the longest a toast waits for the window to be used again, a new figure and a proposal, concern 5), `WINDOW_REFRESH_MS` (240,000) and `WINDOW_ROW_MAX_AGE_MS` (600,000), the two bounds of R9, code limits and no product rule. Check: schema tests accept each valid object and refuse an added key, a missing key, a kind outside the list and a non-boolean; a type test imports each name from `@toki/shared`.

The decision
- R2. `decideNotice(input)` in `apps/server/src/modules/notifications/decide.ts` is a pure function (no database, no clock, no random) from `{ kind, viaChannel, level, muted, preferences, held, windowInUse }` to `{ toast, sound, push, held }`, four booleans. `level` is the level that applies to the person for that conversation (the stored one, or `mentions` for a channel and `all` for a direct message or group: `effectiveNotificationLevel` of `@toki/shared`, D-046); `muted` is the person's mute; `preferences` is `NotificationPreferences` of TOKI-102; `held` says Pause or Focus covers this moment (R10); `windowInUse` says one of the person's windows is in use (R9). The rule, in this order:
  1. Whether the kind is allowed. `message`: only when `level` is `all` and `muted` is false. `mention`: only when `level` is not `none` (the level decides, D-103) and, when `muted` is true, only when `preferences.mentionsInMuted` is true and `viaChannel` is false (D-088; the `viaChannel` part is concern 4). `back_from_snooze` and `reminder` (R11) read neither the level nor the mute, because the person asked for them. A kind that is not allowed gives all four values false and is recorded nowhere.
  2. Hold. When allowed and `held` is true the answer is all false and `held` true (D-072: no toast, push or sound).
  3. Otherwise: `toast` is true for `message`, `mention` and `back_from_snooze` and false for `reminder` (its toast is TOKI-067's, R11); `sound` is `preferences.sound` for `message` and `mention` and false for the other two (concern 3); `push` is `preferences.desktop` and no window in use, and false for `back_from_snooze` (concern 3). `preferences.desktop` and `preferences.sound` are the only places the two switches act, and, in the proposal of concern 9, a toast is never switched off by a preference.
  Check: a table test over every combination of the four kinds, the three levels, `muted`, the three switches, `held` and `windowInUse`, written as data and compared with the rows below (it is the executable form of the table in Design); the cases of AC-039 (muted, switch on: a mention notifies; switch off: nothing), the level `none` (a mention gives nothing, also with the switch on and the channel muted, concern 10), 'Mentions only' (a plain message gives nothing, a mention does), 'All messages' (a plain message gives toast, push and sound as the switches allow), and a held mention (all false, `held` true); the function imports nothing and `Date.now(`, `new Date(` and `Math.random` do not occur in it.

- R3. Who is considered. For every cause of R4, a person is considered only when they are not the author or actor of the change (D-071), have a member row in the conversation (D-058, D-135) and have an `active` account (a deactivated person cannot sign in). The rule is written once, in `selectRecipients(client, conversationId, candidateIds, actorId)` of TOKI-065 R3, which returns the candidates that are not the actor, are members and have an `active` account, sorted by user id; this ticket calls it and does not copy it. `notifications` imports no other module, so `app.ts` passes it in as `deps.selectRecipients` (it asks TOKI-065 to export it from `modules/activity/index.ts`, Plan changes), and it runs with the `tx` of the change. Nobody is considered for a system line (D-100), for a reaction (F15, TOKI-046), for a pin, or for a message that is deleted. A person for whom Activity made no item is still considered for a `message` notice (R4). Check, with the real function and the people named in TOKI-065 R3 (the actor, a non-member, a person who left, a deactivated person, an active member): only the active member is notified for each of the causes; the actor is not notified of their own message; a system line, a reaction and a deleted message give no notice; a test with a fake `selectRecipients` that returns nobody gives no notice, so the handler holds no second copy of the rule.

- R4. Which causes make a notice. The handler (R5) looks at three kinds of change and nothing else, and reads the `cause` of each effect of `change.activity` (TOKI-065 R13: `created`, `reaction_added`, `reaction_removed`, `converted`, `woke`), because `isNew` and `backFromSnooze` alone cannot tell a wake from a reaction on a woken item:
  (a) `message_created` of a message of kind `user` that is not deleted. Each person in `change.activity` with an `upsert` effect of kind `mention` and `cause` `created` gets a `mention` notice (the item exists, so the person was named: TOKI-065 R4, `viaChannel` from the item). Every other person considered (R3) whose effective level is `all` gets a `message` notice, when the message is in the main list or was also sent to the channel (D-160: "unread count and notification level apply"); a reply that was not also sent to the channel gives no `message` notice (concern 1). A person has at most one notice for one message: the `mention` notice wins.
  (b) `message_edited`: each person in `change.activity` with an `upsert` effect of kind `mention` and `cause` `created` (the edit added the mention) or `converted` (the person followed the thread, so TOKI-065 R4 turned their `thread` item into a `mention` item with `isNew` false) gets a `mention` notice; nobody else, and a person who was already told gets nothing, because Activity makes no effect for them (F16: "Editing to add a mention notifies the newly mentioned person").
  (c) `activity_changed` (TOKI-065 R13, R21) with an `upsert` effect whose `cause` is `woke`: a `back_from_snooze` notice for that person (F27: "returns to the top, unread, labelled 'Back from snooze', with a toast"). An effect with any other cause never makes this notice, although the item may still have `backFromSnooze` true, so a new reaction on a woken reaction item, or an edit that converts a woken item, makes no second toast.
  Reaction, being added, a changed role, a message deleted by an admin and a thread item (kind `thread`) make no notice: a reaction is explicit (F15, TS-148), the others are concerns 1 and 2, whose proposal is that they stay Activity items only. The table of causes is one constant, `NOTICE_CAUSES`, so that the answer to a concern is one line. Check: with a recording handler and Activity effects as input, each case of (a), (b) and (c) gives the notices named; the effects of a reaction, of `added_to_channel`, `role_changed`, `message_deleted` and `thread` give none; one person never has two notices for one message; an edit that adds @Sam gives Sam one notice and gives Mai, already told, none; an edit of a reply that names Mai, who follows the thread (her `thread` item becomes a `mention` item, `cause` `converted`), gives Mai one notice; an edit that turns a mention into a `thread` item gives none; a reply that was not also sent to the channel gives no `message` notice to a person at level `all`; a reply also sent to the channel gives one; a new reaction on an item that is woken and unread (`backFromSnooze` true, `cause` `reaction_added`) gives no `back_from_snooze` notice, and the wake of the same item (`cause` `woke`) gives one.

The handler and its reads
- R5. `createNotificationHandler(deps)` of `modules/notifications/handler.ts` is a `ChangeHandler` (TOKI-037 R18) that does R2 to R4 for `message_created`, `message_edited` and `activity_changed`, does the retraction of R13 for `message_deleted` and `message_edited`, and nothing for any other type. It sits after the activity handler (TOKI-065 R13, which it reads) and before the event handler (it returns what that handler writes), so the list built by `buildChangeHandlers` is: system line, the handlers that take locks or add jobs, activity, notifications, events. It takes no lock and adds jobs and rows only, so the counter lock stays the last (TOKI-036 R18). It returns `{ notices }`, an array of `NoticeEffect`: `{ userId, workspaceId, data: NoticeData }` for each person with `toast` true. `createRecordChange` is changed once more, in the way TOKI-065 R13 changed it: a handler may return `{ message?, activity?, notices? }`, `Change` gains the optional field `notices`, and the handlers after read it. Whichever of this ticket and TOKI-065 is built second adds its field to the union the first one wrote and says so in its pull request. The handler works in a fixed number of statements, whatever the number of people: one read of candidates (R6), `selectRecipients` of R3 (two statements, TOKI-065 R3), one read of preferences, `loadNotificationPreferencesFor(tx, workspaceId, userIds)` of TOKI-102 R8, which returns a map the handler reads once and never one read for each person, one of the hold at `change.at` (R10), one of the open windows (R9) and only when at least one candidate has `desktop` on, and, when a notice is held, one insert of held notices (R10), and one job insert for each person who gets a push (R8). A handler that finds no notice returns nothing and issues no read after the candidates. Order of writes: the rows of `held_notices` and the push jobs are written in ascending user id (`selectRecipients` returns that order), as TOKI-065 R25 does for its rows, so two changes never wait on each other in opposite order; the inserts take `FOR KEY SHARE` on the `users` and `workspaces` rows, which the account locks of member-management (R39) do not conflict with, and `push.enqueue` and `push.cancel` touch job rows only. Check: the statement count (the wrapper of TOKI-031 R14) is the same for a channel of 5 people and one of 200 at level `all` for everybody, apart from the job inserts; a change of another type issues no statement; a handler that throws rolls the change back; the handler list has the activity handler before this one and the event handler after it; two changes that notify the same people at once finish with no deadlock (two real connections).

- R6. One new read of `conversations`, `listNotifyCandidates(client, conversationId, extraUserIds, includeLevelAll)`, exported from its `index.ts` (TOKI-028 R18, the export test is updated). One statement over `conversation_members` joined to its conversation. It returns, for each member row, `{ userId, level, muted }` with `level` the effective level (stored, or the default of the conversation's kind, D-046), for the members whose effective level is `all` when `includeLevelAll` is true, and for the members named in `extraUserIds`; it never returns a person who is not a member. So the cost grows with the people who can be notified and not with the members of a big channel. Check: a channel of 200 where 3 chose `all` and 2 are named returns 5 rows; a direct message returns the other person at level `all` by default; a person with a stored level `none` is returned with `none`; a non-member named in `extraUserIds` is not returned; the statement count is one.

The event
- R7. Each `NoticeEffect` is written by the event handler of TOKI-038 (R20 there, "Rows of later tickets") as one event of kind `notification.created`, scope `user` (the person), no `conversationId`, `data` `NoticeData` of R1, after the other events of the change; the effects of one person are written one each. `preview` is the first `NOTICE_PREVIEW_MAX` code points of the message text for `message` and `mention`, and, for `back_from_snooze`, the first `NOTICE_PREVIEW_MAX` code points of the `preview` that the woken Activity item already carries (TOKI-065 R15; no extra read), so the toast can say who and what; it is null for an empty text and for an item with no preview (a reaction, a role change); `actorId` is the author of the message for `message` and `mention` and the `actorId` of the Activity item for `back_from_snooze` (null when the item has none); `sound` is the `sound` of R2; `itemId` is the Activity item for `mention` and `back_from_snooze` and null for `message`; `alsoInChannel` is true when the message of a reply was also sent to the channel. The schema joins the registry of `packages/shared/src/realtime/events.ts`; `kinds.ts` gets a row saying it changes no map; `EventChange` gains the optional field `notices?`. A user event goes to every connection of that person and to nobody else (TOKI-038 R16) and is replayed to that person (TOKI-039). Check, with the real handler and fake sockets: a mention writes one `notification.created` for the mentioned person, received by both tabs of that person and by nobody else; a rolled-back change leaves no event; a person with `toast` false gets none; the payload passes the schema; a woken mention item gives a `back_from_snooze` notice with the item's actor and the first 60 characters of its preview, and a woken reaction item one with the actor and a null `preview`; the registry and `kinds.ts` tests pass.

Push
- R8. The push is handed over, not sent. `PushPort` is `{ enqueue(tx, notice: PushNotice): Promise<void>; cancel(tx, userId, messageId): Promise<void> }` with `PushNotice` `{ userId, workspaceId, kind: "message" | "mention" | "reminder", conversationId, viaChannel, messageId?, parentId?, actorId?, itemId? }`: identifiers and booleans only, no text, so a job payload obeys TOKI-064 R7, and an id that does not apply is an absent property and never `null`, because the job schema of TOKI-074 R12 holds no null id and TOKI-064 R7 walks the allowed property kinds of every job schema. For each person with `push` true the handler calls `push.enqueue(tx, notice)` inside the transaction of the change, so a rollback leaves no push. A push is one per person and not one per device: TOKI-074 sends it to every registered device of that person whose browser allows it (D-142; the table row "App closed on every device: to every device with notifications on"). Until TOKI-074 gives the real port (its R13 replaces the stand-in and its name), `app.ts` and `buildJobDeps` build a stand-in that does nothing, named so in the code. The key TOKI-074 uses for the job is `push:<userId>:<messageId>` (`push:<userId>:<itemId>` for a reminder), so `cancel` can find it (R13). `back_from_snooze` never reaches the port (concern 3). Check: with a fake port, a mention for a person with `desktop` on and no window in use calls `enqueue` once, with identifiers and no text and no `null` property; a person with `desktop` off, one in use, one held and one at level `none` call it zero times; a transaction that throws after the call leaves no call that was committed (a fake that records per transaction); a type test checks that `PushNotice` holds no string property other than identifiers and the kind, and no property that can be `null`.

The windows in use
- R9. Whether a person has Toki in use is stored, because the worker process that fires a reminder has no sockets (TOKI-064 R9, R17). The table `notification_windows` (Drizzle table in `modules/notifications/schema.ts`, one migration `<number>_notification_engine_tables` together with R10, forward-only; not exported from `index.ts`): `connection_id` text primary key (1 to 64 characters), `workspace_id` and `user_id` uuid not null with foreign keys to `workspaces(id)` and `users(id)` written as SQL in the migration (TOKI-019 R22, `ON DELETE RESTRICT`), `in_use` boolean not null, `updated_at` timestamptz not null with no database default; an index on (`user_id`) where `in_use`. The test "R23: one TRUNCATE empties every table" in `apps/server/tests/conversation-tables.db.test.ts` refuses a `TRUNCATE` when a table that points to a named table is not named, and TOKI-036 R24 says each ticket adds its tables there: `notification_windows` and `held_notices` (R10) are added to that list and to any other emptying step of the harness in this ticket, and the test passes after the migration.
  The client frame `{ "type": "window", "inUse": boolean }` (R1, R16) is registered with `onClientFrame("window", handler)` of the gateway, whose fourth argument is the `connectionId` of the sending connection (TOKI-038 R27). `registerWindowTracking` also uses `onConnectionOpened` and `onConnectionClosed` (TOKI-038 R31; a listener gets `{ connectionId, accountId }`, the opened ones are called before any frame of that socket reaches a handler, the closed one once for every registered connection). It keeps, in memory, the set `live` of connection ids (added by the opened listener, removed by the closed one) and one chain of work for each connection id, so that the frame handler and the close of one connection run one after the other and never overlap. The frame handler puts its work on the chain: it reads the person's workspace once (`getAccountsByIds`, injected), then checks that `connectionId` is still in `live`, and only then upserts the row of that connection with `clock.now()`. The closed listener removes the id from `live` and puts the delete of the row on the same chain, after any upsert already queued. So a frame that is still being handled when the connection closes either writes before the delete and is deleted by it, or finds the id gone and writes nothing, and no row outlives its connection. Two guards for a lost process: when the app starts and registers the listeners it deletes every row, because no connection survives a restart (a client sends its state again at each `ready`, R16); and a row whose `updated_at` is older than `WINDOW_ROW_MAX_AGE_MS` is never read as in use (the reads of R5 and R11 filter on it), and the job of R10 (d) deletes it. A window that is truly in use stays fresh because the client sends its state again every `WINDOW_REFRESH_MS` (R16; 4 minutes against a bound of 10, so one lost frame does not make a live row stale). `windowInUse` of R2 is "a row of that person has `in_use` true and is not older than the bound at `change.at`". The rule is for the person and not for the device: one window in use stops the push for all of the person's devices (the table rows: with the app open and in use there is no push; "App closed on every device" is the case where there is none). That reading is a proposal, because the Blueprint rows do not say what a laptop in use and a phone with Toki closed get (concern 8). A frame that fails `WindowFrameSchema` is ignored. Check: a frame of true sets the row, a frame of false sets it false, two connections of one person with one true give `windowInUse` true, closing the true one gives false, a restart empties the table, the frame of another person's connection never changes a person's row, a deactivated person's closed connection leaves no row; a frame whose handler is still waiting for its account read when the connection closes (the read held by a fake, the close delivered, the read released) leaves no row, and a frame handled after the close of its connection leaves no row; a row with `in_use` true whose `updated_at` is 10 minutes and 1 ms old gives `windowInUse` false with the `TestClock`, and one 9 minutes 59 seconds old gives true; the migration check reads columns, keys and index from the catalog, `db:generate` reports no change, `updated_at` comes from the `TestClock`, and the one-`TRUNCATE` test passes after the migration.

Pause and Focus
- R10. The seam for Pause and Focus time and the record of what it held. (a) `HoldPort` is `{ listHeld(client, userIds, at): Promise<ReadonlySet<string>> }`: the ids among `userIds` for whom Pause or Focus time covers the moment `at` (milliseconds on the clock of TOKI-008), in one statement for any number of people. The owner of the state and of the real port is TOKI-076; until it is built `app.ts` and `buildJobDeps` give a stand-in that holds nobody, named so in the code. This ticket calls it with `clock.now()` and never reads a Pause or Focus table (D-072). (b) A notice that is allowed and held is recorded: the table `held_notices` (same migration): `id` uuid primary key (UUIDv7 default), `workspace_id`, `user_id` (foreign keys as in R9), `kind` (`message`, `mention` or `back_from_snooze`; a check), `conversation_id` (null allowed), `message_id` (null allowed), `created_at` timestamptz not null with no database default, an index on (`user_id`, `created_at`). The handler inserts the rows of one change in one statement, in the transaction of the change, with `created_at` equal to `change.at`, the same instant at which it read the hold, so a notice at the edge of a Pause is counted in the period that held it (the wake job has its own `change.at`). The test of R9 (one `TRUNCATE`) covers this table too. (c) `countHeldNotices(client, userId, fromMs, toMs)` of `modules/notifications`, exported from `index.ts`, returns the number of rows of that person with `created_at` at or after `fromMs` and before `toMs`. (d) The job type `notifications.prune`, scheduled hourly (`{ cron: "0 * * * *" }`, empty payload), deletes `held_notices` rows older than `HELD_NOTICE_KEEP_HOURS` (72, a bound that exceeds the longest Pause, "Until tomorrow 08:00" of the prototype's `PAUSE_OPTS`, and is no product rule; concern 13) and `notification_windows` rows older than `WINDOW_ROW_MAX_AGE_MS` (R9), each in batches of 500 (the size of TOKI-065 R22); it is declared in `apps/server/src/job-types.ts` with its handler in `job-handlers.ts` (TOKI-064 R9). A held notice makes no event and no push, and the Activity item, the badge and the unread count are not touched (D-072: "Badges still update"). Check: with a fake port that holds Lan, a mention gives no event, no push call and one row; Mai, not held, gets the event; a plain message to a held person at level `none` writes no row (it would not have notified); `countHeldNotices` includes the lower bound and excludes the upper, ignores another person's rows and rows of a rolled-back change; the prune job with the `TestClock` removes a row 72 h 1 min old and keeps one 71 h 59 min old, removes a stale window row and keeps a fresh one, and removes 1,200 rows in batches of 500; the migration check as in R9.

Reminders and the snooze
- R11. The real `reminderNotifier`. `createReminderNotifier(deps)` of `modules/notifications` returns the `ReminderNotifier` of TOKI-067 R11 (`isHeld(userId, at)`, `notify(input)`), built by `buildJobDeps` in place of the stand-in of that ticket. `isHeld` is `holdPort.listHeld(db, [userId], at)` holding the person. `notify(input)` (called by the fire handler only when the reminder is not held, after its commit) reads the person's preferences (`loadNotificationPreferencesFor(db, workspaceId, [userId])` of TOKI-102 R8) and the open windows (R9, with the bound on `updated_at`), applies `decideNotice` with kind `reminder` and `held` false (it does not read the hold again: `isHeld` was read once by the fire handler for this firing, and the window between that read and a Pause that starts is accepted, Design) and, when `push` is true, calls `push.enqueue(db, notice)` with `kind` `reminder`, `conversationId` and `messageId` of the input, `itemId` the Later item and no actor. It writes no event and no row: the toast of a reminder is drawn by `LaterHost` from the `later.changed` event, which carries `held` (TOKI-067 R22), and the sound is concern 3. A reminder is not tied to a level or a mute (R2). A failing `enqueue` throws; the fire handler of TOKI-067 R8 catches it, writes one `warn` line and completes the job, and a retry of that job would find no row, so the push of that reminder is lost and is not retried (the item stays due, and its row, badge and toast are Later's). Nothing in this ticket or in TOKI-067 retries it; TOKI-074 sends what it is handed and does not see a push that was never handed over. The real port only inserts a job, so it fails only when the database does. ## Design
### Where it runs and why
The decision runs on the server, in the transaction of the change that causes it (`recordChange`, TOKI-037 R18), because three of its inputs exist only there: who follows a thread (Activity), what every other person set for the conversation, and Pause and Focus. A decision made in the transaction can never describe a message that rolled back, and its event and its push are ordered with the message's own event. The one input that only a screen knows (is the window in use, which conversation and thread are on screen, is the person at the bottom of the list) is applied last by the screen (R17), because the server would be one network round trip late and would announce what the person is already reading (F36).
Two outputs leave the server. The in-app toast and sound travel as the numbered user event `notification.created` (R7), to every open connection of the person, and each screen judges it for itself. The push is a queued job (R8) for TOKI-074, decided here by the rule "no window of this person is in use" (R9).

### The window frame and the push rule
A client reports `{ type: "window", inUse }` when it connects and each time the window enters or leaves use (R16). The server keeps one row for each open connection (R9). The push rule is for the person: when any of the person's windows is in use, no push is queued for the person at all, because the person is looking at Toki and the toast is the notification; when none is, a push is queued. This reads "App open … Push none" and "App closed on every device … Push to every device" of the table, and decides the mixed case (a laptop in use, a phone with Toki closed) by a proposal that the table does not make (concern 8). The conversation that is on screen needs no rule of its own on the server: a conversation on screen means a window in use, which already stops the push (F36: "No push arrives while that very conversation is on screen").

### Errors
- A failure of the handler rolls the whole change back, as every handler does (TOKI-037 R18). A failing `PushPort.enqueue` in the handler is a failure of the change; the real port only inserts a job, so it fails only when the database does.
- A `window` frame that is handled after its connection closed writes nothing (R9); a bad frame is ignored; one over 4,096 bytes or beyond the rate closes the connection (TOKI-038 R25).
- A notice that fails its schema on the client is ignored.

## Data safety
- A notice event is for one person only (scope `user`); no other person's connection receives it.
- `preview` is a copy of the first 60 code points of a message in a user event that stays in the log for its retention window (TOKI-036 R20). The `message.created` event of the same message holds the same text for everyone in the conversation, so this adds no new reader; a deleted message's text is emptied (TOKI-047) and a message that is deleted makes no notice.
- Push jobs and held rows hold identifiers only; no log line holds a preview or a value of a switch (R21).
- Whether a person has a window in use is private to the server: no route and no event gives it to another person, and presence (TOKI-055) shows nothing of it.

## Areas of concern
1. Thread replies. Not decided. (a) Whether a person who follows a thread and gets a `thread` item from a new reply also gets a toast, a push or a sound: F14 says only that the reply gives a thread item, and row 13 of the table covers a reply that mentions the person. (b) Whether a reply in a conversation at the level 'All messages' that was not also sent to the channel notifies: D-160 settles only the reply also sent to the channel. (c) Whether an open thread panel counts as the message being on screen and as seen for the sound (R17), the way row 1 treats the bottom of the main list. Proposal, not a decision: (a) no, a thread item is an Activity item only; (b) no, such a reply notifies only when it mentions the person; (c) yes, an open panel is the screen. R4 and R17 follow the proposal; the answer to (a) or (b) is one line of `NOTICE_CAUSES`. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
2. Items that have no stated notification. Not decided. F22, F40 and F42 give an Activity item for being added to a channel, a changed role and a message deleted by an admin and say nothing of a toast, push or sound. Proposal, not a decision: none of them notifies; the badge and the list are their notice. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
3. Sound and push for a reminder and for a snoozed item that comes back. Not decided. F28 gives a reminder a toast and a push and no sound; F27 gives the item that comes back a toast and no push or sound. Proposal, not a decision: build what the journeys say and no more (R2, R11, R12). Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
4. `@channel` in a muted channel. Not decided. D-088 lets a mention notify in a muted channel while the switch is on; row 12 of the table says an `@channel` notifies "per the channel's level" and says nothing of Mute, and F13 says every member gets "the same". Proposal, not a decision: `@channel` counts as a mention for the level (so it notifies at 'Mentions only') but is not the personal mention that D-088 lets through a mute, so a muted channel stays quiet for it. R2 builds the proposal behind one condition. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
5. How long a toast may wait, and a notice replayed after a loss. Not decided. The table says a toast waits "when she returns" and gives no limit, and an event replayed after a lost connection raises the same question. TOKI-067 concern 8 proposes no figure: it proposes that an event applied while catching up shows no toast (a `replayed` flag, comparing no two clocks) and names a maximum age against the device clock only as the alternative it rejects, because a device clock that is wrong defeats it. Proposal, not a decision: a replayed notice shows no toast and plays no sound (the same rule as TOKI-067 R22, with the connection's catch-up state, so the reminder's toast and the notice's toast of one catch-up follow one rule); a toast that waits for the window to be used again is dropped after `NOTICE_WAIT_MAX_MS` (60,000 ms), a figure this spec proposes, counted on the device's own clock from the notice's arrival, so it compares no two clocks and a wrong device clock does not matter; the row, the badge and the item are enough after that. A person who comes back from a short loss gets no toast for what arrived during it, as for a reminder. A consequence TOKI-075 accepts and leaves to the owner: the sound of a notice that waits is judged once, at arrival, so it can be heard up to `NOTICE_WAIT_MAX_MS` before its toast. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
6. A window that is in use but not used. Not decided. "In use" is visible and focused (TOKI-053 R21, its concern 4), with no idle time, so a person who walks away from a focused window gets no push. Proposal, not a decision: the same reading as the read pointer, because the push rule and the read rule would otherwise disagree about one window; the stricter reading adds the last input time to `WindowUse` and to the frame (R16) and changes nothing else. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
7. Where the decision runs. Decided in this spec: on the server, in the transaction of the change, with a numbered user event for the toast and sound and a queued push, and a last check on the screen. Reason: the followers, the other people's settings and Pause and Focus are known only to the server; an event written in the transaction cannot describe a rolled-back message; the screen alone knows what it shows. Cost: one user event for each person who may get a toast (for `@channel` in a channel of 200 that is up to 199 more events while the counter lock is held, on top of those of TOKI-065 concern 12; TOKI-089 measures it), and one job insert for each person who gets a push. A live frame sent after the commit would avoid the lock, but the handler has no after-commit seam. Owner: Thanh Truong.
8. The push rule for a person with several windows. Not decided. The Blueprint rows say the push is "Yes" when the app is hidden and goes "to every device" when it is closed on every device; the mixed case (a laptop in use, a phone with Toki closed) is in no row and in no decision. Proposal, not a decision: the state of the windows is stored in a table and is for the person, so one window in use stops the push for every device of that person; the behaviour is in R9 and the Design. Reason: the reminder fires in the worker, which has no sockets; the rows read "App open … in use: Push none" and "App closed on every device: Push to every device", both about the person. Cost: a write for each change of a window (a handful a day for one person), one frame every 4 minutes for each window in use, and a delete at each close; a phone in the pocket with Toki closed gets nothing while a laptop is in use. The alternative, a push to the devices with no window in use, needs the device of each window and is a larger design. The card of F36 and the settings are TOKI-074's. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
9. What the three switches control. Not decided. The prototype's phone label of 'Desktop notifications' is "Push notifications", and F13 says "A sound plays if sound is on" and gives the toast no condition; D-142 is about who owns the switches and D-176 (their defaults) is open, so no decision says what each switch controls, and TOKI-102 leaves the meaning to this ticket. Proposal, not a decision: 'Desktop notifications' controls the push, 'Play a sound' the sound, and the toast is controlled by neither; 'Notify me for @mentions even in muted channels' is the muted exception of R2. R2 builds the proposal in one place, so another answer is one line there. Owner: Thanh Truong (product owner).
10. How the level and the mute combine. Decided in this spec: both must allow. The level 'Nothing' stops a mention even in a muted channel with the switch on, and Mute stops a plain message even at 'All messages'. Reason: row 7 of the table says 'Nothing' gives none for a mention, and rows 8 and 9 say a mention in a muted channel notifies only with the switch on; D-103 says the level decides; D-088 adds an exception to Mute only. Owner: Thanh Truong.
11. The seam for Pause and Focus, and the record of what was held. Decided in this spec: TOKI-076 owns the state and the real `HoldPort`; this ticket owns the rows of held notices and `countHeldNotices`, kept 72 hours. Reason: only this engine knows what would have notified, and a message in a conversation at 'All messages' has no Activity item to count; the owner of the state is the owner of the time. The summary's count rule and wording are TOKI-076's. Owner: Thanh Truong.
12. Reminders. Decided in this spec: Later keeps the due state, the event and the toast (TOKI-067 R22); this ticket gives `isHeld` and the push (R11). Reason: the toast is drawn from Later's own event, so a second toast event would double it; the push needs the window state and the preferences that live here. Owner: Thanh Truong.
13. Bounds that are not product rules. Decided in this spec: the preview is 60 code points (F13), held rows are kept 72 hours and pruned hourly (R10), a window row is ignored after 10 minutes and refreshed every 4 (R9, R16), and a notice event is one for each person. Reason: a preview and a table need a size; 72 hours exceeds the longest Pause ("Until tomorrow 08:00" is the longest choice of the prototype's `PAUSE_OPTS`); the window bounds only guard a row that a lost process left. They are constants of the code and can be changed. The 60-second wait of concern 5 is a product proposal and is not in this list. Owner: Thanh Truong.
14. Retraction of a push not yet sent. Decided in this spec: this ticket cancels the job of a person whose mention was edited out and of a person whose item a delete removed (the `remove` effects of those two changes only), and leaves the rest to the send-time check of TOKI-074. Reason: the job's key names the person and the message, so a cancel needs no search; a push already running or sent cannot be recalled (the table). Owner: Thanh Truong.
15. Size of the ticket. Decided in this spec: it stays one ticket of size L, the size the plan gives (intent, constraints). It has 23 requirements across the server, `shared`, `client-core`, `apps/web`, one migration and one job. The client half (R14 and R16 to R19) needs only the event and the frame of R1 and R7 and can be built after the server half in the same ticket; if the lead splits it, that is the line. Reason: the pure decision, the event and the screen check are one rule and one table of fifteen rows, which the scenario tests of R15 run end to end. Owner: Thanh Truong.
